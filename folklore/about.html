The collection

Stories, sources and the reader

Swiss Folklore brings together {{ site.data.folklore.archive.stories.size }} published tales in English and their original languages, with historical source links and notes on unfamiliar words and customs.

Begin wherever curiosity takes you

Choose a short tale, follow a topic, explore a place, or search the full text. The alphabetical catalogue keeps every published story within reach.

Translations and notes

Ted Tschopp is the translator and curator. The translation disclosure explains the use of AI assistance. Reader notes give context; the original text remains alongside the English so a reader can compare the wording.

Historical editions

The source credit on each tale identifies the historical expression used. A collector’s name, an old plot or an oral recollection alone does not establish the publication history of a later retelling. Stories awaiting adequate source evidence remain outside the reading collection.

Different tellings

Similar titles can belong to different stories. Collector or place names help distinguish those versions in the catalogue. Shared pages identify their source records; unresolved comparisons remain separate.

Follow the discussion

Every reading page links to its discussion and Webmentions. Use the story’s permanent URL when responding elsewhere.
