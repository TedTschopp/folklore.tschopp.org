This Schatz was permitted to visit the three sisters every evening, either with other companions or alone, as has long been custom and practice among the “Jung-Volk” in the countryside. Only on Thursday evenings were the young men forbidden to come. “We have work to do then,” the three girls protested. Every Thursday evening the doors and windows were closed with shutters, and none of the three girls appeared again until the next morning. At last Nonna's sweetheart became curious about what great and unusual work the three always had to perform specifically on Thursday evening. After the bottle had been “versorgt”—put back in its keeping place—the three seated themselves on broomsticks and said, “Now, away.” In an instant they were no longer to be seen. The young man now knew the truth about his beloved Nonna and her sisters: all three were witches. He did not deliberate long. He found a way to slip into the house, lifted the stone slab in the kitchen, took out the little bottle, did everything exactly as the three sisters had done, and at the end likewise said, “Now, away.” Like the three girls, he flew up through the chimney and out into the night, not knowing where he was going, until at last he arrived before a beautiful house in which he saw light.", "Because the door of this house was closed, he knocked. A woman looked out the window and asked what he wanted. “I want to enter the house,” he replied. The woman withdrew and consulted with those assembled inside, then came down and opened the door for him. What he now saw was a great gathering consisting entirely of women, among them the three sisters, who stared at him in astonishment as though they wished to ask how he too had come there. The dancing began without delay, and the young man enjoyed himself heartily. After a while Nonna's Schatz was asked whether he liked the company. “Yes, why not?
