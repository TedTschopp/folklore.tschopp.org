Around Schwyz, houses containing infected people were ‘placed under ban and nailed shut.’ Food and everything necessary were passed to the unfortunate inhabitants from outside through an opening made for that purpose in the upper part of the house. Such holes could still be seen on many houses long afterward. In the parish of Schwyz alone, 3600 people are said to have died at that time, and on one occasion 99 maidens were lowered together into the same grave. Legend relates that a voice finally called down from the air: ‘Eat Strenzen and Bibernellen, so that you do not all perish!’ With the winter of 1612 the affliction ceased. Around this time, a man in Schwyz—others say in Arth—had a strange apparition. One morning while dressing, he saw near the neighboring church a great funeral procession, all its participants dressed in black, making its way to the cemetery. He saw himself, just as he was, with a stocking over his shoulder, bringing up the end of the procession.
