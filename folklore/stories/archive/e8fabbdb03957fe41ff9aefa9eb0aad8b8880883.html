It is often heard toward autumn as well, though always under a clear sky. ‘There will be rain,’ some then say; ‘soon the little white clouds will gather.’—‘No, those are the Rottalherren,’ others among the people as far as Murten and Solothurn maintain, or ‘the Rottalherren are drilling; different weather is coming.’ In the Bernese Seeland, this noise is also called the Murtengeschütz. The Rottal, or Rotental, was formerly a beautiful Blümlisalp on the southwestern side of the Jungfrau, from which a pass led into Valais. The inhabitants of this part of the country would have had a happier lot had the arbitrary rule of cruel lords not weighed upon them at that time. No one was secure in his property, and even the women and maidens of the valley did not escape these tyrants' persecutions. But their godless conduct could not remain unpunished. Heaven's wrath awoke, and once, when one of them—the worst of all under whom the country had suffered—pursued a young shepherd girl with his savage lust, a great black billy goat that had never before been seen on the Alp suddenly bounded to the fleeing maiden's aid. With a terrible thrust, it hurled the pursuer over the steep cliff into the abyss. At the same time, the Firnen and ice mountains trembled all around, and beneath rolling pieces of rock and masses of ice, the once-flourishing and fertile valley became the sad glacial waste it remains today.
