Hauri v. Reitnau.) ", "Sage aus Reitnau" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "The mountain on whose slope the village of Reitnau lies, in the Zofingen district, forms three successive tiers, each separated from the next by a small high valley. The uppermost is called the Hochthal and was the site of Reitnau Castle, some ruins of which can still be seen on a spur. “If the farmer can mow and cut grain on Sundays,” the lord of the castle used to say, “then surely it would be strange if I could not hunt on feast days as well. And what better could a knight wish for than to hunt forever and ever?” His wish was granted. The knight can still be heard calling his halloo and blowing his horn as he rushes through the air with a whole cloud of horses and dogs. From the castle, a now completely ruined staircase descends to the second mountain tier, to a meadow clearing surrounded by trees and known as the Kegelweg. Whenever the weather is about to change, the Zwingherr can be heard playing skittles there at night. A pair of vigorous young men from Brittnau, who took offense at such superstitious village tales and put no stock in them, climbed up one evening beneath a rainy sky to convince themselves with their own eyes that these supposed events were groundless. Suddenly they heard the balls roll and the pins fall. Since they could find no person anywhere on the mountain, they fled in terror, one in one direction and the other in another.
