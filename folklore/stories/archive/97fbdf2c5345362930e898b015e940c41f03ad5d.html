It is said once to have descended more than a mile farther down the valley; indeed, when one looks from the heights at the moraines and Gletscherrüfen visible everywhere, one can be convinced that the former gigantic scale of the Schalbet Glacier was no exaggeration. The strict vows are still stronger evidence: for example, the prohibition against dancing except at weddings; the prohibition against gambling for money; the prohibition against irrigating on Sundays and holy days; the requirement that the After-Sant-Jodrutag be celebrated like the holy day itself; and the requirement that a verwahrte Person from each house walk in procession, fasting, to the Ried Glacier and back, etc. etc. Two mountain communities bordering this glacier, I say, bound themselves for all time to such onerous promises because of the damage caused by the glacier’s vigorous growth. The legend also relates that two devout missionary fathers were summoned to halt this destructive advance. Through exorcisms they were to command the monster’s devastating tread to stop, and this is said to have happened. It seems that this glacier was more obedient than the Gorner Glacier, which did not obey the spiritual authority of the Reverend Pastor Schulzki when he tried to banish it backward as an exorcist, but advanced more strongly one year later than ever before—or so the people say. — In Schalbetten, people still point out the place to which it was driven back. The men of God are said to have been unable to force it any farther because the glacier was so full of poor souls who had to perform their Abbüssung there and who, if it were made too small, would not have found enough room within it.
