People from both shores of the lake went there for sermons and also brought their dead there. So it happened when the wealthy lord in the castle at Lütwil died and was to be buried on Staufberg. Before the people of Lütwil set out, however, they drank so much in the castle—of which nothing at all can be seen today—that afterward they staggered back and forth in the procession and finally even dropped the coffin. Usually on the third day of the new moon, they set out from Lütwil toward the village of Dürrenäsch; there they put the bier down for the first time and rest. Then they cross the Aescher Feld toward the Retterswiler Dörfli, where they rest and change bearers again. Farther down in the Galgli, a thicket on the Lenzburg highway, they halt once more and feed the horses a little oats. Then they go toward Seon, through the upper village and into the Chilchthal. From this mountain defile they cross to the Heiliggraben and wait, because a second host joins them there. Now they proceed without another stop up to Staufberg. Above in the churchyard, everyone takes off his hat and covers his face with a black cloth, while the priests kneel at the grave and pray for half an hour.
