It weighs more than the largest house in Egliswil but lies at an unreachable depth—to the good fortune of the region. For if a human hand should ever touch it, it will disappear into the ground together with the oak, and an enormous torrent will surge out of the resulting hollow, putting the entire valley under water and thereby restoring the ancient lake that formerly covered Aargau. It was called Hom-Isen, and the many Homberg mountains of our canton derive their names from it; at least, that is how the Bannwart explained the name when he related the foregoing. One place on this mountain is called the Rothe Hüsli, and another in the forest the Metzg’. A dark atrocity occurred here, as a result of which the place is still haunted today." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Bannwart”: a local warden responsible for protected or bounded communal land, often forest; duties varied by canton and period, so ‘forest warden’ would be too narrow in every context." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
