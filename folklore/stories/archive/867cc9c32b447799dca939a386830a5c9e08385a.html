At night, they often fastened two cows to one chain; startled the cattle from their resting places and scattered them on the Staffeln of the Alpen; crashed about in houses and stables; placed chairs and dishes in people's paths at night so that they fell over them; pulled the bedding from sleepers; and so on. In a house on the Natisserberg, there was supposedly a Bozo that made a noise like wool being carded. A brazen lad who found this spirit-noise too troublesome deliberately made indecent music in bed. The spirit became so angry that it pulled away his bedding and smacked the ‘wind instrument’ so soundly that he could not sit down for a long while afterward. People also tell of a laughing spirit that suddenly raised such laughter behind young lovers that mountain and forest echoed. Two hunters spending the night in a stable began talking about the laughing spirit. One mocked it and claimed that it had been a fool and a miser toward its wife. As the two lay upon the straw, they heard the laughing spirit beside them, laughing shrilly. It resembled a large gray woman. Still laughing, it took out a Fäsche and fäschte the mocker like a baby, then placed him on its lap and spooned gray porridge into his mouth until he suffocated. It laid down the swaddled corpse and departed again with dreadful laughter.
