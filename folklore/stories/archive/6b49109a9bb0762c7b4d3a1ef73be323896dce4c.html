One of the local women had a beautiful cow that had just calved and had a fine udder. As the woman was driving this cow across the square, she said to herself, “If only the witch is not somewhere about where she can see my cow; otherwise she will surely take its milk from me or contrive some devilry against it.” And sure enough, as they drove the cow across the square, the witch was sitting right in front of the house. The witch said to them, “May God grant good fortune to this cow.” The woman answered her, “Yes, you hellish witch, I already know what sort of fortune you wish it.” And instantly the cow fell dead. The people who had seen this quickly ran to the parish priest in Stalden and accused the supposed witch of having killed the beautiful cow on the spot. When the priest came to the dead cow in the square, he told the crowd with an angry look, “Leave this person in peace. She is no witch; indeed, if you wish to know, she is better than you. Because today you have stolen her honor, God has allowed, as your punishment, the devil suddenly to strangle the cow. And if you do not wish to believe it, look at the cow’s neck.” And indeed, around the cow’s neck they saw two pitch-black stripes, as though someone had wound fiery arms around its neck to strangle it.
