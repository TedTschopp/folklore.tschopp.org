A farmer from there once entered into a pact with the Devil for money and pleasure and, after the agreed term had passed, was to belong body and soul to the Evil One. While this relationship continued, the still-unmarried farmer took a wife. After some time, she noticed a striking sadness in her husband and would not relent until she had learned the secret. She comforted her troubled husband, for she first wished to try her own hand against the Devil. By virtue of the marriage bond, she claimed just as good a right to the man as Satan did by virtue of the pact. When he came to fetch the husband and the wife protested, he had to agree first to fulfill three tasks if he wished to take the farmer. First, the woman counted beans and sowed them in a grain field. The Devil was to pick them all up without a single one missing. He did so exactly. Second, she counted millet grains and sowed them in the grass. Again, the Devil had to bring back every grain. It was only sport to him. Third, the woman demanded that her opponent stretch a hair she took from her head to as many fathoms as Our Lord had received blows—namely, 6666 fathoms. However delicately and skillfully the Devil attempted the work, the hair always broke before reaching the stated length. At last he had to admit that Weiberlist had defeated him, and ever since the farm has been called Weiberlist.
