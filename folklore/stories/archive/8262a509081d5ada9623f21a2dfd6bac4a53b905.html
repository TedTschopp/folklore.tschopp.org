On his way home, he met some Nachtleutlein, who asked him what he had there. “Wine for my wife,” he answered. They asked him to give them something to drink. The man excused himself, saying that he did not have enough for all of them. But they pressed him more insistently, promising that a great deal would still remain for his wife. So he handed them his bottle. But when he saw it being passed around the circle, with each of the good little folk drinking a great deal from it, and when there proved to be far more dwarfs than he had thought, he grew afraid. He believed that his bottle would be returned empty to the last drop and that he would then have to go straight back to the inn to have it filled again. Yet there was still much wine inside when they had all drunk and returned the bottle to him. They sternly forbade him to tell either his wife or any other person that they had drunk from it. In any event, he brought the wine that remained home to his wife, and behold! His bottle was still full. For a long, long time, his wife drank from this wine, and the bottle always remained full. Eventually, however, this troublesome, perpetually full wine bottle no longer pleased the woman. She began to fear it. She pressed the man to tell her what witchcraft lay behind it. But the man tried to calm her by drawing her attention to the bottle's size and urging her only to drink a great deal, for then the bottle would surely become empty. Soon, however, the woman became completely distrustful and would not drink another drop of this so-blessed wine.", "Then the man revealed the secret to her.
