At its foot rises the Violenbach, which separates the two cantons for a short distance. To the right, toward the little village of Nusshof, lies a flat-topped, spruce-covered mountain called the Geisspitz, on which Geiseck Castle could still be seen in the previous century. A short distance away still stands the count’s unremarkable chapel with its small tower, which the farmer at nearby Pechhof uses as a woodshed and hayloft. No one knows the castle’s remains well; yet it is certain that cellar vaults still exist, whose buried entrances our wholly unadventurous youth neglect to search for. Wandering tinkers of kettles and tubs, rag collectors, and bird catchers know more about them, for they are said often to spend part of the winter there. If one now crosses at night the great plain called the Reiterspiel, one sees the Count of Geiseck ride down from the mountain and marshal his squadrons of knights. Then a tournament begins: the horses shy and rear, the knights lift themselves from their saddles, and others dismount and fight on foot. But experienced old people also claim to have watched these exercises in arms at midday from eleven until twelve and to have clearly recognized the Count of Geiseck as they had seen him in old pictures, while others maintain that they are Bernese soldiers who fell here in the Swabian War and must still atone for the devastation they then inflicted upon the Fricktal." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
