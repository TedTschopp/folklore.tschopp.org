One autumn, during the Alpfahrt from this Alp, the Grosshirt deliberately and with forethought left a head of cattle behind. The next day he sent his Kleinhirt back up to the Alp to fetch the forgotten animal. A Butz had lived from time immemorial in the “Dajagmach” on the Nonnenalp; moreover, the Grosshirt could not abide the little fellow, and he thought that when the little good-for-nothing went up alone, the Alpbutz would surely receive him. At his master’s command, the Kleinhirt set off on foot and came to the Alpine hut, where he found the animal in the Stafel, comfortably chewing its cud. He sat down in the Stafel to rest, unpacked his provisions bag, and began to eat his “Marende.” After a while the Alpbutz came in and, without a word or deed, crouched down on the floor beside the feasting Kleinhirt. The Kleinhirt offered the Butz some of his Marende, and the latter helped himself heartily. When they parted, the Butz gave the herdsman an elegant “Schelmapfifli” as a gift. When the little herdsman came home that evening with the animal and the Schelmapfifli, the Grosshirt looked utterly astonished, all the more when he learned that the Pfifli had such a beautiful tone.
