At Schattdorf, the Fygstuehler family had “a whole great spread of hay” lying out in their meadow. It was already dry, and from a cloudless sky the good sun sent its warmest and brightest rays upon the industrious people of Schattdorf. Around noon, they began turning the hay. In a neighboring house beyond the village stream—the building that is now the Zum Tellen inn—one woman said to another: “I would like to see how the Fygstuehlers would scurry if a shower suddenly came.” “I can easily make that happen,” the other burst out at once. She was Hospital Bailiff Arnold's daughter from Altdorf, who was staying with her cousin, Mrs. Schellhammer: “a Heegerli”—with a hump—but otherwise a quite respectable young lady, the daughter of honorable and wealthy parents. And indeed, before long a black cloud formed over the Fygstuehlers' meadow, and only over that meadow. A rain shower fell and dampened all the hay, so that they had to postpone bringing it in until nearly sunset. But the vigilant and resourceful parish priest learned of it, summoned the young lady, and examined her. Thoroughly contrite, she confessed her misdeed; she had not believed that she was doing anything evil.
