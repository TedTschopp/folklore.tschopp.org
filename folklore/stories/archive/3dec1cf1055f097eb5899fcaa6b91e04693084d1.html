As she herself confessed under torture, she had concluded a pact with the Prince of Darkness that cost her three thalers. For this money, among other things, she received an ointment with which she greased her heels so that she could fly through the air on a broom to the witches’ dance. She held her nightly meetings with the evil one, whose embrace honored and greatly delighted her, on the Moléson. There she also hatched plans for the destruction of human beings. Once, she had formed the dreadful resolution to devastate the villages of La Tour, Epagey, and Pringy with water and reduce Broc to a heap of ashes. On the day appointed for carrying out her hellish plan, she rode a broom through the air from Villllars-volard to the summit of the Moléson. Scarcely had she arrived when a terrible storm arose with thunder and lightning, and such a torrent of rain fell that it seemed the floodgates of heaven had opened. Mountain streams everywhere burst their banks and threatened to sweep away the nearby hamlets and villages, especially the Erbivuebach, which rises at the foot of the mountain and had already destroyed two houses at Pringy. At the same time, fires broke out in several homes in Broc. Fortunately, however, the miraculous bell in the castle chapel of Saint John at Gruyères was rung at the right hour, and within a short time all danger had vanished. Even now, traces of the threatened devastation can be found. Among them is an enormously large boulder on the Mongeron Alp that Catillon had rolled down through the Erbivue ravine, presumably to dam the bed of the Gietzbach and thereby increase the flood.
