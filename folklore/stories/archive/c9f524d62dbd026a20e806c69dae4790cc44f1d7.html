Strikingly, something legendary is usually connected with them. So it is with the Egelsee near Menzingen, of which only slight traces now remain. According to folk legend, after the battle on the Gubel (23rd day of Weinmonat (October) 1531), a rider from Zurich came to this lake in flight. As he attempted to leap across toward the opposite forest, the “Bannholz,” he fell into such distress that he made a vow to God. Yet after reaching dry land, he mocked the vow, whereupon the horse turned around and, despite all spurring, leapt straight into the lake and sank with him while the rider cried “Hüp! Hüp!” In later times, the distressed cry “Hüp!” was often heard at midnight at the Egelsee, in the nearby Bannholz, and in the place called the Kahlen, which is why the specter was called the Egelsee-Hüper or Bannholz-Hüper. Since the time of the French, he has no longer been heard. Yet the placing of ghostly things at the Egelsee goes back far beyond the Reformation era. Between 1269—1275, the quarrelsome Wildenburger is said to have feuded with the Baron of Wädenswil. The Wildenburger and his men were positioned in the Winzwilerholz, were outflanked by the enemy, and tried in vain to escape to his castle on the Lorze. At the Red Brook, which received its name because it ran with blood, hand-to-hand fighting began, and the Wildenburger’s warriors perished miserably in the Egelsee.
