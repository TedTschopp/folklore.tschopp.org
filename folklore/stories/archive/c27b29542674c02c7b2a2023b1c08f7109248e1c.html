The Sennen and herders tell of it:", "In those days, the cows were of enormous size. They had such an abundance of milk that they had to be milked into broad trenches, which were very quickly filled. People went out in boats to skim the cream from these basins. One morning, a handsome young herdsman was performing this work when a sudden gust overturned his craft and the unfortunate man drowned. The young men and women of the valley mourned his sad end and searched for his body for a long time in order to bury it, but in vain. Only several days later, as the butter was being churned, was it found amid the waves of foaming cream. Weeping for joy, the Sennen expressed their thanks to the man of God, and in gratitude for the great service he had rendered, they vowed to offer each year a heavy, rich cheese from their high pasture upon the altar of St. Bernard in the monastery of Altenryf.
