Similar female demons recur under still other names that I will not list in full. Frau Holle, or Holda, who also appears in fairy tales, occurs in northern Germany, and Frau Perchta in the south. Individual traits of Perchta passed to Queen Bertha, wife of Rudolf II of Burgundy, who, like Frau Holle, especially rewards diligent spinners. Frau Faschte or the Fraufastenmütterli appear only in the southwest of the German-speaking region. In Switzerland these figures correspond to Frau Zälti, the Sträggele of the Lucerne hinterland—who also appears in the company of the Dürst—the Chrungele, Frau Chunkle, or Frau Chlungeli. Folk custom preserved their memory even better than legend did. Noisy processions, usually on particular December nights—the Chlungelinacht, Sträggelenacht, and Stupfnasenacht—in which the appearance of the terrifying women is imitated survived in various regions of Switzerland. In the Canton of Bern, however, no similar folk customs can be demonstrated down to the present; the Chlungere of the Bernese Midlands, who appeared at Shrovetide, nevertheless recall Frau Chrungele, at least by name.
