They were not giants and stood not much above average height, but you should have seen their chests—and their sinewy arms! They had the strength of horses! Until the age of 20 they did not have to perform a single stroke of work. Naked, they stretched out lazily in the sunshine before their house, and whenever their mother boiled Anken, they drank the freshly boiled Anken and also anointed and rubbed their backs and arms with it.", "Once a dreadful giant came across the lake to Altdorf and challenged the people of Uri to a ‘friendly Schwinget.’ No one dared engage him. The father of the three brothers happened to be in Altdorf, and through him the Landammann of Uri sent word to the three asking whether they would save the honor of the country. For a long time they passed the duty back and forth, arguing over which of them should answer the summons: the oldest and strongest told the second to go, and the second told the youngest and weakest. This was not because they were afraid, but because they believed the youngest was still more than strong enough. At last the mother settled the dispute; she told the middle brother to go because each time he had drunk the boiled Anken in the Chessi two fingerbreadths lower than the others. The oldest nevertheless accompanied him. They wore short tunics and white pointed caps. The Landammann invited him to midday dinner, but he said he wished to earn it first. As he reached the ‘Platz,’ the foreign giant came up through the village on all fours, roaring like a bull, tearing Bsetzisteine from the road and flinging them wildly around. The man of the Schächen Valley promptly copied this feat. When they met at the Löwen, the giant thought it beneath him to hold merely a ‘friendly’ Schwinget with his comparatively small opponent and challenged him to a Schwinget to the death. The man of the Schächen Valley fearlessly accepted. With a bold, swift grip he seized the giant beneath the armpit, making him roar, crushed his chest like a basket, and hurled him backward over his head, across the village stream and onto the paving of the road. The giant fractured his skull and lay dead.", "Now the brave young man went to eat with the Landammann but refused the reward offered to him.
