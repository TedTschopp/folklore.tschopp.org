At that time, a woman named Chriemhild lived there. She was very beautiful but also very wicked and envious in disposition. Her hatred and envy were directed especially against her neighbors, whose fields and meadows always proved far more fruitful than her own. The shameful woman had long wished for an opportunity to vent her malice upon her neighbors’ property. The arrival of the wandering scholar therefore suited her perfectly, for through his art she hoped to make their meadows and fields, if possible, even more barren than her own. Inflamed with sinful love for the beautiful woman, he immediately agreed to her evil desire. One night, he set about digging a great ditch through which he intended to channel the water from Lake Türlen onto the neighbors’ meadows and fields, thereby flooding them and transforming their warm, fertile soil into cold, wet marshland. The malicious work had nearly succeeded; only a few more thrusts of the spade were needed before the water would have broken into the ditch. But a pious pilgrim happened to come along the road, immediately recognized the wickedness of the undertaking, and with the power of his holy will banished the wandering scholar and the evil woman to the Glärnisch. There the two are condemned to plant a garden on the Mittler, a mountain mass covered with eternal ice. Only when this garden, which the people call the Vreneligärtli, or Saint Verena’s Garden — for that pilgrim is said to have been Saint Verena — is completed will the two be released.
