At home, Ueli was striking the smock with a stick, but the boy in the other house received the blows.", "According to the worldview of primitive man, however, the soul, or something of it, could live in everything that belonged to a person in any way. Bodily excretions and products of growth, such as hair and nails, therefore appear as bearers of the soul. The soul dwelling in the body can also communicate itself to objects through touch. Thus, what the psychologist calls the body-soul passes into clothes, weapons, and objects of daily use. In the witch beliefs of our day and the magic of indigenous peoples, we therefore constantly encounter views rooted in some conception of belief in the soul, as shown by the many forms of the body-soul. These ideas have indeed disappeared from belief in the soul proper, but they continue to operate in magical belief: one must not give worn clothing to a beggar; hair should be burned if one does not wish to suffer harm. The people still have much to tell about what can happen when one gives something away; the story of the Wasendoktor also shows us this: mysterious relationships exist between the human body and soul.
