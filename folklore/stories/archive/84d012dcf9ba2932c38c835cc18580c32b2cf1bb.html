If one tries to pick it up, it becomes ever heavier, and just as one grows anxious not to let it fall from one's hands, it suddenly vanishes. Opinions about it differ. People believe that it foretells the death of whoever sees such a child, or they interpret it as a sign of an especially fruitful year. In general, however, it is held to be a child belonging to the Erdmännchen themselves or one they have stolen from human beings. For an Aargauer calls a small, healthy child a Chrügeli, and the name Chrügelnägeli is also given to those suspicious holes in the rocks of the Jura whose inner nature has never been properly determined. He was once sent out to gather fallen wood in the forest. While resting high upon the so-called Seckenberg, which lies toward the village of Eiken, he heard the voice of a swaddled infant across the forest ditch beside which he sat. Hauswirth had already received enough beatings at home for not diligently minding his master's children, and he paid even less attention to this child that cried so unexpectedly. But when he finally rose to go home, he truly saw such a pretty child lying across the ditch that his heart was moved at once. He tried to pick it up but had wholly indescribable difficulty even getting his arms around it. At last, it had completely vanished, and he stood alone beside the ditch. Great terror seized him.
