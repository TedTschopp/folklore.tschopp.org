One year after the chaplain of Meien had banished those two fiery dogs and brought peace to the valley’s livestock, the people of Meien carried the corpse of a valley man to Wassen, there to commit it to consecrated ground beside their parish church. At the Lychkirmi on the Schanz, the bearers set down their burden according to ancient custom, and the people prayed the Fyfi. When they wished to set out again, they could no longer move the corpse from the spot; it was as though frozen fast, and all their tugging was useless. Then the chaplain, who was also present, said, “Throw him down into the ravine!” They tipped over the bier with the dead man, and the corpse rolled down into the ravine. At once more than a hundred ravens flew over and fell upon it, croaking.
