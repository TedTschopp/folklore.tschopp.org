His roommate noticed that he carried a great deal of money and attacked him in order to rob him. Unfortunately, earlier that day the unsuspecting man had picked up a “Trullihegel” (pocketknife) on the road and put it in his pocket. With this, he defended his property and—almost unbelievably—killed the wretched scoundrel in the process. He was accused of murder and, despite his protestations of innocence, condemned to die at the executioner’s hand. During his pretrial confinement at the Ankenwage in Altdorf, he won the affection and trust of the cantonal bailiff, who allowed him to move freely about the house. The mother of my ninety-year-old woman narrator, and many other people, often heard him singing a wonderfully beautiful song in his cell. When they led him to the place of execution, the bailiff wept bitterly and asked him to give a sign if he arrived safely in eternity. Several days after the execution, the bailiff stood at the window, weeping as he thought of the innocent pilgrim who had been put to death. Suddenly, the little cap that the pilgrim had usually worn upon his head lay on the windowsill.
