He owned the finest alps far and wide, so that his neighbors could rightly call him Rich Thys. He also had an excellent servant, Niggi Pfänder, who looked after his herds and livestock. But the loveliest of all was Grete, the master's daughter. When, at the end of the haymaking month, the dairymen gathered with Niggi on the Bunfahl pasture for the joyful Alpine festival, the master's lovely young daughter climbed up as well, for her father's handsome and industrious dairyman was not a matter of indifference to her. The Alpine festival brought the hearts of the two fully together, and from then on they passed a blissful year until the next Alpine Sunday. At this festival, something happened that was to bring them both great heartache. There was an exalted visitor on the alp, for the young lord from the nearby castle honored the lads and maidens with his presence. He seemed to have his eye especially on Grete, for he repeatedly asked her to dance. Niggi, however, was grieved to the depths of his soul, for he sensed misfortune. Later, when word came up from the valley that the elegant lord of the castle was now a frequent guest day and night in the finest house in Bärswil, his anxiety knew no bounds. He admonished Grete, warned her, and threatened her. It was all in vain. But when the roses had faded and autumn came to the land, it was said that the young lord had gone away to distant lands. Grete, however, crept about sadly and pale. Now Niggi was supposed to be good enough for her again. But the dairyman thrust her from him in disgust and cursed her faithlessness. Then she cried: \"If I have broken faith, then in my difficult hour may your curse strike me and the fruit of my faithlessness! Even in the grave, I will find no rest until I have hacked the Neuenberg above us down into the valley!\" The curse came true for the foolish young woman. Soon, a premature death carried her and her little child away. On the very day—indeed, in the very night—on which she had made the castle lord's acquaintance, a terrible thunderstorm broke loose, sending a mudslide down from the Neuenberg and burying the village of Bärswil and its surroundings. Even today, earth and stones can sometimes be seen rolling down from above. Then the people say: \"The little woman of the Lauigraben cannot find rest.
