He was hot-tempered, insolent, and arrogant; he tormented a poor boy who served him in every conceivable way with hard labor, harsh words, and cruel blows. One day he ordered the boy to perform a task for which the child was nowhere near strong enough, so that he had to refuse. The herdsman flew into such a rage that he seized the boy and plunged his head into the cauldron in which milk was boiling to be separated. Once, however, it happened that the Linth valley was celebrating its parish fair. The merriment ran high, and the evil Senn became stupefied by wine, music, and dancing, so that he cast off all humility and reason and staggered wildly about in his sinful folly. It was too hot for him inside, so he went out to the stream, which was swollen by a heavy warm rain higher up and rushed past more strongly than usual. He knelt beside it and removed his hat to scoop up water. He drank what had run into it, but found a little white bone at the bottom. He stuck it on his hat and returned to the hall. Suddenly the little bone began to bleed, and the people now knew what had happened to the boy.
