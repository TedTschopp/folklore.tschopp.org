Heiden lived at the foot of the Räzliberg. One day, a stranger named Stephanus came to them over the wild mountain path leading from Valais into this quiet corner. Stephanus had just escaped the dreadful slaughter that Maximinian had inflicted upon his Theban Legion in the year 303 in the valley of the Rhône on the other side. Here in the mountains, he found refuge among the simple herders. They helped him build a hut, and in return he shared various arts he had learned in distant lands, as well as his healing skill. He showed them how to use tools they had not previously known and instructed them in planting fruit trees. He also opened these valleys to the teaching of the Christian God, built a wooden house of worship beside his hut, and preached the Gospel to the valley's inhabitants into advanced old age. If the hermit Stephan had enjoyed high esteem in life, at his death that esteem rose into veneration. Everything Stephanus had accomplished by natural means while alive afterward appeared to his devotees as the effect of supernatural power. In the course of time, his grave therefore became a much-visited pilgrimage site. His bones were dug up and kept in a stone coffin. The coffin, together with Stephan's cross, spear, and sword, was enclosed by a grille so that no one could steal the relics. For centuries, people made pilgrimages to this site, for the pope had also declared Stephanus a saint. At the beginning of the 15th century, the little village that named itself after its saint—", "St. Stephan, as it is still called today—received its own church.
