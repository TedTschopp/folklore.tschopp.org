A rich farmer left this great estate to his two sons. But because they did not like one another, they divided it. One took the Oberhof, now called Schlatt, and the other the Unterhof, which received the name Fährenthal after the Aare changed its course there and deposited arable land where the ferrymen, or Fehren, had formerly lived and carried people across the river. But even this did not end the brothers' quarrel. The elder, whose envious heart could not bear his fields adjoining his brother's land, once encountered him on the property boundary by the Hardwald, killed him, and buried him in the woods where the Hinterbänkli now lies. At that time, the region was still sparsely populated, so no one learned the poor brother's fate, and the elder was now sole owner. He married and lived into old age, apparently quite happily. Then once, as he was plowing near that place with his servant, the two sat down by the plow to rest and eat their morning bread. The house dog came leaping from the forest, dragging a human skull, and dropped it into the farmer's lap. He thrust the skull away, but immediately his hand was spattered all over with blood. Frightened and trembling in every limb, he could no longer compose himself. He confessed his crime to the servant, showed him the place where his brother lay buried, and asked him to dig up the body and quietly bring it to the churchyard at Leuggern. The servant promised silence and then buried the bodily remains in the Christian manner as he had promised.", "Although the evildoer has long been dead, he still has no rest.
