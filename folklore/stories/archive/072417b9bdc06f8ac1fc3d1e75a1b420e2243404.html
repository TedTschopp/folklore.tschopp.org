If a renowned marksman came up the mountain, they entered into an agreement with him and, for a certain period or number of years, regularly brought a young chamois within range for him. In return, however, the marksman had to vow to leave the other animals in peace for just as long. Thus the old Landammann Heinrich Jmmlin of Obwalden himself told our Cysat how he once went after chamois on Pilatus and a little mountain man came along and forbade him to climb any higher. One day, while fishing for tasty trout in the Rümlig high up on Pilatus, one of them leaped onto the back of his neck and pressed him down into the stream with such force that he thought his end had come. ‘You too are one of those,’ it said, ‘who have already tormented and scattered my little animals so much!’ Then it vanished and left him lying there.
