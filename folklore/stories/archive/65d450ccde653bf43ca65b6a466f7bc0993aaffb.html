To remain undiscovered, they do not shrink from the laborious journey there and back across the Jura mountains lying between. Joseph Hochreuter of Wittnau was such an indefatigable lover, and on many a summer night he crossed the difficult Homberg in both directions. Like an immense roof with a ruler-straight ridge and steeply falling sides, this mountain runs for hours along the boundary of the Fricktal and Basel country and bears, on its two opposite wooded summits, the ruins of two knights’ castles, Homburg and Rechberg. A double Lebhag, a living hedge half a man’s height, had been drawn across it, and between the two green hedges ran a broad, clean military road straight as an arrow. While he marveled at the long hedge as something that must have been planted and grown to completion in that single night, the sounds of resounding music began to be heard from far away on the wind; it was a mighty, loud march. Expectantly he stood at the hedge and looked into the strange road. Soon a vast procession came marching along it from the direction of the Homburg ruins. Small boys formed the head, followed by larger boys, all arranged so evenly in increasing height that it was very amusing to see. They walked in pairs, each pair keeping the road free between them and moving at equal distances close along the inner sides of the double hedge. These little young lords were all dressed alike and, so far as the night allowed him to distinguish, wore white trousers and little black coats. Behind them followed a band of men, all dressed in black, and immediately after them came the many members of a brass band, blowing mightily on horns, trumpets, and trombones as they passed.", "Now a coach drove up, drawn by six black horses. It was enclosed like a tall mail coach and filled with a large company of ladies and gentlemen. Behind it appeared a swarm of tiny, pretty girls in little white dresses, then another of taller maidens, and these too walked in pairs within the two sides of the hedge. Finally, a crowd of women in black cloaks closed the procession. Everything moved so delicately and lightly that no step, no horse’s hoof, and no sound of the wagon wheels could be heard; nothing was audible but the music, to whose rhythm everything moved. The road remained free of dust, as though the numerous companies walked entirely over snow. Everything seemed brought and carried onward by the breath of the wind playing above it. What most astonished the observer, however, was this: instead of the military road and hedge descending at the end of the plateau in accordance with the steeply falling Homberg, they continued in the same direction in which they had begun below at Homburg Castle, across toward Rechberg Castle. Where the depths began, road and hedge therefore proceeded straight onward through the air. All this happened beneath the clearest starlight. When Hochreuter ran farther toward the summit so that he might still see where the end of the procession would turn, a dense darkness suddenly fell and swallowed hedge, road, and procession together. The following origin is given for it. The lord of Rechberg Castle loved the wife of the Count of Homburg passionately, but he concealed this passion from her and everyone else, and thus the neighboring castle lords remained good friends and made many games and wagers with one another. Among other things, their company once disputed the meaning of the saying ‘to lead someone behind the light.’ That its meaning was the same as deception did not seem to the ladies present to be satisfactorily proved by any of the attempted explanations. The Rechberg lord offered to demonstrate it if the next evening meal at Homburg were conducted exactly as he now proposed. They agreed to the innocent jest. At the appointed minute the following evening, the Count of Homburg sat dining at the open window facing Rechberg Castle. As arranged, the countess placed the final table candle with the others directly before her merry husband’s face. At that moment a bang was heard from the neighboring castle, and the count fell from his chair torn to pieces. The Rechberg lord had shot him through the castle window with a well-aimed cannon and thereby shown the wife how men are ‘led behind the light.’ The wedding the Rechberg lord then held with the widow was preceded by a splendid bridal procession attended by all the lords of the country with wife and child.
