As a reward, every evening the Senne had to fill a small wooden bowl with sweet Rahm and set it on the roof of the Sennhütte; every morning he found the bowl empty in the same place. — One evening, however, the Senne filled the bowl with sour milk instead of Rahm because he begrudged it and had grown unbelieving about the custom. During the night a terrible storm kept him from sleeping; he heard a strong voice call his name but gave no answer, whereupon the mountain spirit who had called him pounded violently on the door until the Senne got up. From outside the door the mountain spirit called further: “Ungrateful man, know that eleven of your beautiful cows have met with disaster at the rock and lie dashed to death in the depths.” Through the crack in the door, the Senne could see the spirit’s dreadful form and was so terrified by it that he fell to the floor and in the morning was found badly injured, lying in the kitchen. — The servants had heard nothing of the occurrence; only the Senne’s account made them fear something terrible.
