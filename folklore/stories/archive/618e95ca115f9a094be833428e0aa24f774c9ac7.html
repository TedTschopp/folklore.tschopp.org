During the meal, the man regarded the briskly grazing goat with greedy eyes; its hanging, beautiful, full udder seemed especially to please him. He therefore said to his wife, “But if a wolf came and ate our goat, what would you say?” The woman laughed. “A wolf cannot come here now in the middle of summer, into these meadows where so many people are all around, and cannot seize our goat.” “Yes, but what if it did happen?” the man continued. “Be silent about a wolf,” the woman said crossly. “I do not care to hear anything about it.” The man fell silent, but went into nearby bushes, fixed his handkerchief on himself as a tail, and returned as a wolf, springing greedily upon the beautiful goat. The good woman was badly frightened but immediately recovered her wits. In a flash she seized the scythe and went for the wolf.
