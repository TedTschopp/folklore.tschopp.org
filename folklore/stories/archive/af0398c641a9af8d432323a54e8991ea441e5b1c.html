Discontented with God and the world, he cursed poverty and work, swore every imaginable curse, and wished for the Devil to come. Then a Mandli came along the road, whom he took for a brush cutter. It wished him a friendly good day and asked sympathetically why he was so dissatisfied. The friendly words did the man from Seelisberg good, and he thawed, lamented his misery, and ended his litany of woe with the words, ‘I would almost rather the Devil took me!’ The Mandli comforted him: ‘You need not despair! You shall have money enough if you remain faithful to me.’ The woodcutter agreed and promised to remain faithful. The stranger now gave him his address: ‘Teufelsbockbart, Zurich, Jakobstrasse No. ...’ and instructed him that whenever he needed money, he should write and throw the letter into any mailbox. It would reach its destination without falling into the hands of postal officials. Then he also handed him a little book. He was to read and study it, and then he would receive what he desired. ‘But remain faithful! And say nothing to the womenfolk or to the Black One’—the priest.
