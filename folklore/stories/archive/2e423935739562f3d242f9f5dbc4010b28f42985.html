Its rubble was used to build a pair of Sennhütten, of which only one now remains. The older one had to be abandoned because of recurring cattle plagues and was finally torn down entirely.", "The reason was as follows. When a wall of this hut was once being altered, the removal of a beam suddenly released a maiden and a little dog from inside the wall. She urgently begged the workers to use the beam for an altar and thereby release her from the enchantment. “I was the daughter of the lord of the castle up there,” she said, “and this little dog here was our huntsman, who abducted me. During my escape, I fell asleep from exhaustion beneath a tree. My father, pursuing us, caught us there and with his curses bound us inside that very tree. This is the beam you have just broken out of the wall.”", "In their first fright, the carpenters knew nothing else to do but pray an Our Father, after which they quickly returned the beam to its old place. With a terrible crash, the two figures plunged back into their corner as well. Other tellers maintain that the beam was not returned to the wall but was immediately thrown into the fire that had just been kindled for burning lime. By the next morning, however, the Senn's finest cows lay dead in the stable. When each was skinned, a large black patch was found beneath the skin of its breast; otherwise, nothing wrong with them could be detected. From then on, this cattle mortality, called the Angriff, recurred every ten years. A white figure followed by a little dog was also often seen passing through the barn.
