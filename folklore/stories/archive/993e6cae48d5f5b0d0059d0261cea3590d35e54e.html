He greatly loved dancing and always found the best opportunity for it on the remote mountain of Jungen, where at the beginning of winter many young people stay to feed the livestock. At a winter evening gathering he danced with such zeal and exertion that sweat ran down him in streams and he could scarcely breathe for the heat. To gain quick relief and take part again in his beloved dance as soon as possible, he went outside and lay down, dripping with sweat, upon the cold snow. — His body could not bear it; he fell ill and died.", "The poor man seems to have to atone for this premature, self-inflicted death even in the next world, for his house became uncanny and has remained so to the present day. An invisible spirit dwells around the Stubenofen and permits nothing upon it. Everything must be removed; even household members who venture onto it are thrown down without noticing how it happens. Otherwise the spirit is very peaceable, and the household is as accustomed to him as to an old housemate. When in the evening they undress beside the Stubenofen and go to bed, the Boze comes in through the room door and takes his place on the stove bench; when they rise in the morning and come to the stove, he is gone again. The spirit dislikes only being followed and takes revenge upon the curious. Once the master of the house wished to follow the departing Boze and see where he went. The spirit descended into the cellar opening.
