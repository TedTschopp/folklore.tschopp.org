Because it was destroyed by Rübenen and rockfalls, the inhabitants moved away and settled in the middle of the valley, where the “Zwyerkapelle” now stands on the Attinghausen road. The church is said to have stood on the site of the Zwyerkapelle, while a cemetery in the Holdermätteli, separated from the chapel by the road, received the dead. Old people, and especially the owners of the Holdermätteli, maintain that bones of gigantic human beings came to light when the old seventeenth-century farmhouse in the Holdermätteli was built and its deep cellar excavated. But the settlement was situated even worse here. It had two very bad neighbors, the Reuss and the Schächen, wild mountain rivers that at times behaved with great violence, not only destroying the light buildings but also eating away the very ground beneath the fleeing people's feet. Then they said to one another: “We will return to the old—that is, former—village,” and moved back to the foot of the Gruonberg. Hence the name Altdorf for the capital of the oldest Swiss canton.", "According to another account, the first or second village stood on the site of today's railway station.
