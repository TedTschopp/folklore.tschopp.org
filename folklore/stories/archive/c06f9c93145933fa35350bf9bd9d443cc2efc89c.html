People could see that an Alpine dairy operation was being conducted on the mysterious pasture: they saw a herd let out in the morning and driven home in the evening; they saw smoke rising from the hut roof as though someone actually lived there, preparing meals, making cheese, and attending to everything else involved in dairying. Yet no person was ever seen to appear; the forces managing this Alp always remained invisible. Who might the strange inhabitant over there be? What secret did the hut conceal? The bold man wished and felt compelled to shed light on this question; the peace of his life seemed to depend upon it. Pleading attempts to dissuade him only poured oil on the fire. He felt no fear, only confidence in victory. He went to the windows and called, but no one could be seen or heard. He went to the door and tried gently to open it; it sprang open, and he boldly set his foot across the threshold. The ground did not yet give way beneath him. He stepped firmly and looked steadily about. Hm, he was in the kitchen; a fire burned on the hearth, and above it hung the cauldron ready for cheese-making. He called to the dairyman, but no sound broke the uncanny silence. Now he pretended to believe the Gäumer was hiding behind the cauldron in the corner of the hearth. ‘Yes, you back there—you don't frighten me. Come out!’ But when he looked, no one was there. Perhaps a resident could be found inside, beyond the place where the door was set. He tried to open it—and a handsome room received him as he entered. The table was already laid with plates, cutlery, and food. To one side stood a fine bed with a curtain before it. But no living being would appear. For whom, then, was this table? ‘Wait,’ thought the investigator, ‘I will surely discover that.’ With this he slipped into bed and drew the curtain so that, as he believed, he could watch unnoticed and see what would happen at the table and who was to come. At last, a mighty, terrifying figure entered. The hideous form certainly had a head, but no human face emerged upon it. Now it counted the plates on the table and then cried out in a terrible voice: ‘The meal for the dead is ready; only one plate is still missing—for the man lying there in the bed!’ So the daredevil had been discovered. Was he doomed to die? Why not? The specter was already striding toward him and seized his arm—oh, how that burned! And listen: suddenly it spoke in a wholly different, unexpected tone. ‘Do not be afraid. I will spare your life and be unspeakably grateful if you redeem me. But the conditions are very difficult indeed. I am afraid! A single hair too many or too few will plunge you irretrievably into death and me into new torment!’ So spoke the spirit. The stouthearted guest, prepared for anything, did not waver in his resolve to risk whatever might bring a happy outcome and immediately offered himself for the dangerous trials. ‘Get up!’ He obeyed. The specter ordered him to approach the table and eat with it. ‘I did not dish it up, and I will not dish it out!’ Then the spirit brought a shovel, a light, and a halbviertliges Mäss, laid them at the man's feet, and commanded: ‘Pick it up and carry it into the cellar!’ ‘I carried nothing up, and I'll carry nothing down!’ the man answered boldly. The spirit then reached for the things, carried them onward, and motioned for the other to follow, which he did. Down in the cellar, pointing to a particular spot, the spirit again commanded: ‘Dig it out there!’ ‘I buried nothing, and I'll dig nothing up!’ the courageous man refused.
