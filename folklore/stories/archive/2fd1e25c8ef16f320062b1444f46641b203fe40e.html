Before its complete decay, passageways and open halls are said to have remained visible there for a long time, and young people often used them for secret dances. Two young fellows once went to one such nocturnal amusement. They came down from the Natisserberg, two hours away. When they reached the castle ruin and entered the dark passage leading to the ballroom, they heard the music and stamping of the merry company. But all at once they dared not take another step. They fixed their eyes rigidly upon something, and their hair stood on end with terror—for before the ballroom door lay a great black ox with a single fiery eye in the middle of its forehead, as large as a plate. As may be imagined, the two young people’s desire to dance changed into such fear and horror that, holding each other by the hand and without speaking a word, they hurried home, feeling as though their feet did not touch the ground.
