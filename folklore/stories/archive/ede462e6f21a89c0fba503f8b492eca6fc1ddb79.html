A herdsman on Seelisberg lived on the best of terms with the Herdmännchen. They diligently looked after his affairs, and he repaid them with many gifts of food or other things. Once he had to go away on business and had no one to tend the livestock in his absence. He therefore said to the little folk: ‘I have already given you so much; now you might do me the favor of feeding, watering, and guarding my cows until I return.’ They were heartily willing, provided only that the bad wind did not come. By this they meant the Föhn. Unfortunately, the bad wind came and lasted three to four days. The farmer was on the other shore of the lake, and because of the storm no one could or would take him back to his home shore; the Herdmännchen were in their hole and dared not come out, while the cattle were in the stable and had long received no food, so that most of them starved. It was a devastating sight for the man when he finally reached home. Alas, Bruni, the finest cow, was done for, and Laubi still looked at her master with dim eyes and a pitiful gaze before dying. Good-natured though he ordinarily was, this time harsh words against his little friends escaped him. They did not take it too badly, however, and tried to comfort him. ‘Until now, much of your milk has also gone to waste because you did not know how to use it. Now we will teach you an art that will richly make up for the loss you suffered because of the bad wind.’ They told him to kill 's Giebeli, the goat. Then they showed him how to use its stomach to prepare Lup and afterward cheese.
