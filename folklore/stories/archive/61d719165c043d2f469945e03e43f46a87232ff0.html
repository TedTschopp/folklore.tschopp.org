When the place was inspected the following morning, many thousands of dog tracks were visible, and a dead dog actually lay there. Many more could be added to the Türst’s territories named above. Very often he follows forests and streams. He hunted from the Nebensperg forest over the Rothbach, past the Trinity chapel between Grossdietwil and Altbüron, and toward the Niserwald. At Egolzwil he stormed north of the village toward the Buchwald, and between Nebikon and Altishofen toward the Flüggenwald. Near Luthern he could be heard on the Barren plain, where people had to step 3 paces to the right to avoid him. In Sursee, Küngen’s house in the suburb, like the one at Brestenegg, had to stand open for him to pass freely through. He hunted toward the Suren. At Menzingen in the Canton of Zug, people believe that in houses where a passage runs through the whole building and has door openings on two opposite sides, the Wild Host has the right of free passage. In some places, images and crosses are said to have been erected because of the Türst. Thus an ancient panel showing the Mother of God is kept on a farmhouse at Zinzerswyl in the municipality of Buttisholz as protection against the Türst, who formerly hunted past there. A former owner once removed the panel, but immediately the Wild Hunt raged around the house and Bresten came among the cattle. After he restored it, peace returned. In the village of Grossdietwil, an old wooden cross is fastened to a barn door because the Türst hunted through the threshing floor there. The Türst formerly hunted in a hideous fashion over Bergegg between Werthenstein and Entlebuch. As a pack of large and small dogs he continued this mischief until people erected three crosses, from each of which the next can be seen.
