To sustain her life, the child took nothing but a goat. Up in the wilderness stood an old, ruined hut. No one dared go up there because a loathsome Lindwurm lived inside, black and a full three Klafter long when stretched out. But the girl did not care. It seemed the only place where she might be safe. She had just milked her goat and entered the hut to find shelter from the hot sun. Then something rustled in the leaves piled high on the floor, which had once served the herders as bedding. Curled around itself on them lay the coal-black-spotted Lindwurm, twisted in spasms and weakly raising its head. From the creature’s head shone a splendid little crown with a diamond gleam in rainbow colors. The girl stepped courageously closer, for she could see at first glance that the Lindwurm was languishing. At once she offered it fresh milk in the hollow of her hand, and the parched monster drank greedily. Suddenly it hid in the leaves, for firm footsteps were approaching the hut outside. It was the girl’s poor, dearest beloved, who tended cattle on the neighboring Alp and had seen his sweetheart climb up. Worried that harm might come to her in the notorious hut, he had crossed over. He was greatly alarmed when he found the girl in the shunned hut, but she calmed him by pointing to the sick monster. The two then sat under the hut’s projecting roof and poured out their hearts to each other. When the young Senn heard what had happened in the valley, he quickly made up his mind. Without delay he led his beloved back to the village. Boldly he stood before the hard father and asked for the daughter’s hand. Proudly the father, who considered him beneath her, drove him from the door with scolding and curses. Punishment followed. Soon the rich farmer’s cows disappeared from the Alp one after another. People said the Lindwurm had devoured them. A malignant disease carried off those that remained. The poor Älpler’s property, however, visibly increased day by day, so that in autumn he came down to the valley richly blessed. One man had become poor, the other rich. Yet in good fortune the heart’s voice had not fallen silent in the man so wondrously blessed. No sooner had he reached the valley than he knocked again at the hard-hearted man’s door. Suddenly a terrible roaring was heard in the air, and the hall door opened as though by an invisible hand. The Lindwurm shot inside.
