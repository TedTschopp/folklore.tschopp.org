One night our wicked lads found a horse standing with a cart at a stranger's house, untied it, and in their malicious high spirits led it together with the wagon off the cart road and into the Erlenmoos. In those days the place was indeed still very pathless and dangerous. The boys feared sinking in the dark, their guilty consciences tormented them besides, and so they soon ran away again and left horse and wagon stranded. The poor animal must have perished there; at least it was never seen again in this region. Before one could turn a hand, the lad who had first given the Nachtbuben this wicked advice died suddenly. At once the outcry arose again in the village that this was God's punishment for the sacrilege, that the dead boy was now banished into the bog and roamed there bellowing.
