Maria Epp, Maderanertal" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "An innkeeper’s son was hunting foxes. A beautiful little fox presented itself, and he took aim, but the animal darted away. He followed it. It presented itself again, and he took aim, but once more it fled. In this way, it made a fool of him for some time. At last, the hunter tired of it and left the hunting ground with the words, ‘Then go in God’s name!’ When he arrived home, his father came to meet him and said, ‘Harness up quickly; you must drive some gentlefolk, and our farmhand has broken a leg.’ The son obeyed and drove off with the party. One evening, when they again stayed overnight at an inn, the daughter of the house made a great fuss over him; she arranged matters so that she was always near him. Once everyone else had gone to bed, she sat beside him and confidentially asked how his hunt had gone that day. He told her everything, and she said, ‘I was the little fox. I was condemned to live in the shape of a fox until someone spoke the name of God over me. You have redeemed me.
