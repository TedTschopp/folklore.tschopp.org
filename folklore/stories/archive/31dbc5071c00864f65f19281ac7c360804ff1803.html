There a presumptuous little people, spoiled by an abundance of worldly goods, enjoyed a carefree existence. In the forest above this district, an old witch lost a ring. When she could no longer find it, she unleashed a Rübi behind Zeisig in her rage, damming the Gruonbach until a lake formed. One evening the Wildmandli, whose high, far-seeing home stood on Zeisig or the Gibel, called down into the valley and the little village: “Flee, flee, you people! Flee behind the Flüeli—the Ringgäribi is coming!” But the people paid no attention to the well-meant warning. The following evening they heard the same voice, and again on the third. At last a few people did tear down their little houses, pack their belongings, and move to the southern end of the lake, behind the Flüeli at the foot of the Gruonberg. Then one night the lake in the Gruon Valley burst out and rolled across the flourishing land with a terrible mass of debris. It buried the people who had remained and their houses deep beneath the rubble and transformed the flowering countryside into a barren, dreadful field of wreckage. Those who had fled rebuilt their little houses and founded a village that they called Flüelen because it stands behind the Flüeli, or below “dä Fliälänä.” Within living memory, three small houses are said still to have stood in the village of Flüelen that had been carried from that village on the Gruonbach to beneath the cliffs; one of them was said to be the house now occupied by Constable Mattli.
