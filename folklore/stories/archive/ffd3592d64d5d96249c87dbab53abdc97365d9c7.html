At first, the woman would not go because of her great fear, but finally she yielded to their urgent pleas and accompanied them. Showing her the way, the Erdmännchen hurried ahead, and before long they came to a cave in the rock that extended beneath the mountain in many passages in every direction. At the end of one of these passages was a small but magnificently decorated chamber in which the Kindsbetterin lay upon a costly bed. When the woman had provided the necessary assistance and the child—an enchanting little dwarf scarcely two thumb-widths tall—had been born, the same Erdmännchen who had summoned her from home led her back out of the cave. Before they stepped into the open air, however, they filled the woman's apron as payment for her trouble with coals that lay in a great heap at the cave entrance. Perhaps the woman thought that escaping with her skin intact was reward enough, or perhaps the gift seemed rather meager compensation for her effort. In any case, she let most of the coals fall to the ground on her way home. Indeed, she would have thrown all of them away had she not feared the Erdmännchen, who were still calling after her from the cave: ‘The more you scatter, the more you will regret!’ At last, she reached home and angrily threw the remaining coals onto the hearth—but behold, they were pure gold. The woman quickly hurried back to search for what she had dropped, but no trace of it remained.
