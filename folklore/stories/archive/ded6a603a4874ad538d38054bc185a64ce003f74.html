Everyone recognized the benefit of such a structure, but all despaired of it as well, since the poverty of the municipality made overcoming the difficulties appear purely impossible. Suddenly, a stranger who, judging by his green Wams, was a hunter approached them from another table. Wrapped in his cloak, with a great Spanish hat and its feather pulled low over his face, he had long been listening to their conversation. He offered to build them such a bridge in the shortest time. He would also provide all the necessary material, and because he cared neither for payment nor honor, he demanded precious little for everything, virtually nothing: they need only promise him as his property the first living creature to cross the bridge after its completion. If the bargain pleased them, someone in the company should give him his hand as a sign of agreement.", "After brief consultation, they accepted the proposal. The handshake was given. But the man who gave it was frightened and turned pale when he placed his hand in the stranger's, and after the stranger departed shortly afterward, he said that he had distinctly felt the devil's claws in his right hand. Only now did those present understand what a sinful contract they had made. Fear and concern for their souls seized them. But among them was a tailor, a very clever fellow whose native wit had already extricated many a person from difficulty. He now said: ‘Dear friends, calm yourselves. I have gotten the better of many things in my life, and I will surely get the better of the devil too.’", "Although the others felt somewhat comforted by this assurance—for contrary to the usual habit of tailors, this tailor never promised what he could not perform—they parted that evening in anxious expectation of what the coming day would bring. None could find his customary rest at home, which in any event would have been impossible amid the terrible weather raging throughout the night. Only toward morning did the storm subside. The sun rose golden behind the mountains and looked curiously over their summits into the valley, and behold! The devil had kept his word: the promised bridge arched boldly across the rushing Sarine. Joy and jubilation filled the inhabitants of the region; only those who knew of the alliance with the devil could not properly rejoice in the splendidly completed work. But their grief and worry did not last long. If the devil was a man of his word, so was the tailor. Carrying a large sack on his back, he came merrily along, whistling and singing. He pushed back the crowd already preparing to rush onto the bridge and instead threw his sack upon it, opening it with a quick jerk. Six rats and six mice sprang out in hasty flight, pursued by the same number of cats, and raced in a Wild Hunt across the bridge. The tailor followed them in joyful spirits, carrying a crucifix, which he planted upon the middle bridge pier while speaking a holy blessing. At the other end of the bridge, the devil lay in wait in the same shape as the previous evening, hoping for the prey promised him by the contract. When he saw this and realized how he had been deceived, he became devilishly mad, immediately assumed his true infernal form, and with his claws tore block after block from the precipitous rock wall, hurling them all at the bridge to destroy it again. But the power of the cross planted upon the bridge thwarted his intent.
