All went well. With his Tregi, the man went down as far as the Kalputraner-Briggu and from there trudged up the Grächen mountain until, with the stolen apples, he reached the Chriz where the paths divide. There he set down the burden and, as other people customarily did there, made the sign of the Chriz and rested. And then what happened? Because he had made the sign of the Chriz, he suddenly fell into deep remorse for taking the apples and thought, “I shall carry them back, or they will weigh upon me all my life.” He slipped his Juogsu, or shoulders, beneath the carrying straps to take the load back upon his Puggul and carry it up again. Then it seemed so heavy that he could not lift it from the ground. He said to himself, “This is not natural; the Bescha helped me carry it over here.” The poor wretch then began unpacking and tried how much he could carry at one time.
