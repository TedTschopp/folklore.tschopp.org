One day, not yet a hundred years ago, an old Müetterli came to Bommatter's Rössli in Schattdorf. Dressed in old-fashioned clothes with a little cap on her head, she begged for Nidel. No one knew her. Old Bommatter said: ‘Yes, yes—take the Nidel off the milk for yourself and then have the blue skimmed milk as well; that seems a little steep to us! Can't you manage some other way?’ ‘It will work itself out somehow,’ the Müetterli shot back venomously. She darted out of the house at breakneck speed, down toward Wyergasse and then up the lane toward Bürglen, all the while brandishing her Sacklumpli. It was a magnificent, brilliantly clear summer afternoon, finer than anyone could have wished, without a wisp of cloud in the blue sky. But scarcely was the enraged little woman out of the house when a cloud as black as a wool hat came racing over the Schwarzen Grat and, in a few moments, poured a downpour over Bommatter's meadow that utterly ruined all their hay lying there. They could have brought it in within an hour; now they had to toil over it all again the next day. Not a drop of rain had fallen on the surrounding meadows.
