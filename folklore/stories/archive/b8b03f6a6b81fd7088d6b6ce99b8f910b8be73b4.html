I shall now tell of it. The entire great Äpelöö formerly belonged to the little town of Klingnau, but the neighboring municipality of Koblenz, which possessed little forest, was allowed a portion of it, at first free of charge and later by purchase. Suddenly, the Koblenz people held a forest circuit and asserted that their Waldbann was not only too small but that the people of Klingnau had to inspect the boundary marks with them and divide the land equally according to what they showed. Klingnau did indeed have the elders inspect the marks, but they found them as the document concerning them required, and they remained unchanged. Now it came to a lawsuit, and the arbitrators for both parties appeared before the Landvogt at Baden. Once again, the forest had to be measured and every individual boundary mark determined. The costs grew great. The Koblenz people were finally rejected and condemned to bear the costs and pay additional special fines. After this, an enemy was planted in the Klingnau people's own nest: one of their officials was bribed, and he secretly had the municipal Marchen shifted so that the people of Koblenz soon gained what had previously been denied them by law. This included the portion they later converted into meadowland and called the Geisswiese. But on that very meadow, things now happen accordingly—for ill-gotten property—but one need only hear what they themselves tell about it. Every boy who ever drove the cows there and came from the Äpelöö onto the Geisswiese has surely, toward evening, soon seen a goat grazing or perhaps encountered the councillor who, since his death, rides the boundary stones there. And what happened there to two hunters around Christmastime can be heard from their own mouths, for both are still alive.", "The two were together in the Äpelöö on the Anstand. A three-legged hare came so near them that either could have struck it dead with a stick. Both missed it; and scarcely had it passed when the whole ground trembled around them, and it whistled as though a hundred crackling bullets were being fired at them. The two now sought each other out and intended to leave matters there for this time. But because they were still young, strong men, they soon felt ashamed of their apprehension and resumed the hunt at a more distant spot. The same thing was repeated here, except that this time, instead of a three-legged hare, another barely recognizable monster swept past them amid the general din. Now they went home. They had already left the forest behind when the trees cracked once more as though everything were collapsing together, and a hideous voice screamed at full strength: ‘O je!’ Throughout the whole Zürichgass road and exactly along the Hag that extends toward Klingnau, this voice echoed after them. Only at the so-called Käppeli did it stop.
