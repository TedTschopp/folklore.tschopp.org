The maidens allowed the two boys to join them every day at the Abendsitz, but never on Friday or Saturday. The boys' curiosity about this prohibition became so great that they could not resist creeping up to the maidens' house one Friday and peering furtively through the window. There they saw one maiden take a little board from a vessel, coat it with an ointment, and say:", "“Zum Kamin hinaus und nirgends an!” (“Out through the chimney, touching nowhere!”) She was gone. The second did the same and vanished after the first. As soon as the maidens had disappeared, the two lads entered the house, went to the magic vessel, and spoke the saying. Invisible hands carried them out through the chimney and as far as the Seefeldalp behind Beatenberg. A magnificent palace stood there, filled with people. Many musicians sat in a row, among them the Sennen's cat; the boys now understood why the animal was always so sleepy during the day. Soon the dancing began. Food and drink were served afterward, but no bread. At last, the guests brought the lads a book in which they were to write their names. Boldly, one of the brothers took the offered goose quill and wrote the name “Jesus Christ” in large, clear strokes across the middle of the page. Suddenly, all the splendor was over: the palace collapsed, and the whole Strüdelgesellschaft scattered. The brothers found themselves alone again on the Seefeldalp and did not know which way to turn. Then the tall Heini appeared, told them where they were, and explained which road they should take home. The magic book, however, had remained in their hands. They brought it to the Landvogt of Interlaken, who had it thrown into the burning oven.
