This legend owes its origin to the following account by a chamois hunter. One evening at nightfall, he was preparing to sleep on the hay in a barn on the Gigerwald estate when a piercing scream, penetrating marrow and bone, rang out in the depths below. A moment passed, and the same scream was repeated, now closer; then, after another moment, it sounded immediately beside the barn. Now the hunter no longer felt at ease. He reached for his Stutzer in order to leave. When he had descended the ladder, the mountain spirit seized him by the hair and carried him away through the air with the speed of lightning. In this need and danger he invoked the three holiest names, whereupon the Kobolt let him fall. When at last he found the path to Vättis, the spirit teased him again and led him astray. At one moment he came down to the Tamina, at another up as far as the rock walls, and so he went up and down in zigzags all night until morning, when he arrived physically and mentally exhausted and dripping with the sweat of fear.
