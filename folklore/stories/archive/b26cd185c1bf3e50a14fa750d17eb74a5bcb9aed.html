He died at Salenegg, and his body was laid in a coffin in the great knights’ hall. During the night, while storm and tempest raged outside and only one little light alone kept watch over the dead man, a mighty carriage drove up in which the good knight was carried away; great stones were placed in the coffin instead of him. His relatives had come, bribed the watchman, and stolen his earthly remains in order to bury him according to the customs of the Roman Catholic Church. — When, on the day of his funeral, the coffin was carried out of the knights’ hall with great pomp, the living form of the valiant Molina was seen at the window above the balcony. As the receptacle for the dead was lifted to be carried away, he called to the funeral procession, “Is it heavy?” and then vanished. — The men who were just about to carry the coffin hurriedly set it down, and when it was opened, a quantity of stones was found inside instead of the supposed knight. —", "It is also said of this Molina that he was the last inhabitant of Aspermont Castle above Jenins and that one fine morning his servant found him lying dead on the floor before the bed, with his face blackish blue and twisted backward.
