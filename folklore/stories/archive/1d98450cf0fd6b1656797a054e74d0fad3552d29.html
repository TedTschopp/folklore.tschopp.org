A quarter of an hour from the village, on the slope of the Lägeren mountain, there lies amid the rocky scree a wooded place called Steinbodenmätteli. Some locate this Vogt’s castle here. Others seek it on the heights of the Lägeren toward the village of Otelfingen, where old fragments of masonry are likewise found. It is regarded as certain, however, that even now the Vogt Metteli drives from Steinbodenmätteli across the Zindelmatten toward the protected Bannholz and from there follows the old road into the village. Today Sulzberg Castle, near Untereck in the district of Rorschach, is popularly called Möttelisschloss. It is said to be the only castle in the region that the Appenzellers left undestroyed in their war of freedom. Rudolf Mötteli, the brother of wealthy Hans, is said to have bought it. It is now owned by the Salis-Soglio family. There two maidens, young and beautiful, dressed in white and wearing red shoes but both walking in chains, guard a treasure buried in the castle cellar. They movingly beg anyone who knocks at midnight for the kiss that will release them. Yet a gigantic dog always prevents anyone from raising Mötteli’s money, and so the castle decays more and more. At the castle site one hears skittles being rolled, the ball striking the pins, and the players’ merry laughter. Cf.
