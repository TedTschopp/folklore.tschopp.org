Consequently, they were often struck by the trunk or a branch and either killed or seriously injured. Tree felling was therefore very dangerous work; people felled as little as possible and made do with fallen and storm-thrown wood. The following tells how people learned from a wild Männlein to fell trees with less danger.", "It had long been known that the wild Männlein possessed such a secret method, but they did not share it. Once, when a man was felling a tall, mighty fir in a forest, one intended as the principal beam for a town-hall roof frame, a wild Männlein joined him. It hopped around him, leaped to and fro like a Gemschen, and exchanged many merry remarks with the woodcutter. When the fir began to fall, the woodcutter ran off with all his might, but the Männlein did not flee at all, and yet the fir did not strike it. The woodcutter escaped with his fright, but he did not notice what the Männlein had done to remain safely nearby. A few days later the woodcutter returned to the same place to fell another fir, and the wild Männlein appeared again. The woodcutter knew well that asking the Männlein for its secret would be in vain, so he approached the matter differently. He said boldly to the Männlein, “Now I know how you manage not to meet with an accident—come to harm—while felling trees. I saw it the last time you were here, and now I am no longer afraid of this work.” The Männlein replied, “Yes, you saw that I stayed beside the trunk and watched where the fir would fall.
