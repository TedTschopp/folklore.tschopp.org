The light shone from the windows of a large house, which they entered. A woman sat spinning in the Stube. She received the children lovingly, gave them food and drink, and hid them behind the mighty stove made of Lavetschstein. After an hour, the door was torn open and in came a snorting giant with a terrible maw. He groped around the Stube and called so loudly that the windows trembled, “I smell human flesh.” The woman denied it, and everything would have gone well if one of the boys had not carelessly stuck out his head, allowing the wicked Menschenfresser to see and devour him in an instant. But this did not satisfy the giant's hunger. He ate another boy and then his own wife as punishment for her lie. He locked the other children in a chicken coop beneath the stove so that they would grow fat, and he lay down to sleep. The next day the Menschenfresser did not awaken particularly early. Yawning, he opened the chicken coop, took the eldest boy, and asked whether he knew how to search for lice. The boy said that he did, and the giant sat down, bent his head to his knees, and let the boy work in his hair. But the boy was clever and tickled and scratched until the ugly Menschenfresser fell into a deep sleep.
