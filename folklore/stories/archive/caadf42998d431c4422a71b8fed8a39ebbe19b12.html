People say that even now, an entire trough full lies there in the ground, with schûflä and chärst—shovels and mattocks—beside it. But they could not manage it alone. The priest, I believe the late Bürgi, helped them—not with the digging, but otherwise—and he said he wanted none of it if they succeeded in obtaining it. They dug until they already had the whole full trough outside, but they were not allowed to say anything to one another. When the trough already stood free and clear, one of them looked ahead and saw someone approaching on a white horse. He thought it was the priest, for that gentleman almost always rode a white horse because he had very bad feet from his zipperlî, his gout. The one riding up, however, called to the others: “I will's halb, i will's halb!—I want half, I want half!” Then the man who saw him coming said: “Do you see there? He is already coming, de sakermentspaff—the damned priest—and wants half, though he did nothing for it. If only the thunderstorm would strike him!” As soon as he said this, the tools and the trough fell back into the hole, and at that very moment, the priest was at home together with his white horse. The money, the tools, and the trough are still there now. If only we knew where they were, we would go pull them out!
