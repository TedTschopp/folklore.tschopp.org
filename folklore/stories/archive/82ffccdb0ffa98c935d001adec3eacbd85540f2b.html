A great treasure is hidden there. A certain circumstance induced a man to try to recover it. The man was working in a nearby field and went into the neighboring grove of beech trees because he wished to enjoy his supper in the shade. After eating his bread, he saw blunt nails at his feet. He casually put several that still seemed usable into his pocket, and in the morning they had all become shining ducats. He then wanted to search for more nails, but found none. To recover the treasure, he went to a Capuchin friar, who told him that on the night from Green Thursday to Good Friday, a boar would appear upon the castle with all the castle keys around its neck. Whoever was at the castle at the stroke of twelve that night would receive from the boar the keys to every chamber. His equally greedy wife helped him. At the appointed hour of the night, they set out while the wind howled eerily in the little wood. Scarcely had they reached the foot of the hill when the witching hour struck. The man sank unconscious to the ground, and his wife, seized by terror, abandoned him. Since then, no one has thought of recovering the treasure. A large gray dog now regularly walks from this castle to the one opposite. It is even said to have thrust itself against my grandfather's chest in the middle of the night under bright moonlight, though without causing him any harm. Mischievous people have thrown stones at the dog without making it turn aside." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
