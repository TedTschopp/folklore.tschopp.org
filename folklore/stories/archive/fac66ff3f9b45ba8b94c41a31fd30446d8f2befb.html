He then lit a fire beneath the animal’s belly so that the flames rose on its right and left and scattered the Reckholder-Holz and the roots into the fire. He had scarcely done so when the same little old woman appeared in the stable doorway and begged most piteously that he not inflict such torments upon the poor animal, for it would not help the creature. But the man made the fire still larger until he saw that the little old woman suffered the greatest torment while the animal felt nothing at all from the fire. The witch (for such the little old woman was) now confessed to the man many things she had “set in motion,” or committed. But on this occasion he showed mercy.
