The city was proud of them but also watched them closely, so that none should ever escape and reveal the secret to others. Once, however, one of these Venediger truly escaped when the desire seized him and reached Lucerne. But the other Venetians were already on his trail, and their envoys arrived among us almost at the same time to secure his arrest under the pretense that he was a great criminal. This was done. Under interrogation, the prisoner solemnly protested his innocence. His pursuers, however, presented false documents. When the accused saw the people of Lucerne wavering, he promised to make them a golden chain with links an inch thick, so long that it would reach around the city. But the other Venetians did everything possible to make the people of Lucerne believe that he was a villain without equal. They truly allowed doubt to sway them: if he were perhaps so wicked a criminal, he could also do them great harm if released, and at the same time they hoped to receive a handsome gift from Venice for their compliance. They complied, and the man was executed. As they led him across the Reuss bridge and along the Reuss to the place of execution, he threw from a glass vessel a liquid or a stone into the Reuss that made the water boil for a great distance.
