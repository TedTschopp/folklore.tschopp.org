The servants complied. From that time onward, the one sleeping toward the window began to waste away and likewise complained of oppressive dreams, while the other recovered and soon became the same strong farmhand he had been before. The herdsman once told his brother, a brave and clever man, about this remarkable occurrence. ‘This business is easily settled,’ said the brother; he would come and lie in the bad place himself. Once in bed, he closed his eyes only as a pretense and did not sleep. Nothing happened until about half past eleven, when the little sliding window moved, something shaped like a half-circle flew in, and—whisk!—went around his neck. Strangely, in his legs it was proclaimed that two times two made four, and his toes became like clumps. Higher up, his stomach developed a craving for oats, and his belly no longer wished to stand upright as it normally did. His face felt as though his mouth and nose had become its chief elongated feature, and he was seized by a strong desire to neigh like a horse. Before he could decide whether or not he really was a horse, he already had to gallop like the Alpschimmel—not over solid ground but high through the cutting air—and away he rushed, far, far over many church steeples. Someone sat upon him, holding the bridle tight and steering him as horses are steered. After a long storming rush, he finally felt solid ground beneath his hooves and had to stop. And the person who dismounted from him, the horse, was his beautiful sister-in-law. Now he knew what a Hexenzaum was. Having flown in from every end of the world to the Hexensabbat in glittering company, the Alpine woman enjoyed herself and at last mounted her horse again and sped away. On the road, however, something compelled her to dismount and tie the horse up. Because the wicked bridle encircled only the outside of the housing of his thought-factory and could not reach the soft material within it, his thinking continued undisturbed and reached this judgment: if the bridle were slipped off, the horse would be a man again. The experiment proved the judgment entirely correct. When the woman returned to the spot, two human hands reached from an ambush and unexpectedly wound the same bridle around her neck.
