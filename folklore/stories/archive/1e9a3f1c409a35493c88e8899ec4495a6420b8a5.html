Ahasuerus, the Wandering Jew, has already passed this place three times. The first time the Schratten was a vineyard, afterward an alp, and most recently, in part, nothing but bare rock. Hear how this came about. Two brothers, one of whom was blind, owned properties here and wished to divide them. The blind man trusted his brother’s love of justice and left the marking of the boundaries to him. But the brother set the boundaries as he pleased and appropriated the richest alp for himself. The foremost sons sought the hand of his only daughter, for she was rich and beautiful but infinitely proud. She would belong only to the man who could climb the Schibegütsch by its steepest side. So many attempts were made, so many deaths occurred, and indignation grew greater and greater. The blind man then learned how he had been cheated and uttered a terrible curse against his brother: may the Devil tear apart the magnificent pasture and deal with the unjust man and his child as they deserved. No sooner was it spoken than Satan laid waste the place, turning it into bare rock and imprinting his claws upon it. They can still be seen. The Evil One cast the unjust, perjured man into a hole near the Schibegütsch, and whoever passes throws a stone inside, as is done at Absalom’s tomb in Jerusalem.
