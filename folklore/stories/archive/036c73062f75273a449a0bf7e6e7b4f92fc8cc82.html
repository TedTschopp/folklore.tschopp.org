At that time, our forefathers were holding a great Tagsatzung at Baden. On the day when everyone knew that the honorable ambassadors of the praiseworthy Thirteen Cantons and the zugewandte Orte were enjoying a splendid banquet in the great Herrengarten there, the Steucheler happened to come onto the bridge beneath the Multer Gate in St. Gallen. There, he found Doctor Theophrastus sitting with other distinguished citizens. He stopped beside the gentlemen, greeted them, and said: \"Now our most esteemed ambassadors in Baden will soon be making merry. If I were there, I could earn a good tip with my transverse flute.\" \"Why, friend,\" replied the doctor, \"if you have such a great desire for a tip, that is easily remedied. Go home, put on other clothes, take your flute, and come back here, friend!\"—\"Very well,\" answered the Steucheler. \"I already know, Doctor Theophrastus, that you can sing one more little tune than other people. I shall be back at once.\"", "With that, the minstrel hurried home, put on his Sunday doublet, stuck three colorful feathers in his hat, took up his Schwegel, and was back at the Multer Gate in a flash. \"Here I am, polished and trim,\" he said to the doctor, \"but unfortunately I see no horse anywhere. Or do you regret what you promised me, learned sir?\"—\"The horse is saddled and bridled,\" Theophrastus calmly replied. \"You will find it tied up outside by the shooting pavilion, where it stands more comfortably than here.
