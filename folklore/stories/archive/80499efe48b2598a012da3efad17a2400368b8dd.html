It often came as far as the village's outermost houses, but never beyond the roof eaves. The people wished to redeem the spirit, and one especially devout woman asked the priest for advice. He told her to have three Masses said for the spirit; it would then come to thank her for its deliverance. She must only take care not to offer it her hand. A few days later, the redeemed spirit appeared and gratefully stretched out its hand toward her with the words, “Chum bald noh.” Remembering the priest's warning, the woman, who was spinning at the time, did not give it her hand but held out the distaff of the spinning wheel. The spirit seized it and disappeared. The flax upon the distaff was completely scorched. The woman died soon afterward.\nG. Kessler (Schweiz.
