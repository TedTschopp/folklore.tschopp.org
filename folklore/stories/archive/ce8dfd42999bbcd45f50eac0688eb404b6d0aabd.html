From here, the farmer once set out with his wife and son on a pilgrimage to Saint James. Along the way, they entered an inn in a city, but an evil innkeeper lived there. He secretly slipped a golden cup into the son's travel bag, then had him pursued, arrested as a thief, and searched. Nothing could help him; the poor young man was found guilty of theft and then hanged. But the father and mother nevertheless wished to fulfill their vow and sadly traveled onward. When they reached Saint James, they lamented their great and grave distress to the dear saint and then heard an exceedingly sweet, comforting voice saying that their son would be returned to them. For he was still alive on the gallows; they should only return there, first going to the bishop in that city and telling him what had happened. They went there and found the bishop at his midday meal. Two roasted little hens and a Güggelchen—a little rooster—were just being served, and when the bishop had heard the pilgrims' story, he said: ‘Just as surely as these roasted little hens together with the Güggelchen will never come alive again, so surely can your son no longer be alive on the gallows.’ And the roasted birds came alive; when the parents reached their son, he too was still alive, and the gallows even bent down and gently set him upon the ground. He was hale and cheerful, and everyone rejoiced with him. The evil innkeeper went to the gallows in his place. Thus, the people of Bösegg journeyed joyfully home. The two little hens together with the Güggelchen were brought into the church, where they lived and were fed. When three years had passed, they laid three eggs, from which hatched two young hens and one rooster, while the old birds died.
