They owned a fine cherry tree, the only one in the entire valley at that time. Thus it was inevitable that the covetous valley folk would now and then beg them for a few of the delicious fruits, but scarcely anyone dared eat them, for people did not quite trust the two women or their generosity. One young, courageous fellow ventured the feat and set to work on the bowl full of cherries that they had placed for him on the parlor table. Then, all at once, he was driven irresistibly out of the parlor and into the kitchen. There the two beauties stood at the hearth, stirring a little pot as though possessed and muttering as they did so, ‘Chämi üff und niänä-n-a!’ Now something lifted the fellow and carried him out through the chimney[1] and high over every mountain, through the air and away into infinite distances, until at last he came down in the densest thorn thicket in an entirely different part of the world and became stuck. There he stood, not knowing which way to turn. Finally he began to pray and implore the Mother of God for her help.
