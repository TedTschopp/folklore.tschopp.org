When he had climbed as far as the spring on the untere Staffel, he saw an old, rusted key lying beside the cliff. He soon noticed a keyhole in the rock as well. He inserted the key and turned it; the wall opened and admitted him through a passage in the rock into two large chambers, one after the other. In the second, a hanging stone blocked the way, though with difficulty a person could crawl beneath it. When he had done so, a voice arose: “Unfortunate man, complete what you have begun; go into the third chamber too!” He obeyed and entered another hall. A maiden sat there in old-fashioned dress, with a pot full of gold at her feet and a golden bell on the wall beside her. She told him that she was enchanted there until a deliverer should come; now he had a free choice among three gifts.", "He could take either the bell or a golden hare, she said; but if he chose the maiden herself, he would receive the other two treasures as well. The young man thought for a moment of his Lieseli and hesitated. At last, he took the golden bell from the wall. While the maiden was about to overwhelm him with lamentations, he fled through the passages. Behind him, the door slammed shut so violently that the mountainside trembled. Now he did not climb farther toward the pastured cows but went down into the valley to Lieseli. If he brought her the golden bell—a golden bell for the Alpfahrt—she would marry him without objection. But when he reached his beloved, she had long since forgotten him, had long since fallen in love with another, had married that man, and already had several children by him.", "What was the young man to do now?
