Charles the Bold of Burgundy is once said to have pitched his war tent there when he invaded the country to defeat Switzerland and besieged Grandson. An oak surrounded by a ring of strong firs stands on the hilltop; it is said to have been planted by Charles's conquerors and to be the same age as the one standing near Grandson Castle.", "An elderly Waatländer farmer whose fields lie by this oak hill is the teller of the following old-fashioned-sounding legend.", "More than a hundred years ago, young Johannes once walked this way. He was deeply troubled in his heart, for he had just come from the people who had held his poverty against him and therefore refused him the hand of their daughter, whom he was courting. The holy season was approaching, the country lay full of snow, and not a soul remained on the cold road. He surrendered himself to the gloomiest thoughts and spoke his heartache aloud to himself in broken, isolated words. It was growing very dark as he passed here toward the so-called Tuilerien. Then, unexpectedly, he heard his name called from the oak hill. A man in very costly armor stood before him and said reassuringly: ‘Johannes, I know well what torments you, but take courage; soon everything shall be different. Come alone to this hill next Christmas after eleven o'clock. That leaves just enough time to carry something for me into the lake, and if you are then skillful enough to unbuckle my old Wehrgehänge before twelve o'clock and can finish it in complete silence, the treasures lying within this hill shall be yours.’ Johannes scarcely had time to promise before the spirit vanished and everything was as before. Two days remained until Christmas. Johannes considered carefully whether he might commit a sin by using the holy night to raise treasure. But he could honestly admit that he undertook it from no greedy desire, but from purest love for his Margarethe. He knew that the faithful girl would give her hand to no one but him. Thus he would release and free her more than merely the spirit of a presumed knight; this was the treasure that concerned him, and he could raise it most successfully if he acquired the riches hidden in that hill and thereby obtained the marriage fortune required by agreement. So at the appointed time he went to the hill and met the armored man. The latter knocked upon the oak, and immediately they both sank together into the earth. Here they found themselves in a vault supported by many pillars. It was bright as candlelight. Banners and weapons hung all around the walls. Along the masonry stood war engines of every kind, even artillery and pyramids of cannonballs. Between them, just as many vessels stood open, some shimmering with gold and others flashing with jewels. But there was no time to look around for long, for a fat, coal-black monster soon approached on all fours from the far end of the hall, and the armored man said to Johannes: ‘Here is my favorite cat. Throw it down from the Tophet into the lake at once, then return here as quickly as you can. Beware of losing a single minute; beware of speaking one little word—it would mean your death. Now go!’ Johannes immediately did as he was commanded. Half breathless, he came running onto the Tophet with the enormous cat. This is a rock on Lake Neuchâtel to which an old road made of pebbles, long since washed over again by the waves, once led—a so-called Heidenweg on which pagan priests had once processed to sacrifice at a temple of the lake god. As he threw the cat down into the water, the struggling monster scratched him so violently that he already had a ‘Bonnerre äe eünt!’ in his mouth; but remembering the armored man's warning, he silently bit back his pain. Now he had to wash his bleeding hands quickly, and this too was accomplished with the greatest speed, for scarcely did he touch the water when the until-then gentle lake surged and stormed to such a terrifying height that Johannes fled as fast as possible and escaped back into the hill. ‘Now pull yourself together,’ began the knight. ‘The hardest task remains. Know that I am Duke Charles of Burgundy. Since I broke my word to the Swiss before that castle there, good fortune has departed from me. I sit here in this hill armed and armored until a person is found who boldly disarms me. Young oaks have since grown above my head and rotted away again; firs then rooted here in their place, but no one of your race has been stouthearted enough to give me his word and keep it to the end. Be that man, and all your own wishes will be fulfilled at the same time. Unbuckle this sword from me. But you must do this as silently as the first task!’ Johannes set to work. The man seemed to grow upward beside his hand; standing on tiptoe, Johannes reached the Wehrgehänge and untied the embroidered straps. Then the heavy sword slipped from its sheath and struck him a deep wound. ‘Ladre du eiel...’ he wanted to cry, but mastered himself just in time, and at that very moment the tower clocks at Grandson and Yverdon struck twelve in a single peal. The work was thus accomplished. With heartfelt thanks, the spirit allowed steadfast Johannes to take his choice from the riches lying open. Johannes had nothing with him but his handkerchief. He filled it with golden Taler, as many as would fit up to its last button. With the final gold piece, he found himself aboveground beneath the oak once more.", "Everything else had vanished, even the fresh wound, but not the money in the cloth. Soon afterward, Johannes married his faithful Margarethe. His married happiness proved so enduring that in old age he resolved to leave a sign of the gratitude he owed to dear God. He therefore endowed his fellow citizens at Montagny with a large sum on condition that they build from it a church of their own. This was done. Thus the village owes it to this adventure that ever since it has had to go to church neither in Grandson nor in Yverdon, between which it lies exactly halfway.
