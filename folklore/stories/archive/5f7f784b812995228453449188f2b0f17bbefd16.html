About halfway down this slope, an enormous rock wall rises high beside the footpath, and footsteps echo eerily in these fissured masses of stone. At the foot of this rock wall one sees a broad rocky grotto descending vertically into the dark depths. A family of dwarfs is said to have lived here in olden times. From this grotto they visited the people round about and often helped them in need. Once a man who wished to buy wine in Rümligen for a Kindbettmahlzeit, but had no money for it, was returning sadly over the Längenberg with borrowed wine. As he passed the grotto where the dwarfs lived, one of them asked how he fared. The man told it of his difficult situation, and—whoosh!—the Zwerglein vanished with the little wine cask the man carried under his arm. It soon returned, however, and gave the astonished man the cask back with the words, “Now go home and serve wine to the Gevatterleuten.” With these words the little benefactor vanished again. Amazed and reassured, the child’s godfather went home and served his guests.
