Here, in the middle of beautiful meadows, two enormous boulders are still pointed out that a witch is said to have carried there upon her head, although their immense weight did not alter her slender, upright gait in the least; indeed, she is even said to have knitted busily beneath them. A peculiar legend is attached to the witch who lived in the Bächen at the foot of the Riffelberg. She carried on quite wickedly in the now well-known Riffel region. High up on the Riffelberg, she is said to have struck the ground with a white apron, or Vorschoss, and thereby set various avalanches in motion even in summer. They then plunged thundering down from the mountains, devastated everything on the plain, caused the country people much sorrow, and drew many tears. Weary of this uncanny conduct, people made preparations to capture the witch. They devised a time when the witch could expect complete safety. She must nevertheless have noticed something of the danger threatening her—but too late, for she no longer had time to flee the house. What did she do? A cleverly devised deception was still to save her. She drew out the rosary and began to pray loudly behind the stove. They found her in this position; she called to those entering: ‘You find me engaged in a good work!’ Nonetheless she was seized, bound firmly to a sled, and dragged down into the village to be delivered to the authorities. On the way, she asked her captors to allow her to retie the laces of her shoes, which had come undone. Fortunately, they refused, for they would have paid dearly. Before her death by fire, the witch confessed that if this had been permitted and she had been able to touch the earth once more with her feet, she would have gained the strength to escape their hands; indeed, with her sharp-nailed shoes, she would not only have knocked out their teeth but shattered their skulls.", "(told by Chaplain Mooser)" ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Strideln”: a regional name for witches or dangerous female night beings, related to Striga traditions; here the collector pairs them directly with Hexen without distinguishing a separate class.", "Swiss term “Vorschoss”: a regional word for an apron, explicitly glossed as Schürze by the source; the witch uses the white garment as a magical instrument to release avalanches." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
