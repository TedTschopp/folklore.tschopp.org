Suddenly at night the cattle would leap from their resting places and scatter bellowing in every direction, so that the Alpine herdsmen had the greatest difficulty driving them together again. Some blamed wild animals, but others blamed Bozen or spirits. Once, after the herdsmen had endured another such stormy night, the Senn slept in the sun far from the hut. When he awoke, a small, ice-gray little man wearing a broad weather hat stood before him. The Senn, who was a gruff and fearless fellow, defiantly called to him: ‘What are you doing there, you gray scoundrel!’ At that very instant the little man rushed at him, rubbed his beard, and placed his mouth upon the herdsman’s. For it is said that spirits or the dead cannot speak with the living unless the living address them and they can draw upon their breath. The little man then straightened up and began recounting the cattle thefts he had committed on the alps, continuing on and on until the morning Betenläuten. Everything he revealed to the Senn and what the herdsman did for his redemption remained secret.
