Every thirty years—this is the number the old people give—the lake overflows its banks. Its waters rear into a roaring vortex and, amid fearful whistling and crashing, rush toward the shore, over high cliffs, and down into the valley, spreading destruction far and wide. The Evital in Silenen bears eloquent witness to their disastrous work. It is the punishment for a great sin committed in gray antiquity high above on the Sewli Alp.", "The same wealthy family lived there for many, many summers. The Alp produced an abundance of the richest milk-giving herbs. The cows did not need, as they do today, to be driven onto the steep Planggen and wild, remote grazing grounds and guarded with difficulty. Milk flowed in streams, and the work was slight. But abundance and idleness bred arrogance. The Alpine herdsmen believed they could live without God's protection and blessing. They did not raise their hearts to almighty God either to ask humbly for his aid or to thank him for his rich benefits. They ended their day's work without calling people to prayer and began it without a pious intention. Once, they invited the valley people to a merry day. Then, by God, together they built a bridge across the lake out of precious cheese and Anken, mocked God and his blessing, danced, and caroused day and night. The measure of their arrogance was now full, and God's patience was exhausted. A storm broke over the Alp. Streams roared in from every side and swept everything—livestock and people, turf and huts—into the lake.
