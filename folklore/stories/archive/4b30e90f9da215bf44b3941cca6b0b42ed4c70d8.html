Anyone wishing to go from there to the high valley of Davos by the old road will notice, not far from the village, a boulder of unusual shape. This is the Geisslerstein. A Grisons peasant from Klosters related the following about it.", "Long ago there were still wild people in this valley. They were good-natured, but although very small in stature, they possessed extraordinary bodily strength. Their whole bodies were covered in hair, and they wore a skirt of hides around their loins. Instead of a staff, each carried in his hand a young fir tree torn up by the roots. In those days the village of Klosters also had the honor of having one such wild man as a neighbor. He was known everywhere as the Geissler and, for more years than the oldest men then living could remember, had tended the community's goats for a small payment in Zieger and cheese. The people gladly gave him this payment, for every evening the goats came home so healthy and gave so much milk that, by year's end, whole wagonloads of both butter and cheese could be carted away and sold for handsome sums. Early each morning the villagers drove their goats as far as the Geisslerstein. The little man was already waiting there and drove them onward, no one knew where; at the appointed time each evening, they were all back at the stone with udders so full that they could scarcely walk for the milk. Yet one thing was strange. The Geissler did speak to the goats, which understood and obeyed him, but he never spoke a single word to people. In silence he took the animals into his care each morning; in silence he delivered them back each evening; in silence he came to the boulder every autumn on payment day and received the cheese and Zieger, which likewise had to be laid there for him without a word.", "At last the people grew vexed by this perpetual silence, and some inquisitive young fellows agreed upon a way to get him into their power and see whether they could coax any explanation at all from him about his mysterious nature. One evening they hid behind the Geisslerstein and, when he was close enough, sprang at him together. But with a few powerful blows the little man flung one this way and another that until every one of them lay on the ground; then he hurried toward the nearby forest with incredible speed. He had conducted this scuffle in silence too. The next morning the people of Klosters again drove their goats to the stone, but one may imagine who was not there that day. They waited and waited, but the Geissler did not come. It was therefore decided that those who had assaulted him must now tend the goats in his place. And so they did.", "But alas, the goats came home with not half as much milk. This helped. He collected the goats as before; once again they were well fed and rich in milk, except that from then on the farmers always had to set down twice the number of cheeses and pieces of Zieger as the Geissler's payment. In time, however, another few curious young fellows grew up who would have liked to become more closely acquainted with the Geissler. Since they already knew his strength and agility, they tried a different method this time. He had a habit of drinking every evening from the little spring nearest the stone. They now made use of this circumstance.
