A man from Urswil near Hochdorf, where they had the famous Streggelen, once learned this. No tooth troubles him now. One day this man went over to Hohenrain and bought several sows from a rich farmer. Yes, they were beautiful, fat, well-grown animals, and he gladly counted out his bright thalers on the spot from the large money belt strapped around his body, set his hat a little askew, and took cheerful leave. The pigs were brought safely as far as the village brook. Then suddenly it was as though the notorious herd of the Gerasenes had appeared: swift as the wind, they darted into the brook and all floated away as bundles of straw. If the man of Urswil pulled a long face at that, he allowed himself all the less time before returning to the farm where so peculiar a breed was raised. Following the custom of his trade, the cheated man unloaded his first polite greeting and outpouring of the heart upon the farmer’s wife, whom he found working in the hall. Once she had received her proper share, the pig dealer entered the room, where he expected to find the farmer. The man had indeed stretched himself along the top of the stove and pretended to sleep very deeply, though the one now releasing a flood of abuse was no consumptive. Determined to wake him, the dealer seized the farmer’s leg, tugged at it, and tore the whole thing off into his hand! He was so frightened that he quickly made himself scarce and had never run downhill so lightly. You see, the artful, cunning farmer afterward laughed and, without a doctor, immediately put his own leg back into place.
