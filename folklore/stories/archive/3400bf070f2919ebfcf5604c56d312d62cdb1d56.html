Each time, he is said to have taken home whole wagonloads of gold-bearing stones that only an expert could recognize. A boy from Sörenberg, who was very faithful and devoted to him, usually helped him search. In the evenings the two commonly slept on a haystack. Once the Venediger wanted to take his good helper home with him, but the boy declined, promising that someday he would travel there on his own with a load of gold-bearing stones he had found himself. The Venediger doubted that he would succeed but accepted the proposal and departed for Venice. Through his dealings with the stranger, the shepherd boy had paid close attention to everything and truly was able to distinguish genuine gold-bearing stones from false ones, but extracting the precious metal remained a secret to him. One day he set out with a fine load, reached Venice, and there entered the quarter of the gold-makers. As an uninitiated witness, he would inevitably have been killed had his greatly astonished friend not taken charge of him and led him into his house, though with the warning that he would be wise to leave again soon. Nevertheless, the man received him very kindly, entertained him magnificently, and gave him enough gifts to make him rich. Among other remarkable objects, he showed him a glass called a mountain mirror. When the boy looked into it, he saw what the people back home in Sörenberg were doing at that very moment. When it was time to leave, the Venediger led his guest to a splendid bed. The boy was not permitted to undress, however; he had to keep his travel bag, in which the gifts were packed, slung over him and hold his walking stick in his arm. Thus he fell asleep—only to awaken on a haystack in Sörenberg, not in Venice in his friend’s palace, for by his art the friend had miraculously enchanted him home during the night." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Venediger”: literally ‘Venetian’; in Alpine mining lore it denotes a mysterious foreign mineral prospector or gold-seeker, conventionally associated with Venice, though such figures were not necessarily historical Venetians." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
