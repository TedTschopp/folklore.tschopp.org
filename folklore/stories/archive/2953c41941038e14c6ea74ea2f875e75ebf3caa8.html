All at once—it must have been August 14, 1799—a military column moved across Guretzmettlen and Seewen toward the Schyen. These were the French, who had come over the Susten to attack the Austrians in the Meienschanze. At the time, however, we had no idea who these soldiers were, and we were greatly surprised when we saw their shining uniforms and polished weapons flashing from afar. Frightened by this unfamiliar spectacle, we ran away and hid behind rocks and bushes. The soldiers caught our goats and sucked milk from them. Then they chose the fattest one, slaughtered it, and immediately ate the raw meat. We now thought that the other goats might fare no better if we did not show ourselves. We therefore stepped out of our hiding place. The soldiers seemed pleased, and immediately asked whether one could advance from here against the Meienschanze and whether we knew a safe way there. We readily said yes, and they gave us some money with instructions to take it to the owner of the slaughtered goat. Gradually, we approached the Austrian outposts and were finally able to point down from the cliffs to the Meienschanze, where the Austrians were positioned. Gunfire began. The French could shoot down, but the Austrians' bullets could not reach upward. The Frenchmen instructed us to gather small stones, which they fired down upon their opponents with large-bored guns. Astonished, we asked the shooters why they used stones. They said stones were good enough because they would travel downward, and they had to conserve the lead. In this way, our companions shot down a large number of enemy soldiers and finally drove the rest from the fortification. The dead were thrown into a mass grave in the stony ground and lightly covered with earth.", "Josef Baumann, like other people from the region, adds the following to his father's account: The site of this grave can still be recognized near the former St. Nicholas Chapel, and from time to time bones appear here and there among the stones. Through the effects of the weather, a human skull also became visible one day not far from the church path in that area. Boys made sport of it and threw it into the Schanztobel. It rolled into the dreadful depths with a strange, supernatural rumble, but the next day the skull was always found back beside the path. The incident was reported to our pastor. He said that the head evidently wished to rest in consecrated ground. He took the skull and placed it in the ossuary by the parish church in Wassen. There it had peace. The vicinity of that soldiers' grave was uncanny in other ways too. At times, something there bstellt people, and a mysterious nocturnal light led many a traveler up into the Steingand or otherwise astray until the sound of the prayer bell released him in the morning.
