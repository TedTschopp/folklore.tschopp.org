He thought of the happy people he had met then, of the winning cordiality with which they had received and refreshed him, and of the beautiful country through which he had passed. He resolved to delight his heart once more with the sight. But gloomy forebodings oppressed his breast as he climbed the Maienwand. Thick fog hid the surrounding country from him. Once above, he saw it scattered by a tremendous gust of wind bursting from the Haslital. He thought he had lost his way. Dark spruce forests covered the mountain's steep flanks. For a long time, he searched in vain for human dwellings; at last he found a pair of huts, and then a few more. On the mountain slopes where vines had once grown green and oaks had rocked their curling heads—on the same slopes that later bore spruce—mighty masses of ice now hung, and gigantic glaciers filled the wild ravines. Torn needles of rock rose from the snow in dark majesty, seeming to soar toward heaven and defying the icy winds that snorted around their summits.
