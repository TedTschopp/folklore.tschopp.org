She accepted the courting visits of a young man from Linn until at last she had to press him urgently to marry her soon. This came so unexpectedly to her lover and so offended his village pride that he resolved to remove the girl by force. Within the next few nights, he again asked her to let him in. Unsuspectingly, she opened the Gadenfenster for him as usual. He immediately stabbed the poor woman three times and fled. In the adjoining room, separated only by a wooden wall, one of the many female pilgrims slept that night who traveled this way every year from Alsace and the Black Forest across the Bözberg to Einsiedeln on pilgrimage to the Schwarze Mutter Gottes. The pilgrim awoke at the wounded woman's groaning and raised the alarm in the house. When a light was brought, the dying woman was just able to speak her murderer's name before she passed away. Meanwhile, he had reached the Sandbrunnen, not far from the inn, and quickly tried to wash his bloody knife there. When the blood would not come off, he threw the knife away and ran home. He was arrested the very next morning. He denied the crime until the knife was held before him; then he confessed that he had been utterly unable to rub away the few drops of blood upon it, which remained as though eaten into the blade.
