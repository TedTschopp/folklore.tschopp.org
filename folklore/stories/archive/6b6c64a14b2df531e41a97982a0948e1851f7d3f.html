Traces of an old fortification can be found upon it—not stonework or remains of walls, but a ditch of considerable diameter that even now is three feet deep. The area within this ditch is known among the people as the castle site. The country folk know no name for this castle, but in nearby Zofingen it is called Staremberg.", "On the southern slope of this castle hill stands a farmhouse called Battenberg, probably referring to a St. Beatus or to a former householder of that name. It was once built so that one gable end faced the valley and the other the mountain. The dwelling was on the valley side and the barn on the mountain side. The latter was subject to a right of way allowing owners of the surrounding properties to drive their plow teams and loaded wagons through the barn’s threshing floor, and for that reason it always stood open. Nowadays a two-horse carriage passes through it, coming down from the Childberg. This apparition is said to be seen there especially in summer before a thunderstorm. The carriage takes its way southwest along the mountain slope, travels to the “Fröschengüllen” (sour meadows), crosses the Hemmeler, and returns to the mountain. People ride in it, but they are not heard to speak.", "Associated with it is a rider on a white horse who follows the same path. At the foot of the mountain he has a little spring of his own and waters his horse there. Whenever he is seen, prolonged rainy weather always follows. At times one hears lively hunting above on the hill. The dogs come across from the other side of the valley, run barking up the slope of the Childberg, and vanish in the forest. The hunter to whom they belong is known throughout the valley by the name Töst, the same name that is Türst in neighboring Lucerne and there designates the leader of the Türstengjaid, the Wild Hunt. The Töst wears a green coat and a hat of shining oilcloth. In former times, when the bilberries were ripe, he could be heard on the Heidenhubel not far from Teufenthal calling his dogs, and he was noticed from Hönneten and Liebigen. A terrible cloudburst would then always break, and the bad weather lasted for eight days.", "Opposite the Heidenhubel lies the Langnauerberg, which already belongs to the canton of Lucerne.
