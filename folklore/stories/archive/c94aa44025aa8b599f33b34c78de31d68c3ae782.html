Because of his plain clothing of brown Trilch, which his noble wife and daughters are said to have spun, as was still often customary at knights’ castles in those days, the Hofschranzen are said to have turned up their noses at him and even treated him contemptuously. His simple rural clothing was attributed now to his poverty, now to his stinginess; only with the duke did he stand in high favor and was always treated by him with distinction. Then a horseshoe was once found in the city’s main street, made of hammered silver. This caused no small sensation. “Who might the noble lord be,” the question went from mouth to mouth, “who has his riding horse shod with silver?” Word of this strange discovery reached even the court. But astonishment now rose to its highest point when people learned that it belonged to the count from Valais in the brown Trilch coat. Several courtiers who until then had scarcely looked at him over their shoulders even sent investigators to Valais to inquire into his financial circumstances. When these men returned and told of his great esteem in Valais and among many monarchs; of his palace, whose three high towers could be seen for miles; of his estates; and of how he could spend each night in one of his own houses from Brig to Milan and Geneva—in short, of his wealth—and, not content with that, also emphasized his generosity toward churches, monasteries, and the poor and the immense sums he devoted to beautifying houses of worship, public works, and charitable institutions, then the court servants who had regarded him so contemptuously for his poverty and stinginess opened their eyes wide. Their mockery of his brown Trilch coat grew subdued. Just as people had previously spoken often and half aloud about his stinginess and poverty, they now spoke everywhere and very loudly about his wealth and generosity. The silver horseshoe caused this change of tone and this new, favorable report." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Trilch”: a sturdy, plain woven cloth such as ticking or drill, associated here with domestically spun rural clothing; the source does not specify fiber or weave beyond its brown color.", "Swiss term “Hofschranzen”: a contemptuous word for fawning, status-conscious courtiers or palace sycophants; their scorn and sudden deference drive the tale’s social satire." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
