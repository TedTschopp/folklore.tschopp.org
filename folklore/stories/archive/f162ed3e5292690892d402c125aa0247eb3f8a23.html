The people believed they could not so carelessly cede to the monastery, without sinning against their grandchildren, this property left untouched since the time of their earliest fathers. A dispute arising from this was about to be decided by the Landvogt in favor of the community when the Klosterschaffner offered to swear that the forest had always been church land. He filled his boots with earth from the monastery garden and placed beneath his hat the milk ladle that the Sennen call the Schöpfer or judge. Then, with raised fingers, he swore that the forest belonged to the monastery as surely as he himself stood upon the Lord's land and a Schöpfer and judge was above him. Since that time, whenever timber is felled in these woods, a green-clad hunter wanders from the heights of Lindenberg as far as Wohlen; or, as a rider wearing tremendously large, spur-ringing boots, he drives the Holzfrevler into flight. He then rides a white horse, brandishes a fiery whip, and simultaneously spits fire from his mouth. Others maintain that his spirit was banished by religious means and exorcisms into the notorious Enziloch in Entlebuch and has at least not been seen in Muri since. In the Entlebuch Enziloch, or Sentiloch, by the Blumalpe, however, there is said to be a cave whose entrance is always freshly swept but is also blocked by a rock hanging from the vault by a silken thread. A hedge four cubits thick encloses the place. In the evening, the neighboring Sennen on the Napfberg never forget to call the englischen Gruß through their milk funnel toward the place of terror, so that no animal in the herd strays or is scattered.
