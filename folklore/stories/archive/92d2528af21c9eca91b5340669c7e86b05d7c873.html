Tradition relates that it was made by treasure diggers who burrowed in the old structure—probably not only here—in search of hidden riches. According to the legend, whose versions differ greatly, they are said actually to have been rewarded, for silver and gold drinking and table vessels, some of them partly melted, were reportedly found there. At last, as they dug farther, a large iron chest appeared, but it was guarded by a terrifying-looking dog. Because no one knew how to break this enchantment, the diggers had to leave the cave, though most reluctantly. When they returned as early as the following night, both dog and chest had vanished. Whether the enchantment had meanwhile been lifted by a more knowledgeable hand, or the chest and its guardian had disappeared again and sunk into the depths, could not be learned. According to another tradition, a large wagon full of gold and precious things was discovered and brought so far forward by magic formulas that its shaft and part of the front wheels could be seen. But when one man, at the sight, violated the strict command of silence with a loud cry of joy, everything vanished again into the bosom of the earth amid terrible crashing and roaring. It has probably remained there ever since and may remain for a long time or forever. This “insipid dog-and-wagon story,” however, occurs in many other places. The latter version, for example, also appears on the Schwendelberg and at the Burgbühl in Lenk. Everywhere, the attempt to raise the treasure after it had already half succeeded is said to have failed in the same way. This form of superstition appears formerly to have had many adherents in the region. Yet it seems to have lost considerable credit because of an unpleasant treasure-digging affair that took place nearby at the beginning of this century and in which even respected men participated. When the participants finally sought the return of the money swindled from them by an Emmenthaler “Schatzgräber,” they instead fell to quarreling among themselves, and a lengthy, disagreeable lawsuit was the bitter consequence.
