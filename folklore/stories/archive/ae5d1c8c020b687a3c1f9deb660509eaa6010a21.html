St. Niklausen (Samiklausen) in the Geschenertal is the oldest house of worship in the canton of Uri, the legend of the upper Reuss Valley stubbornly maintains. Here, where Christianity first took root in the region, Waldbrüder are said to have lived with a priest. Now at St. Niklausen, the people assert, a holy body lies buried. It is said to be not much more than a hundred years since an honest shepherd boy entered the church early one morning. A priest came and asked him to serve at Mass. The boy did so joyfully. After Mass, the clergyman invited him to return every morning to serve, but strictly forbade him to tell anyone about it, whereupon he vanished. The shepherd boy kept his word and was also very discreet. Thus for a long time he assisted the priest’s spirit at the holy sacrifice. At last his mother noticed that her son always left at a fixed time and became very urgent if delayed. She suspected a secret and pressed him, but for a long while in vain. A mother’s tenderness nevertheless loosened his tongue. She then told the boy to go back and serve Mass again; but when he entered the chapel, everything was already over, the priest had vanished, and the lights were extinguished. He tried again, going earlier the next day and earlier still, but whenever he arrived, the Mass was over. Each time he saw only the smoking candles as evidence of what had happened.
