They poured liquid manure down people's throats and tied them to their horses' tails. They then dragged the poor victims above Wil through the Barte, the Härdlenholz, and the Götzelenholz, through the Immenholz along the Altweggässlein to Gippingen on the Aare, and hurled them over the bank into the river. If one asks why they never advanced from here into inner Switzerland, nor even farther into the open countryside locally called the Kirchspiel, people point to an old house in Hettenschwil upon which the heraldic shields of the VIII Alte Orte are painted. These shields, it is said, held the enemy in such respect that they never pushed their troops beyond the Sennenloch, a forest valley near Hettenschwil. Instead, they built entrenchments above in the mountain's hiding place. The earthworks still stood fifty years ago and have preserved the name Schwedenholz for the spot to this day.", "Wherever the name of the Eidgenossen was not proclaimed in those days, matters went exactly the opposite way. Thus, in the Fricktal village of Ittenthal, a spacious mountain cave is called the Schwedenloch because all the unprotected farmers had to take refuge inside it. Today, upon the same forest paths along which the poor country people were dragged to their deaths, Swedish horsemen ride back and forth all night upon half-horses. They drive their horses out to pasture and ride them to water at the place called the Schwetti. One rides ahead of them and can be recognized especially by the sickle he swings in his hand. The people of Wallbach on the Rhine likewise know of him. They say a Fricktal man beheaded him with the sickle when the rider tried to stop him from cutting grain that was already overripe. Near Laufenburg, people say he is the King of Sweden himself, whose own men cut off his head during a riot in camp. In rainy weather, a hundred voices begin shouting hurrah amid such banging of kettles and rattling that no one in the entire region would dare enter the forest at night. This strange uproar has been called the Mattisee. The Schwedenhaus, which the enemy once inhabited high above upon the wooded height called the Schwedenholz, was brought down into the village and still stands there.", "An area high in the forest is called the Hohle Gasse.
