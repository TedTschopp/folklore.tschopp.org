He measured the grain in with the large Viertel and then issued it with the small Viertel. That is why he can still be seen fetching the large Metzenkübel from the mill across the road; and when, with groaning and crashing, he has dragged it into the granary, he knocks the Viertelsstreicher rattling back and forth. Then the people know in advance that bad weather will soon set in.", "While the old granary was being converted into an inn several years ago, scarcely anything more was heard of Niggel. But when the new innkeeper moved in and had the first day of dancing held, the kobold raged and stormed about so horribly that it sounded as though someone were tearing a great bundle of chains through every floor. Then he rudely knocked over a pair of dancers, or a table when it held full dishes or poured glasses. In short, he drove the guests into such terror that some developed swollen legs and others became seriously ill from it. So they had to begin once more to rebuild the rooms and passageways differently, and since then he has not caused much fear. Some think this is because he can no longer find his old path; others, because he truly reformed after bread was distributed for a long time to the poor of the village for the repose of his soul. Older people, however, maintain that Niggel was the former Junker of Rued himself and was transformed into a horse as punishment for his greed.
