They too were people, only very small, and had one human foot and one goat’s foot. They helped people drive the plow in the fields. In return, before going home from the field, the people laid cakes upon a plow for them, and when they came back, there was gold upon it. In the Ifang (the name of meadowland), there were seven houses, and the Härdlüt always came there to visit by lamplight. Then a Härdwîbli bound a rîstä (a bundle of dressed flax) onto a woman and said she must tell no one. From that same rîstä she was able to spin for three years. Her godmother often asked how she managed it: she spun continually and always had the very same rîstä? At last she told the godmother that a Härdwîbli had bound it onto her. Afterwards she could spin only two more little heaps from it, and then it was exhausted. Surely she would have done better to remain silent. People say the Härdwîbli are inside the Strihä (Strîchen, a Jura mountain) and wash there, which is why the Äschäbrünneli runs cloudy every Wednesday and Friday.\nOberhof dialect.
