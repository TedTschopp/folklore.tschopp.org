They fall away vertically on the southern side, and their notches and holes offer the birds nesting there a secure refuge. The larger rock is called the Lampohrenfluh and contains a deep cave that is still visited from time to time. It was once the home of peaceful dwarfs. They carried their flour up here from the valley mill a little sack at a time, and in return brought luck and blessing down upon the mill. From the flour they baked cakes for poor people, and from all kinds of herbs they prepared medicines for the sick. They were held in high esteem throughout the district, and nothing at all could be said against them except that they had unusually large ears, which even flopped—lampten—out from beneath their caps. The master miller gradually grew rich, then miserly, and finally tired of his benefactors’ visits. He therefore mixed plaster into their flour, thinking it would kill them. But they merely laughed at his foolish greed and threw all the poisoned flour into the millstream, from which he had to water his livestock. His horses and cattle then perished. One misfortune followed another, and the miller lost everything he owned. Completely impoverished, he took his last sack of flour and climbed to the cave to bring it to the dwarfs.
