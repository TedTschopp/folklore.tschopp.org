After the work was happily completed, the pleased husband filled her apron with coals and instructed her to carry them carefully home. The ‘weise Frau’ had probably expected a better reward for her important aid than mere coals, of which she produced enough herself every day. On the way home, she contemptuously threw them away handful by handful, without heeding the fine warning voice that called after each handful: ‘Je mehr ass de zattest (zerstreust) je weniger de hattest!’—the more you scatter, the less you have. When she arrived home displeased and wished to remove her apron, a bright-ringing object fell to the ground. Astonished, she picked it up, and behold, a gleaming gold coin smiled at her. Now, too late, she understood the Erdmännchen's warning. She had thoughtlessly forgotten the well-known rule that even the least gift of the Erdmännchen must not be despised. In culpable displeasure, she had thrown away the good little folk's rich gift and cruelly punished herself. Naturally, she quickly followed the same road back to search for the once-despised, now-valuable gift. But her efforts were in vain; neither coals nor gold coins could be found.
