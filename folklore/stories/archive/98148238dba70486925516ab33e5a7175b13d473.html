One day its lord rode past a farmhouse and saw a magnificent tree trunk lying there. He brusquely ordered the farmer to have the fine timber brought up before the castle by noon the following day at the latest. With that, he galloped away. The man was not only sorry to lose the fine trunk; he was also anxious about how he could bring the heavy piece up the steep castle road with his weak team. So he told his neighbor of his distress. “Never mind, and do not grieve; I shall help you. It will be done.” Sure enough, at the appointed hour, just as our farmer was preparing to begin the work, the neighbor was already on the spot. He had harnessed three horses and was ready to set off. But what proud, spirited horses they were, all gleaming black! In no time they were up before the castle. The Zwingherr rejoiced and marveled at the lively and incomparable team. “They are mine!” he barked. “Indeed they are yours. Look there! That is your father, that one your grandfather, and that one your great-grandfather—and if you continue in your sinful and unjust conduct, you will become what they are; then there will be two pairs!” And the animals attested to it: when each was questioned, it nodded its head. The castle lord turned pale.
