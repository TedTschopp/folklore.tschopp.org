Because they often quarreled over their shared Abätzung, they agreed to divide the high pasture. They arranged that both brothers would leave Naters at the same time, one climbing on the right and the other on the left, and meet again above. The place where the brothers encountered one another would become the central boundary between the pastures to be divided.", "Both set out as agreed. The brother who climbed on the left by way of Birgisch acted honestly and kept to the prescribed route. Not so the other brother. Instead of climbing toward Aletsch, he went straight up into Belalp and thus cheated his brother badly, meeting him in the ‘deep gorge,’ roughly halfway between Belalp and Nessel. That is how this pasture boundary is said to have been set. But such a clever division did not satisfy the cheated brother. The brothers fell into a violent quarrel. When they reached the great stone at ‘Kapbag’—at the bottom of the Belalp meadows—they beat each other so fiercely that both brothers died. The year 121 was carved on the stone, where it can still be read, and is intended to recall this fratricide.", "The Arabic numerals alone show that 121 cannot be the complete and correct year. As for the division of the Alpine pasture itself, it must indeed be called very unbrotherly.
