A rich, grasping farmer envied his poorer neighbor, who owned a beautiful but completely debt-burdened farm. It would have rounded out his own property so nicely. But the poor little farmer could not be persuaded to sell his holding, so the other man allied himself with a cunning lawyer. Through litigation, stratagems, and tricks, the lawyer helped him strip the indebted farmer—who had also borrowed money from him—of everything and reduce him to beggary. Later, however, the unjust farmer’s conscience stirred, and he grew afraid because of his crime. He went back to the Fürsprech and complained of his pangs of conscience. The Fürsprech reassured him and said that he would take everything upon himself. They agreed that whichever of them died first should appear to the other and reveal what had happened before the divine judgment. The Fürsprech died first, but he did not appear, and the farmer already thought that the man must have died so wickedly that he had not been granted the power to show himself. One day, however, a little black dog met him in his meadow, followed him home, and behaved so strangely that at last he asked whether it was a dog or what it might be. Then it began to speak and confessed that it was the lawyer who had helped him rob the poor neighbor. He was lost forever and ever.
