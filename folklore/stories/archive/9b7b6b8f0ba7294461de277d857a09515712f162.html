They gave him a pewter jug, which he was to bury in the earth at a certain place in such a way that it would fill drop by drop with spring water, and a wooden mallet with which to tamp the earth over it so that no one could see the vessel. Once this was done, he was to hang the mallet on the nearest fir tree and never let it out of his sight; the jug would then fill with gold dust. As soon as it was full, he was to empty it, and the jug would go on filling itself again and again. He was to send the dust to them in Venice, and they would reward him richly for it. — Flury did all this; even from the church at Serneus he watched the mallet hanging on the fir tree, and meanwhile the jug filled by itself with gold.
