He crossed the mountain at night and noticed a bright little light in the darkness that remained the same size and in the same place. He went toward this light, but the brightness came only from a stone. It was summertime and the weather was good, so he lay down beside the shining object to examine the remarkable thing more closely in the morning.—In the morning he could no longer find the stone; it must have rolled downhill, and he continued on his way in annoyance. Near where he had slept was a Rüfe that he had to cross. As he was about to cross it, he saw a beautiful yellow metal in the blue clay of the Rüfe, took some with him, and tried to melt it, but he did not understand the art of separating metals.—On his return journey, he loaded his packhorse at this spot with a load of the metal and brought his find to a refiner in Feldkirch, who gave him 16 Philippstaler for it.
