The footpath there passes through the Galgenhölzli. In this little wood, the boy once gathered several twigs on his way home and carried them back together with his bread. But there in the brush, he saw a snake with a golden crown upon its head and stood still in fright. The snake, however, approached him and addressed him kindly: ‘Where do you come from, my boy?’ When he had told her that they had little to eat at home and that he had fetched cheaper bread there, she replied: ‘You certainly are very poor people, but such need can soon be remedied if you will follow my word. Just come back here next Saturday. Then I will show you the treasure I have had to guard for a hundred years. If you do not flee and do not scream, it will be yours, I will be released, and you will be finished with fetching bread forever.’ The boy was soon willing and asked only whether he might at least bring his mother. But the snake said: ‘Certainly not! No person may accompany you; you must be entirely alone, or you cannot encounter me.’ He promised this too, and the snake vanished. The boy went home as well and had no peace there until he had told his mother of his adventure. She was foolish enough to tell it further, and within a short time, all the neighbors knew of it. When the boy set out for the Galgenhölzli the next Saturday night, a crowd of curious people had already run ahead of him and another followed behind him. In short, the matter had been betrayed, and the snake did not appear.", "The community became very angry over this, believed itself deceived, and called the boy and his mother liars.
