A little bell, bright and gentle, rings in an unknown place—yet certainly somewhere here on this Alp. Its sweet tone entices the Senn. The Senn listens, staggers toward it, and searches; but suddenly the little bell has fled far away and makes itself known to the seeker from a great distance. He hurries after the fugitive, pursues it without stopping, and wants to see and know it. Herzog, Swiss Legends", "***", "Long ago, a man lived beside the Steinerbach who, because of his godless way of life, could find no rest after death. When dark night had fallen and the ghostly hour struck, he was seen climbing about on the roof of his house without rest or respite. This so frightened the survivors that they left no means untried to rid themselves of the uncanny guest. At last, a pious man of God succeeded in binding him inside a basket. Carrying this basket, he climbed to Petruns Alp to assign the poor soul a resting place there. He buried the basket beside a little stream among rocks and brush. Yet even here, the unhappy spirit has not found perfect rest. When the sun has set in the evening and dark shadows settle over this lonely little valley of Petruns Alp, his sighing and calling can plainly be heard from deep beneath the ground, sometimes fainter and sometimes stronger, audible throughout the Alp. Curious people have often tried to dig for him and investigate the nature of the strange call. A futile undertaking! They have scarcely begun when the call sounds from an entirely different place, as if mocking the curious seeker, and so no one has yet succeeded in discovering the mysterious Dätsch.\nB.
