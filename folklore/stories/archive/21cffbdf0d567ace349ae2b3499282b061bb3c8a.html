But the determined young man secretly fled with his beloved. In Sargans, he hoped to attain the object of his wishes. The enraged lord of the castle, however, sent his other servants after them. They overtook the fugitives at the Schollberg, and when they met, in a moment of the greatest agitation, Friedrich killed one of the servants—his friend Hans. As soon as he realized this, terror paralyzed his limbs, and he allowed himself to be led without resistance before the stern lord of the castle. The lord pronounced a sentence of death upon him. Friedrich received the judgment with composure and did not beg for his life. He asked only that one request be granted to him in his misfortune: with the golden ball and a single throw, he wished to knock down the king in the golden game of ninepins. His request was granted without hesitation. Once the pins were set, Friedrich took aim and knocked down the king—and the king alone. At the grim lord's command, he then explained the meaning of what had happened. The fallen king represented the lord of the castle, who would soon fall before the people's awakened desire for freedom, while the eight pins left standing represented his victorious subjects.", "Meanwhile, the outraged people had gathered before the gate, and as Friedrich's last word died away, they broke into the castle courtyard.
