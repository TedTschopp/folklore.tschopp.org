But he accepted the wager; a handsome sum of money was at stake. They had to give him a bottle of schnapps and two candles, one for the dead person and one for himself. Thus equipped, he set out, taking his shoemaking tools and shoes with him, settled himself comfortably in the little house, and began cobbling away. But in the deathbed they had secretly placed a living person instead of a corpse. Suddenly the man stirred beneath the sheet covering him and raised his head a little—‘hed üffg'g upfet.’ The shoemaker looked on and said nothing. A second time the man raised his head. The shoemaker saw it and said aloud: ‘Tot isch tot; wenn d'tot bisch, sä gupfisch nimmä!’—‘Dead is dead; if you are dead, you do not raise your head again!’ After a while something moved beneath the sheet again.
