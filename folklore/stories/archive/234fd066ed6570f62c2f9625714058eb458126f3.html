Half an hour from the village, he owned a smallholding called “Sereins,” from which he could support two cows. One winter, while feeding the cattle on the property, he went there on a fine morning and found a strange cow in the stable, chained together with one of his own two cows. He tried for a long time to unfasten it, but in vain. In anger he took a Bundhaken and struck the strange cow so hard that it fell down. Now the poor unlucky fellow became frightened and ran out of the stable, though he soon returned. But behold, the strange cow was no longer there. It had vanished and remained gone. — No more than half an hour had passed when his boy came to him and reported the news that old “Billa,” who lived in a wretched little house at the end of the village, had somehow been struck upon the head by no one knew whom and had just died from it.
