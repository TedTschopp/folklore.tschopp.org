Schwirren are wooden stakes used as boundary markers or to protect stream banks. Nocturnal Schwirrenschlagen is the punishment of Markenrücker and, along streams, of Leidwerker who destroyed bank protection to divert damage away from themselves and toward others. The inhabitants of the Bunte on the lower Schilz cannot be persuaded that they do not often hear Schwirren being driven at night in the Schälli. There, the culprits are probably timber thieves. Old Peter Walser in the Wiesli often heard Schwirren being driven at night and complained of it to Pastor Aschwanden. The priest assembled all the neighbors, and everyone had solemnly to promise forgiveness if anyone, knowingly or unknowingly, had harmed another's possessions. From that time onward, the Schwirrenschlagen was never heard again.\nJ. B. After his death, he had to wander in punishment and set the markers. Several times during the year, returning on the same nights every year, people heard him knocking as though a wooden stake were being driven into the earth with a wooden mallet. Thus he had to March rucken through the Hösle to the dye works and along the Schilzbach almost as far as the Walensee. At Dyer Rupf's house, he even had to pass through the kitchen, and at a certain Gantner's, through the stable. People then whispered mysteriously to one another, “Der Schwirrama ist wieder umganga—the stake man is wandering again.” He is said still to have been heard 10–12 years ago; now people believe that he has been redeemed.\nRob.
