Colshorn’s book of fairy tales.", "God knows how many years ago, around midnight, a Sigrist at a large church in Upper Valais was crossing the churchyard when he heard a banging in the church as though skittles were being played and saw a faint light. He reported it to the priest, who laughed at him. The following night the Sigrist heard the same thing and again told the priest. This time the priest became thoughtful, went with him to the church door, and looked through the keyhole. He saw several men dressed in black throwing two balls at a set of skittles arranged in the crossing. Then one o’clock struck—and in a flash everything vanished. The following night they stood watch before twelve. At the stroke of twelve they saw the choir door open, just as on the day before, and the black men carried in a coffin. They took from it two skulls and dead men’s bones, which they set up where the skittles had stood the previous night. All these bones and skulls began to glow, and by their light the skittle game continued until one o’clock, when everything again vanished. The priest now resolved to put an end to this mischief in the church with exorcisms. Courageously, equipped with every holy thing and three consecrated candles, he went early into the church with the Sigrist the next night, drew a circle where the skittles were set, placed the burning candles upon the circle, and stood with the Sigrist inside it. When twelve struck and the spirits appeared as before, he began his adjuration: “I adjure you in the name of God: who are you, and what are you doing in this house of God?” The black men answered: “We are judges and must play here because we made an unjust game of people’s lives. We must bring this coffin here from the place of judgment on the Tempertage. It contains the bones of two persons innocently condemned to death whom we had burned as witches and buried by the executioner’s hand in unconsecrated ground. That is why they glow so terribly, and we must suffer so much, so much from them when we are forced to roll them as skittles.” “But can you be helped?” asked the exorcist. “Yes,” they answered, “if you are willing to fulfill the conditions without which we can hope for no release.” “And what are they?” “First,” replied the spirits, “the innocence of these persons must be publicly proclaimed in the church. Then the bones must be dug up from the place of judgment and buried on consecrated ground according to Christian custom. Finally, the property unjustly taken from their kin must be restored.” The judges all raised their pleading hands toward him and asked in tearful voices, “Will you do that?” “Yes! God be my witness, I will do it,” replied the priest. Then one o’clock struck, and judges, skittle game, and coffin—all had vanished and did not return, for the priest sought to fulfill the promised conditions as swiftly and conscientiously as possible." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Sigrist”: the Swiss German term for a church sexton or sacristan; here he tends the church and first witnesses the nocturnal game.", "Swiss term “Tempertage”: a regional shortened form of Quatembertage, the Ember Days or quarterly fast days of the Christian calendar; the spirits are compelled to carry the coffin at these liminal sacred times." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
