Both regions sent him a capable Kriegsharst of valiant men, and the emperor was greatly pleased. He had them equipped with good halberds. Finally, he ordered his master of arms to give them a donkey for their baggage train. In the battle against the Turks, the men of the Oberland proved their worth. Therefore, when the campaign had ended, the emperor did not wish to let them leave without gifts. But when they were asked to state their desires, they knew of nothing to request and might, in their modesty, have had to return home without a gift had the donkey not chosen that moment to make itself heard behind their backs. ‘Exactly! There we have it,’ said the men. ‘If we may ask for something, it is the donkey.’ It was gladly given to them, and, after taking leave, they traveled happily from the Rhine toward the mountains of home. Along the way, however, a dispute arose over which of the two parties should own the donkey. The men of Hasli claimed it because they had supplied the emperor with more men. The men of Brienz believed they had earned it through greater bravery. Once home, the Hasler made short work of the matter. Being stronger, they simply took the donkey, and the Brienzer returned empty-handed across the Brünig. A great outcry followed. A messenger was sent to the imperial palace to complain about the neighbors' misconduct. The emperor, however, was a very wise man and knew what to do. He had a piece of flag cloth brought before him and a donkey painted upon it. ‘Thus, from now on, you people of Brienz shall also have a donkey,’ he said, handing the messenger the new Panner.
