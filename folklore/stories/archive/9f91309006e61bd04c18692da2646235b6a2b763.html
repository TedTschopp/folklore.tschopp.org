He was held to be one of the richest burghers. Later he entered the government and married a wealthy widow, who afterward alleged that Meyer, her husband, was a Schwarzkünstler; for that reason, she had herself divorced from him. But he would give her nothing until people began to verganten—auction off—his household goods. Then, because he was ill, he was carried in a chair to the Rheintor; thus nothing of his temporal property remained to him. Meanwhile, it was also proved by a Jew that he had a mirror in which he could see all those ill-disposed toward him. Shortly afterward, he was verbannisiert—that is, banished or confined—to a house in the Lesser City, whereupon he turned completely gray from great grief. One of his sons lost his reason entirely over his pitiable condition; the other son fell into ruin.
