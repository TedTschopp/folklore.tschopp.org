No doubt he had crossed these high and wild mountains in winter, dragged himself into the hut exhausted by cold and hardship, and fallen asleep there never to wake. Because people did not know whether he had been a Christian or a heathen, they buried him in the sand not far from the hut. People passing there not long afterward saw one hand of the dead man protruding and heard a little bird singing beautifully nearby. They bent the hand back beneath the earth, but soon afterward a foot of the dead man showed again. This too was pushed back beneath the soil. Whenever anyone passed there, now a foot and now a hand of the strange dead man protruded from the grave. In vain did people try to cover them with earth, and always a little bird could be heard singing beautifully nearby. Then they conceived the idea of digging up the dead man again and laying him on the cemetery wall of the parish church. This wall had the property of determining whether unknown dead people found in the mountains were Catholic or non-Catholic. It happened in the following way. If a corpse placed on the wall was thrown outside the Gottesacker during the night, it was regarded as non-Catholic. If it was found in the morning on consecrated ground, it was held to be Catholic. The next day, to everyone’s joy, this corpse was found lying almost in the middle of the cemetery.
