For this, immediately after her death, she was seen sitting about on the roof of the little wooden raised storehouse whose lower portion housed the pigsty. Her relatives consulted the priest. He advised that they address the miller's wife and ask what must be done for her rest, but only on the condition that they retain control of the first and last words of the conversation, for otherwise they risked being talked to death by the spirit. This was done; the miller's wife related her misdeed and named the means by which she could be freed from wandering. But for this purpose, she demanded such a vast number of Masses for souls and such a load of wheat, to be distributed to the poor by the Malter, that the heirs grew anxious for their own fortune. They therefore gave the poor nothing and, until further notice, left the mother sitting outside on the pigsty. She did this for thirty nights, for that is the duration of the period called the \"Folge,\" during which church prayers are said for the departed.
