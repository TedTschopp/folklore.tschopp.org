The first time, it raged so severely that one morning, nine cows were found dead together. A Capuchin friar was summoned from the Lucerne region to banish the evil. He came, bound it beneath the ground in a pit, and closed the opening with a piece of wood. He also commanded that henceforth, every year on Sankt Johannisnacht, the yield of half a day from the cows then summering on the Bohl and Ällgäu Alps be distributed in cheese and Zieger among the region's poor. He promised these Alps protection from livestock disease for as long as the command was faithfully obeyed and that hole remained closed. This is the origin of the custom, still observed, that on these Alps, everything milked on the morning of 4 July is processed into cheese and Zieger for the valley's poor. On 5 July, the unsalted cheese and Zieger are carried out to the Twirrispeicher and distributed there among the poor. This is called St. Johannsen because it occurs on St. John's Day according to the old calendar.", "According to others, the Oberland miracle doctor Brühlfritzli placed the plague inside a growing tree.
