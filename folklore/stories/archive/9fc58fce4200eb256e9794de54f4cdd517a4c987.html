The miller of Wölfliswil had to take it after selling an ox at the Frick market far better than expected, then drinking beyond his thirst in several inns, and now, rather unsteady on his legs, wanting to go home quite late at night. Surprised by this unexpected apparition, the miller took to his heels. Breathless, he entered his house; his wife wanted to know what had happened to him, but he concealed it from her and sought his bedchamber. He had hardly lain down in bed when there was already a knock at his window, and a voice outside said: ‘Come, redeem me!’ He sat up and recognized the same green-clad figure outside. It was the same the following night; the knocker at the window would not let him sleep.", "At last he turned to his parish priest and received the advice to follow the apparition the next time. He did so, trusting in the church's protective means that had been handed to him, and the next night followed the specter to that place on the Geistwegli by the Thierstein forest brook. Here the figure stopped and related: ‘I was keeper of the hounds for the Counts of Thierstein when their castle still stood up there on the mountain. My lord had announced a great hunt in this region, and numerous guests arrived at the castle for it. But on that very day, I had accidentally oversalted the dogs' morning soup; as soon as they were released, they all rushed together to this brook. Instead of tracking the game, they remained lying here, lapping water. I tried everything to put them on the scent; in my distress, I even vowed to make a pilgrimage to Maria Einsiedeln. Nothing helped. Then the count came along, saw who had ruined his pleasure that day, and, no longer master of his anger, shot me down with an arrow. Since then, I must wander here without rest until someone redeems me by undertaking the vowed pilgrimage for me.’", "The miller did indeed make the pilgrimage to Einsiedeln the following spring. The path once more led toward that brook in the Sulzmatten. At the familiar spot, the hunter stopped and said: ‘Here rest my bones; this is where the count had me buried. Mark this place so that you can give my remains an honorable burial.’ When the miller had promised this, he was also granted three wishes, among them the choice of whether he wished to be in heaven immediately with the man he had redeemed.
