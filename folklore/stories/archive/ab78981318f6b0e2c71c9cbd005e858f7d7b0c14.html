The crest of the slope just mentioned forms a plateau upon which, until a few years ago, stood a black sandstone slab covering an area of about five square meters.", "The slab, like the place itself, is called the Hexenplatte by the inhabitants of the region. A legend still living among the people gives the following account of the origin of this peculiar name.", "In ancient times, a witch is said to have caused all kinds of harm in this region. She is said also to have sworn the destruction of the little village of Ruft and its inhabitants. With the aid of the evil spirits of the underworld, she called up a terrible storm for this purpose, which moved threateningly down from the Kuhmettler toward Ruft. The witch herself is then said to have seated herself upon a stone slab between the Kuhmettler and the Federi. At her evil magic word, an enormous mass of earth and debris broke loose and moved toward Ruft, bearing the witch upon its back, in order to cover the village. Suddenly, amid the dreadful terrors of the violent storm, the little bell in Saint Leonard's Chapel began ringing by itself. Just as suddenly, the power of the hellish weather was broken. The clouds divided, and the Erdschlipf stopped. The witch herself departed with the cry of woe, ‘Das Rufner Hündli fängt an zu bellen!’—‘The little dog of Ruft is beginning to bark!’ Unfortunately, the Hexenplatte was blasted apart and removed a few years ago during land-improvement work.\nA.
