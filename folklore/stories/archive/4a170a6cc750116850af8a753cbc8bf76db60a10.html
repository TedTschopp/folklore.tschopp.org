He had much success with the cattle entrusted to him, but not with his own goats, which he was allowed to keep on the Alpine pasture. Every summer, they contracted the Gelti, a disease that deprived goats of their milk and often even killed them. They went blind and wasted away to skeletons. At night, they could often be heard crying and making such strange noises in the stable. A Capuchin father to whom the afflicted herdsman complained comforted him and thought matters would surely improve. He was mistaken: once again, one goat after another fell victim to the plague.", "But the following summer, when the first goat lost the milk in one side of its udder, the herdsman resorted to a radical remedy. He put the Gabälä carrying frame on his back, descended into the Ruosalper forest, loaded up a fine bundle of wood, and carried it to a place not far from the hut. There, to everyone’s astonishment, he silently threw it into a deep ravine without explaining his mysterious behavior to those who questioned him. Toward midnight, he rose, stole secretly out of the hut, took the sick goat, threw it onto the burning pyre in the ravine, and burned it alive. At that, a terrible uproar arose among the other goats in the stable, waking the farmhands who were sleeping in the hayloft and sending them running outside. The ridges and cliffs roared as in the worst hailstorm; black clouds raced like ghosts over the Alpine pasture. The people trembled, the cattle stormed in a dense herd toward the cliff, and the earth thundered beneath their hooves. The farmhands lamented the lost cattle. Franzsepp too became afraid, but he gave no answer to the herdsmen’s reproachful questions and looks. Instead, grave and silent, he took the Volla, stepped out onto the ridge, and proclaimed the Gospel of Saint John with all his strength toward each of the four winds. Now the ridges and rocks grew quiet, the wind subsided, and the clouds brightened. Yet the herdsmen spent an anxious night. Only then did the herdsman explain what he had done. How astonished they all were in the morning when every one of the cattle lay safe and sound in its accustomed resting place. None was missing; none was hurt! From then on, the goats were spared the Gelti.", "Previously, an old, unknown woman wearing a large Schinhuet had occasionally been seen on the Alpine pasture.
