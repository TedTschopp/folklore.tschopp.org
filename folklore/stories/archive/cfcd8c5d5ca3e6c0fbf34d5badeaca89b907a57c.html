This was a riddle, and they had to solve it; there was no other choice. A ladder was soon fetched, set against the building as quietly as possible, and they climbed up and peered into the room. What they saw there was truly interesting and richly rewarded their curiosity. A host of cats filled the room and were having a tremendously merry time. Some made music from written notes, while the others danced as if possessed. Dust and smoke flew everywhere. The young men could not stop watching. At last, however, the company grew tired of dancing, went out into the kitchen, prepared a Znyni, and ate and drank. One of the cats sprang onto the hearth wall and crouched there. ‘Babi,’ the cook said to her, ‘you must not fall asleep, or you might tumble down into the fire.’ ‘No, no,’ the other reassured her, ‘I will not fall asleep.’ I do not know how the nocturnal revelry ended. When one of the young men sat at his Kalazzen the next morning, his own cat came over and jumped onto the bench beside him. A mutilated tail was its distinguishing mark. The young man regarded it kindly. Suddenly he remembered having seen just such a stumpy tail among the nocturnal musicians.
