Late one evening he was walking through the Wassnerwald with a companion when robbers appeared and demanded ‘Money or blood!’ The Neapolitäner removed his money belt, threw it at the robbers’ feet, and said, ‘There, take it!’ But not one of them bent down to seize it; they stood rigid. At last the Neapolitäner himself picked up the belt again, remarking, ‘Very well, if you do not want it, I shall take it back into my keeping.’ Then the two men marched on. After a while the companion asked, ‘It seems you know all kinds of things. Would you not teach me too?’ ‘No,’ replied the former soldier, ‘no, one does such a thing only in the direst need.’ Only when he had reached a safe distance did he allow the spellbound robbers to move again. Once his little house on the Bristenberg caught fire. His brother in Ried noticed it and hurried up the mountain to help. When he reached the place called Donnderchlapf beneath the Hirrenbiechli, the brother on the Bristenberg thought, ‘Ah, my brother is running himself to death,’ and stopped him immediately.
