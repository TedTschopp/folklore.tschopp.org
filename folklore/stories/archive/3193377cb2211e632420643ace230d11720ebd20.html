The legend of how this Alp came into the latter’s possession is as follows:", "In the middle of the seventeenth century, the municipality of Vals had built the present large church at the “Platz,” a construction that plunged the inhabitants deeply into debt, for which reason they found themselves compelled to sell two Alps, namely Tomül and Lampertsch. The latter, the best Alp in the valley, was allegedly sold to Blegno for the small sum of one thousand guilders. An advocate from Bellenz is said to have drawn up the deed of sale with a precise statement of the conditions and boundaries. It is said to have expressly noted that on the east side the Alp extended as far as a certain seven-sided stone, where a stone cross stood as the Marche, while on the west side it went no farther than the “Hornbach.” — Two identical copies of this deed were made, and each party received one. Through carelessness or deceit the people of Vals lost theirs. When the people of Blegno heard of this, they promptly falsified their document by inserting into it: “On the west side it extends just as far as on the east side.” — When the people of Blegno then drove their livestock across the “Hornbach,” the people of Vals retaliated in kind, whereupon the former maintained that the purchased Alp extended equally far eastward and westward, that this stood in their deed of sale, and that no Marche stood at the Hornbach. The leaders of Vals investigated the matter and found no Marche either; a man of Blegno—or, according to others, a man of Misox who served one of the people of Blegno—had thrown it down into the stream. — Now, of course, the matter was soon decided: there was no Marche, and the people of Vals no longer had any documents; the lawsuit was decided in favor of the people of Blegno. — The villain who had removed the Marche soon afterward fell into a glacier crevasse and so ended his life miserably. — For a long time he had to ride a fiery white horse, up and down the valley in every storm, terrifying the shepherds and herds by night, until he was banished up onto the Lenta Glacier, where he is said to carry on his mischief for all eternity." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Marchstein”: literally a boundary stone, a marked stone defining a territorial or property border; moving the marker enables the territorial fraud and brings the culprit a posthumous punishment that this tale never says he escapes.", "Swiss term “Alp”: a high mountain pasture and its seasonal livestock operation, not simply a mountain peak; Lampertsch and Tomül are valuable grazing properties whose ownership is disputed.", "Swiss term “Marche”: a Swiss regional word for a boundary, boundary line, or boundary marker; here it denotes the stone cross whose removal undermines Vals’s claim.", "Swiss term “Bellenz”: the historical German name for Bellinzona in Ticino; the source’s period form is retained." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
