A chambermaid had to guard it, and her redemption was bound to the raising of the treasure. They said they would not run away in fear if the young lady appeared to them, as so many had already done. While they talked, sand and small bits of masonry were thrown at them. At first they thought the wind had blown them down. But as the rain of sand continued and larger and larger stones hailed upon them, they supposed that some mischievous boy might be hiding above and wished to tease them. “We will see who is up there and cannot leave us in peace,” they said to each other, and ran up the long stairs and dark passages. When they reached the top, all was still and no one was to be found; meanwhile it became so dark that they had to feel along the walls to find the way back. Suddenly they heard a small gate unlock; quick footsteps and a clatter like a violently shaken ring of keys rushed past them. They pressed closer together and softly told each other, “That is the chambermaid; she wants to give us the keys to the treasure.” At that same instant a great key fell as though thrown at their feet and rang brightly in the dark passages. Both were so terrified that they did not dare speak a word. As they cautiously advanced, both of them—first one, then the other—stepped on the key lying on the floor, but neither dared pick it up. One pulled the other down through the dark passages and stairs as well and as quickly as possible, and they let the key remain a key. When at last they approached the main gate, there—to their renewed horror—in the middle of the gate and filling its breadth stood a woman dressed in a wholly old-fashioned manner with a ring of keys; her back was turned to them. With soft, scarcely audible steps they crept along the wall past the ghostly gatekeeper and pressed themselves against the walls as firmly as they could so as not to touch her. They did not look back. Only when they were already far from the castle did they venture to say half aloud to one another, “That was the lady of the castle! Ah, she wanted to make us happy, and we were meant to redeem her!
