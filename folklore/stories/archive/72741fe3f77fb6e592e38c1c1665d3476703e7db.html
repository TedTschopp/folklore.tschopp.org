He was a great benefactor of the poor, a devout and upright man. He had brought his young, beautiful wife from Austria to his country. But vanity dwelt in her heart. Therefore she was not happy in the lonely castle and longed to return to her homeland. To comfort her, the baron invited a noble companion of his wife, named Friedrich, to his castle. The knight showed the young nobleman every duty of hospitality in generous measure. In return, the latter betrayed him by allowing himself to be caught in the treacherous young lady's nets. Indeed, she even managed to beguile her lover into killing her husband so that they could then pursue their sinful love with impunity. One day, when the two nobles, host and guest, rode out to hunt together, the young lord treacherously stabbed the knight to death. Scarcely had this bloody deed been done when the murderer was seized by the most terrible pangs of conscience; and when the baroness then tried to place the golden ring on his finger, he flung it far away into the castle moat, swung himself onto his steed, and, as he fled, cursed the faithless instigator of the shameful crime.", "Before his death the baron had lived in the best accord with the Talleuten. When his testament was opened, it was found that he, having long since seen through his wife's treachery, had entrusted his estate to the poor. The needy were to receive one hundred white cows and an Allmend for 1400 cows. But the rich now likewise asserted a claim to the gift. Thus the poor received only a little. Since that time, a spirit has been seen walking the pastures at night, giving the cattle something to lick from a Lecktasche. If the cattle of the rich lick salt from his hand, they grow lean and perish; but if the cattle of the poor lick it, they grow fat, thrive, and remain free of every disease.
