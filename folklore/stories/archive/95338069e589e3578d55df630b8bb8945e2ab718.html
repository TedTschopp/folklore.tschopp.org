Their ridges then extend into an arc whose narrow valley floor contains the Sesseln, farmland whose owners still enjoy special privileges today.", "The origin of these special rights is told as follows. Each of the two mountains, now covered with mature forest, formerly bore a mighty castle. Of the one on the Homberg, the broad moat remains, together with many enormous blocks of stone set atop one another. A leather bridge connected the two castles. The Count of Thierstein lived here. His equally powerful neighbors were Hans von Rechberg, who occupied the Reichstem near the village of Wittnau, and Thomas von Falkenstein with his several castles in the Jura passes. The power of these lords was feared far and wide; their numerous vassals seemed invincible and the walls of their fortresses unshakable. Moreover, fortune smiled upon the Count of Thierstein in his own household. His wife was pious and gentle and had just borne him a little daughter. Then the notorious earthquake broke out that in the year 1356 buried the city of Basel along with many Jura castles. When the Homburg began to shake, the part of the castle in which the countess lay in childbed with her newborn baby was the first to tear loose. By chance, however, the castle tower shot down the rocks on its own while mother and child, together and unharmed in their Rollbette, slid down the mountainside and were pushed a considerable distance into the grain fields of Oberfrick. In gratitude for this miraculous preservation, the countess made all the land as far as the Rollbette had traveled forever free of tithes and ground rent. This tract from the mountain's summit down into the valley, which received that right, is the Sesseln.", "It is also told that at the time when the Basel earthquake of 1356 destroyed that city and most castles in the surrounding region, the Countess of Thierstein was staying at her castle in Pfeffingen. The chronicle says: The child's Götti was the Bishop of Basel. The next morning, he came riding and wished to go to Basel. Then he asked whether his godchild had perished. They said no. He ordered them to search for the child on the slope. It was found between two great stones, weeping in the wagon. It became a woman and bore many children.", "Later, the knights of Aristorf and Bärenfels entered into an alliance with the Landgrave of Thierstein. Together with all their noble retainers and mounted men, they assembled solemnly on a mountain meadow in that Frickthal forest which has since been called the Junkernholz. When they offered one another the hand of reconciliation, the country people watching in great numbers shouted with joy. The three knights then set a stone at that place on the Erfenmatte, and it is still known today. At the mountain's summit, it marks the place that is land without a lord because the three borders of the cantons of Basel-Landschaft, Solothurn, and Aargau meet there in a triangle.
