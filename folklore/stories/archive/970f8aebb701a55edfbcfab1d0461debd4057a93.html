Then I shall boil them, skim off the golden foam with a ladle, and we shall be able to make money from it.’ So far, so good: he put the newts in a kettle over the fire and stoked it like Satan. When the heat came, the newts put out their little horns. But he thought they were all little black devils that meant to seize him, and he ran away. From then on people called him the Tyfelisieder, and even today his descendants in the upper Reuss Valley are called ds Tyfälisyders.", "b) Once some people fattened newts with blood and then meant to roast them in order to obtain gold. The stranger comforted him and said that he need only go home again, look for red-bellied newts, and boil them in his own blood; then they would provide him with money enough. The little farmer turned back, searched, and found a number of such newts. He boiled them in blood drawn from his wife and his own children until they had grown so weak that they could no longer walk. But he could boil as long as he wished: no gold appeared. Then he grew angry and poured the newts down into the Standtal. Yet he did not get rid of them. They gathered and crawled toward him. He fled and fetched the local priest, but the priest could not master them. A Capuchin friar had to come; only he was able to banish them.
