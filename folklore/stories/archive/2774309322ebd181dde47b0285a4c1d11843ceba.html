One day, when he once again went hunting with his companions, in the zeal of the chase he inadvertently became separated from them and lost his way so completely that he could no longer orient himself at all in the dark forest. He ran and ran, but he did not emerge from the woods. This astonished him greatly, for it constantly seemed that he heard the voices of his hunting companions not far away. Yet however he called to them, only his own voice returned from the rock walls. He felt uneasy. He thought he must surely have stepped upon an Irrwurzel and must now keep running in the same circle. But as he rushed forward through the thicket in agitation, he suddenly stopped in astonishment, for before him stood a cave that he had never noticed on his many excursions through the forest. He lowered his hunting spear and entered the cave, for perhaps a bear slept inside that he could kill. But the cave grew ever longer, and after he had walked several hundred paces, he suddenly stood before an iron door. For a while, he regarded it suspiciously. What might be hidden behind it? Although he felt somewhat uneasy, curiosity prevailed. He pushed against the door, and it sprang open with a creak. Startled, he stood for a moment, dropped his spear, and covered his eyes with both hands, so dazzling was a marvelous radiance. When he looked again, he saw before him an infinitely broad hall whose walls were made of the purest gold. In the hall, many hundreds of little dwarfs with long beards and little blue coats hurried about as busily as ants. In baskets, they carried loads of gold that lay around the walls in crumbly pieces to the middle of the hall, where stood an enormous smelting furnace from which the molten gold flowed away in shining, narrow channels. He kept himself afloat until, in a faint gleam penetrating the dreadful depths from an infinite distance, he saw a water bucket descending toward him. When the bucket plunged into the water beside him, he quickly sat upon it and clung in desperate fear to the thick rope from which it hung.
