This Schenni was a very rough and wild fellow, but at the same time he was the guardian spirit and friend in need of the valley people, who therefore held him in high esteem. The power and standing that Schenni had won among the Valser now made the Prince of Hell envious; but the good-natured giant paid him no heed and carefully avoided all the Evil One’s snares. The latter intended nevertheless to “überhöhen” Schenni, surpass him in rank, and appropriate for himself the giant’s dominion over the Valser.", "Once Schenni was returning home from work—he had laid the beams for the Valser in the construction of a bridge, something no one else had dared to do—and beneath his arm he carried a raven that he had raised from a young bird and took everywhere with him; on his shoulder he bore a mighty spruce trunk that he intended to take home. He went innocently on his way when suddenly the raven escaped his keeping and flew toward the sun, croaking terribly. The giant, unaccustomed to his favorite fleeing, looked doubtfully after it and soon recognized his guardian spirit in the feathered creature, for high above him the Spirit of Darkness hovered in the air, carrying a great block of rock in his gigantic claws. — Schenni sensed his adversary’s treachery and played the innocent in order to deceive him. He calmly continued on his way, though looking attentively upward. The Prince of Darkness let the block fall, intending to rid himself of his gigantic rival by smashing him with the stone. But Schenni, who had foreseen the Evil One’s plan, sprang swiftly aside, and the rock immediately crashed down near him. From the force of its fall it split into two pieces, one of which remained embedded upright in the ground, while the other lies at its side. — After this grand trial of strength, the Evil One considered further efforts to destroy the giant useless and from then on avoided the valley. The Valser, glad not to have received the wicked guest as their ruler, preserved their grateful memory of Schenni as before. The Devil heard of it and soon devised a means of blessing the building in his own fashion. First he let its construction proceed at leisure, intending on a fine night to turn the little church, built at great expense, into a heap of rubble. For this purpose he selected large blocks of stone half an hour farther down the valley with which to smash the chapel. He soon found a truly large and heavy one that appeared to stand there for precisely this purpose, and with genuinely devilish delight he prepared to take it upon his back. He drove his horns deep into the rock, did the same with his long, sharply clawed fingers, raised it, and flew with it toward the chapel. He had already reached the meadow—where the stone still stands today—when he heard a mighty roaring; lightning flashed, thunder crashed, and winds howled through the treetops. The stone grew heavier and heavier for the fiend, for the Holy Virgin pressed upon it with her all-powerful hand and formed impressions in the shape of a cross. Then suddenly came a dreadful crack, and the stone split into two pieces that crashed to the earth. When the Devil saw the shattered blocks fall to the ground and thus saw his first attempt fail, and moreover felt utterly exhausted by the terrible exertion, he abandoned the thought of destroying the chapel and returned to his shadowy realm filled with fury and anger.
