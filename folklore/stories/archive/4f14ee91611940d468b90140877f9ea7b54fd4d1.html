If it is not precisely “Fluonazen's Brünnlein,” which offers fresh water to the thirsty in upper Kipfen, then it may be the desolate, densely wooded, rutted, unstable, and down-valley-sliding nature of this region that must claim the traveler's attention. This wildly beautiful, uncanny district is said once to have attained the rank of a free Meiertum. The occasion was as follows:", "Once in Kipfen, a man fell into the Vispe and was carried away by the foaming waves. A woodcutter working on the bank saw it, leaped after him, caught him, and pulled him back onto land with his iron hook—admittedly a little carelessly, for the hook he applied tore out one of the rescued man's eyes. The latter complained to the authorities and sued his rescuer for compensation for the lost eye. This was quite a thorny legal question, in which the law on the one hand and fairness on the other could not readily be reconciled. With pinched expressions and deeply troubled faces, the learned jurists inspected the site. A goatherd who happened to be present noticed the wise gentlemen's perplexity and, after asking about the dispute, said with a smile that he knew what to do. The plaintiff should throw himself back into the water at the same place and let it carry him onward; if he saved himself without the woodcutter's help, the woodcutter must pay him for the eye; if not, it would surely make no difference whether he died with one eye or two. What a happy inspiration! The judges breathed freely again.
