People imagine that this thunder or cannonade comes from the Gurnigel, by which name in this case they mean not only the well-known mountain in Bernese country but also the Schratten range. Once in autumn, as a shepherd drove his flock down from the Alp on the Schratten, he noticed that a sheep was missing, but he could no longer find it. His astonishment was considerable when, as the flock was driven up again, the lost sheep sprang toward him well nourished and happy. Yet it would not graze with the rest of the flock. One could see that this pasture was no longer good enough for it and that it knew a better one. The shepherd thought he would get to the bottom of the matter. He no longer let the sheep out of his sight but crept after it along its paths. It went toward a cave in the rock and then through a long, broad passage into an immense hall that sparkled all around with bright crystals and where the finest warhorses stood at magnificent mangers, countless in number. There the sheep had long had more than enough. As the shepherd walked about, he came to a tall, proud gate that sprang open at the lightest touch and revealed an enchanting sight to his astonished eyes. Another hall, shimmering and flashing with gold and precious stones, extended its luminous chambers deep into the distance, and at splendid tables sat and slept a great many handsome, well-equipped warriors. At the very back, opposite the gate, the commander rested at his own table. He now raised his head and gravely and solemnly asked another officer beside him: ‘What time is it?’ ‘One thousand eight hundred thirty!’ the other replied. ‘Then we must wait another 45 years,’ said the first, and went on sleeping. The shepherd now thought of withdrawing. It did occur to him that the subterranean folk could easily spare a few handfuls from their immeasurable treasures of gold and diamonds. But he resolved first to consult his pastor. He marked the entrance carefully and left the sheep behind for the time being. Later, he returned there to take as much of the splendid wealth as possible.
