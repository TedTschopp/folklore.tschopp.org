The Intschialp, on the sunny side of the Arniberg and rising between the Leutschachalp and Gornern, belongs to it. On this Alp there is an ancient lime kiln, so old that its construction is placed in the time of William Tell. When the “counts” of Silenen built their castle down near Amsteg, they had the lime burned here. Afterward the kiln stood idle for some time, and during that interval a spirit took up residence in it. At last it was to be used again. Many people felt heartfelt compassion for the soul bound fast inside, regarded the kiln’s heat as its purgatory, came there, knelt, and prayed for its redemption. Indeed, when the lime was nearly burned, the spirit, visible to those praying, came out of the fire opening entirely white, walking along a board.
