His thoughts and wishes may have been to bring down capercaillies and forest fowl. But behold, his compassionate heart was moved to preserve a life instead of bringing it death. A little dove—a sacred bird to every inhabitant of the monastery—was being pursued with furious cries and croaking by the black tribe of ravens, which is said to have hated the dove since the time of Noah's Ark. He hurried after the flock and ran into the forest and down into the depths where the little dove was to become the victim of its enemies. To rescue persecuted innocence and tear the dear little dove from death was now his only thought; he had completely forgotten every other quarry. In his zeal, he entirely overlooked that he himself was in the greatest danger of his life. He descended so far into the dizzying depths that he suddenly noticed steam rising from the gaping chasm. After bringing the little dove to safety, he had himself lowered on ropes and saw blood-warm water bubbling from fissures in the rock.", "It was the fair reward for his noble heart when, ‘through the special grace and mercy of God, for the use and benefit of humankind, he discovered the most noble, precious, and healing water,’ without yet suspecting ‘how many inexpressible effects it possesses.’\n(Euler.)*", "Some name the two monastery hunters Vils and Thuoli of Vilters as his hunting companions and attendants.
