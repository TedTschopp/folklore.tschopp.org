A robber castle once stood here, so strong that nothing could be done against it by force. The peasants therefore conceived the idea of driving a tunnel into the hill above the castle and undermining the castle's foundations from within.", "They succeeded. The proud castle collapsed, and the robbers were buried beneath the rubble, along with all the money and gold they had extorted from the people of the valley. Since on every Good Friday night all enchanted things must stir again and wish to be asked how they may be redeemed, several bold men went to the Burgstal for this purpose, put their question, and at once received the desired information.", "On the following night, Holy Saturday, they had to come there dressed in white and dig up the Marchstein. Beneath it lay a key that opened the iron door behind which the entire treasure was hidden. They had to finish digging before the bells rang in the little town of Kaiserstuhl for Christ's Resurrection, and they had to bring the treasure chests under a roof before the morning bells began. They could divide one chest among themselves; the other had to be used for good works and given for church purposes. Throughout it all, no one was permitted to speak a word. The men carried this out without mishap or error. They found the key, the iron door, and the treasure chests. Hauling the chests away was hardest of all, yet they brought them home as well. Just as the foremost bearer set his foot beneath the eaves, the bells in Kaiserstuhl began to ring Betzeit, and what the spirits had foretold now came to pass. Precisely as much of the first chest as had not yet passed under the roof disappeared. What remained to them was the smaller part, but it was large enough to be divided among them all and to give each a fine fortune.
