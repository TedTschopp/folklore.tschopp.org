The animal had now seen him too and reared up. The herdsman now knew that a mighty dragon stood before him. It was two Klafter long and had a compact head resembling a cat’s head, with garnet-sparkling eyes, a white stripe around its neck, and scales on its feet like a fish; it was also furnished with appendages. Thus the beast kept running after the herdsman around the stone in vain until at last, in its rage, it charged the stone with great force in order to topple it. As a result, the dragon injured its head so badly against the hard stone that it writhed on the ground in pain and, stunned, was for the moment no longer able to pursue the herdsman. The dragon’s helpless state gave the herdsman time to load his gun and shoot at the enemy. But the ball did not strike the dragon with enough effect to make it unable to fight. — Rather, the wound revived the stunned dragon and drove the beast into such a fury that it again shot back and forth like an arrow along the sides of the stone block in order to reach the herdsman; then it charged violently against the stone a second time and again fell to the ground. Because the herdsman had spilled his ammunition while running around the stone, the gun was of no further use to him. He therefore picked up stones from the ground and killed the dragon with them. After assuring himself that the dragon was dead, he wished to drag it down the mountain but was too weak to do so. He had to leave it lying there.
