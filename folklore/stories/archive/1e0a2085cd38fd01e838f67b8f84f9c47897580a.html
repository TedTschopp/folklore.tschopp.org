The two large bells in this tower are also said to be among the oldest in Valais. The great bell weighs fifty Zentner and at its baptism received the names Morizius and Antonia: Morizius because he is patron of the country and the church; Antonia because the bell’s Gotte, or godmother, was a Countess Blandra of Weingarten in Naters. When this countess was present at the casting of the great bell and heard the master despairing and lamenting that the cast must fail because there was too little geschworenes Metall, she hurried forward with an apronful of silver vessels and threw them into the crucible. The casting now succeeded, and because so much silver went into it, the bell also received a majestic tone such as few bells in Valais possess. As far as the sound of this bell can be heard, it is said to exercise a wholesome influence upon storms and inhibit the powers of harmful spirits. Once, during a great storm, two mountain spirits wished to push the Fuchs-Gufer above Naters down onto the village. One spirit called to the other, ‘Push, push!’ But the other replied, ‘I cannot anymore; I have no strength left, for great Antonia is ringing.’ This meant: ‘I hear the tone of the great bell Antonia and no longer have the power to do harm.’ The second large bell is said to be nearly five hundred years old and is therefore also called ‘d'Alta.’ It is said to bear Greek and Hebrew inscriptions and to weigh about twenty Zentner.", "It is also related that the church stands on alders, which likely means on pillars of alder wood, because the ground beneath it was so swampy that no proper foundation could be dug. This church, one of the oldest parish churches in Valais, suffered greatly in the second-to-last major earthquake. This earthquake occurred at half past two in the afternoon on the ninth day of Christmonat 1755. In Naters, a third of the church vault collapsed and shattered the portal and the organ together with the pews.
