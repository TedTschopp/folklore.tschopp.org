From time to time she would stop briefly and look across toward “d’Bäch.” And then—this struck us as so curious—she would always reach one hand beneath her clothes around her heart and afterward spread that hand out toward “d’Bäch,” as though she had been delousing herself or picking herself clean and were shaking the lice or fleas off that way. We watched her, and the other lad finally said, “Could that be, God preserve us from it, an old witch?” But Father did not want to say much about that; he merely said, “She will probably pass us, and then we shall see.” When she came up toward us and passed us, she did cast a frightfully ugly, hateful look at us. She looked—I can say nothing else—like a spiteful cat, so treacherous and venomous. Her face was entirely wrinkled, and on her head she had a scrap of cloth that was pitch-black but covered all over with tiny white dots. We lads nearly had to laugh, but Father said, “Your laughter may yet leave you!” Well, the woman continued on and kept looking across toward “d’Bäch.” One thought she was walking very slowly, but damn it, she covered ground; we tried to follow her but could no longer catch sight of her. Toward evening, a fine Risel came driving down between the streams; it poured rain in earnest.
