One day, it is told, a father and son rode down the road to look for enemies. At some distance they encountered a French cavalryman. Neither side wished to turn back, because that would have betrayed cowardice, and for two men to fight only one would have been equally dishonorable. The son therefore wanted to go first. But the father, who had carefully measured the Frenchman with his sharp gaze, said, ‘Wart Bub! dem bist du's nit’—wait, lad, you are no match for him. The son immediately halted and let his father go ahead. The father drew out his tobacco pipe and calmly began to fill it. Thus he approached the Frenchman and let him come near.
