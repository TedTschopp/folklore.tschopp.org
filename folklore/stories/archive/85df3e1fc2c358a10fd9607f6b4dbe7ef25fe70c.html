One castle, still well preserved, is called the Sälischloss; the other, entirely destroyed, is called the Wartburg. They belonged to two brothers who both courted a granddaughter of their father. Her name was Bertha, and when she gave her hand to the younger brother, the father built the new castle for them on the hill opposite. One day, while the married couple were sitting together outside on the parapet, the elder brother appeared across the way on his castle, set a target upon the wall, and called to Bertha's husband to do the same so that they might hold an archery contest. It was done, and the two marksmen's arrows flew back and forth, skillfully striking the bull's-eye. “Now for the master shot!” the elder finally cried. “But set your target in a better light for me!” The younger man did so, and at that same moment the man opposite released his bolt. Instead of striking the target, the arrow lodged in the younger brother's heart. Bertha seized the crossbow of her collapsing husband, aimed, and before the man opposite could leave the wall, he too was writhing in his blood. She then hurried into her castle, set it ablaze, and allowed herself to be buried beneath its ruins. People from the nearby little village of Oftringen say that even now she can be seen upon the rock, sitting over a man's body, weeping and wringing her hands.", "According to another account, a barren patch of ground is shown below the castle where no grass grows amid otherwise fine pasture.
