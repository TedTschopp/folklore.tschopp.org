One day, a Frisian boy was grazing his herd in this region. At sunset, when he counted the animals entrusted to him, he noticed that they were not all together. The most beautiful goats were missing. He quickly shut those that remained into the Bergstatt and set out to search for the lost ones. Through forest and gorges he hurried, always calling and coaxing, until at last night overtook him. Overcome by great weariness, the boy sought a place to sleep for the night beneath a large fir tree. In his sleep, strange bells reached his ear, and when he looked around, he saw a little church standing in beautiful open country amid green pastures. When he awoke, the sun was already shining through the branches. He quickly took his staff and herder's bag and hurried onward after his goats. At last a broad and beautiful valley opened before him, encircled and protected by high mountains. It was the valley he had seen in his dream. But no little church could be seen anywhere, nor was there any trace of a human being within it. Yet at a fresh spring in the middle of the meadow, he saw his lost goats drinking. He too drank of the delicious refreshment and, turning to the spring, said, “You shall be called Geissbrunnen!” Full of joy over his discovery, he hurried down the valley to bring the glad news to his people. Fifty householders then resolved to move up into the higher, rich Alpine valley with wives and children. The place where the boy found his lost goats was therefore called Geissbrunnen, and the name has remained to this day. Because the valley floor was full of rich grazing with magnificent, vigorous fodder grasses, the newcomers called the place where they now erected their huts Adelboden.
