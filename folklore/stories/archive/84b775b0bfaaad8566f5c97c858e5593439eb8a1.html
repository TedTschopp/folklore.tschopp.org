Because of where she came from, people had given her the nickname “Schwabenhudi.” She commonly spun in farmhouses and always walked last in line during church processions. On the side, she practiced fortune-telling and black magic. She once stayed for some time as a spinner at Upper Pültli with a family whose descendants are still known in Menzingen. The family included three sons and one daughter named Ammili (Anna Maria). Ammili longed for a handsome, wealthy suitor. One day, Schwabenhudi said to her, “I will procure a handsome, rich bridegroom for you if you come with me.” The foolish girl went with the fiend to the Lorzentobel. There, Ammili saw a stately carriage approach. A young man in a gray coat stepped out and asked the maiden questions, but in her complete embarrassment she could not answer them. Her consciousness gradually faded into a swoon. Meanwhile, the daughter of the house was missed at Pültli. Her mother pondered and searched for Ammili for a long time in vain. At last, she too took the path into the ravine and, to her great astonishment, found her dear daughter sitting at the very top of the covered bridge's roof.
