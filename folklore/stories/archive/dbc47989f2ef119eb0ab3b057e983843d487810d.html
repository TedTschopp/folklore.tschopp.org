At night, it often seemed to him that he heard a bell ringing and a great crowd approaching in loud prayer. He thought this indicated a pilgrimage site and believed a Bethäuslein should be built. One evening, he sat on his property beside a thorn bush and was struck by a severe disease of the limbs from which neither medical remedies nor pious works and pilgrimages would free him. Then, he vowed to build a little prayer house in honor of the Holy Family if he recovered. He immediately became well and wished to keep his promise by building the prayer house beside his home in 1773. But every night, invisible hands carried the tools to the thorn bush where he had fallen ill. He understood that he should build there and therefore began burning the brush and digging. He found a stone slab and beneath it, a human skeleton with a key and a Hirschhörnlein upon which was drawn an image resembling Saint Barbara—objects still preserved. Encouraged by this discovery, Peter Wicki continued the work eagerly, and soon, a prayer house had been erected where the Holy Family was venerated behind an iron grille.
