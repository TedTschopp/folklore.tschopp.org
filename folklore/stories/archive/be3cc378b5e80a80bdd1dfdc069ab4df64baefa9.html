But the farmer quickly took his pocketknife and hurled it with all his strength into the middle of the swirling bundle. The storm ceased at once, but the knife did not return and could not be found, even though a fellow worker had watched carefully to see where it fell. A year later our farmer, who dealt extensively in cattle, made his annual journey down through the canton of Ticino (Graubünden) and stopped as usual at the customary inn. When the innkeeper opened the Gänterli to take out a glass for his guest, the farmer saw his pocketknife inside. Certain marks allowed him to recognize beyond doubt the knife he had lost in that whirlwind. But he gave no sign of it. In earlier years the innkeeper’s pretty daughter had always served him; he had exchanged many a jest with her and fought many a teasing battle of words, sometimes victorious and sometimes defeated. This time she was nowhere to be seen, so he asked after the missing daughter’s health. The innkeeper took the fateful pocketknife from the cupboard, showed it to his guest, and explained with a dark expression: ‘Yes, look here! This little knife cost my daughter her life! She understood Sympathie; once, while she was playfully whirling hay for a farmer across there in Uri, the brute stabbed this knife into her heart, and she had to die of it.
