The history and legend of this part of the Freiamt, however, tell how those very villages were deprived first of their rights and then of their property.", "An Alsatian count, Guntram, had become involved in a conspiracy against Emperor Otto I, lost his fiefs because of it, and was placed under the imperial ban. He then withdrew to a small hereditary estate that lay on the tongue of land at the confluence of the Aare and Reuss in Aargau and is still called Im Eigen today. Here he took his name from the ruins at Altenburg and Habsburg; afterward, he also built himself a manor house in the Freie Ämter and, as the new Landgraf, meddled in the disputes of the people around Muri and Althäusern. These inexperienced people often chose him as arbitrator. But in doing so, to use the monastery chronicle's expression, they had entrusted the bacon to the cat. He knew how to trick them so thoroughly that the communities soon could no longer cut timber in their own forests; and the individual peasants who at first cleared the land for him or leased it from him, he gradually made into people bound to his soil. His sons continued these exactions, and his grandsons increased them further. When Count Ratbot took Countess Ida of Lorraine as his wife, he gave her the Freiamt as a Morgengabe, as though it had belonged to him from time immemorial. His brother Rudolf, possessed by equal greed, was enraged by this; feuds arose between the two, and they burned down each other's villages when they could not agree how to divide them. Meanwhile, that Countess Ida of Lorraine had arrived in Aargau. When she saw the land devastated as far as Zofingen and the towns of Aarau and Brugg going up in flames, she asked the cause of these distressing events. From Werner, the third and youngest brother, who lived apart from the conflict as Bishop of Strasbourg, she learned of the ancient wrong already committed by the forefather of the house. The humane woman was horrified by it; but by the standards of her time, she believed that she could atone for the evil and do good beyond that if, instead of returning the plunder to the peasants who had been robbed, she used it to build a monastery. Thus, upon those very lands that had been given to her as a bridal gift, she founded the Benedictine Abbey of Muri in 1018.", "The whole subsequent history of this religious house and the surrounding countryside is contained in the words of a monk who wrote the monastery chronicle of Muri.
