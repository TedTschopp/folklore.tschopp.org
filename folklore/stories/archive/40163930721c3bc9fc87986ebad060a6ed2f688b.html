If you remember my instructions, however, you can easily catch it and then need neither patch trousers by day nor steal manure in the evening to fertilize your little field. But you must make it handsome clothes and dress it freshly every day. The tailor needed no second telling and in the very first night waited at the crossroads until twelve o’clock. The animal appeared. Admittedly, it did not exactly match the Capuchin’s description and, if one overlooked the glittering eyes of its incomprehensible head, resembled only a medium-sized house dog. All the more quickly the tailor went after the creature of fortune, packed it into his Hutte, and threw it into the goat stall at home; before locking up again, however, he carefully placed his only Sparthaler beneath its belly. He could scarcely wait for morning. No sooner had he entered the stall again than, instead of one coin, he found a hundred new thalers on the bedding, each exactly like the first from the day before. He quickly gathered up all the thalers and bought his neighbor’s field adjoining his turnip plot for a hundred and one thalers. Tomorrow, he thought, once I have opened the stall again, I shall also pay him for the meadow bordering it. Morning came. The Alraun lay patiently on the bedding, but not a single thaler lay beside it; not one Rappen could be seen. The tailor had forgotten to keep the first thaler he placed there. Now it had been spent, and the animal’s magical power had dried up with it. What was to be done? He fetched his Hutte, carried the animal back into the forest, and ran home to sell his new field again quickly.
