She returned with her udder almost empty from the rich pasture near the castle where she grazed each day at that season with her horned companions. At first, the servant suspected the woodcutters and charcoal burners working nearby. But because matters did not improve after they left, he resolved to watch for the unauthorized milker. On the very first evening, he observed that as the time to go home approached, the cow always remained near a large rock and often paused in her grazing as though expecting someone. He crept silently closer and hid very nearby behind a Schermtanne. Suddenly, he saw a large, beautiful snake wind majestically down from the rock in great coils. He could not tell whether it had emerged from beneath the rock or even from a crack within it. Rigid with amazement, he watched the snake attach itself to the swollen udder and visibly savor the sweet liquid. Only then did he notice that the strange milkmaid's head bore a gleaming golden crown adorned with flashing stones. The shining reptile's slender body grew ever thicker, fuller, and rounder. Without making the slightest movement, the cow watched the supple animal with apparent pleasure. When the udder was nearly empty, the snake slipped away for a few moments. The cow did not leave the spot; she seemed to be waiting for something more. Sure enough, the snake soon returned carrying in its wide-open jaws a mighty bundle of juicy herbs unknown to the cowherd, and held them out for its milk-giver to eat. The cow accepted the refreshment with visible pleasure, and it seemed to taste exceptionally good. After the last trace of this richly deserved delicacy had vanished, the snake crawled back into its hiding place, and the cow prepared to follow the others home. The herdsman also set out for home. He now felt very strange. The shining snake with its precious crown flickered continuously before his eyes. Yet another mystery had also been partly solved for him: why his beautiful brown Liesel grew ever sleeker, lovelier, and fatter despite the abundance of milk she gave, while the pasture steadily diminished in the continuing drought, as the other cows' appearance showed all too plainly. The little herbs bestowed by the snake possessed this wonderfully nourishing and sustaining power. He realized that knowing them would have to make him a rich man. All night, he dreamed of golden crowns; gleaming snakes that changed into splendid cows and then fed upon rare herbs he had never seen before; and mysterious passages and caves in the castle rock filled with treasures of every kind. When he awoke in the morning, his resolution was firm: the golden crown and the knowledge of the miraculous herbs must become his, whatever the cost. In feverish impatience, he could scarcely wait for evening. At the desired hour, his heart pounding, he crept down with his shooting gear close to the peculiar rendezvous and again hid behind the old Schermtanne. His sleek brown Liesel was already grazing unsuspectingly near the rock, and before long, her strange friend was once more hanging from the sweet spring. Because the watcher wanted not only the precious jewel but also the mysterious herbs, he did not wish cruelly to interrupt the snake's final pleasure. He intended to wait for the moment when the grateful milkmaid presented its nourishing gift, hoping in this way to seize both treasures at once. He had no doubt that such herbs grew nearby. If only he could see them properly once, he thought, he would then be able to find and propagate them. At last, the reptile was fully satisfied and slipped away to fulfill its obligation. The herdsman strained his eyes in vain to see where it went; nowhere could he see it gathering the herbs. He did not dare leave, for the moment had come when his skill must prove itself and make him the happiest of marksmen. With anxious expectation and feverish haste, he bent the bow, laid the swift arrow upon it, and raised the deadly weapon to aim, for his magnificent target had just emerged from behind the sheltering rock, more beautiful than ever. Liesel was smacking her lips and about to stretch her tongue toward the fragrant herbs when the hissing arrow pierced the lovely giver's flashing eye. With a terrible leap and her tail raised high, Liesel fled the dreadful place. But why did Hans, the successful marksman, not move from the spot to pocket his costly prey? He had good reason. At the very instant he loosed the fateful shot, a piece of rock came bounding powerfully down from above and shattered his right leg. Only with the greatest effort and in terrible pain could he drag himself to the place where he believed the snake should be lying. He could find neither snake, crown, nor herbs. Every search was in vain. This did not seem natural to him. He now also understood why a stone had broken loose above him at that very instant and crushed precisely his leg. How easily the stone might have shattered his head instead! He had scarcely reached the point in his reflections where he regarded his situation as a well-deserved and comparatively merciful punishment when he heard approaching footsteps. The castle physician soon arrived and, with the other three men's help, properly set and bandaged the leg.
