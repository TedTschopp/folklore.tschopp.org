At the landing stage in Brunnen, a dark-complexioned Wybervölchli joined them and said that they had a treasure in their house that she would reveal to them if the woman gave her the dangling gold earrings and, I believe, the gold finger ring as well. My aunt gave her what she wanted. The stranger said that one day she would come to their house, and then went away. Sure enough, after a long while she came to the house. She showed them an egg and spoke over it twice: ‘In the name of the ninety-nine!’ Then the egg opened, and a Totäschidälä and hair appeared inside it. The stranger now said, ‘Never throw hair outdoors, or you might pick up a lifelong headache! As for the treasure, I still require eight Duplen; then I will give you the information.’ After some hesitation, my aunt gave her the eight Duplen. The stranger then said, ‘I shall return. I have no power to stay overnight with you!’ and left. This was on the eve of the feast of the Mother of God in mid-August. After she had left the house, she was heard screaming. She never returned. My cousins now went to the Jesuits, who said that yes, there was a treasure in their house, guarded by a spirit. A noble lord had lived there in former times. Every evening, the couple, their relatives, and everyone who had a claim to the treasure should gather in the house and pray together. Then the spirit would appear, and an innocent child should address it. No one else, however, must interrupt. The relatives now gathered in the house and prayed every evening. One evening—by chance, my father was not among those praying that time—the spirit appeared. It looked like a clergyman in a surplice. An innocent child who had been instructed stepped forward and addressed it: ‘I address you in the name of the Most Holy Trinity. If I can assist you toward eternal salvation, I reserve the first and last word for myself.’ The spirit then extended its hand, and the child's mother, fearing that the spirit might burn the child's hand, cried, ‘Maryeli, give him the Dechizipfel!’ A dreadful G'schäry und G'rimpel arose in the house; the spirit vanished, and the treasure remained unraised.—The narrator introduced the story with these words: ‘Father, I do not believe the stories you tell there; you are trying to pull the wool over our eyes.
