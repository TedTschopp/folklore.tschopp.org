As high as this heap of bones rises, it is said to extend just as deep into the ground or beneath the earth. A Liechtji often burns there for half the night, indeed until broad daylight. Devout people go there to carry in and light it for the comfort of the departed souls, and by this light, they pray reverently for the Armu-Seele until late in the night. Once, a malicious Nachtbuob wanted to play an evil trick on a devout person at prayer. So that he could properly frighten her, he took a freshly skinned cowhide and threw it down from above so that it would fall over her head. But what happened? At the very instant when he threw the hide down, the departed souls flung it back over his own head. This so terrified the malicious Nachtbuob that he staggered home from Chlupf—sheer fright—fell ill, and within a few days was a corpse. The departed souls held their benefactress in honor so that no harm would happen to her. Hence comes the saying among the people: ‘Das dem und dem nüt g'scheh ist, ist as g'fällig's Wunder! — dem heint d'Abg'storbnu gholfu!
