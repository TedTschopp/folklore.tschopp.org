The mountain divides the valley into two smaller valleys. The one on the right, containing the two villages of Wittnau and Kienberg, is watered by the Sissel, a spring-fed brook descending from the Schafmatt pass. The other little valley on the left runs narrowly upward between wooded heights by way of Wölfliswil and Oberhofen to the Benken. There another spring of the Sissel rises and soon becomes the sizable Oerkenbach. Along the whole of its course down to its confluence with the Wittnau brook, the Oerkentier haunts the stream and its banks. It is the ghost of a Swedish soldier from the Thirty Years’ War. At the place where the farmers killed him, a low, moss-covered stone cross still stands at the roadside in a field, already pushed half askew by the plow. A few hundred paces to the left of the brook, the ghost has its dwelling. Here is a cave into which, until only a few years ago, a stone staircase is said to have descended. Until four years ago, a block of Nagelfluh conglomerate the size of a hay wagon also lay before the entrance; the owner of the neighboring meadow has now blasted it apart. The Oerkentier stood on this block when the people of Wittnau visited the church at Wölfliswil during Kreuzwoche. If it let its cry of “Hop, hop!” be heard, unfavorable spring weather could certainly be expected. It did the same from its other station, a slope on the Alteberg. From the woods it called to the young men of Wittnau returning at night from the Wölfliswil church-dedication dance. If they answered with the same call, thinking their companions who had gone ahead were calling, they were certain to reach home with swollen heads.
