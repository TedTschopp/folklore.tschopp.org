Then two strangers, Beatus and Justus, came walking over the black mountain called the Brünig, wearing haircloth cloaks and carrying long pilgrims' staffs. They followed the shore of Lake Brienz into the Bödeli, which still looked inhospitable in those days. They continued their pilgrimage at the foot of the sunny mountains, crossed the Lombach, and came to the ancient little village of Sundlauenen. Friendly herdsmen lived there in smoke-blackened huts. The strangers were hospitably received. They told the people that they had come to bring good news to the inhabitants of that region. The one true God had sent Jesus Christ into the world to bring salvation to sinful humanity. The people of Sundlauenen asked Beatus to remain with them and instruct them in his new teaching. The worthy stranger gladly promised to do so. Then they told him that a fierce Lindwurm lived in a neighboring cave, to the harm of people and livestock. ‘The earth is the Lord's, and all that is in it!’ Beatus declared. ‘In the name of almighty God, I will drive away the dragon.’ At early dawn, the devout man and his companion set out. They reached the cave at sunrise. The dragon hissed horribly at those approaching. Beatus raised his pilgrim's staff and adjured the monster in the name of the Almighty. With a powerless howl of rage, the dragon rushed out. It sped upward over the vertical rock wall and, in dreadful anger, struck the Fluh so violently with its enormous tail that the image of the dragon remained upon it as an everlasting sign.
