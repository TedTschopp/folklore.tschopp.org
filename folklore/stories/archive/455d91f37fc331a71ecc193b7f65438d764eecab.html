Since his household had already gone to bed, he searched the kitchen to see what they had prepared for his “z'Nacht,” but found nothing except a heap of nutshells and little snail shells in a kettle over the fire. In a “Täubi”—a rage—that they had made such a fool of him, he took the kettle from the fire. Suddenly, however, he saw no fire at all, and all the nutshells and snail shells had vanished from the kettle. The good man could not explain this, and he became thoroughly afraid of what might lie behind it.—He therefore gave up his search for something edible and went to sleep. Throughout the night he dreamed continually of a great kettle full of nutshells, snail shells, and the like.—In the morning he related both the occurrence and the dream. Outside in the kitchen, however, his supper—the very meal he had searched for but had been unable to see or find—stood on the hearth itself.", "His sister then reported that some time before, when she had wanted to enter the cellar, a white-clad monk had barred her way. The man became deeply thoughtful about all this and concluded that a spirit lived there. He considered the matter and resolved to ask two priests for their help, for alone he did not dare get to the bottom of the spirit he had in his house.", "For this purpose he made the journey over Schlapin into Montavon, went to two priests, and told them everything. The priests were immediately ready to help the man and returned with him to Closters. All three entered the cellar, where one priest read all manner of formulas from a large book he had brought. As if by accident, this reading priest dropped his handkerchief. The homeowner noticed and picked it up. The priest continued reading and dropped the handkerchief again. The man was about to pick it up once more, but the other priest signaled him to leave it lying, stooped himself, and picked up the handkerchief together with a handful of earth. He then put the handkerchief and the earth inside it into his pocket.—At this, the other priest stopped reading, and both immediately returned to Montavon.", "The following night the man made his sleeping place beside the stove. He could not sleep, so greatly did the story occupy his thoughts. It was a bright night; the full moon stood in the sky and shone through the windows. A little before midnight he heard steps like those of a person, and immediately afterward the door opened and a tall man entered. By movements of his head, the stranger conveyed his thanks. The man felt very strange. He wanted to address his late guest, but no word came forth, nor was he capable of any movement. The tall stranger then left the room. The man who remained behind heard him depart and also heard him dragging something after himself down the stairs. Only then could he rise again from the stove-bench where he had sat as though bound fast for as long as the other had remained in the room. They revealed to him that a monastery had formerly stood on the very site where his house now stood, and that silver plate and coined money had been hidden there in the cellar. The mute visitor—a monk who had been required to guard the treasure—had taken it to Montavon because, under the terms of a bequest, the treasure belonged to the nearest Catholic church.—", "The house where the treasure lay and the monastery formerly stood is still called “im Winkel” today.
