In summer, when cattle occupy all the surrounding high pastures and the ringing of the herds echoes most pleasantly through the mountains, the Älpler of the whole region gather here once each year on a Sunday for what is called a Dorfet. From Mannried and Grubenwald, from Schwanden, Zwischenflüh, and Diemtigen, they climb up for the merry mountain festival, and the young Sennerinnen must not be absent. Then things grow lively and exuberant. At the height of the Alp, on the Gestelengrat that forms both the watershed and the boundary between the two valleys, everyone drinks, sings, shoots, bowls, and dances together upon the green turf. Even the toes can be clearly distinguished. This rock is called the Teufelstritt, and the following legend is told of it:", "One morning, when it was once again Dorfsonntag, a beautiful, wealthy Sennerin walked proudly up the mountain to attend the Älpler festival on the Gestelengrat. Again and again, she looked complacently at her fine Sunday dress, its flowers and fluttering ribbons, and thought, “Today, I am surely the most beautiful and celebrated of all the dancers.” To make this come true, she swore solemnly and vowed that she would belong to the Devil if she could not dance the first dance and lead the round. Thus she reached the Alp. Because it was a splendid, sunny day, a great number of Älpler and Älplerinnen had already gathered, and more kept arriving. At last, the musicians struck up a cheerful Ländler to open the dancing. Everyone rose, and each man sought a partner. But however longingly the beautiful woman let her gaze wander, no one came to invite her. The young people flew merrily across the meadow in a leaping circle dance, while she had to stand alone. Scarcely had the last notes of the music faded when the Devil stood there. With a grinning, scornful laugh, he seized her around the waist, hurried away with her, set her upon the block of rock just described, and vanished. She now stood fixed by enchantment and could not move from the spot. Instead of being first in the dance as she had planned in her pride, she did not take part at all and could only watch from afar. No one knows what later became of her.
