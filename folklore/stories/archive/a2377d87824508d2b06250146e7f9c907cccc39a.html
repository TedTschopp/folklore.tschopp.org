She wanted to fetch medicine for her little grandchild, who had suddenly fallen ill, and could easily make the journey there and back within two hours if nothing else delayed her. It was already beginning to grow dark when she reached the little Roggenhausen valley, which lies close to the town, and came down to the footbridge that crosses the Wöschnau forest stream. There, however, a hunter barred her passage. The man was very tall and, in addition to his otherwise entirely green attire, wore a red plume high upon his hat. When the man raised his gun against her, she at first took it for a foolish hunter’s jest and called to him, “Go ahead and shoot; I am not afraid!” But the hunter kept his aim unchanged and smiled not at all kindly, but like an executioner. “I have no time to play the fool with you here,” said the woman. “Let me across!” Without moving, he remained in firing position and continued to aim. At so late an hour, the little old woman could not linger long. No longer distinguishing between jest and earnest, she confidently entrusted herself to the protection of the dear saints. “If it must be so,” she said boldly, “then shoot, in God’s name!” At these words she saw nothing before her but the open bridge. Only then did fear seize her, and she did not dare cross. She went back to one of the few thatched houses lying a hundred paces away at the rear end of the valley and asked the farmwife there to accompany her across the bridge as far as the main road. But the woman was herself alone in the house, was just cooking the evening soup, and could neither set aside her pan of boiling milk nor let it run into the fire. She also felt obliged to justify especially her forced unfriendliness toward the little old woman and therefore shook her head in disbelief over the little tale said to have occurred at the bridge. Thus the old woman went home without the medicine and had the joy of seeing the grandchild she had thought ill run cheerfully and happily to meet her as far as the hedge.", "Not long afterward, however, that farmwife in the Roggenhausen valley who had been so unwilling to help was herself to be taught better. On her way home from the weekly market in town, she saw a new red silk neckerchief lying at the very place where the hunter had threatened the little old woman. She shifted her heavy market basket onto her left arm and reached down with her right hand toward the pretty cloth in the stream ditch. She gave a cry of pain: it suddenly burned her as though she had grasped a hedgehog skin, and the neckerchief was no longer to be seen.
