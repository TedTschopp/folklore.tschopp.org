Where the lovely City of Roses stands today, there were then only a few fishermen’s huts. The place was called Endingen. The lake causeway had not yet been built, nor even the wooden bridge that led across the lake to the Hürden peninsula; the fishermen carried all traffic in their boats.\nAcross the water, a little southwest of the village of Lachen, stood Rapperswil Castle, and Count Rudolf lived there, beloved by all the people, respected and rich. Once, when he returned from a long journey, his Vogt proposed that he build a new castle at a favorable site—specifically near Endingen, on the scenic Lindenhügel, where a stronghold could be well defended against any enemy and where a city could also be founded to afford its citizens the desired security.", "Count Rudolf immediately set out with his entire retinue to inspect the place. When they arrived, the hunting dogs tracked down a doe that had borne two young in a cave on the hill and was bravely defending them. The countess took pity on the courageous animal and asked that the dogs be taken away. Then, when the distinguished guests sat down to rest for a moment and admire the beautiful view, the doe approached trustingly and nestled against the noblewoman to show her the gratitude she owed. The count then had the two young fawns brought aboard the boat so that they could be sheltered in his castle garden; their mother followed contentedly. And the very next day, the boat brought over the builders, who felled the forest, dressed the logs, and built the city.\nP.
