From then on, his conscience tormented him without end. He took up a pilgrim’s staff and cloak and fled, more than journeyed, to Sankt Jakob, where he confessed his dreadful sin. Because of his great remorse, he received absolution, but only on condition that he undertake a severe penance. Never again for the rest of his life was he to walk, dress, and feed himself in the manner and custom of human beings. Instead, like an animal to whose level his sin had debased him, he had to wander and conduct himself for as many years as heaven pleased. One day the end of his penance would be announced to him.
