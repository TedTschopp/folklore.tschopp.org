The following is still told in the Visp valley about this victory:", "Count Amadeus of Savoy came up through the country with many troops as far as Visp. He demanded that the people admit him into the fortified town, surrender, and swear obedience to him; otherwise, he would burn and destroy everything. The good people were greatly frightened, for they were poorly prepared and poorly equipped for war. Quite unexpectedly, so many enemies had appeared at their doors. In their fear, therefore, they requested three days to consider—not because they wished to surrender voluntarily, but to gain that much time. The Savoyards granted them the requested time, took quarters along the Vispa, and waited for an answer. — The people of Visp urgently summoned help from farther up the valley and from Goms and Brig; otherwise, they kept as quiet as mice so that the enemies would notice nothing. But when the third night came, with which the period for consideration ended, everything in the fortified town came alive. The summoned help arrived; only the people of Brig were delayed and did not come until the affair was over. Water was diverted into the town's defenses; in the severe winter cold, it froze into ice and made paths and footbridges impassable. In workshops and many a kitchen, people busily forged pointed foot-irons and shoe nails so that they could stand securely on the ice. They prepared large blocks of wood and wagons heavily loaded with stones, to which they also attached cutting implements, so that these could be sent rolling down across the ice into the enemy ranks. Even the women were not idle and had more than enough to do; they vigorously worked the bellows for the smiths, brought up coal and iron, distributed the sharpened irons and shoe nails among the warriors, and busily helped equip everything and prepare for the attack. They even carried water on their backs to make ice in places where it could not be directed through conduits. People worked with such zeal and haste that seven men succumbed to the exertion.", "At daybreak, everything was ready for battle. As promised, the people now wished to bring an answer to the enemy officers, who were lodging in a barn because of the cold. They took a lamb and tied its four feet together; carefully opened the barn door; threw it inside as a morning greeting; and then barred the door again with a Reisteisen in such haste that one man knocked the thumb from another's hand. They then set the barn on fire, and the flames rose high. Even as the officers cried for mercy through the roof battens, none was given. Melted gold and silver ran down from the barn to the ground in a stream as thick as an arm.", "Meanwhile, the remaining well-equipped warriors, marching securely over the ice, also attacked the enemy army and sent large pieces of wood and cutting, heavily loaded wagons into the enemy on the smooth ground.
