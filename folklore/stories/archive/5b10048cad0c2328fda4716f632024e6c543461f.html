At the foot of this cross grew a modest rosebush that many did not notice at all. It is said to descend from the rosebush on which Mary, the Mother of God, hung the Christ Child’s swaddling clothes to dry during the flight into Egypt. How this rosebush came there, however, remains a mystery known to no one.", "This little rose tree had a single bud. It is said never to have wilted or withered. The bud remained tightly closed throughout the year, even when the warm spring air made other roses swell or the sun’s rays brought roses into bloom in summer. The bud remained so until near year’s end. In the darkest time of the year, shortly before the holy feast of Christmas, when everything around it stiffened in the cold and snow had fallen, the bud slowly began to unfold. On Holy Night, when the church windows over in Mariastein shone with the light of many candles and the bells proclaimed the wonder of Christmas night across the broad St. Annafeld and into the darkness, at that hour, when the hope and longing for redemption seized millions of Christian hearts, the bud opened in all its splendor. When it had opened fully, a bright radiance issued from it, together with a wondrous fragrance. A rustling and living movement suddenly passed through the frozen winter landscape. It is said that in sheer astonishment even the dark firs lowered their crowns, just as though they wished to look at the marvelous thing happening there. Even the moss at the foot of the cross began to move. The animals all around came and marveled at what was taking place.
