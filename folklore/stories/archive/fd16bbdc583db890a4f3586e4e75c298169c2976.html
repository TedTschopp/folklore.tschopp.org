He threw the mother and child into an underground dungeon of the castle and for many years brought their food down himself. Later, when the fugitive was able to return to his homeland, he strangled the maid in the dungeon. Her boy, who had meanwhile grown to nearly ten years old, he placed secretly with a farmer on the mountain. From the child's own mouth, the farmer soon learned of the crime and reported it. Far away, the authorities again found the murderer's trail, and when he saw himself discovered, he took his own life. A letter was found upon him in which he had expressed the wish to be buried at his Castelen Castle. When he thought he had reached it, it went out, and in its place lay a red handkerchief spread across the hedge as if to dry. In this region, where there was no dwelling for a great distance, the cloth puzzled him. At first, he meant to take it, but on further thought, he decided against it and threw it away with the contemptuous words, ‘De näm' i ned ewegg!’—‘I would not take that away!’ Scarcely a few steps farther on, however, another red Tüchlein lay there. The farmer now turned back to see whether the earlier one still lay in its old place; it was gone. He began to feel uneasy and hurried away. But after only a short time, the ominous Tüchlein lay in the footpath before him once more. Then he took courage, began to curse and swear, and swung his stick. The Tüchlein vanished immediately, and in its place returned the light, which now shone before the farmer all the way to his house.
