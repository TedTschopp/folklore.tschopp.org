Stoop" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "Between Halbmil and Grünhag, where the great beech forest on an ancient landslide reaches down to the valley floor, an abundant spring, Saint Catherine’s Spring, rises a few steps from the country road. Centuries ago, the chapel of Saint Catherine stood there, and the church owned substantial properties in the area, especially vineyards. All of it lies deeply buried by the Rüfebach that comes down from the Spitzbühl. Alders and beeches now stand on the stony ground. The names Brunnenwingert and Meiersberg still tell of times gone by. The Rüfe is also said once to have brought a little bell back to daylight. The sunny, wind-sheltered corner by the country road, rich in water and wood, has always been a favorite stopping place for traveling people, Gypsies, kettle-menders, and basket makers. To the local people, the place is the scene of apparitions. At midnight, mysterious figures can be seen walking there, and through the murmuring of the water and the whispering and rustling of the forest, the little bells of the vanished Saint Catherine’s Chapel can be heard faintly ringing.\nJ.
