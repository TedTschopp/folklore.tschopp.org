Among other things, it is said that on one journey he carried 7 Centner of iron out of the Melchtal, where an iron smelter formerly stood, while casually fishing in the Melchaa along the way. On his return he carried a barrel of salt weighing 7 to 8 Centner into the valley, again occupying himself with fishing. From half a quarter-measure of flour he cooked himself a so-called Kohlermus for one meal, but then ate nothing more for the whole week. He used his gigantic strength to take unfair advantage of the other members of the commonage. When hay was gathered on the common land and everyone had the right to take the same number of bundles or loads, each time he packed into a rope or net and carried home as much as four other men ordinarily could. They possessed the same rights as he did but naturally could not carry as much as Rotzer.—After his death he was often seen being led and handled on a chain by seven evil spirits. People considered how he might be saved, and later Chaplain Troxler accomplished it through a conjuration on the Walsli Alp.
