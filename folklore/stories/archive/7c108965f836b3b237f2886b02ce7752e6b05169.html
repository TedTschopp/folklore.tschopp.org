But when the Catholics gained the majority and therefore the power, they rejected the Reformed people's petition for the free practice of their worship. The Reformed were enraged and secretly planned a coup. When the Reformed realized that their plot had been betrayed, they encamped, likewise well equipped, on the other side of the river. Thus the city's two parties, Catholic and Reformed, soon stood facing one another ready for battle, trading vicious insults and challenges back and forth across the Aare. In fury the Catholics now fetched the great cannon from the arsenal and aimed it at the Reformed. A first shot was fired, fortunately without causing harm. But when the cannon was to be fired a second time with more precise aim, Mayor Wengi, a tall, battle-ready man, hurried forward, placed his hand upon his heart, stood before the cannon's muzzle, and cried: ‘If citizens' blood must flow, let mine flow first!’ At that, the gunners lowered their arms, and no one dared oppose the valiant man.
