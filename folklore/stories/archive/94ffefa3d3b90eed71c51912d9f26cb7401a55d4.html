She wore a self-woven striped little dress with a Gstältli, as had formerly been fashionable, and an old-fashioned little cap on her head. In her hand she carried the stick of an old umbrella, and over her shoulder she had tied an old-fashioned striped Dach. She stopped nowhere, but whoever she met on the road she asked about Lehkühe, although she never took any with her. She wandered to Ursern and over the St. Gotthard. If she came early, there was an early Lanxi—spring; if she came late, a late Lanxi. No one saw her return before autumn. Then the selfsame Wybervölchli came back from the Gotthard through the upper Reuss Valley, in the same clothing, with the same stick, and with the same Dach tied over her shoulder. She asked the people she met on the road about winter Lehkühe, but again took none with her. If she came late, there was a late winter; if she came early, an early winter. She stopped nowhere before reaching the Güetligaden in the Wassen forest. People observed her going inside, but no one saw her come out, and no one who searched inside ever found her; there she seemed to have vanished. Throughout all the many years she always wore the same little dress, had the same Dach tied over her shoulder in precisely the same way, and carried the same stick in her hand. People believed she was an old witch. ‘When I was still going to school—ah, people told and believed things in those days!
