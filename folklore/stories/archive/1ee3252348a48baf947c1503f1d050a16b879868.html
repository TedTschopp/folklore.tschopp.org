In the same way they brought in hay and field crops. They also knew how to advise people about what and where to plant, when the sign was favorable for sowing flax and cabbage, when manure should be hauled and spread over the land, and similar matters. Once such a Heidemanndli met a farmer who was still very far behind with his fieldwork. ‘Go on, dear neighbor,’ it told him, ‘and sow turnip seed today. The sign is good; it will surely yield something!’ The farmer knew the Heidemanndli well, hurried home, and sowed a large field with turnip seed. It was already late in autumn, and the people laughed at him. But although only five weeks remained for growth in field and meadow, the seed nevertheless thrived, and that time the farmer obtained the finest turnips in the region.", "These Heidemanndli also understood many arts of witchcraft. They needed only to knock three times with a hammer on the doors or locks, and all of them sprang open. Then they went into the kitchen and began to cook and roast. Whatever they used and ate was present all the more abundantly in the morning. They did the same with meat: they merely went into the stable, cut a fine piece from the cows and oxen, and cooked and ate it. In the morning everything had grown back, and such cows became the fattest and finest in the whole country.
