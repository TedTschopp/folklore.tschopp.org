Then the hunt stormed through mountain and valley until daybreak. In a nearby Voralp the hunter always seemed to rest a little before midnight, for there he gathered his scattered and strayed dogs again and stored the game he had killed in a rock cave. The Sennen could scarcely endure staying there. Unless they kept quiet as mice, he devastated everything on the Vorsatz, smashed roofs and fences, the hounds dug deep holes in the ground, and the cows in the stable of the Staffel were invisibly tormented and tortured so that they bellowed loudly and gave only a little poor milk the next morning.", "Once a young, inexperienced Küher stood before the Alpine hut, and when he saw the Nachtjäger approaching, he boldly cried, ‘ho, ha, ho! ho, ha, ho!’ But suddenly, although the huntsman was still far away, he received such a violent blow in the back, as if from a gunstock, that he fell to the ground bloodied, bruised, and half unconscious. The Sennen could pick him up and tend him only the next morning, for they had not dared to step out of the Staffel as long as the storm of the hunt continued and the barking dogs seemed to besiege it. The dogs did the poor Küher no harm, however; they only sniffed him until they departed with the restless hunter.
