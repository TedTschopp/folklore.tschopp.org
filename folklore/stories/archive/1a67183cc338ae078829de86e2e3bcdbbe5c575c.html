Because it was very cold, Lord Wernhardt took pity on him and sent him his cloak to cover himself. But the next morning, the pilgrim and the cloak had disappeared. After this, Lord Wernhardt set out on a pilgrimage to Mount Gargano, where the Archangel Michael had appeared in the year 320. Before the knight began the journey, however, he broke his wedding ring into two halves. He gave one to his wife Susanna, kept the other himself, and said: \"When you see this half again, it will be a sign to you that I am still alive. Afterward, when the knight had begun his journey home, he was taken captive and spent four years in a dungeon in Lamparten. One evening, a stranger suddenly appeared to him there, brought him the remainder of the stolen cloak, and revealed himself as the Devil. He told Wernhardt that he had been the pilgrim but had now come at Saint Michael’s command to return him to his homeland. It was high time, for his wife Susanna, who believed him dead, was about to remarry and would hold her wedding that very night. The Devil then gently lifted the knight from the ground and in a few moments carried him safely and unharmed to his castle at Strättlingen. Lord Wernhardt told his people, who did not recognize him, that he was a foreign minstrel and adventurer. When he was invited to the table where the wedding feast had just been held, the knight threw into the cup from which his wife usually drank the half of the ring that he had kept for himself and guarded faithfully ever since, then withdrew without being noticed. But when his wife took up the cup, she saw the half-ring, fitted the two halves together, and cried: \"My husband is not far from this place!\" At that same moment, she recognized him in the corner of the hall to which he had withdrawn.
