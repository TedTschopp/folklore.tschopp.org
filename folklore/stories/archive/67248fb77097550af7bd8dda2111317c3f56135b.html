Johannes did not wish to take him in and resorted to all manner of flimsy excuses. He was surely a great lord, Johannes said, and his wretched hut was too poor for such a man; he himself was an impoverished fellow; the hut was cramped; and he offered still other objections. After the stranger assured him that he would certainly make no demands and would be content with the humblest food and simplest G’liger, the goat farmer allowed himself to be persuaded to lodge him. The next morning, the stranger asked Johannes to accompany him to the top of the pass because he did not know the way well enough.
