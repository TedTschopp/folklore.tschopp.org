They had arranged to meet in Killwangen and travel together from there to Baldingen. On the last stretch of the way they saw magpies flying ahead of them with incessant cries. The birds accompanied them as far as Baldingen and continued to swarm after them there when they later went outdoors and inspected the fields with their brother-in-law. For that reason he immediately suspected something bad. When they left him again two days later, he particularly urged both to be very careful on their way home. In Killwangen the two sisters separated again, one to go toward Spreitenbach and the other to follow her usual road home to Bellikon. The first reached home safely, but the second now met the misfortune that the Galgenvögel had sung to them. When she reached the Remetschwil Weidgang, she saw a lovely little hare hopping ahead of her between the woods and the communal meadows. “If only I had a little gun!” she said to herself, and she was heartily delighted that the animal hopped so prettily ahead along her path. But when she reached the Kreuzwegli, the hare suddenly ran back toward Remetschwil. The woman likewise turned around, without considering what she was doing or why, and instead of taking the Bellikon road ran back along the one to Killwangen, always in the firm belief that she must reach her home in the next quarter-hour. But she climbed for several hours through brush and scrub, reached steep, precipitous places, and was entirely exhausted and lost when at last she came to a house in Killwangen where several young men happened still to be awake with a light. She was so completely convinced that she stood before her own home that, astonished, she asked the first man who opened the door how he came to be there and whether her husband Hans was not at home.
