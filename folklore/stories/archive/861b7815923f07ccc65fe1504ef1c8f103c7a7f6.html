The true sources of the Reuss actually flow from beneath the glaciers and perennial snowfields of the Göscheneralp. An hour and a half into the valley from the little village where the imperial toll was collected in olden times, toward the Dammafirn, stands Samiklausen, a chapel once dedicated to Saint Nicholas, Bishop of Myra, by the Bishop of Constance at the farthest boundary of his diocese. Everyone in the Göschenen Valley and on the Göscheneralp will tell you—and in Urseren you will hear it confirmed—that Samiklausen is the oldest church in the canton of Uri. It is now falling into ruin. Here, where Christianity first took root in the region, Waldbrüder are said to have lived with a priest. At Sankt Niklausen, the people claim, a holy body lies buried. It is said to be little more than a hundred years since an honest shepherd boy entered the church early one morning. A priest came and asked him to serve at Mass. The boy did so gladly. After Mass, the clergyman invited him to return every morning to serve, but strictly forbade him to tell anyone about it, and then vanished. The shepherd boy kept his word and was very discreet. For a long while, he thus assisted the priestly spirit at the holy sacrifice. At last, his mother noticed that her son always left at a particular time and became very urgent whenever he was delayed. She suspected a secret and pressed him, though for a long time in vain. A mother's tenderness finally loosened his tongue.
