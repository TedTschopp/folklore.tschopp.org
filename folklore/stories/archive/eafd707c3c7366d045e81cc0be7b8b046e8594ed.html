Würenlingen.)" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "The Rossheiri once dwelled on the so-called Siggenberg opposite the Geissberg. Every night he hauled wood and stones down from his mountain height into the villages of Würenlingen, Endingen, and Siggingen. His team consisted of four white horses, and two large white poodles ran beside the wagon. His voice was a wagoner’s dreadful cursing, a savage urging and goading of the horses, interspersed with such prolonged cries of woe that the birds in the woods started from their roosts and everyone traveling the road between Thurgi and Würenlingen fled in terror for shelter. Although he has been dead for many centuries, he can still be heard hauling stones, cracking his whip, and shouting curses. This nocturnal tumult is loudest at the so-called Geisterstein, a misshapen boulder beside the well-traveled field road on the Siggenberg. At appointed times all the evil spirits of the region gathered there, stopped passersby, and asked them for news. If someone would not oblige by recounting the news to them, he was carried off into the forest, and in former times many people thus lost their lives.", "(Seminarian K.
