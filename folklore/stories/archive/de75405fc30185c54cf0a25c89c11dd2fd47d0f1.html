The man was a good, simple farmer, yet so contented and cheerful that scarcely a day passed when he did not set to work at sunrise with jubilant cries and song. But one morning, his yodel had fallen silent. He crept along gloomily, and his face showed a strange pallor. Illness had overtaken him, and sorrow now entered the friendly little house. One morning, when his worthy wife again found him leaning against the rock wall with ashen cheeks, the sight nearly broke her heart. To hide her pain, she hurried out into the forest. There she fell to her knees to send a fervent prayer up to heaven. Scarcely had she finished when she suddenly saw a dwarf standing before her in an unearthly radiance. He wore a crown upon his head and held a scepter in his hand. ‘I am the dwarf king of the Niesen,’ he said. ‘Do not be frightened, good woman; I bring you only good. Do you see that thicket up there? A spring has flowed there for centuries, but until today it remained hidden from humans. Climb up, draw from the spring, and give your husband a drink. He will recover for you.’ With that, the dwarf vanished. But the woman rose with a comforted heart. She did as she had been told and diligently gave the sick man water from the spring. As though by a miracle, he recovered, and happiness returned to the little house at the foot of the Niesen. Ever since, the fountain at Heustrich on the Niesenberg has been known as a healing spring." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
