They left the man they had struck from his horse lying there and fled. Here he gave up his spirit in the lap of a poor itinerant woman who had come from the little town of Brugg.", "His widow Elisabeth and his sister Agnes, widow of King Andrew of Hungary, first built a chapel with a little house at this place of disaster and entrusted both to two Waldbrüder for safekeeping. One was Brother Klaus of Bischofszell; the other was Berchtold Strebel of Oftringen. Strebel had been a daring knight and friend of King Rudolf but had become a Franciscan after the king’s death. While the two hermits prayed here for the emperor’s soul, his wife and sister carried out that terrible blood vengeance in which so many guilty and innocent people were slaughtered that for a long time afterward Aargau and Zurichgau stood full of deserted castles. Two years later the two women appeared again and laid the foundation stone for the great house of God and the two monasteries belonging to it at Königsfelden, whose ruins still stand today. The small cell of the Waldbrüder had to give way to this proud structure.", "Just as Agnes had the ground leveled to place the new Fronaltar upon it, Strebel approached the queen and prophesied that this new building would last only as long as the great hazel shrub growing beside his demolished cell remained alive. It was spared. The shrub lasted until the year 1520 and was called the Berchtoldenstaude. All the country people regarded it with reverent awe. It had been shown to the reformer H. Bullinger while he was still a child. (Tschudi 1, 252.) Then it visibly withered. Only four years later, the members of the order fled the monastery; four more years, and the reforming Bernese had made a manor farm from all the buildings of the dissolved abbey. ‘As the brother prophesied, so the much-mentioned foundation became zu nütti—came to nothing—in the year of Christ 1528.’ H. Bullinger, Chron. Tigur. I. lib. 7. cap. 9. Finally the French invaded the country and used the rooms as a military hospital. The rest decayed and burned; the last surviving wing is now an asylum.
