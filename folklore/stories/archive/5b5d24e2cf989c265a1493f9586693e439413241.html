Each of its branches is itself a mighty tree full of countless boughs. Before the storm broke its highest crown, its forest was much greater, as a local man expressed it. It stands some distance within the mountain plateau, yet it overtops the fir and deciduous woodland that has grown up before it, so that for miles around one can see the single tree looking out above it. On one side the trunk is split and decayed; nevertheless, from the burst bark it shoots new, fresh branches as thick as oaks. One can climb a few ells into the hollow. How astonishing, at such a height, to see new roots descending from the interior above—air roots that, themselves as thick as trunks, boldly seek the ground through the empty hollow. A light breath of wind through the treetop reminds the person lying beneath it of breaking water. People believe its shadow might reach as far as the distant Birrfeld, where stands that thorn of fate whose death is said to bring the country’s destruction. (See Thornbush on the Birrfeld.)
