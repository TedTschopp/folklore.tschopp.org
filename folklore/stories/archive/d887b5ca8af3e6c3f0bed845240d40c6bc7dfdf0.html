Bl. 1833, 227.)" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "On the right bank of the Rhine between Laufenburg and Bünzgen, in the rocky valley of the Antlesbach, hammer works are said to have stood until the Thirty Years' War, and ruins of walls and slag in the ground still speak for it. People say that much money from that time of destruction lies buried there. Around the time of Fronfasten, a man from Laufenburg fetched wood in this forest valley. In the heat of midday, he sat beneath an oak and rested. Suddenly, not far from him, he saw ordinary charcoal slowly rise from the earth and gradually grow into a heap the size of a basket. At the same instant, an enormous toad appeared sprawling upon the pile of charcoal and stared at him with fiery eyes. The man gazed fixedly at this apparition until, as unexpectedly as they had come, charcoal and toad sank into the earth. Everything was swept away. When he returned home, he related the event, and people explained that he should not have let so rare an opportunity to become rich pass unused. Without further ado, he should have taken possession of the charcoal and toad. It had been a heap of money, and the Evil One guarding it in the form of the toad could easily have been driven away with Besegnung and suitable spoken charms.
