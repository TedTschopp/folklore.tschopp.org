In his bitter isolation, he renounced the Catholic faith, although this had not truly been his intention. Just as unintentionally and without understanding it himself, he recklessly signed himself over to the Devil. The journeyman tailor lodging with him at the time told how this happened. One day, a distinguished gentleman dressed in black entered the man's room. Nothing about him was remarkable except one crooked foot. He introduced himself as the traveling representative of a merchants' association in Zofingen and Basel for which he was to gather subscribers in Catholic Switzerland. On condition that a person renounce the saints, hear no Mass, but distribute the little tracts sent by the association, every shareholder was guaranteed a corresponding degree of prosperity, while those in need were promised support. The traveler then brought out a book and read from it a long list of names, especially those of wealthy Zofingen families, all of whom had long been members of this extensive society. The poor farmer noticed that these were exclusively names of Reformed people and that not one Catholic was among them. Nevertheless, he thought that whatever such distinguished merchants found advantageous must also help a common man. Because there was no talk of any further contribution, he took the subscriber's book and wrote his name beside the others. In return, two different things immediately entered his house, which he had to use and treat according to exact instructions. The first was a collection of portraits of every member of the association. He had to hang them together in a room that was kept locked and check each day whether any individual portrait had changed. A picture whose colors faded indicated that the member was becoming unfaithful to the association. One that tore and developed holes announced the death of the member concerned, which would inevitably follow as punishment for disloyalty. The second thing to enter the farmer's house was a peculiar creature called the Geldschisser, which was likewise assigned its own dark corner of the home. Just as one always leaves a nest egg for laying hens, every evening he had to place a small coin beneath this animal. By morning, it grew into an entire heap of identical coins from which he could fill his pocket each day. He was permitted to take only a certain sum and never everything at once, for that would weaken the creature, and he would then have to die immediately after it.", "The man continued in this way for a long time, observed the contract he had made, and lived in prosperity. Later, however, he fell ill with a lingering and peculiar affliction. The remedies prescribed by the physicians were equally peculiar and costly. For example, every single day, he had to eat ten Pfund of Anken—an entire market tub—and drink a bottle of cod-liver oil with it. The tailor lodging with him at the time personally observed that these quantities were indeed brought into the house each day and regularly consumed by evening. Yet the sick man did not improve. Instead, he tormented himself with reproaches for having abandoned his old faith and sometimes spoke of how he would use the first day of his recovery to return to the village's Catholic church and have a Mass read. He never rose from his bed again, however, and finally decided to write to the association in Zofingen and ask it for a pill against his ailment. Meanwhile, people in Tägerig had already learned of his relationship with the Ständler in Zofingen, so they intercepted the reply from there while it was with the letter carrier. The Lädeligugger-Xaveri was informed in the letter that the requested pill would be sent to him at three o'clock the following Friday and would be as large as a bomb. The municipal councillors then tore up the letter and went to the patient's house at the appointed time on Friday. He lay in the back chamber and allowed no one to enter. They therefore waited in the sitting room until three o'clock. Suddenly, a muffled report sounded as though a cannon had been fired many hours away, followed at once by a short groan from the chamber. When they forced their way inside, he lay dead in bed. Not a drop of blood flowed, but his nose and mouth had been blasted apart, and his entire face was blackened with powder. At the same time, an enormous toad sat beneath the bedstead and remained there until the man was buried. It then marched out of the house in the company of an unknown black dog that suddenly appeared.
