His freight wagon had already stopped before the inn door, but he still had his shoulder braced against the rear wheel and was pushing with superhuman strength, as if he still had to keep driving the load onward. He was completely rigid and confused, so the stable hand pulled him out and brought him inside. When he had come to himself again in the warmth of the taproom, and the stable hand also appeared with a worried expression because a completely unfamiliar voice outside had just thundered “Unharness!”, Rudi had to report what had happened to him that day. He related that he had lingered too long in Zurzach that morning and had loaded too much. But instead of feeding the horses more, he had thought how very expensive oats were now and how the nags might pull uphill all the better if their bellies were not too full. On the road he had stopped once more, but had not had more than four Batzen worth of hay given to the horses there either. The stable hand there had indeed come several times and asked whether the horses should not be unharnessed for a while, but then it would have become far too late, and so he had let the matter rest. Soon after they continued, the horses no longer wanted to pull, and at the Degerbach they stopped completely. All his tugging on the reins, whipping, and shouting did no good. He put burning tinder beneath their tails and uttered one curse more terrible than the next. “Now I wish that our dear Lord God’s two most beautiful angels in heaven...” he shouted, and before he had finished speaking, someone came out from behind the tree, tore the whip from his hand, pressed him into the spokes of the rear wheel, and shouted: “Just you wait, you murderous carter; I will drive you!” Woven into the wheel at the back of the wagon, he then had to help push it uphill until he thought his heart would burst from his body; and if it did not move forward steadily at once, lashes came flying down as though his trousers and jacket were being cut apart with a razor.", "As Rudi told this, he trembled, and his teeth clattered like a stork’s beak.
