According to the legend, a distinguished and wealthy gentleman of Brig is also said once to have experienced it. His enemies sought to spread the claims that this gentleman had long supplied Valais with salt and amassed great sums from it; that he had drawn to himself all the Pensionen, Fried- und Gemeingelder of the territory and used them at will for his private interests; indeed, that he had secretly stockpiled arms in his house to subjugate the country. With these and other such suspicions, they tried to stir up the people. The people regarded this highly fortunate favorite of fate, benefactor of churches and monasteries, and father of the poor with ever more envious and suspicious eyes, until at last harsh persecution, incited only by envy and ill will, broke over him. The acts of violence that deprived him of most of his fortune and all his offices and possessions were so severe that he had to flee with his family to Domodossola.", "Among other things, he is said to have been ordered to pile all the money he possessed on a particular side altar at Glis and, holding his hands above it, swear that all his money was there. In his distress he secretly sought out a Jesuit father and asked how he might save part of his property without injuring his conscience. The priest gave him the clever advice to place half on the altar and bury the other half beneath it; then he could raise his hands over the whole and swear in good conscience. This pious stratagem happily saved him part of his fortune. It is known throughout the world that great services to one’s native land are often repaid with ingratitude.
