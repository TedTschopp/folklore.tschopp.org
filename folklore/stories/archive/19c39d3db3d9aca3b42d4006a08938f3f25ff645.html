At the very next court where he appeared, he liked the old king’s beautiful daughter so well that he asked for her hand. But she rejected him and said that she desired no king’s son and did not intend to marry for the time being. The rejected suitor took his leave but did not lose heart. He waited a year, then returned to the foreign royal court in peasant’s clothes and applied for a position as a stable hand. The king hired him, and he performed his duties with great loyalty for several years. The daughter often walked in the garden, where it happened that she frequently caught sight of the handsome stable hand. She spoke to him and, in time, grew very fond of him. Soon it was said that the stable hand was her betrothed, and the daughter also confessed to her father that she intended to marry him. Her father grimaced and threatened to drive her away and disinherit her if she persisted in her decision. The daughter was not intimidated and fixed the wedding day. After the wedding, her father cast her out, and because she possessed no fortune, she had to work. Her young husband was in good spirits and said, ‘If you are not afraid of work, let us open an inn!’ That suited her, and so she became an innkeeper. People gladly drank their Schöpplein with the beautiful young woman, and she did excellent business. Her husband then took leave for a few days, returned to his own country, put on his royal clothes, and appeared at the inn with a great retinue. The woman did not recognize him and had no time to look more closely at the distinguished visitors, for with her husband absent, she had enough to do serving them. The guests feasted all day, then smashed the glasses and bottles to pieces and made themselves scarce without asking for the bill.", "When her husband returned through the door in his townsman’s clothes, he found his wife sitting sadly at the table. Her husband pretended to be sad and said that matters could not continue in this way; they would have to begin something else.—‘Tomorrow is market day, so I shall buy you a heap of crockery, and you can begin dealing in dishes!’ The woman agreed, dried her tears, and took her place behind the crockery stall the next day. Business was brisk, and she took in a handsome sum of money. Her husband had gone out to earn money, as he had told her that morning, and she looked forward to showing him the fine proceeds that evening. Suddenly, a wagon drawn by two horses came up and headed straight for the crockery. The woman cried a warning: ‘Careful, careful, you drivers! That evening, when her husband came home, she told him with a careworn expression how much money she had earned and how badly the wagon had ravaged her stall.
