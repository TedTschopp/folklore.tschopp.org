A poor Hausvater had been accused of arson and condemned to death, although even under torture he steadfastly denied the crime. Still at the execution site, where the Bürgerspital now stands, he loudly affirmed his innocence and declared that he would nevertheless endure this shameful death steadfastly if only the judges could give him the consolation that his orphaned children would be cared for. ‘Only promise,’ he cried, ‘to maintain them for as many years as I shall still take steps after my head has been cut off.’ They promised him publicly, and composed, he knelt for death. Scarcely had the blow fallen when the torso rose on the execution ground and ran nearly three hundred paces to this place at the Anna Chapel; it would probably have gone farther had someone in the crowd not knocked it down there from horror and pity. At the place where it fell, that cross was erected.
