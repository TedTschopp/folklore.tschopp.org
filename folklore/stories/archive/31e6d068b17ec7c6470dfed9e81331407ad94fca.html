It called plaintively from the Juchliberg. The dogs sprang up restlessly, opened the doors themselves, and fled outside into the open. Believing that a traveler was calling for help, the servant likewise hurried before the door. Outside the sun shone bright and friendly, but around the Juchliberg something hovered in the air that he could not see clearly. No traveler appeared near or far. He called the dogs, which were wandering aimlessly, and returned with them to his work in the room. Then the sound rang out a second time. Again he sought the supposed traveler who had called for his help, and again in vain; but a reddish glow shimmered around the Juchliberg. He returned to the house. Only when the sky above him seemed to collapse with a terrible roar did he recognize—too late—who had called him. The roof rafters snapped like straws, and immeasurable masses of earth rolled before the door so that every exit seemed blocked. Only the strength of the walls had preserved the house from complete destruction and him from instant death. Yet in fleeing, the Hauri had flipped open the chimney cover and thus opened a route of escape for him. He climbed through the chimney into daylight while the Kobolde, believing they had buried him, celebrated their triumph on the Aare Glacier. He pulled the dogs after him and carried the tale into the valley.
