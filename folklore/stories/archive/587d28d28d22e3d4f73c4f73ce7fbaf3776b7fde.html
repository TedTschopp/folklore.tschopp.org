Its decaying trunk traced a fairly straight line along the ground that may have indicated its former height exactly. The remains of its branches lay beside it, bleached white-gray. This tree was called “Annelis Grötzli” for the following reasons:", "An old woman named Anna had died—whether at this spot or somewhere else is not said—and because of a crime committed during her lifetime, she had to wander here until someone released her. She appeared in black clothing and a white-gray cap, walked in a stooped posture, was not infrequently seen by herders, and nodded toward them when she saw that they had noticed her. Yet the hour of release came for the poor woman. One day, a farmhand told the Senn that he would no longer drive the cattle there to graze because the Anneli had appeared to him again and raised a threatening finger. In annoyance, the Senn seized his staff, hurried there, and immediately found the Anneli in the form of a half-rotted tree stump as tall as a man, adorned on one side by a gray-white fungus. The Senn tore it off and, after returning to the hut, handed it to the farmhand with the words: “Now I have released the Anneli. In gratitude, she gave me her cap, and I now give it to you as a remembrance of the heroism you displayed today in this tale of terror, which has now reached its happy conclusion.”\nO.
