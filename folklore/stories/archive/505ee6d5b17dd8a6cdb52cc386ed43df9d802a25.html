Its waters are not said always to have washed the foot of the Albis. People once walked there on dry paths through a lovely valley between grassy meadows and fertile fields. This valley belonged to the barons of Schnabelburg, whose castle stood high above on a summit of the Albis, and its fields and meadows were managed and farmed by a tenant of these nobles, whom an old chronicle calls counts. According to the legend, one such tenant sold his daughter, whose beauty had attracted the castle lord’s attention, in exchange for possession of those fields and meadows. He personally brought the daughter, who thought herself safe in her father’s company, to the count at the castle, where his shamefully acquired property was guaranteed to him in a written deed. But scarcely had he returned to his new estate with this document in his hands when a dreadful hurricane arose. Mountains burst apart and filled the approaches to the valley, which torrents from heaven then transformed into the lake it is today, forever erasing the unnatural father and his new property from the earth.
