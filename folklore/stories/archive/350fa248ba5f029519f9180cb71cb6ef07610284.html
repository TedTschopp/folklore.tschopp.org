Every year that God gave, a gentleman had to hire a servant for the cattle on the alp, for until then the Alpine servant had always died mysteriously in the hut on the evening before the homeward drive. At last a sturdy Tyrolean applied for the disreputable and shunned position. He knew the danger and did not fear it. Cheerfully he went up to the alp with the dear cattle and passed the summer there undisturbed. The day for departure approached, and with it the fateful preceding evening. The Senn gathered Holderknebel, filled a large Kessi with them, and boiled them in it. Nor did he lie down to rest; he remained awake and close to the sticks. At midnight many cats instantly entered from every side, leaving the servant almost no room. Among these wicked animals he saw a very large one, the queen of all the cats. She stood directly beneath the roof and immediately threatened to leap into the Tyrolean’s face. He was quick to act: he snatched a Holderknebel from the Kessi and hurled it so skillfully at the great cat’s paw that the paw fell off. Suddenly all the cats scattered. When he picked up the severed object, he saw to his horror that it was a hand, and one of its fingers bore a gold ring. He recognized his mistress’s ring and hand, wrapped it securely, and carried it home. He had scarcely arrived there, to his master’s astonishment, when he was told that the mistress lay ill in bed and longed urgently to see him. He went in to her, and the first words she spoke were: ‘I will give you as much money as you want for what you have in that little cloth.’ But the servant would not surrender the hand at any price.
