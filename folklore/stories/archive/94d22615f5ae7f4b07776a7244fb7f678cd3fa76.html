Cattle were allowed to roam freely over the heights throughout the summer, and a few herdboys to watch them and one Sennknecht to milk, churn butter, and make cheese sufficed for an entire herd of twenty to thirty head. Meanwhile, the landowning farmer remained below in the valley. When the time came to settle with the Senn over the yield of butter and cheese, or when young calves were to be brought down for sale, he too left his village for once and visited what was known as his mountain holding. This was still the practice in the Fricktal at the beginning of this century. If the farmer climbed the mountain before daybreak and left home without breakfast so as not to lose any time, chance might very well have it that, on reaching his alpine dairy hut, he found it completely empty: neither the hoped-for bowl of milk nor the cows or cowherd outside could be found. What else could he then do but make the same hard journey home again, hungry and thirsty?", "Yet helpful beings still lived in the mountains, and an honest countryman who treated his people fairly could always rely on them in such a plight: these were the Heidenweibchen. They already helped the herdboys keep the wolf away, tend the cattle, search for a stray animal, and lure it in for milking. They also made provisions in advance for an honorable master when they saw him coming up the mountain on the appointed days. At the last pasture gate, where the upward path was usually at its steepest, they laid out Wähen and loaves of bread that still smelled of the oven, resting them neatly either upon the leafy hedge or even on a snow-white Zwehel. Farther up on the meadow, they had lit shepherds’ fires for him, and the potatoes they placed in them never finished roasting a moment before the expected traveler passed by. The favorite abode of these kindly mountain maidens was the Martisbrunn. Its location is as follows. The Homberg and the Horn are two long mountains near the village of Wittnau, and the narrow Fahrental rises between them. Farther up, it becomes a narrow gorge with room only for the cart road down which hay is dragged or carted and for the fairly strong brook flowing beside it into the valley. More than twenty smaller brooks plunge at the same time, loudly roaring, from the steep slopes of the Homberg into this narrows. One of them is the Martisbrunn, and it was beside this stream that the Heidenweibchen lived. Yet they were not confined to this mountain solitude alone. They also came farther down into the Wittnau valley and brought the farmers sweet, tenderly baked Wähen at the plow.
