R. Suter v. Seon.)" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "In the previous century, a Junker lived in the old mill at Seon. He made himself greatly hated by the people of the surrounding area because he forced them to have all their grain ground at his mill. He had a dog he loved above all else, and when the animal died, he had a coffin made for it and a grave dug in his garden. The following day, as noon rang from the church tower, the Junker put on the mourning cloak worn at the burial of blood relatives and walked behind the coffin, which two servants carried on a bier. A weeping willow was planted on the grave, and for a long time afterward the Junker and his household could still be seen wearing mourning clothes. Since his own death, his spirit has been unable to find rest. At certain times he is seen in mourning clothes walking behind his servants as far as the dog’s grave and then returning to the mill. When people dug at that supposed spot in the miller’s meadowland several years ago, they actually came upon a grave containing a very large and fairly well-preserved human skeleton.", "(Seminarian J.
