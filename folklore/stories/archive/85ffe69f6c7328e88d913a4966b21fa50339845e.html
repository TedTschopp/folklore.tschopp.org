For centuries, folk belief had spoken of the rich gold treasures in this cavern, and gold diggers from Venice were said often to have gained immense wealth there. Once, such a Venediger came to the alpine hut at Arni and demanded that the Älpler provide him with a companion for the descent into the Arni-Loch. They steadfastly refused until a young Handbube stepped forward and boldly declared that he would go along. The Venediger took him, lit two wax candles before the cave entrance, and forbade the youth, on pain of death, to speak a word aloud no matter what he might see. He then took out a thick, large book and began reading all manner of things from it in a foreign language. Suddenly, a terrible cracking and thundering arose in the depths, and the boy began to be afraid. The Italian continued reading, but the subterranean din grew ever louder until a sudden cry of terror escaped the boy. A fearful thunderclap followed at once; the lad was hurled from the cave and tumbled in dreadful bounds over rocks and trees into the depths. The Älpler waiting at the cave entrance had seen him and heard the thunder. They hurried after him and carried him back to the hut for dead. The boy recovered, however, and was able to tell of the Italian's prohibition and of his own cry, but nothing more. For the rest of his life, he remained what the source calls a Noggel.
