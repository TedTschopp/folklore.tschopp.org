He received permission to use the ownerless stones lying around the ruins of Ehrenfels Castle. He went up and moved a large stone so that he could roll it onto the path; this stone had sealed an opening leading into the castle. He climbed through and reached a spacious cellar. There he found a large wooden trough built into the masonry, in which many flattened hazelnuts could be seen, all of them pierced through. He examined the hazelnuts and, because he had seven children at home, took several for them. On arriving home, he reached for the hazelnuts to give them to the little ones, but instead of nuts he found gold coins in his pocket. Overjoyed, he called another man and shared the secret with him. In great haste they equipped themselves with vessels and set out for Ehrenfels. The “Haselnussmann” climbed into the opening first and the other man followed; then both slipped into the cellar and were just about to lay hands on the chest when it fell apart and all the hazelnuts rattled onto the ground. They began picking them up, but the whole lot vanished into the earth before them. They could still hear the nuts first striking the walls of an underground cellar and then, transformed into gold, landing below with a loud ring. Had the man come alone to fetch the hazelnuts, without the other, the treasure would have remained his." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Haselnussmann”: literally the ‘hazelnut man,’ the story's temporary nickname for the poor discoverer after the enchanted nuts first become gold in his pocket." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
