She does the same in the ravine of the stream north of Meggen, in the ravines of the Dorf- and Sekibach at Walchwil, and in the Schwarzenbach at Allenwinden. At the last of these places, people heard a dreadful howl made up of many different animal voices, as though pigs, dogs, and cats were with the host or were chasing one another. Two men saw the thing in the form of a great black bundle of hay moving downhill. Others claim to have seen the specter as a large sow with nine to twelve grunting piglets making its way down the Schwarzenbach. The being appears in the guise of a Mohre—a sow—on the Steinerbach at Steinen near Schwyz. Its appearance is usually followed by bad weather. Near Altdorf in the canton of Uri, the Grosskellerin rushes through the Birebäumlital at night and rages down the nearby Sisigenbach from the mountain and into Lake Lucerne. People even tell of her high up in the mountain village of Gurtnellen, away from the Gotthard road. She, the old woman, is followed by “young ghosts,” whom she lures with the cry “Su, su!” Thus it is once again in the shape of a pig that people imagine her. Always at the head of the procession, the old woman took the route over the Gurtnellerberg into the Eulensaul ravine, from there to the Jnschitobel, and then through the Schwaudental to the summit of the Nonnenstock. Whenever she traveled, the weather became very bad. Once, on this journey, a Nachtbub met her. He had a stout stick fitted with a spike at its lower end and was brazen enough to throw it after the specter. When he looked for his stick the following morning, he could not find it for a long time. At last he discovered it in the Eulentobel, driven into the very top of a tall linden tree. On another occasion, she raced past a Gaden and a boy caught sight of her. He told his father, who ordered him to come inside at once. But the little boy lingered in the doorway so that one of his legs still stuck out into the open.
