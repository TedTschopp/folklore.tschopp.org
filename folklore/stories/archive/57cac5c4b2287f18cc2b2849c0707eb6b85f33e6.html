Once, as he walked sadly and pensively through a forest, he met a strange little man who spoke kindly to him and asked the reason for his dejection. He lamented his distress and was advised to seek a particular place, which the little man described to him precisely. There he would find a green frog—or, according to others, a toad. He was to wrap it in a little rag, take it home, and place a coin beneath it; on the next day, he would be able to remove twice as much. He was to continue in this way until he was rich enough. He followed the advice, placing one franc beneath it on the first day, two on the second, and four francs on the third, and continued until he had amassed a handsome fortune. Then he tried to rid himself of the animal. He took it to the market and secretly slipped it into someone else’s sack, but when he came home, it was back in his own sack. Now he grew hot and frightened. He sought refuge with a Capuchin friar, who rebuked him sharply for his wrongdoing and said it was fortunate that he had not died in this state, or he would have been lost forever. “But what can be done? Good advice is dear!” The father pondered for a long time and seemed unable to find a way out. At last he uttered a redeeming word and lifted the stone from the little farmer’s heart: “Bring the Alraune here to the gate tomorrow evening, wrapped tightly in a little rag. I will stand ready and open the gate for you. Throw it inside as quick as lightning, close the door, and make off! But I fear it will be difficult.”", "Evening was still nearer to day than to night when the frightened little man rang at the monastery gate.
