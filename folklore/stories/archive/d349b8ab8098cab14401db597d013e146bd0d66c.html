His wicked wife was now single again at home and could look for another suitor, but none appeared because everyone knew her malice. She attributed this, however, to the fact that she had two girls from her first marriage. She therefore resolved to rid herself of them. One winter, when it was bitterly cold, she took them unnoticed by the neighbors and led them far from her home deep into the forest, where, despite their trembling and weeping, she left them without mercy. Having returned home, only on the next day did she ask with feigned great concern whether anyone had seen her children. Although the good-hearted neighbors did everything possible to find the children again, they were not found and must have died miserably in the forest from hunger and frost. Only a long time afterward did a woodcutter find the two poor girls locked tightly within a covering of snow and ice. Their mother, whose wicked deed was suspected, still found no husband. When winter came again, she went mad, ran into the forest where she had abandoned her children, called their names in terrible tones, and tore at the ice and snow with bloody fingers to search for them. She continued until she died. Yet she is still seen wandering through the woods and ravines of that region on dark, cold nights. When the time comes for deep snow to cover the fields and poor people's children go into the forest to gather Reisig for a warm room, they often hear very near them now a moan, now a groan, now a pitiful shriek, and with the cry ‘the Nachthuri is coming!’ they hurry home in fright." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Nachthuri”: the Lucerne name of the raging nocturnal female apparition created by the murderous mother's madness and death; Nacht means ‘night,’ while the regional Huri element suggests a frenzied, rushing, or shrewish woman but is not precisely defined here.", "Swiss term “Hundsrücken”: literally ‘dog's back,’ the local ridge running from the Rotsee toward Buchrain where the woman's story is placed.", "Swiss term “Reisig”: brushwood, twigs, and small branches gathered as household fuel; the poor children collecting it echo the vulnerability of the abandoned daughters." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
