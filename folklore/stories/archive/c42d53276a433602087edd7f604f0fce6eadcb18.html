There lies the snow-covered Alp, blessed in ancient times and rich in succulent herbs. A small piece of this Alp belonged to a poor widow, the remaining part to a rich and violent man who envied the widow her share and contested it. He claimed to have become owner of her portion through a transaction with the widow's late husband. The widow sought protection and help before the court. Her complaints and tears did not move the miser's hard heart. He swore before the court that the matter stood as he said, and the widow's property was awarded to him. Then the widow called to him before the judges: ‘If the Alp belongs to you, I wish that it may serve you well; but if it does not belong to you and you have sworn falsely, I wish that heaven may cover it with snow and that it may never abere and grow green again.’ The rich man laughed scornfully while the poor widow burst into tears. The judges sat silent, and scarcely had the widow spoken her words when the storm wind was heard roaring and rain struck the round windowpanes of the courtroom. Thick snowflakes fell in the mountains, proving that the rich man had sworn falsely, for in three days and nights the Alp was buried in snow and has never since become free of it.
