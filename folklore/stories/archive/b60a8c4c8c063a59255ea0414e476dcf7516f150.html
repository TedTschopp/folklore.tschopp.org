It belongs to the parish of Grindelwald but is closed off from the valley by two rocky projections that extend almost into the Lütschine. A folk legend from this region relates:", "Where Burglauenen now stands, in olden times there was a village named Schillingsdorf inhabited by wicked people. One evening, in stormy, rainy weather, a dwarf went through the village and asked for lodging. He was always turned away until, at last, he was compassionately taken in at a house where only poor people lived and a child was expected to be born, and he was cared for as well as possible. The dwarf announced to the people of this house that Schillingsdorf would perish that very night and that only their one house, as the worthiest, should remain. At midnight, a terrible cracking began. A section of the Burg that had already split away earlier tore itself loose, formed an enormous debris avalanche, and rolled toward the village amid dreadful thunder. The village was almost entirely buried and shattered, so that few lives escaped apart from those who kept the dwarf overnight. This house is said to have been saved because a boulder as large as a house, which had run ahead of the debris, lodged firmly directly behind the house and held back the debris or deflected it to the sides. Clear traces of such a rockslide can still be seen. At the Burg, one can see that a section of its entire height and width, several Klafter thick, has been torn away. Below, one can perceive the flows of debris. Great rocks lie scattered as if sown, some projecting from the earth and some lying on the surface. Everything is now covered with plants once more. The rock said to have saved that house also still appears to stand as a witness, and a house standing there is still powerfully sheltered by the rock." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Klafter”: an old fathom-like measure of length whose regional value varied; the torn rock face is described as several Klafter thick rather than given a modern metric measurement." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
