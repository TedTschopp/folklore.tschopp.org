When he reached the millstream in the area called the Kessel, he saw large icicles glittering on the rock in the sun, as clear as those that hung from his thatched roof only in the middle of winter. The great crystal-clear ice astonished him considerably. He broke off a piece, and when it did not melt in his hand, he put it away and took it home because it was such a rarity. When he tried to show it to his people, who had just returned home from the field, they were all greatly amazed, for instead of the piece of ice he now placed an equally large icicle of pure silver on the dining table. They truly believed him only after he told them the simple course of events.", "The same thing happened on the Homberg between Reinach and Lake Hallwil. There in the high forest, a poor weaver found a deep pit in the earth of which no one had ever heard before. He climbed down and entered such a neatly excavated underground passage that one might believe a mine had once been there. At the far end, long rods stretched like a grating against both walls and barred the way through. But when the man struck them, they rang as clear as bells, and all sorts of small pieces broke off and gleamed like gold on the ground. He carefully gathered up these fragments and then quickly made his escape, for everything he had ever heard about the Erdmännchen suddenly came to mind, and great fear overcame him. Nevertheless, he later returned to fetch more of the gold.
