They chose the Gornerweiden in the neighboring Kiental at the foot of the Blümlisalp as the site. The masonry had already begun rising from the ground when hostile powers made themselves known. What the stouthearted men built by day, the Evil One destroyed at night. Neither prayer nor struggle helped. People offered one piece of advice and then another but could find no way out. At last, the builders agreed to let a yoke of oxen wander freely. Wherever the animals lay down, the church would stand. The oxen ran out of the valley and stopped in the alder thicket beside the Reichenbach. The people rejoiced and praised the miracle. The little church was built where it should least of all have stood: in a hollow, with the noisy country road beside and above it and the rushing Reichenbach likewise beside it on higher ground. The stream has often covered the church and those sleeping around it in silent graves with a substantial layer of debris. The folly soon became apparent. Barely half a century later, a debris avalanche came down the valley and buried the lower part of the house of God. But the people knew how to help themselves: each time, they raised the church roof, and whatever portion of wall had been covered by the debris they rebuilt above it.
