Its houses consist largely of the curtain walls and remains of Rore Castle, which, according to documents, stood here as a sanctuary until the year 1337. This part is called the Halde. In earlier times, a monster blocked people's way here at night. Its head is said to have filled the entire town gate. It had eyes like the round lantern panes of a coach, and its tail reached from the town gate by the house called the Adler all the way down the lane. Horses coming along this road at the same time could not be moved from the spot. It exercised an especially strict police over gluttons who left the taverns only at midnight. Now, like its companion the Hokenmann, it serves only to drive forgetful children from the street into the house in good time when the evening bell rings. People believe it dwells particularly in the rocks upon which the town church stands. A rich man had his treasures buried in this rock by a poor day laborer. The latter did not resist temptation for long, later dug up a large part of it, and killed himself when his conscience tormented him. At times, it was a dog, at times a calf or dragon, but its eyes were always the size of meat platters or plow wheels and sprayed sparks like freshly glowing iron beneath a smith's hammer.
