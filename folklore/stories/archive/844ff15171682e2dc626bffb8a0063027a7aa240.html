Its heights are said to have been far more fertile in former times than they are now. The cows, which were milked three times a day, fed especially upon an herb called Zyprion, as well as Muttern, Ritz, and Milcherkraut. Places that are now desolate were formerly clothed in the most magnificent green. Merry dairywomen lived upon these green pastures. But one day, an evil spirit awoke among them. At midnight, they went to the Hexenboden, where black grass still grows today. There they assembled and held their nightly dances. Once, they were surprised by a hunter who had followed a chamois there. The youngest dairywoman approached him and smilingly offered him her hand. Blinded by her beauty, he let her detain him for a long while. When dawn came, all the women hurried down into the valley to milk the cows. But the dairywoman remained seated beside her hunter, from whom she could not part. Then one of her sisters called to her, “Come, the cows are already waiting down below!” “Oh,” she replied, “milking forever! I wish, I wish that the herbs had long since withered.” Thus she cursed the Zyprion, Muttern, and Ritz. When the hunter heard this, he leaped up and cried, “May God preserve Muttern and Ritz for me!” He had forgotten the Zyprion. After that, all the pastures faded. Even now, the Zyprion can be seen as a pale herb, withered as though by winter frost.
