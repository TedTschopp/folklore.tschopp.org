During a merry drinking bout, they entered into an agreement with each other that whichever one died before the other, the survivor should deliver a bottle of wine to the dead man at a certain place not far from the Rapperswil bridge, above the little village of Hurden. Thus, after one had died and the other, still living, remembered his vow, he appeared at the appointed hour and place with the bottle of wine. There the dead man, or someone else — God knows who — asked whether he was present and had the bottle of wine. With a deeply frightened heart, he answered yes. The voice demanded that he hand over the bottle and consider himself fortunate, for if he had not fulfilled this duty, he would have been torn to pieces. Related to this is what, on December 15, 1609, here in the city of Lucerne, a learned and highly experienced gentleman and leader of his homeland among the 4 Forest Cantons, namely Johann Conrad von Beroldingen of Uri, truthfully related to us: that an almost identical event and promise had taken place around 1490 between his ancestor, or his great-grandfather’s father, Andreas von Beroldingen, Landammann of Uri, and one of that man’s good companions, except that one was to bring the vessel of wine and the other bread. When the latter died before him and the survivor, mindful of the vow, came to the appointed place with the vessel of wine, the dead man’s soul or spirit appeared there with the bread. It did not allow itself to be seen, but only its voice to be heard. The living man then asked how matters stood with the dead man’s soul, whether he was saved or damned. He was answered that he was not permitted to know this, for the conclusive judgment had not yet been pronounced, since the dead man still had to fulfill or discharge the promised vow made during life as a matter of justice. Otherwise, matters stood very perilously between him and the judgment of God, and all people ought to be warned not to indulge in such presumption but to contemplate their last things more carefully. The man who was then still alive took this business closely to heart and also became highly renowned in his homeland. Wherever people requested his stained-glass window and coat of arms for their houses, he did provide them, but as a memorial and reminder of the event, he always had this story painted and fired into the glass above the vessel." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Landammann”: the traditional chief magistrate or presiding official of a Swiss canton or rural polity; ‘governor’ is only approximate and misses the office’s specific confederal and cantonal setting." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
