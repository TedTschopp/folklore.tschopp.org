It was older than the neighboring settlements and already had its own little church before the large village of Erlinsbach possessed one. Until a few decades ago, its ruined walls still stood above in the wooded ravine beside the Ramsfluh at the healing spring where the Lorenzobad inn has now been built. At that time, the old foundation walls could not be dismantled and had to be blasted with powder. The principal load-bearing stones and cornices had already been taken away to be built into the village chapel of Upper Erlinsbach. The present inn beside the spring occupies the site of the old burial ground. When the soil was turned during new construction, so many skeletons appeared at once that they had to be carted down to the Catholic churchyard in the village. A silver ring was found upon one skeleton and preserved. More surprising was an entire layer of horse bones discovered at the same place, lying in good order outside around the walls and partly exposed by the forest stream flowing past. The people regarded them as remains of pagan worship, for it is known that a hermit lived at this spot and baptized the surrounding inhabitants as Christians with water from the Lorenzo spring. Before he came, a female being guarded the spring. She is still generally known simply as die Frau. In recent years, the Bannwart who lives above in the little mountain village of Hard suddenly encountered her once in the fir forest and was just able to step out of her way. She is not feared, but people avoid her with reverent awe, for she alone remained when everything—the village of Wil and its church—was buried in an earthquake. Even the holy spring, which formerly flowed hot, has been only lukewarm since then, because it now emerges from the mountain mingled with other inner streams.
