For a time they were given food and drink, but it was made clear that they were a burden upon the household. The Lord of La Sarraz now resolved, with the support of his merciless wife, to cast out his father and mother. Poorly clothed and with empty stomachs, they were therefore led before the castle gate, which was immediately barred. While they wandered at night over rough paths, grieving and weeping, their hardhearted son sat before the flickering hearth fire at the laid table to consume his evening meal. A game pie, which he greatly loved, and a Maß of strong, foaming beer were placed before him. Without remorse for his deed, he rocked in a softly padded armchair. But scarcely had he removed the thick crust forming the pie's lid when he recoiled with a terrible scream. His wife looked at him in horror and called for help. Two loathsome toads had leaped from the pie and sunk their claws firmly into the warrior's cheeks; they seemed to have been sent by a higher power. After the young woman had with difficulty overcome the disgust inspired by the hideous animals, she exerted all her strength to tear them from the places they had bitten.
