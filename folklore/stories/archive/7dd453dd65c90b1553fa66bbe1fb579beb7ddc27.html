When they carried her to the church for burial, Hansi entrusted his child, a girl not yet seven years old, to the care of the wife of his neighbor Peter Walker. There the girl suddenly became very sleepy, and the woman laid her, still in her clothes, upon the bed in the Stübli, where she fell asleep at once. Peter had gone out and was standing on the village square when the funeral procession passed. Behind the bier walked Nuschi-Hansi, leading the girl by her right hand. When Peter came home, he told his wife, “Nuschi-Hansi has apparently taken the girl along to church too.” “Oh no,” the woman replied, “she is lying there in the Stübli.” Peter acted as though he had not heard correctly. After a while he said again, “Nuschi-Hansi has taken his girl along to church with him too,” and received the same answer from his wife. At the third repetition, he did begin to wonder. He went into the Stübli, looked, and indeed found the child asleep upon the bed in the very same little dress she had worn at the funeral procession. She slept until her father fetched her.
