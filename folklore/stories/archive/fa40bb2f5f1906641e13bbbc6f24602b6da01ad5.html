The beautiful forest was the ancient property of the people of Linn, but their documents proving it had long since been lost or rotted in the municipal chest. Now the people of Thalheim, who lived beyond the mountain, had likewise set their sights on this property. Just as they are still called Hegel und Wetzer, they began cutting wood there without asking and trespassed so brazenly that the people of Linn brought a complaint against them before the bailiff at Thalheim. But then it was simply declared that the forest belonged to Thalheim. The people of Linn therefore let the matter go to a Landtag, where the bailiff was to decide. Both communities assembled in the middle of the forest on the beautiful meadow; those who found no room below climbed the trees, whose branches teemed and swayed with spectators. Suddenly, all grew quiet. A path opened through the crowd, and the provincial bailiff, high on horseback with a feathered hat on his head, rode through toward the platform built for him inside the circle. He dismounted but did not step onto the platform, so that the oath he was about to utter could not be misinterpreted. He raised his hand and extended three fingers. Everyone removed his hat, while he alone kept his head covered and said, ‘Citizens of Linn and Thalheim, hear me! As surely as I have my Creator and Judge above me, so surely do I stand here upon land and soil belonging to me and to Thalheim!’ All the people of Linn immediately left the place of judgment in the greatest indignation. But already the next morning came news that the perjured bailiff had been struck dead by lightning while riding home. When his followers tried to aid him, they found in the cuffs of his boots earth that, according to the wording of his oath, he had filled into them in the Thalheim castle garden. Beneath his feathered hat were a comb and a serving ladle, implements respectively called Richter and Schöpfer—‘Judge’ and ‘Creator.’ Ever since, the pleasant forest has been haunted. Ever since, during the yearly period of the Landtag, he rides through Gättibuch. His horse and the little dog running beside him are both snow-white, and he ceaselessly utters his false oath in broken words.
