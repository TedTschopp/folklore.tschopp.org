The following event gave rise to this privilege.", "In ancient times, a veritable army of snakes inhabited the Alp of Les grosses Combes, which lies within the jurisdiction of Altenryf monastery. The vermin bit people and livestock and caused great harm. At every step one took, snakes hissed at one. They forced their way into the Staffel, drank cream and milk, stole bread, cheese, and Ziger, and wound themselves around the necks of the bellowing cows, which then gave blood-red milk. The cows scarcely dared go there any longer. The distressed Älpler now went to Altenryf to the venerable Abbot Hugo, who had a reputation for holiness, and told him of their suffering. The following summer, as he had promised, the monk came to Les grosses Combes. Undaunted, he stepped into the midst of the army of snakes, which was not permitted to touch him but reared high, surrounded him, and hissed at him with foaming jaws. The sky darkened, and a terrible storm drew near. Thunder and lightning continued without cease, the earth rumbled, and nut-sized Schlossen mixed with human hair fell.", "The Sennen watched from afar with dread and horror. Calmly and gravely, the man of God said his prayers, sprinkled the venomous snakes with holy water, adjured them, stretched out his hand in command, and banished each and every one of them to the deep bottom of the nearby lake. In their fury, the snakes spat out venom and fire. Scarcely had Hugo spoken the final, decisive words of adjuration when the snakes clumped themselves together into balls and rolled with a terrible din down the steep mountain slopes, over stick and stone, into the depths of the nearby lake.
