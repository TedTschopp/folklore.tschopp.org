Behind Mathis Jörgi's house at Nebikon runs a Kilchweg on which something spectral is ordinarily heard every night. Once he had been drinking in that house with good companions. In the meantime he had to go outside, and the thought came to him that he would like to see what sort of specter was there. Then Jörgi heard a noise approaching, saw something, and wished to speak to it. The specter immediately rushed at him and carried him up and away, so that he no longer knew anything of himself or his surroundings. His companions noticed, ran after them, caught him, and led him home, where he lay for three days before coming to his senses again. He then made confession and promised a pilgrimage to Sankt Jakob. He even had to pay a penalty to the authorities because the Devil had tried to take him. Sixteen men actually set out on the pilgrimage. In Toulouse, however, they could go no farther because of Condé's troops and had to turn back. Later, during the fair, Jörgi went to Willisau for the Schiesset. After he had eaten supper there at the Kronen, several men provoked him; he resisted and said, ‘I take no pleasure in singing unless I have been to Sankt Jakob and completed my journey.’ In the end he sang anyway. But a man from Bern was present who did not like it, and so they became quarrelsome. When the innkeeper also attacked him, saying he was a Hudler and should leave his guests in peace, Jörgi flared up violently: ‘I am no Hudler; I am as good as you.’ Then Hans Marti, the innkeeper, replied that he was not, because the Devil had once taken him. Now the fury truly broke loose, and several gross oaths escaped Jörgi, though he scarcely knew how. He thought a great injustice was being done to him, for he had not entered the specter's power willingly and had paid dearly for it. But things went still worse for him. Accused because of his curses, he was fined one hundred crowns in Willisau. His little property would scarcely have yielded so much. He therefore appealed to the gracious lords and superiors in Lucerne, related and presented all of this to them, and humbly begged for mercy.
