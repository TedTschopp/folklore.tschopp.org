They confirmed themselves in this belief through gold-glittering clay washed out of the mountain by a spring, but still more through the stories, alive even now, of dwarf activity associated with these forested mountains and of the golden gifts that some people were said to have carried down into the village.", "Thus they resolved to excavate into the interior of the tremendously broad Strichen. Halfway up its slope, near the road that leads over the Benken toward Aarau, they began an exploratory digging. Even today, a large ring-shaped pit can be seen there in the meadow beside the left shoulder of the road, lying within its own spoil bank and already covered with grass once more. A Bergknappe was soon found who explained the mountain's inner structure to the people and told them how they should accordingly drive and extend the gallery. Because this man's predictions continued to prove entirely accurate, the participants submitted to his direction and drove the gallery onward underground for perhaps a quarter of an hour's distance. The farther they advanced, the more exactly every assertion of the strange Bergknappe proved correct. Nevertheless, the enterprise soon came to a halt because, as the men explained, no gold whatsoever could be found. From their account, however, their fear of the Erdmännchen dwelling in the mountain emerges so clearly that it was more likely these beings that induced the men to stop work. They described their experiences as follows.", "At the beginning, the diggers encountered a cone of rock rising powerfully inside the mountain and threatening to block their direction. As they tried to go around it, they found that it rose separately in a circular form like a freestanding tower, so they could leave it standing beside their route. But it displayed a peculiarly black opening at its base, shaped like the mouth of an oven, and this tempted one of the farmers to climb inside. He found the interior much like a castle kitchen. From above, however, a single rock hung down, moving like the clapper in a bell and threatening at every moment to crush the daring man beneath it. Farther inside, the digging men came upon a chasm that, once again to their good fortune, extended only to the side and did not obstruct them. It was so astonishingly deep that from then on they emptied into it all the rubble from the gallery, which they had previously been forced to cart out of the mountain with much labor and loss of time. At night, as they lay together in their chamber, they saw a thin flame burning for a long time upon their tool chest.
