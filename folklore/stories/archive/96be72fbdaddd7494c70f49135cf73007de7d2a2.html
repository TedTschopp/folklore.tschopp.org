Soon afterward God ordained that a devout Catholic glass carrier from Escholzmatt should pass by Trub. Suddenly he saw something gleaming behind a hedge. When he looked, it was the rejected image. At once he set down his glassware and reverently took up the image, while keeping careful watch that no unauthorized person was spying on him. All went well. He brought the image home, where the Reverend pastor devoutly placed it on the altar of the Mother of God in the church that very evening, intending to seek a suitable place for it the following day. In the morning, when the Sigrist entered the church at the Aveläuten, the image was nowhere to be seen. He reported this to the clergyman, and while people searched throughout the village for the lost image, a shoemaker came down from the Schwändelberg to report that, strangely, a beautiful St. Anne was standing upon a tree at a certain place. They climbed up and discovered to their astonishment that the image had indeed journeyed in the night from the church to this distant spot. They took it back down, assigned it its place in the church once more, and two men had to watch beside it at night so that it would not go away again. But behold, in the morning it was gone once more, and they could not say how. It had again chosen its former favorite place. A third time they took it down and kept watch, and a third time it floated miraculously and invisibly upward. Now they understood. “The saint does not wish to be below but above on the mountain; we shall build her a chapel in a suitable place,” the people said. They immediately chose a site about halfway up the mountain so that no one would have to walk too far to the miraculous image. The holy precinct was staked out, and building materials were brought to the spot at once. In the meantime they placed the image very near it. Behold, in the morning it was no longer there but was again at its chosen place on the summit. After this had happened three times again, just as before, the people yielded and built the little church where the saint wished it.
