Between the jagged peaks, where rigid seas of ice spread today, lay lovely valleys whose green meadows were strewn with the adornment of brilliantly colored flowers. The forests reached far higher than they do today. Human beings had built their peaceful dwellings in these lofty regions. Chamois grazed nearby in great herds and trustingly descended as far as the huts. In former times the vigorous Alpine soil produced no poisonous or unpalatable herbs and, compared with today, yielded many times the return. No glaciers loomed down into the high valleys so that the meadows beneath might suffer harm from their ceaseless work. One morning, however, as a handsome young herder performed this task, a gust of wind overturned his skiff, so that he miserably drowned in the white sea. The lads and maidens mourned him and searched for his body for days in order to give it an honorable burial. For a long time they searched in vain. Only after several days, when the people wished to make butter, was his body found amid the foaming, swelling cream inside a butter churn as high as a tower.
