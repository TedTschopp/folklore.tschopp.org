This Stockalper was held in high honor by the people and enjoyed a reputation as a man of great talents, wide-ranging knowledge, and cunning practices. He is also said to have stood in great favor with high-ranking princes, including the Duke of Aosta. When the robbers caught him, he acted so stupid and simple-minded that, amid mockery and great laughter, they brought him to their companions in the cave. There they played all manner of foolish tricks on him and gave him a drink from a human skull. The fool made several grimaces, laughed, raised the bowl to his lips, drained it, and cried: “The soup is good!” Because he claimed to be a master cook, he was employed as a Bratenwender. He now had the finest opportunity to examine the robbers' nest closely and devise his plans. In the cave, and outside in the forest when he gathered dry wood, he observed the band and memorized the secret approaches to the cave. He had long noticed that not all the robbers trusted him. Several regarded him with suspicious glances, and one day he heard them say to their companions: “That is no fool at all. He is only pretending. He is a spy in disguise, and we must kill him or he will betray us!” The others, however, replied: “Nonsense, let him live. Even if he is no fool, he understands cooking splendidly, and that means good days for us!” The fool, who was stirring eggs in a bowl as he listened, twisted his face into a grin and said in a silly tone: “Es geht nicht immer so, sondern auch so!”—“It does not always go this way, but sometimes that way!”—and instead of stirring to the left, he stirred to the right.
