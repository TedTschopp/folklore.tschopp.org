His wife was a daughter of the lord of the old tower in Zuz. The marriage was happy and blessed with two little boys, but the peace of the household was soon disturbed. The noblewoman noticed that her husband was no longer as loving and open as before and, for some time, had left the castle every day at fixed hours in the morning, returning only late. She questioned the huntsman who often accompanied his lord, but could learn from him only that an oath bound his tongue. That very day, the cunning noblewoman brought the huntsman two bags, one filled with gold and the other with coarse sand. The huntsman understood that he was not required to speak, yet nevertheless understood his lady's instruction. He kept the gold and scattered the sand unnoticed when he again accompanied his lord the following morning. The noblewoman followed the trail of sand, which led her to the cave in the Assatal, and kept to it until she stood before the entrance. There lay the huntsman, asleep or seeming to sleep, and in the cave she found her husband in the arms of a beautiful mountain fairy, both in gentle slumber. The riddle was now solved.
