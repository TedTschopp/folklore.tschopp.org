The stately Alpine hut, which raised its proud ridge into the blue sky, stood amid a field of glowing alpine roses. Three brothers had inherited it from their aged father. When, at the end of the spring-warm Austage, they once again occupied their mountain home on Gfällalp with their herds, they quarreled over the division of the pastures. When Uli, the youngest, drove his cows toward the Stocki, he found Menk, the eldest, already there, and Menk began to scold him. If he turned his steps toward Schönbühl, he could be sure that the lead cow of his brother Jörg would run toward him from there, wearing the great Treichelglocke and keeping the herd together. She bellowed at him as though the second brother's voice spoke from her: ‘What business have you here? Schönbühl is my Alp!’ A good part of the summer passed in this quarrel. With bitter pain, Uli had to watch himself being displaced more each day. At last the other two would not even let him sleep in the hut. One morning, after he had spent the cold night beneath a Felsbalm, he said to the others: ‘Brothers, let us end this unjust affair. The heavenly judge has shown me a way. Here are three Strahlen that I found at the glacier. One has a reddish glow; that is Gfäll with the hut among the alpine roses and everything around it. This clear crystal shall signify Schönbühl, and the dark one the Stocki. I place them here as tokens on the wall shelf beside the cross. Let each man go and search at the glacier for Strahlen, and whatever he first finds shall show what is to be his property.’ ‘Agreed!’ cried the two, for in their wicked hearts they thought: ‘We shall get the better of you, you foolish boy!’ They went about their day's work as usual. When they met again that evening at Gfäll, the youngest ran radiantly toward his two elders. ‘The hut is mine!’ he cried. ‘God has let me find a Rosenstein.’ The other two laughed maliciously, reached into their herdsmen's pouches, and each drew out three Strahlen: one red, one pure white, and one dark. ‘The whole Alp is ours, Hansnarr! From then on Gfällalp was cursed, for the spirits of the unblessed wandered upon it, and anyone who drove his herds up there was thereafter disturbed by them in his peaceful daily work. Avalanches swept down and devastated the mountain pastures; falling rocks killed the livestock. Then one day a monk, returning from the Holy Land, traveled over the Lötschenberg road. With it he bound the restless souls of the dishonest brothers inside a hollow larch, drove a plug into the hole, and brought peace and quiet to the Älplern.
