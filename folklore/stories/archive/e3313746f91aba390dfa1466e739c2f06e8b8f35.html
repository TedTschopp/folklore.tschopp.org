A noble Fräuli guarded it, or had to wait beside it until someone came who was still a pure young man and had been born in ner Temperwuchu, an Ember Week, and could carry her upon his back to a specific appointed destination. If someone was capable of doing this, he would redeem her, the guardian, receive the entire treasure, and be happy bis zum nintu Grad, to the ninth degree or generation. But if such a man could not carry her to the appointed destination, she would become heavier and heavier, finally so heavy that before reaching the appointed place, the bearer would think an entire mountain lay upon him. If he then let her fall before reaching the destination, she would curse him so that he could not be happy bis zum nintu Grad. A man from Brig driven by desire for money, who lacked none of the other conditions, is also said once to have tried to carry her to the destination so as to win the great treasure and become happy. Before he took her upon his back, the spirit maiden, hands raised and tears in her eyes, earnestly begged him not to dare carry her if he would then let her fall, for otherwise he would make both of them unhappy. Sadly, he did not pass the test. She became as heavy to him as though an entire mountain lay upon him, and he had to let her fall before reaching the destination. The spirit maiden began to weep so that mountain and valley echoed from it, and indeed his family was unhappy bis zum nintu Grad. At last, however, someone came intending to redeem her, not from avarice and usury or desire for money, but to do good and distribute most of the treasure among the poor. He successfully carried the Geisterbrut to the destination. Then she suddenly stood before him in a snow-white dress, beautiful and lovely as an angel, showered him with the finest words of thanks and praise, and prophesied that he and his descendants would be happy bis zum nintu Grad.
