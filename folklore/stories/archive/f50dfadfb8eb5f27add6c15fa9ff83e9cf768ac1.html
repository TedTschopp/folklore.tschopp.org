Yet it has an oven with an oven bench and other seats for resting hewn into the stone, and from the Winterhalde one can still clearly recognize remnants of the stone cross-frame belonging to the window broken through the rock. All of this was made by the dwarfs who took up residence here when they immigrated into Switzerland. Our ancestors claimed that they had come to our country from Asia because they could no longer endure the sun’s heat there, and in this way people also explained why the Erdmännchen had entirely black faces and such an exceedingly indolent nature. For they did not care to work at all. For as long as the summer days lasted, they walked about near the Senn upon the mountain, and for as long as the winter nights lasted, they sat below with the farmer at the Zeindlimatt farm and amused themselves with the village news. There they sometimes declared quite openly that they too understood how to plant and cut grain, but had no need to do so and preferred to do nothing at all. Yet they would not admit where they obtained all their flour and their great supply of wine. They did, however, gladly share it. At every Heuet they carried wine to the people on the Büttihalde, and at harvest time even omelets and coffee, everything packed quite neatly together in a Hozzli (carrying basket). They surpassed every lawyer in cunning, and yet apart from their faces they had almost nothing human about them. If one happened to see through the little cloak to their bodies, they resembled a tailless Welschhuhn more than a human being. The young men at the Zeindlimatt farm therefore suspected that they must have chicken feet as well, and while the little men were there for a visit, they scattered ashes across the kitchen floor. When the clock struck ten and the time came at which the dwarfs punctually ended their evening, one young man took the light to see them out and another politely opened the kitchen door. For in old farmhouses one can enter or leave the living room only through the kitchen, and the men now thought they had solved the whole matter. But that was far from clever enough. “Hünggi, üf! Little hens, fly up!” the Erdmännchen cried at the threshold, and like a Kitt of wild fowl they all whirred together—purrr!—out through the kitchen.
