In volume II, pp. 83 ff., of his Naturgeschichte des Schweizerlandes (Zurich, 1746), the scholar Johann Jakob Scheuchzer tells the following legend, which he heard in August 1705 on his fourth mountain journey.", "The inhabitants around the Klariden Alps tell a strange story, which the honored reader may inscribe according to his pleasure either among true occurrences or on the roll of little tales. In former times, they say, a Senn there kept a wanton whore and held her in such high honor that from the dwelling or Senn hut to the Käsgaden he paved the otherwise muddy and filthy path with cheeses so that she would not soil her shoes or feet. Once his poor mother came to him to fill her hungry belly with milk and Suffy, but the godless son mixed horse urine into the milk dishes and sent her away again with such wicked treatment. Thereupon the poor woman wished every misfortune upon her wasteful and depraved son and begged God to show His just avenging hand against him. And so it happened: the earth opened its mouth and swallowed this useless burden upon the earth together with his wanton harlot. At the same time the upper snowfields and rocks collapsed and covered the Alps, formerly rich and fertile with grass, so that from that time onward they have been entirely barren and bear nothing.", "Whether this story actually occurred or was invented by the pious clergy in order to deter the common people, especially the Sennen, from the vices of fornication and disobedience toward parents, I shall not investigate. At any rate, the inhabitants are so convinced of the truth of this example that anyone who cast doubt upon it would not be well received.", "It is also claimed that this villain will make his presence known if one calls or challenges him. A learned and worthy priest, Karl Joseph Arnold, pastor in Unterschächen, himself told us that once in his younger years he went into these Klariden Alps and, at the place where the Senn hut had stood, boldly challenged the Senn who had been swallowed body and soul. Just imagine! They bathed and washed the filthy boy not merely in Schotten but in pure whole milk! The boy grew up, and before long the blind parents tasted the fruits of their upbringing.
