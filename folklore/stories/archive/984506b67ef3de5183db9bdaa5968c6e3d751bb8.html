No means could be found to remove this plague from the land. Then one day a stranger came into the little village whom people called the Schlangenbanner. He offered the people his services on the condition that, in his battle with the snake queen, they obey his every word and come to his aid. They agreed and, the very next morning, proceeded to the Hungerbühl armed with scythes, axes, and pitchforks. Here the people had to arrange fieldstones into circular walls and light great fires inside them. As soon as everything was ready, the stranger began to play a pipe. At once snakes came crawling out of the little wood in great numbers, writhed up over the stone walls, and met their deaths in the flames. From wall to wall, the man had lured them with the sound of his pipe. The people watched the undertaking anxiously. The Schlangenbanner told them that today there was still no reason for fear. “But when the queen comes tomorrow,” he said, “woe to me if you do not keep your word and stand bravely ready to help.” On the second day the fires blazed once more, and another great quantity of vermin met its death, but the queen did not appear.", "Scarcely had they taken their places on the third day when the Schlangenbanner’s piping was answered by a dreadful hissing. “That is the queen—help!” cried the Schlangenbanner, and he climbed the nearest tree. At that moment a tremendous snake rolled toward the fire site. Its body was gray, and its head was adorned with a crown. It fixed its glittering eyes first on the farmers, then on the Schlangenbanner, and darted like lightning at the tree to crush the man above. He loudly cried down for help. The people were already on the point of fleeing when one of them found his courage, sprang forward, and stabbed the beast against the trunk with his pitchfork. Then the others came as well and beat it completely to death with clubs. “Spare the crown!” the stranger called to the people as he climbed down.
