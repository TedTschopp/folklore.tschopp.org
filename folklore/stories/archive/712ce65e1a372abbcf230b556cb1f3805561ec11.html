An old woman said that on Easter Day at midnight the wagon’s oaken shaft projected from the sloping hillside on the eastern side, and that a horse or ox could then be harnessed to it; but that after an hour had passed it rushed noisily back deep into the hill and no one had yet succeeded in drawing the wagon out.", "The covetous farmer longed to gain that treasure wagon. When that was in vain, he finally went to a conjurer of devils and Geisterbanner who knew the counsel of evil arts. This man went scouting at night, made all kinds of figures in the sand, and finally reported that the hill had been found. On the next Easter Day, the shaft would project klafterlang—a Klafter’s length—into the air, and for every coal-black billy goat the man slaughtered there at night before the appointed hour, the subterranean beings would load another chest of gold onto the wagon. The farmer now sought out every black-haired billy goat in the country and secretly slaughtered them at midnight on the treasure hill. He impatiently awaited Easter Day and held four mighty bulls and four strong horses ready. No one but the Geisterbanner knew of it. When darkness fell, they went out with the draft animals and equipment. They reached the hill without a light. The conjurer murmured his Bannsprüche and impressed upon the farmer’s soul that every word and sound during the spirit hour would destroy their hope for life. Twelve struck. A crash came from the slope of the hill, and a wagon shaft rushed swiftly out beside the men. A bluish sulfur light at its tip gave a dim glow. They quickly hitched the animals, heaved, and drove the resisting teams onward. Every rim and spoke shone with sulfur light. The wagon was already in the open when the farmer gave vent to his feelings with a joyous whoop. Suddenly he fell unconscious to the ground; horses, bulls, and wagon rushed backward into the chasm. The Geisterbanner vanished, and the farmer did not regain consciousness until dawn. Apart from the trampled grass, the insatiable man saw no trace of wagon, animals, or harness.
