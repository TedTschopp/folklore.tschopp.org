It occurred to him that he should try whistling, for he had heard it said that when one whistles at the Herumjini, they become furious. More and more of them came out, drawing nearer and nearer to the person who whistled. When he had whistled loudly several times, ever more Wissle emerged, and at last there was such an innumerable multitude that he thought a Herumji head peered up beside every stone; indeed, he feared all the stones around him were moving and were nothing but Wissle. Afraid they might attack and eat him, he took off his Tschopo (overcoat, jacket, vest), threw it toward them, and fled as fast as he could. When he returned later to see what they might have done with the Tschopo, he saw that they had torn it into tiny pieces.
