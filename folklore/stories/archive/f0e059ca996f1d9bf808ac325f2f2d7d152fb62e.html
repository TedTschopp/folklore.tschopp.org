A knight named Kuno once lived there. Gold was his god, and avarice was called his greatest virtue. One day, a conjurer of devils came to him, whom he had summoned from the Orient to mediate contact with the Devil and thereby make him the richest man in the land. Since the Devil does nothing for nothing, Kuno and the conjurer pondered for a long time what he might give as a countergift. Miserly as he was, he would surrender none of his earthly treasures, and so he resolved to offer the Devil his soul. Once he and the conjurer had agreed on this point, the latter immediately set to work, and soon the Devil appeared before them. In a few words, Sir Kuno declared his wish to become the richest man on earth and said he would gladly give his soul for it. The Devil nodded his head in agreement, and soon the masses of stone and blocks of wood in the castle courtyard had all turned to gold. The brilliance of this mass of gold blinded Kuno so completely that the light went out of his eyes. Despairing at this, he no longer wished to live and immediately threw himself into the Devil's arms, whereupon the Devil vanished with him. All the treasure sank into the bosom of the earth. Only on Good Friday do a few pieces appear in the form of old stones and rotten wood. Whoever finds the right one, takes it home, and keeps it in a dark chest for seven days will—if nothing else extraordinary intervenes—find solid gold inside it on the eighth day." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
