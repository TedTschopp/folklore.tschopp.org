There lived an upright, industrious farmwife who loved and honored her husband and also raised her children well. — On many winter evenings, two beautiful girls came to Vulpèra from the little valley below the high Pic Pisoc. They wore white dresses, had flax-blond hair, and brought spinning wheels; they spun with extraordinary industry. They particularly liked to place the farmwife’s bundles of flax upon their own distaffs and spun it as fine as the finest silk. But they did not speak. Only when a thread broke did one say, ‘Thread broken,’ whereupon the other simply replied, ‘Tie it on.’ When a pair of bobbins were full, they were reeled or wound, and then the beautiful skeins of yarn were hung on the wall and admired with pleasure by the farmwife. — When their hour came, the nocturnal spinners rose and set out for home, always taking their little spinning wheels with them but always leaving the farmwife all the flax they had spun. — The farmwife now intended to show her gratitude to the two girls at the end of the spinning season, and on one of the final evenings prepared a great feast. All the spinning women in Vulpèra were to take part in it in honor of the strange spinners. The latter did attend, but they were very sad that they already had to depart, for the time of their farewell forever was near. — At the end they gave the woman a ball of yarn and said, ‘For your goodwill, reward for reward,’ then went away and never returned. — But the ball of yarn was never exhausted, no matter how many skeins the farmwife chose to reel from it." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
