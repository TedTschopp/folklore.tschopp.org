Many years ago, on a particular day, this part of the boat could be seen wreathed with the area’s most beautiful roses. In olden times a young pastor is said to have brought his young wife home to this cozy dwelling by the lake, whose floral ornament had always been an object of admiration. As the happy pair returned from their wedding and entered the parsonage, the sun sank crimson toward the strange tooth of the Stockhorn, behind which it would soon disappear. The lake at their feet lay like a fiery stream of glowing embers. “Come, my love,” said the pastor, “I will show you in the most beautiful light this land that the chronicler calls ‘In Paradise.’” So he took a little boat and rowed out onto the lake with his young wife, where they watched the glowing ball slowly sink. A mighty enchantment came over them. They could not see their fill and did not notice a storm rising from the far side of the lake. It suddenly whipped the waters into a fury and tossed the pastor’s little boat back and forth. At last a great wave struck it and dragged it into the depths. The young married couple miserably drowned, and their bodies could never be recovered. The prow of the ill-fated little boat, however, was fastened by chains to the timbers of the parsonage arbor, where for many years faithful love still afterward brought flowers to those buried in the waters." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
