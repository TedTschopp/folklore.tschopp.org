It is said to have been connected with Ramswag by underground passages and inhabited by wealthy little dwarf men. For nearly a hundred years, whenever the farmers of Schuoppis were plowing nearby, the dwarfs brought them cake and other things in both the morning and the afternoon, serving the food on silver plates; the cutlery too was heavy silver. The dwarfs never spoke. When the farmers drove toward Schuoppis, the little men simply laid the food upon the Furrenen and later collected the dishes. They were said to be the size of a five-year-old child and very numerous. They did no harm to anyone.\nOnce, the farmers were rude enough to keep the plates, spoons, and forks brought to the field. After that, the little people never brought anything again and were never seen at Schuoppis. They were seen in the Buchholz for some time afterward, but then disappeared from there as well.\nIt is further told that, after vanishing, the dwarfs revealed their treasure by laying it out to sonnen—to sun—in the open air in the Buchholz, but only in the form of shining, multicolored shards of glass and porcelain. If anyone managed to obtain them, they proved to be gold. Once, two men went into the ruin there and saw heaps of money. An invisible voice allowed them to take as much as each could carry, but they were not permitted to speak a word or help one another. The men filled two sacks. When one could not lift his, he signaled to the other, whereupon everything immediately turned to shards, which they carelessly threw away. People claim that the Schatzsonnen has been noticed even in the most recent times, but only when passersby knew nothing of the circumstances and afterward told of masses of shining shards.
