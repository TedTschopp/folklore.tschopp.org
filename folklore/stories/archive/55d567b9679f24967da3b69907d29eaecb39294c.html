It was raining, storming, and snowing when, on his return, he entered the Hohle Gasse. The wind scarcely allowed him to open the umbrella he had taken with him from Klingnau. While he struggled with it and ran across the fields beside the Aare toward his boat, he encountered a man of excessively tall stature traveling in the same direction. He asked whether the man was also going to the ferry, but received no answer at all. The servant now scarcely dared raise his umbrella from time to time high enough to glance across at the stranger. The latter wore a broad hat and dragged a mighty sack behind him. He continued in evenly measured, enormous strides; his feet could be heard more clearly than they could be seen. Thus they reached the river. It was pitch-dark, and nowhere at the familiar spot could the servant find the boat in which he had crossed only a short while before. For hours he wandered among reeds and bushes, and just as long his silent companion stumbled beside and over him. At last this enraged the servant. He forgot himself and burst into loud curses and oaths; that helped. The Schwarzer vanished instantly, and in his place the servant saw the boat. He was already beyond the middle of the Aare when heavy loads of stones came plunging after him from above. He in the boat was covered with earth and sand. Thus the giant hurled things after him and whistled shrilly through his fingers in two tones. Only when the servant was home beneath the eaves did he cease to hear him.", "This is the Schwarzer in der Au, who takes his name from the black clothing in which he wanders.
