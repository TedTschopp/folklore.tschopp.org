The Älpler, the alpine herders, call it Schnyders Horn. Formerly it was simply called the Horn. But there lived in the valley a dissolute fellow, a good-for-nothing who would not work and only wanted to roam about after chamois. At first fortune did not favor him in the mountains either. Then he made a pact with the Devil. From that day on, he became the most famous chamois hunter far and wide—a feared man in the mountains. From ancient times, the finest chamois grazed in the Bänder around Schnyders Horn. The Devil carried the hunter there from the Alpstafel every morning. ‘Shoot all the chamois for me,’ said the Devil, ‘only not the white one, for she is my favorite.’ The hunter killed to his heart’s content, bathing hands and feet in chamois blood because this was believed to make one’s hold and step secure. He also drank the blood of the poor slaughtered creatures, and the more he drank, the more bloodthirsty he became. One morning he was again hunting on the Bockpfäden. Suddenly the white chamois stood before him. ‘You are mine,’ said Schnyder. ‘I do not fear even the Devil.’ The frightened little animal turned and ran away along the Bockpfäden. The hunter hurried after it. But just as he took aim and sent a bullet after the chamois, the Devil stepped before him and struck him over the rock wall so that he shattered on the stones far below.
