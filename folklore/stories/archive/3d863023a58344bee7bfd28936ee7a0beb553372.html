Only three days later, all the livestock in the stable except for one black goat were strangled. When this misfortune was repeated, the people sought refuge with the priest. The clergyman tried to help but could not discover where in the house the dead man had now established his seat. By chance, a band of homeless people came to the farm that evening and asked for shelter for the night. They were lodged in the barn. They had a miserable rest there: pounding and crashing frightened them all night, and in the morning none could find their clothes. Only when the gate was opened to admit more light and someone looked up toward the ridge did they see all their belongings hanging in confusion beneath the roof. Now the priest knew where the trouble lay and immediately climbed beneath the roof beams, all the way to the ridge and rafters. ‘Cuckoo, cuckoo! Do you want me? You still do not have me!’ the kobold called mockingly to him from beneath every roof tile. But the conjurer did not allow himself to be distracted and now stood at the gable opening beside the little pulley used to draw sheaves into the barn. ‘You once stole a turnip too, Father!’ it cried. ‘Yes,’ the priest said, ‘because I was hungry, but I placed a kreuzer in the hole and afterward repented. If only you had repented of your deeds as well.’ ‘You also once stole a whip!’ it continued to jeer. ‘I did not steal it,’ the priest answered, ‘but merely picked up the whip to crack it, and then repented.
