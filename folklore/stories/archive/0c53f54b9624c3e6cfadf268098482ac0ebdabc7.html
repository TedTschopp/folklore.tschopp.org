It takes the form of a three-cornered sack of leaves or a white goat; then it appears as a dog, a pig, a cat, a bird, etc., always with the corresponding cry of the animal in question. Once, when old Nef was going home from our house at night, he encountered a large, loudly grunting pig behind our house. He thought our pig had escaped because we owned a similar one at the time. So he came back and reported it. Our pig, however, was safe and sound in the stall, and no such pig was missing anywhere in the neighborhood. On another occasion, Nef was going home from the Laue. By the maples in the \"Dicket,\" so many \"Wickvögel\" flew down in front of his feet that he could go no farther. The birds kept crying: \"How far, how far!\" He gathered his courage, leapt over several of them, and said: \"You blasted fools, home is where I'm going!\" Then the birds scattered, and such roaring and raging arose in the Eggliswald that one might have thought the whole world was going to fall apart. The \"Dicket\" was a favorite haunt of the Tuutier. The \"Choche Liess\" was once in his hut in the \"Dicket.\" A strange clamor broke out, and a three-cornered sack of leaves laid itself across the little door of the hut. The man behaved as though he noticed nothing, whereupon the sack of leaves rolled away as it had come. Once, by the old Hüttli in the Türli, a white goat was standing. The goat grew larger and larger and then suddenly vanished. The \"Melcherlis Bueb\" maintained that it had not been a real goat. The \"Gällelis Bueb\" was once on his way to a Stubete. On the bridge between \"Seinis\" and \"Sessen Uelis,\" a large pig lay crosswise, blocking his path. He made a tremendous leap over the monster, but the next day his entire face was swollen, and he developed a stubborn rash said to have been caused by the Tuutier. Nikolaus Bollhalder in Kühboden also told me that he had once crossed the \"Drecktöbeli\" at night.
