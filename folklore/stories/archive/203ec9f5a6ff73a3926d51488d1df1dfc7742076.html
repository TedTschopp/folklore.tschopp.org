When he wished to go back down that same evening, dreadful weather arrived, so that the poor devil had to stay overnight. Everything in the hut looked deserted and was quiet as a mouse. Before long this became too tedious for him. He bolted the door, climbed the little ladder to the Tril and the hay bed, prayed an Our Father, and then fell asleep, for he was tired. Around midnight he heard something below in the hut. He peered down and saw a pair of enormous men come in through the door. They struck a fire, made light, threw a heap of wood into the fire pit, lit it, hung the Chessi over it, and began to make cheese, although not a drop of milk remained in the cellar. Christa did not make a sound on the Tril, so that they would not notice him. Neither man cast a single glance upward. When they had finished making cheese, they placed a Muttlä full of Schottä on the table, and one looked up toward the Tril and called, ‘Come down now, Christa, and eat Schottä with us!’ Christa was soaking wet with the sweat of fear and did not betray himself. Then he heard one of them climbing the little ladder. The man glared at poor Christa like a stabbed buck and said, ‘If you had not prayed an Our Father before falling asleep, you would no longer need the milking stool!’ All at once the fire and light went out as though blown away. They groped outside again as they had come, and Christa fell asleep once more from sheer exhaustion. As the day began to brighten, he awoke, climbed down from the Tril, and saw not a trace of the entire spectacle: not one spark of fire in the pit, no Chessi, neither Muttlä nor Schottä, and the door bolted on the inside just as he himself had left it the night before. When he had recovered somewhat from the fright, he set out for home with his milking stool.
