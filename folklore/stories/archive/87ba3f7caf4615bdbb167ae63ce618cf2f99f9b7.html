The unfortunate woman had killed her child in the Bibern, the stream that rushes down from Heiligkreuz above Entlebuch and flows into the Emme near Hasle. Now she wanders up and down the stream until Judgment Day. Many have heard her lamenting. In the old Chreiehus, the Mummeli long had its place upon the stove, where it crouched and grumbled and allowed no one beside it. It took food from the household table and smashed all the Bekle und Kachele. At last, it was banished to the Estrich. At the Wandten beside the Bibern, it sometimes screams like a cat. She ran with the child to the dead man and said, “There, now take care of the brat!” With this, the evil spirit gained power over her and spoke from the corpse: “Throw it into the water. If God let your husband die, you may kill the child too.” She did so and threw it into the Bibern rushing past the house. No sooner was it done than desperate remorse seized her. See how she runs breathlessly down along the stream bank, staring at the little child's pale face turned toward its mother! She wishes to rescue it but cannot. So it went down as far as the Emme, where the mill stands. The child drifts toward the wheel—hurry, hurry! Alas, the wheel has already caught it. Slowly, slowly, so that the murderess could distinguish the pale face and every little limb, the body is raised and then disappears beyond the paddles like a great white snowflake. The woman now begins a grievous howl. Something strange and irresistible drives her back up to the stone in the stream where she threw in the child. There, she plunges into the water herself, swims down to the mill wheel, which seizes her and crushes her in its tearing whirl. But the poor soul has found no rest to this day.
