Beneath it lived a fiery dragon, as long as a Wiesbaum and as thick as a Jauchenfaß. At night it slept beneath the stone, but at midday it flew, spraying fire, across the field to an oak out on the open ground. There it ate the two sheep that the people of Rohrdorf had to place beneath the tree for it each day; if it found nothing, it came into the village and struck down livestock and people. It carried on this way for many years, until the municipality had raised a great white bull that had never left the stall for seven years. After seven years, they let it out. It ran straight to the field where the great dragon lay at its midday meal. When the dragon saw it, it shot after it, and the two fought so fiercely that their blood flowed like a stream. The people watched from afar in the greatest suspense, and when at last neither of the two animals moved any longer, they gradually dared approach the battleground. To everyone's joy the dragon was dead, but the bull that had delivered the people from the scourge also lay lifeless beside it in the blood.
