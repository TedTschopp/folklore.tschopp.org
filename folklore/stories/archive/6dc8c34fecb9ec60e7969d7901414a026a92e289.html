Before him he saw a great city with high walls and twelve gates. Upon each gate stood an angel who was to guard it. The king awoke and wondered greatly what the strange dream might mean. He therefore had inquiries made throughout the land. For a long time no one could interpret his vision, until at last he found a priest who revealed to him that God had given him a sign that he should increase divine worship and have twelve churches built, which would be daughter churches of the Church in Paradise. The king began to do as he had been advised. He built the twelve churches listed hereafter, namely: Frutingen, Leuxingen, Eschi, Wimnis, Uttingen, Thierachern, Scherzlingen, Thun, Hilterfingen, Sigriswil, and Amsoldingen. The last was to be a collegiate foundation with a fixed number of canons. Yet the king also had such a foundation erected at the Goldener Hof, at one end of which there already stood a castle with a high tower that is now called Spiez. Beside the high tower he had a town built and endowed with the liberties of other Roman cities.", "But the king was an inconstant man who allowed Satan to tempt him into thinking no more of the Church in Paradise, which his ancestors had built. But St. Michael would not allow such a thing to happen to his house in Paradise. A great sickness came upon the king, and while he lay ill he dreamed that the devil accused him before God and demanded his soul. When judgment was convened over him, the angels Gabriel and Raphael appeared for the king to defend his soul. But the devil came forward three times, each time with a new accusation. Then God delivered judgment and said that a scale should be brought to weigh the good against the evil. In the anguish of his soul, the king now begged St. Michael to assist him in this peril and also promised him every good thing for the Church in Paradise.
