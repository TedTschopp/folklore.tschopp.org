Stoop" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "At Portels on the Flumser Kleinberg, in the Harzloch, stands a house that, like many very old houses, has a large chamber or hall. In former times the young men and women of the Kleinberg used to dance in this hall. Once, while people were again dancing in the Harzloch, a wonderfully beautiful maiden came to the dancing place. No one knew her, but all the young men felt drawn to her and wished to dance with her. The first would no longer surrender her to anyone else and danced only with her. At midnight she told him she now had to leave. The young man accompanied her across the farms to the Bettlerbühl, where the Schilz rushes through the eerily deep Tobel beneath the upper natural bridge, the Gwelb. There she revealed to him that she was an unredeemed spirit and had to wander. If he loved her and possessed enough courage and self-control, he could redeem her. She must appear to him as a toad, and he would then have to kiss her three times. At the first kiss her appearance would be most bearable, at the second already much more loathsome, and at the third most terrible of all. At the third kiss, however, he was permitted to cover her with a little cloth and kiss her only through it. Under no circumstances could he utter the name of Jesus. The young man vowed to do everything. Then the maiden vanished and the toad came. The young man overcame his revulsion and kissed it, whereupon it vanished. Then it returned, but in a much uglier shape. Love and pity prevailed, and the young man kissed it. The third time it appeared in so terrible a form that the young man, overcome with horror, cried, “Oh, Jesus!” A deathly sorrowful wail shrilled through the Tobel. A fir cone fell from the crown of the nearest fir to his feet. The maiden stood there again in human form, shaken by deep pain, and lamented that she must now suffer until a fir had grown to its full height from the cone that had just fallen. A cradle would be made from the boards of this fir, and a child laid in that cradle would be destined to redeem her. Then she vanished. But the young man never attended another dance.\nJ. B.
