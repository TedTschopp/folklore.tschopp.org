They often came down from the Flühen in whole crowds. This happened most often at harvest time, when they watched the laborers in the fields while reclining on a stone or sitting like birds on tree branches in the shade of the foliage. It often happened that when a reaper lay down for a little sleep in the hot sunshine, he found a cooling drink and refreshing food when he awoke. Moreover, the greater part of his work had been completed. It even happened that the next morning the entire field had been mown and the grain stacked in sheaves, ready to be taken home. Indeed, at times these friendly beings came right into the houses to be helpful and of service there too. For their help they asked nothing but a little milk, which was placed for them on the threshold of the house. They were also skillful physicians, and their little potions, prepared from aromatic Alpine herbs, were equally wholesome for people and livestock. They showed particular compassion toward poor children sent by their parents to gather wood in the forest during winter. Sometimes they laid the finest brushwood on the paths for them; sometimes they gave them small, delicious cheeses that were never used up. When a farmer was plowing in the field and the work stretched far beyond the midday bell, they crept up compassionately and set a delicious meal upon the grass. Finally, now and then on long winter evenings they also helped hardworking maids prepare flax.
