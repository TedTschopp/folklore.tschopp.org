No. 7. 52.", "Every year pieces of tile and remains of walls are still turned up by the plow, renewing the people’s belief in the former town. But where the highway from Aarau to Lucerne passes, a thornbush had grown from time immemorial, whose trunk alone was said to be three feet thick. Everyone who came along the road regarded it as a marvel and added a stone to it, and thus little by little a low wall rose around the thorn. It thrived under this protection, gained its own small plot of land, and so that ground remained unplanted. The people who had fields nearby would tell one another what treasures lay hidden beneath the bush and that a female figure could be seen there in the heat of noon, with gold seeming to shine beneath her steps. Yet everyone who coveted these treasures also feared falling into the power of the evil spirit, and for that very reason, they said, the thornbush must not be cut down.", "One Saturday night the young men of the place had gathered at the Rössli inn, where several older men were already sitting over their wine. When the talk between the two groups turned to the thorn, naturally it was the old men who firmly maintained that it could not be destroyed. The young men grew heated against this superstition, made a wager of it, and went away with saw and axe. Before long they dragged the old thornbush, hacked to pieces, in through the door. The people were greatly alarmed. They regretted the tree, which stood recorded in almost every chronicle, which had so long been an important thing in the valley, and by whose budding and flowering they had reckoned years and events. The young men would surely have been severely punished in court if anyone had wished to bring the whole affair fully to light. But now it was done. Even afterward, however, people claimed still to have seen the apparition of a white maiden at that Mauerhübel." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
