Now, as the saint looked over the valley floor from Schauensee and surveyed the long Erlenschachen covered with stone rubble, the distress of the beautiful countryside and its inhabitants moved him. Seeking a remedy, the man of God immediately examined the area of the Schachenwald and observed that the torrent had its natural outlet into the Emme river through the saddle between the St. Jost and Littau mountains. The rocky ridge there, over which the Renggbach probably plunged as a waterfall at its ordinary volume before flowing into the Emme, consisted only of crumbling sandstone. He therefore proposed that the countess have a cut 12—20 feet deep made through this barrier to divert the recurring devastation from Kriens and Lucerne.
