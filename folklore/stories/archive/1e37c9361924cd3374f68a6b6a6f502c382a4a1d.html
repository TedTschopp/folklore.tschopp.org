The government sold it after the property finally yielded almost nothing. The blame for this was placed upon the castle’s Schaffner. The last is said to have been named S. On his way home from Aarau, he stopped at the priest’s house in Densbüren, drank there, and could not be dissuaded from crossing the mountains to Castelen late that same evening. In the darkness he lost his way, reached the crest of the steep Wasserfluh, and fell to his death there with a wild crash. His cries were heard, but the people believed it was the Thier in den Nüschelen and did not search. At last his brother set out and found him lying below in the depths, entirely scratched and torn. Since then he has again been seen in the castle rooms at every hour of the day. His own maid was among the first to see him: when she fetched wine in the castle cellar, he sat behind the largest cask and held out his chalk-white hand to her. Out of compassion she would gladly have taken it, but terror drove her away. The female fork maker of Oberflachs has also often seen him walking down the broad castle staircase in a black tailcoat.
