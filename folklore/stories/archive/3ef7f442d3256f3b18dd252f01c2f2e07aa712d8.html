Instead, a hare immediately appeared and stood upright a short distance away. Dietrich aimed carefully and fired. Yet not only had he missed the hare, it did not even move from the spot. The hunter loaded once more and missed once more. Annoyed, he left his stand. Then it occurred to him that the hare might be bewitched. He quickly fetched Osterkohlen from home, mixed them into his powder, returned with it to the former spot, and the hare was still there. Again, standing upright in all sorts of ways, it sprang across the path only a few steps before the hunter. This time, however, so solid a shot struck it that it tumbled head over heels. Yet it rose again and limped on three legs into nearby bushes. Dietrich ran after it and, to his astonishment, found there an old woman from the village who was generally feared as a witch. She was just about to wrap her left leg with a handkerchief. “How did you come here?” she snapped at Dietrich. Then she added quite calmly, “Because I fell a little while ago, I stepped off the path to bandage myself here.” Angered by such insolence, Dietrich left the woman lying there and told everything that same evening to the old doctor in the village. In the morning, however, Dietrich was found dead in bed.", "The woman is said never again to have practiced witchcraft from that time onward.
