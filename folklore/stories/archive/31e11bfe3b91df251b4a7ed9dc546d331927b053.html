One man named Ruckli supplied the sack. Holding it open, they went through the streets into the forest, with two men holding out the sack and the others playing the hunters. According to their agreement, one of them was to wait in the forest and jump into the sack as the Streggelen. But he arrived too late, for the sack had already become full. They now went downhill toward the village, arriving drenched in sweat with their catch, which they placed behind the stove. They mocked the Streggelen and cried, “Ragöri, wo bist?—Ragöri, where are you?” How deathly pale they became when a strange voice answered uncannily from the sack, “Hinter Öf; hier hinten ins Rucklis Sack.—Behind the stove; back here in Ruckli’s sack.” At that moment the man who was supposed to jump into the sack also entered the room, and it became clear that they had been punished for their insolence and had caught the real Streggelen. Only when accompanied by cross and banner could this monstrous being be carried back into the forest. Others relate that a catlike animal then sprang out of the sack and escaped through the locked door. A hole remained in the door and could never be closed again.
