She had one child, a girl of six. She kept the child very strictly at work; if the girl did not accomplish what she was supposed to, she received nothing to eat. One day, the little girl had to search for bilberries all day and came home late in the evening without having completely filled the container she had taken with her. Her mother became angry and sent the girl out again to make up what she had failed to gather in the bright moonlit night. The girl did not want to go because she was afraid. At last she went, but only as far as the Plöhli, a mountain meadow north of Rüti, where she began to scream pitifully. Her mother hurried after her, but now the child was even more frightened because she knew her mother's anger. Driven beside herself by the child's screaming, the mother struck her until she fell dead to the ground. The woman was horrified and tried with every sort of caress to bring the child back to life. All night, she wandered through the Plöhli with the body in her arms. In the morning, people came, took the dead child from her, and laid it in a grave at the spot where its mother had murdered it. The woman soon afterward fell ill and died. She could find no rest in the grave, however, and from then on had to wander through the Plöhli on moonlit nights as a snow-white goat whose plaintive bleating could be heard all the way out to the Rhine. Nothing has been sensed of the Plöhligeiss for a number of years. She has probably now atoned for her crime.\nD.
