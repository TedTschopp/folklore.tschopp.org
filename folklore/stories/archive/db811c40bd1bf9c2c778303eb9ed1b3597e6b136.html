Driving his spurs into his horse's flanks, without further thought, he made a daring leap 2400 meters downward without horse or rider suffering the slightest harm. Even today, on the so-called Höhe along the path to Calfeisen, one can see the shapes of four horseshoes on a stone slab upon which the horse landed. Because of this miracle, Martin is venerated as a saint, and as such, he is also patron of the chapel in Calfeisen that bears his name and contains a statue of him on horseback. Every spring, the church administrative council of Vättis clothes it with a red riding cloak. During summer, the Alpenbesitzer come, and each cuts off a piece because it is said to be good against illness and livestock epidemics. Thus, by autumn, Saint Martin once again sits unclothed upon his horse, and the relic is exhausted.\n‘Oberland Gazette.’" ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Höhe”: literally ‘the height,’ the locally named elevated spot on the Calfeisen path where the miraculous hoofprints are shown.", "Swiss term “Alpenbesitzer”: owners or rights-holders of Alpine pasture holdings, not owners of the Alps mountain range; they cut protective pieces from Martin's cloak for their livestock." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
