Even the boldest mountaineer feels an icy chill when he looks down into these dreadful gorges of the Massachin. A listening ear hears a distant, hollow roar rising from a fearsome depth, made by a raging mountain stream. Gray-black rock walls, separated in places by only narrow gaps, rise from an uncanny depth to dizzying heights. Forest trees crown them, some trembling as they sway out above the abyss and standing like silent sentinels to warn the unwary traveler of the threatening danger. In the dizzying depths, the rocks press so close together that it becomes completely dark. One can only wonder how the wild Massa, which rises from the Aletsch Glacier, manages in summer to force its roaring waves through these narrows. The deathly silence of the forest is broken only by chance by the distant ax of a woodcutter, the cries of the ravens swarming here, or the shrill whistle of a bird of prey circling fearlessly and majestically above the abyss. This silence, the dark depths from which a cold breath blows upward, and the subterranean, muffled roar of the glacial stream echoing eerily from the sheer rock walls are uncanny and terrifying to the curious mountain traveler, so that he leaves the place as quickly as possible. All the more so when he learns that several men have already met their deaths in these hellish gorges. On the other side, one looks in astonishment at a truly daring and costly water conduit along the dreadful rock walls, seemingly suspended in the air, which is carried out of the Massachin as far as Mörelried. Only narrow beams run beside the wooden channels. The keeper or warden of the watercourse must walk along them when the water fails in order to discover what is wrong. To proceed at such a dizzying height over such narrow planks requires a bold and daring fellow who does not grow faint.", "Many of the courageous men who undertook this dangerous office are said to have fallen into the dreadful abyss. Folk belief holds that spirits caused their deaths. An ancient legend supposes that a seductive water nymph, or even an ice maiden from the Aletsch Glacier, lives in these terrible places and from time to time hunts men. When she has tired of the first, she sends him down the Massa without scruple and then seeks to enchant and capture a new one, lure him into her cold embrace and dreadful bridal bed, and cast him down. These legends may owe their origin to the following account. A herdsman is once said to have driven his goats into this region to graze. Then he heard a clear voice call from the Massachin, ‘The hour has come—but the man has not!’ It called this three times. Suddenly a young man came striding swiftly along the dizzying water conduit. Scarcely had he approached the place where the spirit voice had been heard when he fell into the terrible abyss.
