He was a great sorcerer; from time to time he swam through the lake, the Reuss, and the Schächen as far as Bürglen in the Canton of Uri. There he did the people great harm by piercing the cows’ ‘Burdi’—that is, their wombs—with a thin pointed stick. At last they asked a fahrender Schüler or a clergyman for advice. He told them to obtain a Leithund or bloodhound that would seek out the sorcerer’s trail for them. If a dog bears nine pups in each of nine successive litters, the ninth pup of the ninth litter is a Leithund. They searched and inquired for a long time until they found someone who owned such an animal. He was willing to place it at their service if all of Bürglen stood surety for him. This was done, and they received the bloodhound. It found the wrongdoer’s trail, followed it to the Schächen, then leaped into the stream and swam through the Reuss and the lake as far as Lucerne. Several people followed it in small boats. In Lucerne—or Brunnen—it ran straight toward the hohlä Gässli, burst open the shoemaker’s house door, found him sitting on a stool in his Butig—he was a little Mandli—leaped at his throat, and killed him at once.
