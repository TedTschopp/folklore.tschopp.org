He simply informed his faithful people of Uri, Schwyz, and Unterwalden, who immediately set out, crossed the Gotthard, and marched toward Rome. In a village in the Canton of Ticino, they met a smith known and feared far and wide for his gigantic strength. ‘Where are you going?’ he asked the company. ‘To Rome, to help the pope; come with us!’ ‘At once,’ answered the smith. He quickly welded three crowbars together, swung this weapon onto his shoulder, and followed the procession. In Rome, the pope offered them a meal. But the worthy men declared that they wished to settle matters with the enemy first. They immediately attacked the Italians. When the battle began, the pope climbed onto the cathedral’s flat roof to pray his breviary, and before he had finished the canonical hour, the Swiss had already disposed of the enemy.
