Behind it toward the west, a little more than a stone’s throw away on a hill, stood the high court, or gallows. Many years ago, a chaplain of Glis prepared two poor criminals who were to be executed for death and accompanied them to the place of execution. He exacted a promise from both that when he read Holy Mass the next morning in the Breitenweg chapel for their consolation, they should give him a clear sign whether things had gone well for them in eternity. Both promised to do so if it were at all possible. On the day after their execution, as he was about to read Mass in this chapel for the soul of the man who had been executed first—behold, an invisible hand set the crucifix down upon the altar beside the chalice, just as he was about to perform the Staffelgebet. ‘Thanks be to God,’ he thought to himself. ‘You surely died well!’ When the next day he wished to perform the devotion there for the second man and had just reached the chapel portal, a large, dreadful toad sat upon the lower ledge of a column and turned toward him. The nearer he came, the more it swelled and the larger it grew. ‘Ah, poor, unhappy man!’ the priest cried. ‘Is this the sign you are able to give me?’ The toad stared at him sorrowfully and grew ever larger. ‘Ah, poor man,’ the cleric cried with folded hands to the dreadful figure, ‘can I not help you at all?
