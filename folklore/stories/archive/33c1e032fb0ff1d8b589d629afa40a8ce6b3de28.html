As he descended the Homberg, he found the entire broad plain barred. He was just about to try to cross it when the night wind carried distant, resounding music to him. Little by little it became clearer, and he heard that it was a march. Astonished, Joseph Hochreuter stood still and gazed expectantly up the broad military road. Soon a mighty procession approached. At its head walked small boys, followed by larger ones, and larger ones again. Thus they kept increasing in size like the steps of a staircase. They advanced in pairs. Each pair walked close along the double green hedge at equal intervals from the next, leaving the road open between them. These boys were dressed like pages, but all wore identical little black-and-white coats. After them came a company of men dressed entirely in black, and directly behind it marched a band, blowing its horns, trombones, and trumpets with tremendous force as it passed. Then, at a graceful distance, came a fine gentleman’s coach drawn by six coal-black horses. It was closed, yet a number of ladies and gentlemen could be seen inside. A company of tiny girls followed the coach, then larger girls, and larger ones again, up to full-grown maidens. All wore snow-white dresses and likewise walked in pairs along the hedge. A company of women, all clad in black cloaks, brought up the rear. Everything advanced so delicately and finely that not a sound could be heard except the music, to whose measure all marched. Not a speck of dust rose from the road. It was as if the entire strange procession were moving over snow. It also seemed as though a gentle night wind had carried the procession there and was now wafting it past.
