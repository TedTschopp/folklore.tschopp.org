If one walks to the highest Alphütten, where the cattle are not driven until the beginning of August, and descends toward the blue- and green-shimmering bands of glacier, one reaches a place where weathering has broken the rock into hundreds and thousands of separate blocks and slabs. Such a place of destruction is called a Felsenmeer. The blocks lie crisscrossed in confusion. Some are great cubes, some pyramids and slabs; some stand upright, while others lie horizontally like stone tables. This last form is the most common, and so the place is also called “zen Tischen.” A great treasure has lain hidden here since ancient times. Old herdsmen boldly claim to have seen it, especially around sunset. The silver that shone and glittered upon these slabs certainly tempted them, but they were always frightened away by a noblewoman in snow-white clothes who sat beside it and could only be an evil spirit.", "Once a poor Senne, a Quatemberkind who knew nothing of these treasures or the white woman, passed the slabs while going after a lost sheep. Not far from him a butterfly with great golden wings fluttered up. He snatched at it and thought he already held it in his hand when it escaped again and hurriedly flew away. He chased it, and the butterfly vanished into a crevice between the tables. The Senne suddenly forgot the butterfly, for he saw that the stone slabs were covered in shimmering linen upon which lay as much silver treasure as any heart could desire. The brilliance of these precious objects dazzled his eyes so badly that he had to raise a hand before them—and now, how he started! Very near him a beautiful woman sat upon one of the tables. To her left lay a heap of gold and to her right a heap of Silberlinge. She shifted the gold to the right and the silver to the left and was so absorbed in moving them to and fro that she seemed not to notice him. Her dark, heavy hair was bound in a knot, and her clothes, spun from the finest silk, shone like moonlight. Around her neck she wore a golden chain that flashed like dewdrops in a ray of morning sun. Now she opened her black eyes, fixed them upon him, and beckoned him to come nearer, but the strange, dazzling-white marble figure made him shudder. He struck his forehead with his fist to see whether he was awake or dreaming. “Surely I may not flee from a woman; that would be too schämig.” And so he stepped closer and greeted her cordially. Only then did he see that her dress was strewn with diamonds, which glittered like ice crystals and sparkled in every color of the glacier and the rainbow. Never, even in a dream, had he seen anything so noble and beautiful.", "The cold cast of the woman's face disappeared. He took flight and fairly flew across the slabs toward the Alp.
