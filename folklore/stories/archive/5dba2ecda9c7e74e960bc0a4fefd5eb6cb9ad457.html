He did not have to wait very long before one of the red-coated creatures appeared. It behaved most strangely. A few paces from the bait, it stood upon its hind legs, danced, and performed all kinds of antics. The hunter did not like this. He thought it was no proper fox and went away. Although he passed very near the curious animal, it did not so much as twitch. ‘Sit there now for as long as you like,’ he said to himself and went home. Five or six years later, while staying for business in a distant region, he came to an inn, where he was welcomed very kindly and served an abundant and excellent meal. When he wished to pay his account the next morning, the innkeeper looked at him and asked, ‘Did you not, in such-and-such a year, on such-and-such an evening, lie in wait for foxes at Seelisberg in Uri and spare a little fox that presented itself before you and performed its capers?’ The man from Seelisberg had to say yes, and the friendly innkeeper added, ‘It was well that you did not shoot, or I would no longer be alive.
