For eighteen months, every approach to the castle had already been watched with the greatest care, and no one received permission either to enter or to leave. For a long time, the surrender of the fortress had been expected daily, because people claimed to have found the occupants leaner and more wretched even in the first months. — Then one morning, as the rising sun shone so pleasantly upon the castle, everyone's eyes again looked up toward it with the greatest expectation and hope, and behold! A row of the finest, freshest hams hung there before the windows, and with crystal-clear wine the garrison mockingly drank to the health of the astonished besiegers. — The courage of the Upper Valais forces was sorely tested. — Yet they did not lose it; they doubled their vigilance and searched anew for hidden entrances. They found one that led through the mountain into the Lötschental, from which the garrison had been abundantly supplied with provisions. This enraged the Upper Valais people so greatly that they made the people of Lötschen subject to the upper Zehnden.
