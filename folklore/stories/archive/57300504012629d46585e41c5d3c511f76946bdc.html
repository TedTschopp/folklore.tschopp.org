He possessed two hundred of the finest Grattiere, which he himself had tamed so that every morning and evening they came into the cave of their own accord and allowed themselves to be milked. A poor, one-eyed little boy from the valley who herded goats found refuge and food in the cave during bad weather. The Gemskäslein were so sweet that they melted in one’s mouth, he once told his brother. The brother asked how they were made. That was the wild little man’s secret, the child answered. Whenever the cheesemaking began, he always had to crawl beneath a pile of heather. Then the little man sang, “Little One-Eye, fall asleep,” and whenever he awoke, the Gemskäslein was finished. When the treacherous brother heard this, he forced the little boy to exchange clothes with him. Then, wearing his brother’s clothes, he went into the wild little man’s cave himself and sat upon the heather. The cave looked very clean. Green heather was spread over the floor, and all around, on a stone ledge, stood small Gebsen made of fir wood and filled with chamois milk; no kettle or hearth could be seen anywhere. The wild little man took the boy for his Little One-Eye, had him crawl beneath the heather on which he sat in the corner, and sang, “Little One-Eye, fall asleep.” The sly boy closed one eye and peered out from beneath the heather with the other. But when the little man noticed the willfully open eye, he flew into a rage and hurled the Gebsen and their contents at the boy’s head.
