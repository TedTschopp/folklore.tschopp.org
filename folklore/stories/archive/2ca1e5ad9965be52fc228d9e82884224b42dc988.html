In addition to his Castelen Castle, which lies in a Jura valley above Schinznach toward the Aare, he had purchased the Bilger alpine dairy farms. These are situated on the Jura slope facing the upper Fricktal at the height of the watershed and today form eight different farmsteads, divided into the two groups of the inner and outer Bilger. The farms of the inner Bilger, to which the following story belongs, are called in sequence Kohlwald, Kalkdarren, Kammer, Saal, Gottlisacker, and Böpperler. The Wölfliswil stream rises among them, flows down toward Frick as the Oerken, and contains within it the valley spirit called the Oerkentier.", "Beside the last-named farm, Böpperler, which now belongs to a man named Lenzi, is the site of the old alpine dairy house that centuries ago stood alone in this mountain solitude. Here Rudolf von Erlach, in his old age, used to seek rest and peace through summer and autumn. Two servants and two great hunting dogs formed his entire company, and no one disturbed him in this seclusion except when the uninvited Rudenz sometimes appeared. Rudenz was his son-in-law and lived across the Aare in the Rueder valley at Rued Castle. He had received Erlach’s only daughter as his wife and great wealth with her, but he was a drunkard who, after squandering and gambling away his property and domestic happiness, became a burden upon his aged father-in-law. The old man had already been forced to answer for the frivolous spendthrift repeatedly. On this autumn day, Rudenz appeared here in the heights with the same concern once again. When he entered the room, Erlach had just been taking his midday rest and still lay upon the camp bed, with his two mastiffs before him. No one else was on the farm; the two servants were hunting outside in the forest.", "Rudenz began with his familiar demand, repeated it, and was repeatedly and firmly refused. He then sprang for Erlach’s heroic sword, which hung on the wall above the bed, and struck the old man’s head off with one blow. He fled down the mountain toward the Aare and intended to swim across, but the two mastiffs pursued him howling and blocked him from the bank everywhere. Thus he could not reach Rued Castle and turned in flight back toward the mountain. With the dogs always at his heels, he reached the hiding place of an isolated hay barn near the inner Bilger farms, threw the door shut, and concealed himself in the hay. The dogs tracked him down even here. With bared teeth, they kept watch outside the hut, and their howling penetrated the entire mountain; within a very short time, it was bound to draw Rudolf’s two servants up to the place. Seeing himself trapped and betrayed, Rudenz hanged himself.", "This barn lies above the house now belonging to the farmer Bitterli.
