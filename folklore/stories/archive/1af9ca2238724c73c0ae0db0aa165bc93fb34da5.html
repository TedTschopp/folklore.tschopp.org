For this reason, repeated ordinances are found requiring millet to be served on festive and joyful days. In 1587, the parishioners of Risch in the canton of Zug had a dispute with their priest. Among other things, they demanded that, according to ancient custom, he furnish their poor with a kettle full of millet on Saint Verena’s Day. At Cham, the old custom of distributing millet ended in 1798. It had formerly taken place on the Day of the Ten Thousand Knights, and thirteen kettles were cooked. The woman recognized by the administrator as the best cook received a pair of red stockings. At the hospital in the city of Zug, it was ordained that millet be cooked on the eve of the four principal feasts. But the name Hirsmontag especially attests to the grain’s general popularity, as does the well-known voyage made by the people of Zurich carrying millet porridge to Strasbourg.
