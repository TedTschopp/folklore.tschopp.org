It is said not to be altogether unlike an ordinary drum, except that its notes are much duller and more melancholy and the marches it plays sound very old-fashioned. Remarkably, the drumbeats are heard much more clearly if one listens only casually and indifferently. A man of fifty who died two years ago heard the Totentrommel beating in the mountains so often that he could easily whistle its melodies afterward. People believe that the trains and processions of the deceased wander about to this drumming. Thus the Totentrommel never remains still in the same place but can be heard moving ever farther onward. Much is told everywhere about such processions of the dead: how the deceased keep to certain paths and roads that must not be blocked or closed with wood or stones; how, in huts and stables that stand too near the course of their procession, they trouble people and livestock; and so forth. I do not wish to repeat here all that has been heard and told, but shall relate just one more procession that an upright young man is once said to have witnessed. He lived alone and apart from all other people in his mountain house. He usually spent the long winter evenings without a light, praying the rosary behind the warm room stove. On a dreadful winter night, just as the winds were fiercely driving about the freshly fallen snow and he knew himself to be entirely alone in the little village, he suddenly heard the footsteps of many people passing before his windows. In all haste he slipped out from behind the stove and saw with astonishment a great crowd of people hurrying past. He recognized no one, but noticed that the last person carried a removed stocking on his shoulder. With horror he then realized that he himself was carrying just such a stocking on his shoulder. In coming out from behind the stove he had torn it from the wall and, without noticing, had loaded it upon himself.
