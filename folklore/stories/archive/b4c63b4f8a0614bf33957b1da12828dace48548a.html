Often, when they wished to feed their livestock in the morning, they found everything already done: the stable was cleaned, the milk tub rinsed, the Richter and funnel returned to their place—in short, everything put in order and already made ready again for the evening. Naturally, they tried to learn who their unknown helper was and watched and waited at every entrance. It did not take long before they saw a tiny little man enter the barn. There, he crawled through the feed opening into the cattle stable; and once he had quietly completed his work inside, he nimbly took the same way back and vanished again. The Sennen were greatly delighted by this, and because a certain sense of reverence told them that they must not thank him orally, they had a neat Senn costume made and laid it in the stable for the little one. At his next appearance, the dwarf took the new Senn shirt down from the little bench, adjusted the leather cap, and tried it this way and that. Then he looked at himself in the little mirror set out for him and, proud of his new uniform, called again and again: ‘Now I no longer want to be a Senn!’", "After this incident, he was no longer to be found in Muri but hired himself out in the village of Buttwil. Once, the farmer there told his wife: ‘All the work in stable and barn is always done before daybreak; the hay does not diminish, my cows give the best milk and bear the fattest calves. We must watch for ourselves how this happens. Tonight, we shall hide in the barn opening, where we can see everything without being seen.’ They did so. Soon, a rustling and swishing was heard coming down over the top of the haystack; then came the patter of a child's steps, and at once a tiny little man sprang down by the climbing hook, shook the hay from his hair, and stood there in the middle of the threshing floor—but alas, stark naked. He began pulling hay, then curried the livestock, and all this went so neatly and with lightning speed that the little man was finished in an instant and had already vanished again. The farmwife was seized by heartfelt pity, for it was a hard winter and just around New Year. ‘We should have trousers and a Wamms made for him,’ she said. It was done, and on New Year's Day, the finished clothing was laid for the little man in the hayloft.
