It belonged to the herdsman Segnes, who received it from his dying father on condition that he care for his mother. His house stood down in the valley. But Sardona, the Senn's beloved, also lived down there on the Rathausboden. She was a beautiful, wealthy girl who owned the Alp Kratzere above on the Muttentalergrat. The girl was proud beyond measure. The herdsman's old mother did not like this and therefore did everything she could to break off their love affair. In vain! The black eyes on the Rathausboden were more powerful than the mother's word. The two lovers discussed how they might get the old woman out of the way.", "When spring came to the land, Segnes climbed to the Alp with his livestock. His mother could not follow him, for she was old and ill. Her food soon ran out, but her son remained on the Alp and sent her nothing. How was she to sustain her life? Filled with bitter grief, she resolved, if possible, to climb to the Alp and demand help and care from her son. Slowly, near death, she dragged herself up the mountain. But two fiery eyes had spotted her; unnoticed, along hidden paths, Sardona crept after her. The mother reached the hut and begged her son for a little piece of bread; she also threatened him with God's punishment for forgetting his duty and for his wickedness. Mockingly, the son fetched from the Scherm a bowl full of liquid manure and set it before his mother. She complained no more; with mortal pain in her breast, she turned to leave and die below in the valley. Just then the proud girl from the Rathausboden came striding haughtily forward and passed the pale old woman contemptuously. Segnes saw his beloved coming, hurriedly fetched several wheels of cheese from the cellar, and laid them in the bride's path so that she would not soil her shoes. Maliciously, he called after his mother, ‘That is a different kind of visitor!’ But she turned and spoke with her last strength: ‘Then remain together always and forever!’", "The mother had spoken; Heaven had heard! It began to rain and rained all day; in the evening it began to snow and snowed through the entire night. The next morning no hut and no herd could be seen; a mighty glacier had covered every living thing, and it covers them still today. Every year, however, on the day the outrage was committed, a crevasse opens in the glacier, and the accursed ones climb down to its edge and call imploringly to the mother so that she might take back the evil words. They listen and listen in every direction to learn whether the redeeming voice might be heard. Until now it has always been in vain; again and again they must return to the cold prison, and again and again the icy gates close. Sometimes the Sauren looks so gloomily across the land and shakes its snowy locks; then avalanches plunge down over its brow and thunder toward the glacier. It is angry that it too must bear the curse. On dark stormy nights, the laments of the accursed can also be heard from within the glacier. The foreign traveler, of course, thinks it is the whistling of the wind in the firs and rocky pinnacles or the cracking of bursting ice; but one who knows the country knows better and shudders to remember the guilt that demanded such punishment.\nF.
