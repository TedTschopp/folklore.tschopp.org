An old hut stands in Sagenboden, where the neighboring cattle herders often gathered in the evening to chat away a couple of idle hours or pass them with Tarokenspiel. They were frequently joined by potash burners, collectors of herbs and resin, or other people who had business in the mountains. From time to time, a strange little man appeared among the mixed company. He had a pale-yellow complexion; ash-gray, blinking, deep-set eyes; red, bushy hair; a green cap on his head; and wore a gray smock, long tight trousers of light-brown cloth, and short boots. He always held a fiddle beneath his left arm, which is why people called him the Spielmännlein. He usually remained quite quiet and still in a corner, where he crouched like a cat, or warmed himself by the fire in a half-kneeling, stooped posture. If they wanted to cheer him up, they gave him food and drink. He would then thank them in a strange foreign language of which they could understand only a few words, and finally he would begin to fiddle all kinds of old and new dances and songs, making one's heart leap for joy along with one's feet, as when a person can dance with his sweetheart at a Kiltgang, a wedding, or a Kilbe. Indeed, the Spielmännlein could entertain the Sennen so delightfully that they often forgot their duty and obligations, which then also led to disputes and bloody quarrels in the Stafeln.
