It is said to lie buried beneath the Riedmatte, a marshy meadow crossed by a stream at the foot of the slope. At various times the owner of the meadowland has dug up fragments of masonry there, even decaying pieces of parchment, and, as recently as 1852, a cylindrical vessel. The vessel was copper, one foot high and half a foot in diameter. It was completely filled with coins of different sizes and shapes: thick, angular, and concave, roughly stamped, and without any writing or numbers. Most pieces showed the figure of a man or an animal. Because only about ten of them were silver, the money was divided on the spot among the laborers and other people who had come out of curiosity.", "Thus one might perhaps still find one or another of those pieces among the farmers of Niederwil and Nesselnbach.", "A wood once adjoined this meadow and the Isenbühl slope. When it was completely felled about seventy years ago, some of the woodcutters saw an ancient woman making her way through the thicket. She wore short clothes and a broad hat, with a little basket on her arm and a rosary in her hand. At the same time, a similar woman approached two woodcutters who were eating their supper and sat down silently between them. The workers looked at each other in amazement but likewise did not dare address her. She then vanished to the sound of heavy horses’ hooves. That was the Heumüeterli.
