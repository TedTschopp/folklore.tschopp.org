One July day he came from the Zofingen region by way of Adelboden and rode hard toward Olten, hoping to reach the little town before a threatening storm broke. ‘Keep straight on,’ he shouted to his outriders. ‘A few sheaves do not matter!’ So with the entire retinue they galloped through the middle of the ripe crops toward Olten's Aare bridge. Then the earth trembled from a thunderclap, and at the same moment Froburg Castle on the heights of the Hauenstein Pass burst into flame, as though every lightning bolt in heaven were raining down upon it. ‘What is that?’ the startled count cried when he saw the fire blazing above in the Jura. ‘Your castle is going up in flames!’ the hoarse voice of an old woman answered. She stood before him on the town's Stalden, near the arch of the covered Aare bridge. From her mocking tone, he recognized the woman at once. That very morning, as he rode through to Aarburg, she had foretold disaster for him at the same place. ‘Then,’ the criminal cried furiously, ‘the people shall build me a new castle until no plow is moving anywhere in the land. And where mortar does not serve, peasant blood will!’ But at that very instant a lightning bolt tore him from his horse. When his squires looked after him, he already lay lifeless on the ground. They woke the nearest residents and, when no one would open a door, carried the body into the old church, the sole remaining building among the many houses the knightly line had once owned there. He was laid out and buried there. On the slope by the bridge, a public inscription declared that the Froburg line had once died out on that spot. The inscription was later replaced by a painting showing the count thrown beneath his horse by the lightning bolt. An iron wire grille protects it. Weather and time have faded it. Froburg Castle was never rebuilt. Today, a handsome and much-visited Sennhof stands on its site.", "The Basel chronicles have the Count of Froburg perish together with his castle at the time when the earthquake in the year 1356 buried the entire city of Basel and reduced 84 fortified castles and strongholds in the surrounding country to heaps of rubble. Nevertheless, the earthquake left hardly any castle with such great marks of destruction as his.
