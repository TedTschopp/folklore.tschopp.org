Some such fiery spirits have appeared to living people in such a way that one could distinctly see the human figure and shape of a dead, already withered and emaciated human body, some directly before their eyes. It looked as if a burning light or fire stood behind a wicker Zeine, with the fire striking out through the body—through the sides, ribs, eyes, mouth, nose, and ears. When the flames had passed, the body remained lying there, glowing like a burned-out stump in a Rütti. Some people encountered them on the road at night, at first like a fiery ball before their feet and then suddenly flaring up like a great fire; in such a flare, some distinctly saw the form or shape of a person amid the flames. The common folk firmly believe that people who, during their lives, cheated their closest companions or neighbors by moving the Marchen are tormented in this way after death and must wander and show themselves at the places where the offense occurred. That is why two or three are sometimes seen running toward and striking against one another in blazing fire, so that it produces a great fire and a roaring crackle. Sometimes a third comes between them. It begins with a small glow, like a little blue light, then suddenly shoots up and makes a fire, wandering and darting back and forth. It is true that not everyone can see such things—or at least anything so clearly visible—as happened to me when someone pointed them out, particularly, almost as a rule, when the night was at its darkest or the stars seemed to announce a change in the weather. I had indeed let the matter rest in its customary belief and afterward still always held some doubt, until at last I had ample opportunity to observe it continuously for a full two hours—more than enough to put all doubt to rest. This happened on the twenty-third day of December in the year 1609, when I, together with persons of high rank and honor from this city and in good company, crossed our lake at night from Bürgen and onward across the Trichter. The boatmen first called the matter to our attention. There, along the lakeshore in the jurisdiction of Horw at Kestenbaum and Langensand, we saw first three or four of these Züsler, which repeatedly rose from a small glow into a fire and hovered around one another, in places where there were neither houses nor people walking with lights at night. Farther down beneath the rise, three more appeared not far from one another, with the same motion and alteration. The boatmen said that a Hagmarch had also been moved at that same place, adding that if the hedge were put back where it belonged, those souls might come to rest. Some of these Züsler had indicated as much to the living by beckoning and gesturing, and afterward, when the matter had been set right again in the old way, they were seen no more. Farther below, we saw another that suddenly flared up at the full height of the forest in Birchegg, where in such a wild and rugged woodland neither houses nor barns are to be found, and this happened a third time. Finally, there was yet another near Tribschen, closest to the city. It suddenly blazed up with the greatest fire, greater than any of the others, and, so far as one could discern, it hovered over the lake. Now it rose into the air in a crooked sweep like the Rosen; now it lowered itself again onto the lake and also traveled up the lake over the water, always ascending along the left side. It then swung around so that the stream of its radiance reached across the water all the way to our boat, although a very great distance lay between us. Then, in the blink of an eye, we saw it shoot across the lake like a bolt from a crossbow to the shore at Kirsiten, two hours away, where it arrived with extraordinary speed and continued burning in our sight for a long time.
