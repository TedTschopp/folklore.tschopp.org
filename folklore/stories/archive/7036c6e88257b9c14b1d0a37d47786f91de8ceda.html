There were metal mines at the Rothorn that were still worked in the seventeenth century.", "The town of Plurs in the Veltlin, then a subject territory of Rhaetia, operated these mines. In the year 1618, Plurs was buried by a landslide. It was a rich little town between Cleven and the present Graubünden border at the mouth of the Bergell valley. But the people of Plurs had not become so rich by honest means. As legend tells, they were allied with subterranean powers, which caused a jug full of pure gold to flow for them each day in a mine at the Rothorn. From this came their wealth, which they used very badly, only for gluttony, luxury, and lust. But their hour was to come. In the year 1618, a landslide covered Plurs with all its treasures, and not a mouse escaped. Only a Bündner Säumer, a Graubünden pack-horse driver, was miraculously saved. He arrived in the town with his packhorses and wished to stable them. But the lead horse broke away, and the others followed it. He hurried after them and brought them back. A second time the lead horse broke away, and the other horses followed. He caught them a second time and returned them. But the lead horse made its way out a third time and hurried at a slanting gait toward the Bergell, with the remaining horses following on their iron-shod hooves. The Säumer then thought better of the matter, let his horses trot, and went the same way with them. The following morning Plurs was no more. From that day the gold spring at the Rothorn had also run dry, and no one has found it again, although much gold is still in the mountain. Since that time, the country around the Rothorn has also been uncanny, probably because the buried people of Plurs wander there as spirits. One place on the Arosa sheep alp in particular was often visited by evil spirits and witches, and witches’ dances are frequently held there at night. In a stone at that place one can still clearly see the hoofprint of a billy goat and the footprint of a witch who dismounted there from the goat she had ridden to the dance." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Bündner Säumer”: a pack-horse driver from Graubünden who transported goods across Alpine routes; his occupational attention to the lead horse’s repeated flight is what saves him from the destruction of Plurs." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
