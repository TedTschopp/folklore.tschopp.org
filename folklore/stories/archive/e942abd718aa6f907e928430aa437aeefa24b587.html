To the northeast, the Olden descends like a valley and is grazed for three weeks each summer. The Oldenbach rises there and then joins the Saane. The smaller peak, the Oldenberg, is said formerly to have been inhabited by wild Bergleute. These guarded the herders' cattle, especially at the most dangerous places. As payment, people set Nidel with curdled milk or with Ziger in vessels upon the roofs, which the beings fetched and consumed when no one was nearby. Thus these wild ones were good guardians of the livestock for a long time, and no animal was ever harmed.", "Once, however, malicious people threw dung into one or more of the vessels left for the wild ones. From that time, the Bergleute no longer guarded the livestock and rarely or never showed themselves, and soon cattle met with accidents here and there. A real disruption also entered the herding: in the evenings, the livestock no longer came back in good order, often several animals remained out, and once all their cows were drawn in one procession down the western slope into the Oldental, beyond the Olden and onto the steep slopes of the Homades, which stand at dizzying height above the so-called Bödemli and the bed of the Oldenbach. They remained there until the third day. At times they could be seen, but usually in places that scarcely anyone could reach. After three days, they finally returned, exhausted and terribly thin, almost ergustet, without milk, and between their toes they had Grangel, bad flesh. The livestock are said to have made such a procession repeatedly.", "This caused the people grief and discouragement, and they greatly wished to be rid of the misfortune that had struck them after losing their guardians. For a long time, they knew no remedy, but at last they went to Capuchins for advice, and the friars recommended three things. I no longer know one of them precisely; I believe they were to wash the cows' feet while praying. Then they were to appoint one Sunday each summer on which they did good for the poor from the yield of their cows, and they were never to permit games or dancing on a Sunday of the Bergfest. Finally, they were also to keep a black rooster on the mountain. They followed these instructions, and the disorder among their livestock ceased.
