In many places, old people used to say of this rolling of stones, ‘The beggars are rolling their food back down!’ When someone asked why they said that, they answered: ‘It comes from this: many beggars collect more food and alms than they need, let it grow moldy, and then throw it away. Indeed, there are such shameless beggars that the alms they receive are far too poor for them, and they throw away the pieces of bread or cheese. But God does not leave this unpunished, because many people poor in their own homes give such beggars alms saved from their own mouths and which they themselves otherwise need most urgently. After death, therefore, such ungrateful beggars who have misused and despised God’s gifts must gather all the pieces they threw away. Those pieces are as heavy as great stones, and the beggars must carry them up onto the high mountains. When they are almost at the top, all the heavy beggars’ food rolls back down the mountain into the valley floor. This happens again and again, and they must carry it back up as many times as there were crumbs in the alms they scorned. That is why, when rockfalls descend or an avalanche has rolled through a ravine, people say: “The beggars are rolling their food back down,” or “They have emptied out the sack of provisions again.”’" ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
