Its water did not differ from that of other small mountain lakes. But near its shore lived a maiden who had given her heart to a shepherd boy. On bright moonlit nights, the two often went to the Alpine lake surrounded by ancient fir trees, upon which the boy had placed a boat for amusement. On the silver waters, they then dreamed away pleasant hours of their young lives. But once, while the boy was high in the Flühe trying to gather hay in a Seiltuch, he fell over a rock face to his death. From that time on, the maiden was inconsolable. At the midnight hour, she often crept to the hidden Seelein, sometimes imploring Heaven in heartrending lamentation to return her beloved, or quarreling with it over its cruelty. Thus, little by little, the child's senses became confused. Her parents' admonition to give up the nightly visits was in vain. A mysterious power drew the unfortunate girl back there again and again. But one morning, boat and boatwoman were found at the bottom of the water. From that hour on, the Seelein had assumed a deep-blue color.
