The wife of Urban Waldmaier in Münchwilen used to begin this way when she wished to launch into one of her principal stories, which runs as follows:", "One summer afternoon she was working in the vineyard when a magpie repeatedly flew up, perched on the nearest vine stake, and screamed fiercely at her. The woman knew well that in such a case the best thing was to recite an Ave Maria silently in order to avert the threatened misfortune. But when the magpie came back to her crying once again, she became frightened, gathered her things, and hurried home. On the way to her house she met the boy, her young farmhand, who had just driven the oxen down the mountain from the hay barn and looked so dazed and pale that he might still be in the grip of the greatest distress. The affair could no longer be concealed, and the boy had to tell her every detail. ‘When we were trying to drive our wagon full of hay into the Chrumbe’s barn up there,’ the boy said, ‘where the way rises so steeply through the gate, the master stood beside the wagon between the wheels while I drove the bulls from the front, so that he could help by lifting. With a bound the wagon went through the entrance. But the master was wedged so tightly between the wagon wheel and the ladder to the upper threshing floor that in the next minute he would have been crushed against the fodder wall if the driven bulls had made only one more movement forward. The Chrumbe saw this. Lame in both feet, he stood at the rear of the barn on his two crutches. Quickly he struck the bulls such a blow with a crutch on the Schnorren, the front of their heads, that at this decisive moment they stopped as though nailed in place. Thank God, nothing happened to the master, and now the hay is under cover as well!’" ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Schnorren”: a regional livestock term glossed by the source as the Vorderkopf, the front part of the bulls’ heads or muzzles; the crutch-blow there stops them at the critical instant." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
