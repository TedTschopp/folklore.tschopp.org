Without fear for honor or life, in the times preceding the Battle of Morgarten they sided with no one. They robbed without distinction and reveled in it. Werner Rycha is said to have been the last Wildenburger. One day, from the battlements of his castle, he saw the beautiful Elsener daughter crossing the bridge toward Wulflingen. He had her seized. She was deaf to every art of seduction and to the threats of an aroused passion. She would love only in freedom, and by naming a place and hour to the lecher, she allowed him to hope that she would yield. She used her freedom, however, to have her honor defended through vengeance. Her father put on his daughter's clothes and cloak and verbutzt and concealed himself—and his battle-ax—as well as he could. Werner was already waiting, saw the clothing of his beloved, and hurried—to his death. Elsener cut off the Wildenburger's thigh, stuck it upon his Helparte, hurried to the city, and called for vengeance. The citizens went out with him, stormed the castle, and razed it. For a long time, Werner's bloody shade howled over the ruins at midnight. Anyone who dug for the hidden treasures was strangled by him or hurled over the rocks. In more recent times, Ammann and council prohibited digging.
