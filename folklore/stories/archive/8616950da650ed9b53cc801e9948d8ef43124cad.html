A fahrender Schüler came there and asked whether he might come and go for about 14 days. He was permitted to do so. The wanderer searched along the cliffs, gathering unusual stones and herbs. After 14 days, he wished to pay and leave. The hospitable Senn accepted nothing, saying that the man had stayed with him only a short time. The stranger nevertheless wanted to give his host something that might be useful: either the ability to play music so that no one could surpass him, the ability to gamble—to Neesen—so that he would always win, or strength enough. I have no time for playing music, the Senn thought, because I would have to travel; by gambling, I would have to take other people's money, so I choose strength enough. The fahrender Schüler gave him a little Kraftwurzel. He had to bite it three times before sunrise to possess sufficient strength for the day. A few days later, the Senn wanted to test whether there was anything to the matter. Before sunrise, he bit the root and then tried to lift the hut's foundation sill. He raised it with ease. Although only a small fellow, he tested his strength against the best Schwinger and threw every one of them cleanly, leaving only one deliberately undefeated. Then the people of Entlebuch had to measure themselves against the powerful, strong Schwinger of the Bernese Oberland. Every Entlebucher was thrown, but Lustenberger's small, strong son had not yet wrestled. The Entlebuch men said they did not yet consider themselves defeated, for they still had a Geissbub with them; only if he too were overcome would they concede. The Oberlanders laughed and mocked the little fellow, saying they would crush him. The Entlebuch men insisted, and the second-best Bernese wrestler had to face him. Lustenberger did not need his full strength and dragged the Oberlander back and forth until the man spat blood. He then said the best wrestler should try, for this boy had untamable strength. The champion was now thrown to the ground as if in play and with perfect ease.", "Back at home, they intended to build and needed to move a heavy sill. Two horses could scarcely manage it.
