Accordingly, the reports about its essential nature and meaning vary greatly. It is generally accepted, however, that its fixed dwelling place is the Oerken and that it is itself the spirit of a murdered man. The latter is sometimes said to be a farmer from Wölfliswil whom the Swedes captured, filled with liquid manure, tied to a horse's tail, and dragged back and forth in the stream until he gave up the ghost. Conversely, it is sometimes regarded as a Swede whom the farmers are said to have killed in a similarly agonizing manner. When the Swedes advanced plundering from the conquered towns of Rheinfelden and Laufenburg into these small mountain valleys as well, many of them were killed by the watchful Jura farmers because they roamed about only in smaller, disorderly bands. Thus a severely wounded Swede lay here beside the road near the Oerken stream. The farmers leaped upon him and smashed his limbs still further. He begged for mercy. He would have to die, he said, but despite all his torments he could not die before sunset. They should therefore abuse him no further. But they merely mocked him for this. And when he still lived after their repeated blows, they finally tied him to a stake beneath the water of the shallow stream. Even that could not drown him; he is said truly to have died only after sunset.", "The spirit goes about both as an animal and as a man. The old mill hand at the mill in Gipf, near the village of Frick, once encountered a riderless horse standing across the road while he was returning home from Wittnau. He immediately thought of the Örkentier and therefore cautiously went around the horse without speaking a word. A few steps farther on it had already placed itself across his path again, and once more he had to go around it in the same fashion. Thus the two pursued their silent contest until they reached the appointed place marking the Örkentier's boundary. There it had to vanish, and the levelheaded mill hand had won. Sometimes it is said to have invisibly stopped the wheels of his mill cart. He would then walk around the wagon three times with frischem Feuer.
