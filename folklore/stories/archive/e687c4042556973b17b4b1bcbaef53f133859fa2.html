It is the last landmark testifying that the castle of the lords of Gafertschinken once stood here. History names no member of their line. Nevertheless, these ancient valley lords still keep the countryside in terror today. When threatening clouds gather over the mountains and black slugs appear along the hedges below in the valley, when toward nightfall the Hauri sends its lamenting cries through the valley, then whoever is outdoors flees beneath a secure roof. Even the livestock grow restless and race as if mad toward safe shelter. “The Tschinggenreiter are coming,” people then say. At midnight, a knight in a fiery red cloak is seen appearing high on horseback above the rocks. Hastily he takes a great horn from his hip and blows into it three times. Thereupon a scream arises that pierces marrow and bone. The storm wind roars in and blows down even the sturdiest firs; Marchsteine fly through the air, and the rocks crash and rumble so terribly that the bloodiest battle seems to be raging. Now a spectral procession begins to move, led by the red rider with glowing red eyes. Amid the howling air, the uncanny troop moves toward the highest tooth of the mountain range. It stops at the Rosengarten. Now a truly hellish din begins. With spear and pike, the nocturnal riders assail the rocks, break great blocks from them, and hurl them with dreadful force down the mountain gullies into the valley. If lightning suddenly illuminates the region amid this terrible clamor, one can see here and there in its glow the riders’ Trabanten waiting. Only when morning enters the valley does the dreadful procession disappear.
