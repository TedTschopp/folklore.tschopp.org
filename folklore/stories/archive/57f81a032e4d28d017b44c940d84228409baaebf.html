Once, at night, I passed the shooting range in Silenen. Then, down through the Teeltschi, a horrible black cat came straight in front of my feet and walked ahead of me. At first, I wanted to take her into my arms and pet her. But then I thought after all: No, do not do that; you never know! At night, all black cats are old witches. The cat was constantly under my feet, and at last I began to grow annoyed and said to myself: Now you are going to give her a little kick; surely she will walk faster then! I picked up a stone and meant to strike her with it. But good heavens! She had turned around! The devil behind her—how she arched her back! And her eyes! They gleamed like little lights. And how she hissed and spat, like a locomotive! I dropped that stone from my hand at once. A proper shudder rose through me, and my hair stood on end; it nearly lifted the cap from my head. From then on, the cat would no longer leave my feet. I could do whatever I wished, and by the time I climbed up to the Ellbogen chapel, I was sweating; the water ran out of me as from a Zeintli. ‘Now you are going to try,’ I then thought to myself, ‘whether you cannot drive this witch away after all. There are houses nearby now; if things go wrong, you can call for help.’ I picked up a stone and made a cross over it. But as soon as I made the cross, that damned cat was nowhere to be seen. Yes, she certainly tormented me that same evening!
