He possessed two hundred of the finest Grattiere, which he himself had tamed, so that morning and evening they came into the cave of their own accord and allowed themselves to be milked.—A poor, one-eyed little boy from the valley who herded goats found refuge and food in the cave during bad weather. The Gemskäslein were so sweet that they melted in one’s mouth, he once told his brother. His brother asked how they were made. That was the wild Mannli’s secret, the child answered; whenever the cheesemaking began, he always had to crawl beneath a pile of heather, and then the Mannli sang, “Little One-Eye, fall asleep.” Whenever he awoke, the little cheese was finished. When the treacherous brother heard this, he forced the little boy to exchange clothes with him. Then, wearing his brother’s clothes, he went into the wild Mannli’s cave himself and sat upon the heather.—The cave looked very clean. Green heather was spread over the floor, and all around, on a stone ledge, stood little Gebsen made of fir wood and filled with chamois milk; no kettle or hearth could be seen anywhere.—The wild Mannli took the boy for his Little One-Eye, had him crawl beneath the heather where the boy had been sitting in the corner, and sang, “Little One-Eye, fall asleep.” The sly boy closed one eye and peered out from beneath the heather with the other. But when the Mannli noticed the willfully open eye, he flew into a rage and hurled the Gebsen and their contents at the boy’s head.
