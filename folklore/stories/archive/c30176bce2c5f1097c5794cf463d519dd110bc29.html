It calls and weeps and groans until dawn. Anyone who knows what the voice means crosses himself, prays an Our Father, and quietly goes on his way. Everyone in the valley knows that it is the spirit of Jörgen's Änneli, who must remorsefully lament the dreadful end of an unhappy girl here until Judgment Day. Änneli was secretly betrothed to her father's servant Franz. But her father wished to give his child to a rich merchant. One day it happened that Franz and the merchant had to climb together to the Alp to inspect the herds. On the way, they were surprised by a terrible thunderstorm. At a precipitous Fluh, past which only a handbreadth-wide path ran, lightning struck the ground before them. The merchant was so frightened that he slipped and fell into the abyss. As he lay dying, he gave Franz his money belt with everything in it, for he had no one who could inherit from him. But suspicion fell upon Franz that he had killed the merchant. A Landtag was convened, and despite his declarations of innocence, the poor fellow was sentenced to death on the wheel. When Jörgen's Änneli saw the eye of her innocent beloved go dark after terrible torments, she left the village, and no one ever saw her again. But the people say that she quenched the burning pain of her breaking heart in the cool waters of the Engstligen.
