Because no one bought anything from her, she asked for lodging for the night. The housewife did not refuse, but told her to come out to the field and help set up the sheaves. Thus the two of them, the girl and the grandmother, reached the first narrow footpath, careful not to trample a blade of grass on either side. The grandmother went thoughtfully ahead, now and then asking the girl a question; the girl followed, answering politely and obligingly carrying the hand basket on her head. ‘Now where are you going? The path is over there,’ said the woman when she looked back at the peddler girl and noticed that she had left the footpath and intended to cross a field far to one side. ‘Oh!’ cried the girl. ‘Did you not see the man? How could you step right over him? Horror forced me aside; there behind you in the footpath he lies as though dead!’ The woman looked back, cried, ‘Lord Jesus God!’ and crossed herself, for she now truly saw a man lying in the path whom she had just stepped over as though blind. ‘I suppose I know that man,’ she then said more calmly. ‘My grandfather often described him to me, and that is exactly what he looks like. How can you still be lying here, you wicked man! His name must not be spoken, but there in the field he killed his neighbor with the blade of a spade and stole his property, and no one dared make the slightest sound because he was master of the village.
