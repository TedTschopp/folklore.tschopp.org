A mother there had an impudent son who feared nothing. A specter inhabited an old house in the neighborhood and caused great terror. The mother told people that she wished her son might encounter this specter; she hoped he would lose his insolence in the process. Arrangements were made. One evening the lad set out, and his mother gave him food so that he need not go hungry during the night. As the insolent fellow prepared soup in the haunted house, a frightful black Bäuzi appeared on the Rüesstili and threw a heap of soot down into the soup. But the lad did not take fright. ‘If you mean to be quiet,’ he called upward, ‘well and good; otherwise I am coming.’ The specter above now shook and shivered like someone trembling with cold and frost. ‘If you are cold, then come to the fire,’ the lad called. It came and sat beside the fire, and the lad, without flinching, cooked himself a second soup. When it was ready, he carried the specter into the parlor and invited it to eat with him. But it, apparently trembling, spilled the fine soup. The lad struck its fingers with the spoon and told it to show proper care for the food. When he tapped it on the fingers for the third time, it suddenly stood there dressed entirely in white and said that he had now redeemed it. It had been forced to wander there for thirty years. As a child it had always wantonly wasted food without being punished for it by its parents. And now it vanished.
