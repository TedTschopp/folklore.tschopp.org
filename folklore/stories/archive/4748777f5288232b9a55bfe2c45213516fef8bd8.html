A witch has inhabited one of them for centuries. This witch begins her activity at nightfall and appears in the form of a cat with large, fiery eyes. She pounds terribly upon all the barn’s walls, rattles the cow chains, and drives the cattle into such fear that they will not eat—and at times when one believes there is least reason to fear her. Sometimes she also tears entire pieces from the wall with a dreadful crash, yet the next morning the wall is whole again and everything quiet. Her raging can be heard far away, and people pass the barn only with the greatest reluctance. More than once, this cat wedged herself so tightly into a cow’s neck-ring that the cow threatened to suffocate; but if the farmer who owned the barn came near, she whisked away and could no longer be seen. The farmer lay in wait for her several times but never caught her until one occasion when she was tired from excessive crashing and raging and once more laid herself in a cow’s neck-chain. Her exhaustion kept her from noticing the farmer lurking in hiding. He approached quietly and struck away the witch’s lower jaw with a small hand-axe. She gave a faint cry, staggered only briefly, and quickly became invisible.—The next day, when the man went to the Mayerhof, he learned of the misfortune that had befallen an old woman who lived nearby but was liked by no one. He was told that an unknown hand had beaten her so badly the previous day that she died that same day.
