On Sundays he often neglected divine service or attended it only fleetingly from the churchyard so that he could devote himself wholly to his wild passion for hunting. After his death, people wished to bury him in the parish church’s cemetery, which formerly stood upon the hill where the chapel of Dauda still stands. But the horses could not or would not draw his body beyond the spot on that hill where an old moss-covered wooden cross still stands, and so they had to bury him there. For a long time after his death, Chilier’s spirit roamed as a wild hunter, especially on the nights from Saturday to Sunday during hunting season. As soon as the Betglocke had rung, the blare of hunting horns, the hunter’s “Ho-ha-ho,” and the baying of hounds could be heard in the forests and steep slopes above Grandvillars. He usually rested on a Voralp there to gather his scattered dogs and store the slain game in a rock cave. The Sennen could scarcely endure staying there. If they did not keep completely quiet, he laid waste to everything in the outer enclosure, smashed the roof and fences, let his dogs dig deep holes in the ground, and finally tormented the cows invisibly in the Stafel’s stable until they bellowed loudly and gave only a little poor milk the next morning.", "A young, inexperienced cowherd was standing before the Alpine hut when the night hunter arrived and once boldly shouted back, “Ho, ha, ho, ha!” But the venture ended badly for him. The Sennen did not find and lift him until the following morning, for they had not dared step out of the Stafel during the storm of the hunt, while barking dogs seemed to besiege it throughout the night. The dogs did the fallen young Aelpler no harm, however, but merely sniffed and nosed him until they departed again with the restless hunter. On mornings after such nights, people usually found traces of blood all around, hair from chamois, hares, foxes, and badgers, and footprints of humans and dogs. Yet no slain game was ever found in the rock cellar.
