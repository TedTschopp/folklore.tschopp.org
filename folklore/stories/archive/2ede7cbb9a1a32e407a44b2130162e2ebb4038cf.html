Whenever they wanted to enjoy their well-earned rest at night, it began. There was a crashing as though whole wagonloads of gravel were being unloaded and flung around in the passage; then again it sounded as if a troop of cats were thundering up and down the stairs and raising a Choge-Gschrei. Every so often—especially on the heilige Öbed and during Advent—a black shadow, either a person or a dog, would always flit about. At times it came onto the bed and snatched the covers away from the people; then at times it vanished again. In the evening the good people often barely dared go to bed. From fear they sweated so much that the water simply ran down them.
