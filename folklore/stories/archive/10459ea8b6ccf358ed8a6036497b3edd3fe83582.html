At last the villagers agreed to give the wild herdsman a fine suit of clothes for his services.—This wild being never drove the cows all the way into the village, but only as far as a stable above it; from there he always returned to a forest cave, his dwelling. Every morning, however, he waited at the same stable until the people brought their cows there, and then he went with the herd to pasture; he never came down into the village.—One evening they laid out new clothing for him at this stable, and the following morning they secretly watched to see how he would receive their gift and how it would suit him. He came at the customary hour to drive the cows to pasture, saw the clothing, immediately took it in hand, and tried to put it on. For a long time he could not manage the new finery; only after many attempts did he complete the transformation.
