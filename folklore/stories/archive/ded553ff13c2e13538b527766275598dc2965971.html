Between it and the nearest mountainside is a meadow with two strange rings of grass lying one inside the other, between which the grass always grows taller and greener than inside or outside them. A surly servant was once plowing here and threw a clod of earth at a homeless boy who was not guiding the oxen well enough in front of him. Contrary to expectation, the boy immediately collapsed from the blow and lay dead. The servant buried him there and, back home, was able to explain the boy's absence convincingly enough. The boy was considered a runaway and forgotten. Many years later, the servant was cutting sheaves in the same field. The workers' conversation had turned to the proverb that nothing is spun so fine that it does not finally come into the sunlight. The servant refused to believe it true and said that many things might well have happened in this meadow that the sun had not yet brought to light. While speaking this way, he cut deeper into the ground with his sickle and struck a brittle bone lying there. It immediately began to bleed. In vain, he wiped off the sickle, covered the bone with earth, and pretended to have cut himself. Before everyone's eyes, so much blood burst from the decayed little bone that the fellow finally confessed the murder he had committed here long ago and was handed over to the judge. He was beheaded and buried in this field. The same story is told in the Fricktal as an event that occurred in the village of Wölfliswil." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
