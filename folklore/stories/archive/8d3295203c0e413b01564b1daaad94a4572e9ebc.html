Wilhelm, the handsomest of all the young men, alone remained at home. When people danced beneath the linden tree in the evening, he stayed away or watched without interest. Many a beautiful maiden, rich in virtue and worldly goods, beckoned kindly to him, but he coldly returned such greetings, scarcely offered thanks, and left the beauties standing. Once at Pentecost, the shepherds' horns and shawms sounded merrily. Everyone moved cheerfully in the round dance, and the girls, festively adorned with colorful wreaths, resembled a field of flowers in the wind. This time too, Wilhelm did not dance. Look! Suddenly, a girl with friendly, bright eyes stepped out of the bushes. Her clothing resembled the others' finery, but everything was silk and decorated with silver. Her golden braided locks flowed far down around her neck and breast. Now Wilhelm awoke, stood up, approached the stranger, and asked for her hand in the dance. She granted his request, and the two were seen dancing together all evening. Before the bell struck eleven, however, the beautiful dancer had vanished.", "The following Sunday, and whenever the horns called anew to the dance, the beauty appeared again. Sometimes she came as a shepherd girl wearing a little reaper's hat, sometimes as a young lady adorned with pearls and gemstones. Yet she always came with love and grace, and Wilhelm was always her partner. No one knew her name. One day, while her beloved accompanied her along the lakeshore, he pressed her to reveal herself. She told him that her father was a prince of the water. Sometimes he could be seen sitting upon the lake's waves. In the depths, it was wondrous: everything there gleamed, but all was utterly still and lonely. On the bottom where she lived stood tall towers and stately palaces with splendid gardens spreading before them. The water people in these subterranean buildings were covered with serpent scales, their beards and hair overgrown with reeds and shells. These monsters often listened at the shore, she said. Suddenly, they would spring onto land and seize youths and men who then had to serve as food for the lake creatures. If they caught girls, however, the water princes took them as wives. This had also happened to her mother, who had been looking for flowers along the shore when she was abducted. Her father was stern and cruel. If he ever learned that she associated with human beings, death would be her mildest punishment.", "The youth now waited every day by the lake for his water beauty. The depths surged wildly, but after a short while a reddened wave passed before the youth, who was clinging to a willow stump and leaning far out over the bank.
