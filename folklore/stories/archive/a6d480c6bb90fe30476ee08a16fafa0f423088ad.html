In the middle of the wood lies the Geissenrain, a hill into which an enchanted castle with all its treasures is said to have sunk. The few glasses of wedding wine that Lux had drunk in excess made him bolder in his present thoughts, and at this place in the forest he said, ‘If only I knew how to get inside, I would certainly fill my pockets.’ ‘Come along at once!’ answered a dwarf who suddenly stood before him. ‘The noble company is waiting inside right now, and you must play for them,’ the dwarf continued, ‘but mind that you make no brazen demand when they ask you afterward about your gratuity!’", "Lukas took this advice to heart and immediately followed his little guide. In the forest he knew so well, he had never seen such dreadful paths. They went straight through the densest brush, which bent apart on both sides as though struck by a gust of wind. At last, they stood before a brightly illuminated gate in a mountainside. It opened before them, only to close rapidly behind them again. Through several decorated, candlelit chambers, they reached a broad hall that shone as magnificently as though lit by a sun. Lords and ladies in old-fashioned dress moved about sociably within it.", "At a signal from his guide, Lux began to play. The company arranged itself for a dance and performed it with such grace and decorum that the fiddler had never seen its like in all his life. The sound of his instrument also grew ever more magnificent. An infinity of dances occurred to him, each more beautiful than the last, until he was enraptured by his own playing. Suddenly, a tall skeleton placed itself before him and asked what he demanded as payment. The fiddler wisely remembered the warning he had received. Silently, therefore, he reached for his hat and held it out quietly and respectfully to the questioner. The figure filled it to the brim with coals. Then the dwarf stood there again and immediately led the fiddler out of the hall and the mountain.", "Outside, he was entirely alone in the darkness and had difficulty finding his old footpath without smashing his violin against the tree trunks. Late the next day, he finished sleeping off his weariness and displeasure at the poor payment. Hunger now drove him from his nest, and, as usual, he intended to go to the nearest tavern. When he tried to put on his hat, a beautiful gold coin that had slipped into the lining fell ringing to the floor. Only then did he understand the gift. He immediately ran outside to the place where he had cursed and emptied his hat the day before. The entire heap still lay there—coals. He returned to the forest and searched a long time for the previous night's footpath.
