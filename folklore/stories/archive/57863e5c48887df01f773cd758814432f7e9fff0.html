A beggar often came there, and each time the master gave him whatever he asked for, immediately and without the slightest hesitation. He also instructed the Senn: “If I should ever be absent when this beggar comes, grant his request at once, without any delay, and give him whatever he wishes, no matter what it may be!”", "It really did happen once that the master was away when the beggar appeared and demanded something. The Senn was sitting beneath a cow and milking. “At once!” he called. “I only want to finish milking this cow.” And so he did. As soon as he had milked the animal, he set aside the milking stool, poured the milk into the Chessi, and gave the beggar what he had asked for. The beggar accepted the gift but, contrary to his custom, remained in the hut and sat down at the hearth pit. “What is wrong with the old owl that he will not leave today?” the man from Uri thought, and he continued milking and began to make cheese. When he added what was needed for the milk to set, it would not thicken at all, and it gradually occurred to him that the beggar might be to blame. He left the Chessi and went up to the attic, where he had his sleeping place, then returned to the dairy hut with a scapular in his hand. He hung the scapular on the Turner. Like a gunshot, the shameless beggar now flew out through the hut door. The milk in the Chessi began to set, and it made a proper, good cheese. The beggar never appeared on the Alp again.
