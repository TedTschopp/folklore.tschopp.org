One day they said to each other: “We do not like it here any longer. We shall set out on a journey now and go to another town, but we must take food with us, and our tools as well!” The tailor said: “You are the stronger of the two of us, so you can carry a few more loaves along with your tools. I shall take only three loaves, for that is enough food for me!” The shoemaker said: “No, it must be seven at the very least, or we shall find ourselves in difficulty. Each of us will take seven, and that will last exactly a week; by then we shall surely have found work!”\nThe shoemaker went into his room, packed up his tools, and added seven loaves, while the tailor took only three. Then they wandered through the world together. On the first day each ate one loaf, on the second day another, and likewise on the third. The following day the tailor had nothing left, so he said to the shoemaker: “Then share yours with me!” The shoemaker was angry because the tailor had not followed his advice, but he shared his bread with him, and did so again on the fifth day. That used up all the loaves, and they had nothing more to eat. There was no house to be seen for miles around, and so there was no work either. The shoemaker flew into a rage, put down his toolbox, and said: “Because of you I must now starve to death.” He seized his shoemaker’s knife, stabbed out both of the tailor’s eyes, and went on his way. The tailor suffered terrible pain and writhed on the ground, but eventually he too groped his way onward, throughout the seventh day, and toward evening he came to a thicket beneath which he lay down. Soon afterward he heard birds settling in the branches and saying to one another: “If blind people knew what kind of dew is falling tonight, they would wash themselves with it and regain their sight!”\nThe blind man heard this and could scarcely wait for the sun to shine. Then he washed himself and regained his eyesight. He now saw that he was beneath a gallows. It was the ninth day, and he felt terribly hungry. As he continued on his journey, he came to a beehive and thought: “Aha, there is honey here!” He was about to reach inside, but the queen bee crawled out and pleaded: “Please leave us our combs and do not disturb us; we shall reward you richly!” The tailor lowered his arm and continued on with his stomach growling. Then he saw a horse standing in a field. He had a needle and knife with him and thought: “I shall kill it and eat a piece of it!” But the horse began to speak and said: “Let me live, and I shall reward you richly!” The tailor went on. It was the tenth day, and he had eaten nothing for four days. Then he saw a wall appear, and soon he stood before a curtain wall where the gatekeeper was just about to close the gate. The tailor called to him: “Can you not give me a little piece of bread? I am so hungry that I shall die otherwise!”\nThe gatekeeper fetched him the bread, and after he had devoured it, the tailor asked whether he might find work in the castle. The gatekeeper said that he did not know; the tailor should wait there while he went to ask the lord. He soon returned with the message that the lord said that, if he was a good tailor, he could find work there. And so he obtained work as a tailor in the castle.\nThree days passed, and then the shoemaker also came before the castle and was given work. The shoemaker was greatly astonished to find the tailor there and able to see again, but he did not dare say anything about it and instead behaved as though they were the best of friends. The tailor had to make a fine coat for the lord, who had been invited to a great banquet, and the shoemaker a pair of shoes. The lord was exceedingly pleased with the work and said: “You may accompany me to the banquet; perhaps you will receive further orders there!” The shoemaker, however, was still furious with the tailor for having taken only three loaves back then and for having made him suffer such hunger. He therefore denounced him to the lord: “The tailor does not deserve his position in the castle, for he is a wicked man!” The lord had the tailor brought before him and assailed him: “You are a scoundrel, it seems, and so you must leave this place!” The tailor began to lament and plead. Then the lord said: “If in half an hour you run three times around the curtain wall and turn the well in the courtyard into a fountain, you may remain, and then the shoemaker must leave!”\nThe tailor ran out through the gate to look for a horse. Then it galloped three times around the well with him, and the well immediately began to spout.
