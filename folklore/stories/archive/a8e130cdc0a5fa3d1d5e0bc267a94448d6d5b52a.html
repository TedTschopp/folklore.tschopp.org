It was once the common grazing mountain of a Gaugenossenschaft, of which nothing now remains but the historical memory attached to three separate farmsteads.", "These are the Talmühle farm, now in Zurich, and the Hägelen farm on Aargau soil, although both belong to the parish of Bachs, a Zurich village. The third is the mountain hamlet of Waldhausen, now assigned to the Aargau municipality of Fisibach; all three lie in the district of Zurzach. Beside the last-named hamlet still stands the ruin of the noble castle within whose Twing these free farms once lay. They were separated from one another when the noble farmer of Waldhausen donated so much of his land to Saint Blaise Abbey in the Black Forest that the abbey was able to establish the provostry of Wislikofen from it. But this foundation too came to an end, and around the still-standing, empty provostry building on its hilltop, a little village of Wislikofen grew up. A number of other farms that belonged, and still belong, with the former ones also remain: Mühlebach, Bauernmühle, Lochmühle, Goldenbühl, Belchen, Böbikon, Imberg, Rütihof, and Hasle. They can all boast equally great age and trace their history not merely to the days of knights and abbots, but back to those earliest times when the benevolent Bergmännchen still inhabited the region. They had their home very near the Talmühle, in the great rock face of the precipitous Schanzenberg. From the valley, one can clearly see the Erdmännchenhöhle in the Fluh, but no one knows how far it branches within, for it is accessible only to the ravens that nest there and fly in and out in flocks.", "When the Bergmännchen lived up there, such prosperity reigned in the land that people still tell of it today. When the mower arrived at the meadow in the morning, the plowman at the field, or the reaper at the standing grain, behold, he often found the work half done and sometimes already completed. In the morning, the carter found the horses groomed and fed, the maid found her laundry washed and laid in the sun to bleach, the baker found the dough kneaded and already pushed into the oven, and throughout the night the miller’s servant had grain poured regularly into the hopper without needing to turn over. The carter needed only to hitch the team, the baker only to sell, and servant and maid could sleep an hour or two longer without losing any wages. The people were grateful for such help. The Bergmännchen received many a fine bread roll and were allowed to carry away many a full jug of cider or wine and return it empty. Their mutual friendship went so far that the Erdmännchen sometimes came to Stubeten and brought the children all kinds of toys and sweets. Even their tricks were charming and surprising. If a child sprang toward them too greedily, they said, “Open your hand and close your eyes, and now carry it back into the corner of the room!” The child obeyed. Although it saw that it had received nothing but a small coal, it remained silent out of awe and crept quietly into bed. But when it went to take the coal in the morning and throw it out the window, it found a precious stone. From then on, the stone was carefully preserved or set in a wedding ring and was often handed down into recent times.", "Yet there have always been idle people who must pry into everything at the wrong time, and the young miller of Talmühle was one of them. Indeed, they behaved as if they had none, so light and soundless was their gait. The scheme he devised succeeded, to be sure, but only to his own harm. Before the Erdmännchen came to him again, he had already scattered flour across the passage and the sitting-room floor. When he then took a light and shone it around, he discovered nothing but tracks from geese’s feet everywhere.", "But such misuse of beautiful fine flour, God’s precious gift, was to cost him dearly. From that hour onward, the little beings stayed away and ground no more flour for him. Indeed, when the following spring he drove his cattle as usual to graze on the Schanzenberg, they strayed and plunged to their deaths over the steep rock face. No one doubted at the time that the wrath of the Bergmännchen had caused the misfortune, and every child who knows the rocks there can still tell of it today. The Erdmännchen emigrated, the Gaugenossenschaft broke apart, knights and abbots took possession of the farms, and in the end they were even divided between two religions and assigned to two different cantons.", "(A.
