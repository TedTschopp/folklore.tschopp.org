In a time of great need, Gebistorf had to pledge the forest to Baden’s hospital fund for a small sum. But when the municipality tendered repayment after about seven years, Baden would hear nothing of returning or redeeming it and instead claimed that the forest had been formally sold to the city for that money. To the Gebistorfers’ great misfortune, their pledge deed had been lost. Trusting in their rights, however, they left the matter to an oath by which the Badeners were to demonstrate before the Landvogt that they had truly purchased the forest. The city of Baden sent its Schultheiß, a grim man. He swore the oath before the Landvogt, and Gebistorf lost the forest. From that hour the Schultheiß had a pale and distraught appearance, and when he died seven years later, he had to ride about the disputed woods in a carriage every midnight. The people of Birmenstorf relate that he rides a white horse, swings an open white umbrella through the air, and cries to his steed, ‘Hüscht umme!’ A godless young fellow who, out of sheer insolence, often rose from bed at night and shouted, ‘Devil, come and take me!’ was badly mauled by the white-horse rider only in recent years when the rider caught him among the brushwood. The spirit slipped into a bottle belonging to a glassware peddler from Bünzen who came through the forest laden with many green Guttern, and pressed upon him so oppressively that the man had to cast off his entire load.
