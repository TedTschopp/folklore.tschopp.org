He sought the counsel of wise men, and they selected a meadow on Lake Wendel called Under the Boundary. Many workmen spent an entire day digging the foundation for the holy building. But when they returned to the site the following morning, they found the earth excavated the day before smooth and level again, as though no hand had worked there. As they stood restlessly pondering this, Saint Michael appeared and showed them a site and garden where the church was to be built. The place was called Paradise. There was a spring in the garden. Saint Michael showed it to them and said, ‘I will stir the water as the angels did in ancient times. Here people shall find health of body and soul.’", "The saint then vanished. From that time onward, anyone who bathed in the spring called the Juckibrünnlein was healed of every physical infirmity. Even our grandfathers sat fully clothed in this holy water to allow the miracle to work upon them. But since human hands diverted the Kander into Lake Thun in 1714, the spring has dried up. Lord Arnold of Strättlingen went and purchased the land in Paradise from an honorable matron named Margret Vischerin for forty pounds in pennies. He then sent messengers throughout the land to obtain what was needed to build the church. He also had a bell placed in the tower and altars erected, one to Saint Michael and the other to the Virgin Mary. Later, when the workmen had raised the doors and roof ridge, he again sent messengers, this time to the bishop of Lausanne, asking him to consecrate the church.", "When the bishop came, he was received with great splendor. All those present then proceeded to Strättlingen Castle, fetched the sacred object, and brought it to the church.
