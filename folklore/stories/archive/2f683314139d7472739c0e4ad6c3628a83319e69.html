For some time an evil Kobold had haunted it. At night he made a commotion in the hayloft, or knocked hard and coarsely, like a Tyrolean or a Guggisberger, on doors and window shutters, or untied the cows in the stable so that in the morning they were all found loose inside it. In various forms—now hideous, now foolish, and at times even dainty and pleasing to behold—he frightened, teased, and tormented people and livestock so badly that, especially at night, they often could get no rest at all. Once the farmhand was flirting very intimately with the maid in the loft when the haunting spirit suddenly appeared to the two lovers with a terrible howl as an enormous black tomcat with fiery eyes. In their horror they tumbled down in ridiculous disorder onto the threshing floor, which fortunately was covered with hay, and there they trembled with fright and fear like aspen leaves when the wind blows through the forest. Every customary means of getting rid of the troublesome tormentor had already been tried, but without success, for he continued his mischief as before. At last, however, the priest of Montbovon succeeded in driving him from the house and binding him within a nearby hole in the rocks beside the Hongrin brook, where even now the water flowing into it cools his malice and his desire for wicked tricks.
