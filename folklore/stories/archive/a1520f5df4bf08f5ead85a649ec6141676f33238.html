A projecting piece of Nagelfluh there is said to be the last remnant of the sunken castle's wall; people say the passage of time hardened it into this mass that cannot be blasted apart. A fairly new little house stands nearby, and it is claimed that the owner built it solely so that, upon his own land and unchallenged by the watchful authorities, he could dig for the treasures hidden there. Beside that Nagelfluh block on the northeastern side of the hill, signs of a buried entrance are still said to be visible. Formerly, when the gatekeepers heard neighing, cracking whips, and the many-hoofed approach, they hurried to the gate in hopes of earning a good gratuity by opening it quickly. But then the whole procession, with dreadful howling, raced not through the gate but either around the town moat or through the air over the gate, touching down upon the street pavement only at the Rothes Häuschen. From there, they drove up to the town clerk's office and the St. Urbanshof. Black men sat inside, all of them headless. From the head each held beneath his arm, a piercing, fiery gaze stared out.
