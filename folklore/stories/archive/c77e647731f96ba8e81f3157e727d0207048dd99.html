In his fearless manner, Hans sprang behind the stone block with his ax ready. There, among the fir trunks, he caught sight of a hideous Stollenwurm trying to strangle something within the coils of its body. Courageously, the woodcutter leapt forward and split the monster’s head with his ax so that its black blood discolored the surrounding snow. Only now did Hans see that a dwarf had been the Stollenwurm’s victim. He took the Wichtelmännlein upon his knees, rubbed its temples, and dripped several drops of reviving Enzenwasser from his bottle into its mouth. At last, the dwarf opened his eyes and looked gratefully upon his rescuer. “Do you not know me?” he then asked. Hans reflected. He inspected the little creature from head to foot. “I have never seen you,” he replied, “but my little mother told me of your great white beard, your red cloak, and your golden crown.” “You have saved me,” said the dwarf king. “I was just on my way to visit the dwarf princess, for eight days from today we shall hold our wedding. You shall be one of the party. I invite you and your sweetheart Marie to it.” With that, the dwarf vanished. When Hans told his girl about the encounter with the Stollenwurm that evening, she was greatly astonished and told him of a dream she had experienced. Marie said she had slept in a flower-filled meadow when a tiny little man came to her and brought her a bouquet, likewise inviting her to the dwarf wedding. There was now no question that the two would attend the wedding celebration. The day dawned, a true day of wonder, for winter had suddenly vanished overnight, and the Föhn had swept the valleys clean. The pastures had turned green, and gentian and Alpine rose competed in bloom upon them. Dressed for the festival, Hans and Marie slipped away from home unseen because Marie’s father, who had chosen a rich young man for his only daughter, would not have tolerated their being together. Hans and Marie quickly climbed the Grimsel road behind the village. Scarcely did they see the first huts of Handeck when a door sprang open and a charming wedding procession came out. Its leader approached the two valley dwellers and informed them that the king awaited them above in the Rätrichsboden. The wedding had been prepared there in all splendor and magnificence. A purple tent stood erected, and the birds of heaven competed in song with the little musicians.
