At last, a man who did not appear to be especially clever offered himself and insisted that he was not afraid and that nothing would happen to him. They were glad to have him and hired him. He was devout and prayed the rosary every evening at nine o’clock. On the very first evening, a stranger joined him, accompanied his prayer, and then left him again; it continued this way throughout the summer. On the day of the descent to the valley, the spirit asked the devout Alp farmhand whether he might go down with him too, for it could sometimes be rather cold up there in winter. The Älpler allowed him to do so, and at home he continued his God-fearing habit: every evening at nine o’clock, he prayed the rosary aloud, and the spirit came and prayed with him. The master heard the two of them murmuring and wished to know what it was. At first, the farmhand would not explain. After consulting with his mysterious companion, however, he confessed everything. But they wanted to know still more: why the spirit had to wander, what troubled it, and what they could do for it. The simple man addressed it and learned from it: “Long ago, I wrongfully took possession of half the Alp and livestock, and I cannot be saved until the property is once more in the owner’s hands. Your master’s wife was my only daughter and brought the wrongfully acquired property into her marriage with him. I therefore have power over her, so that she must remain ill until he has restored half the Alp and the livestock inherited from my daughter to their rightful owner.” Indeed, the mistress had already been ill for many years and suffered from a Bresten no one could identify. The farmhand faithfully reported everything to his master, but the man refused to believe him and thought it had nothing to do with him. When he saw how much money he had to spend on doctors for his wife, however, he relented and had a Capuchin question the spirit. Everything the simple farmhand had said was now confirmed.
