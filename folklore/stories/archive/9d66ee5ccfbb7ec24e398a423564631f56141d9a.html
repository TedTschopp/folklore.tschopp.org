On this wall stands a small stone image of the Mother of God. Formerly it stood below by the road. A man prayed to it but went unheard. When he returned, he reached down and pelted the image with filth, and the image wept. Nevertheless, he pelted it once more, whereupon the image rose high up to the rock wall. There it now stood, and no one could reach it. The valley people lamented this; they had loved the little image and greatly venerated it and would gladly have had it below again. But the rock on that wall was too steep, no one could climb it, and no ladder reached such a height. Then in St Nikolaus they agreed upon a plan: they would try from above. A group climbed the Räti summit and made guide marks for themselves. Directly above the image, a man was lowered on strong ropes to bring it up. The man was already almost at the image and could see it standing there when he saw the rope grow ever thinner, like twine, and thought it would not hold and that he would plunge miserably into the deep abyss. He cried:", "‘Pull me up, pull me up, the rope is growing thin!’ — But they continued lowering him. Now he was at the image, now he could have taken it, but the rope had become as thin as a hair, and he cried again: ‘For God's sake, pull me up, or I am lost!’ Then the men pulled him upward, and the higher he came, the thicker and stronger the rope became again.
