Long ago there was once a hunter who understood many kinds of things that not everyone can do today. But once, while he was hunting, lightning struck him and shattered his arm. He put aside his gun and took up the Waldhorn. Soon he traveled through town and village, playing his Waldhorn as the blackbird sings or the Älpler calls out in joy. If somewhere in the morning a parish priest looked out the window, the horn player sounded the melody “Awake, My Heart, and Sing”; but if somewhere in the glow of evening a happy couple walked through a meadow valley, an evening song full of peace rang after them from his horn. Yet whenever he caught a thief in the act or anyone else upon a wrongful path, he gave an unseen signal. Soon the Grünrock was loved everywhere. Whoever saw him coming offered him a friendly hand, called him into his hut, and gave him food and drink. One late night he entered a forest by Lake Brienz and, while playing one final little piece, felt his last hour draw near. Not far from him stood a beggar leaning on his crutch. The hunter called to him and said, “You shall be my heir! Here, take this little bag full of money and my Jesusring—the bride once gave it to me. But I give you these only on the condition that, when I am stiff and cold, you bury me here in the forest in which I have lived. My Waldhorn, whose tones delighted and cheered me, you shall bury with me under pain of my curse! If after my death I can still accomplish something by playing it, then, God willing, it shall not lie idle with me in the grave.” Thus spoke the old hunter, and he died.
