Here, where the lord of ruined Thierstein Castle is said once to have planted his vineyards, they intended to raise a long-famed treasure, and their leader played the role of devil-conjurer. In the middle of the level ground, he set a barrel whose lower end had been knocked out. As soon as the treasure moved up from the earth into it, he said, the barrel must quickly be overturned, sealed, and rolled down the mountain. Above all, however, they must take care not to laugh. While he read various formulas from his book of magic, a most strangely dressed man came riding toward them on a pig. The animal’s back was shaped like a cooking cauldron. The rider stirred it without ceasing with a wooden Sennenkelle and asked whether the castle folk for whom he had to cook the wedding porridge had already passed by. The treasure hunters burst out laughing at this foolish-sounding question. Louder still, and altogether dreadful, was the shriek the rider now raised. The men fell to the ground in terror as if dead. Only in the morning did they awaken from their stupor and seek the way home. Then, however, they realized that the devil-conjurer was missing. He was found only several days later, far away beneath the roots of an old pine. His face had become wild, and his mind remained deranged. (Th.
