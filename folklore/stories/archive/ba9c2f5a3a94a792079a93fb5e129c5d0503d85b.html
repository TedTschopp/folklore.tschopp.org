It once belonged to the lords of Hohen-Sax, whose ancestral castle stood by the nearby parish village of Sax. Whenever one of its owners was about to depart this life, a mighty rock broke loose from the mountain and, crushing everything, rolled with a terrible rumble into the castle's outer court.", "Once, when a young Baron of Sax was hunting in the nearby forest, he suddenly noticed a cave and entered it out of curiosity. After walking several hundred paces through the wide, dark passage, he saw before him a solid iron door, which, after brief consideration, he carefully opened. A dazzling brilliance struck his eyes. He looked into an immense, spacious hall whose walls were of the purest gold. Hundreds of little Zwerglein with long beards and brown coats were busily breaking pieces of gold from the walls, carrying them in baskets to the center of the hall, and pouring them into a mighty smelting furnace, from which the molten metal flowed away through narrow channels. The young baron had watched the strange miners for perhaps a quarter of an hour when he suddenly had to sneeze. At once, the dwarfs fell into the greatest agitation and ran about threateningly. A thunderclap shook the hall, and the young man felt himself irresistibly swept away, hurled through clefts in the rock, and thrown into water. A faint glimmer of light fell from above into the dreadful depth where he found himself. Before he could think further, however, a water bucket descended. Instinctively, he sat upon it and was slowly but steadily drawn upward. Soon he found himself in the courtyard of Forsteck Castle beside the deep Sodbrunnen, facing the old kitchen maid who had unwittingly wound him up and now could not contain her amazement. Since then, no one has ever seen the marvelous gold cave again. But in July and August, bright tones were often heard around Forsteck, resembling the jingling of horses' bells during a sleigh ride. People called this the Bergklingeln. According to some, it arose when the mountain dwarfs chiseled gold from the walls and let it fall to the floor; according to others, when they made music in their chambers beneath the surface.\nH.
