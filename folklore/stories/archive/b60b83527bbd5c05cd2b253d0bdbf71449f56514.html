Josefa Aschwanden, Sisikon" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "a) The Clariden legend is repeated with slight changes on Uri's Blümlisalp, a basin-shaped valley filled with snow and ice on the Uri-Rotstock. The Senn, hardhearted toward his mother but extravagant toward his sweetheart, is said even to have given his finest cow—the Treichlenkuh—a Christian baptism and named her Bäbi. At once the whole beautiful Alp was transformed into a desolate Firn, and from then on the cow gave entirely black, curdled milk. She is still said to wander on the Firn and be milked by spirits. On Good Friday, while the Passion is being read in the nearest church, she is said to appear, walking quite tamely over the Firn. If she were milked at that time until she gave white milk, she would be redeemed, the Firn would disappear, and the Alp would stand rich in grass and flowers once more. A resolute farmer is said to have tried it once and approached the cow with a large pail, milking stool, and Melckschmutz, while she stood peacefully for him. The udder was warm and the milk black and curdled; soon the udder grew warmer and the milk brownish red, then the former hot and the latter red. At last the udder reached a glowing heat and the color of the milk was already turning rosy red; his Melkschmutz had completely melted away. Only a little more endurance, my Senn! But alas, the heat was too great for him, and he sprang away.
