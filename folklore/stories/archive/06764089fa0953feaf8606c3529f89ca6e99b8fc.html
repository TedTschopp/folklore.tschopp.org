He quickly made up his mind, slung the rifle over his shoulders, whistled to his dog, and set out to look for the Zytgeissli. He wandered uphill and downhill until deep into the evening without discovering a trace of the animal. He still stood high on the Alp when it became completely dark, and so he resolved to spend the night in the Sennhütte and continue the search the next day. He whistled to the dog, drew back the bolt, and entered the hut. Plenty of wood was available, so he lit a fire and cooked himself something warm. Then he hung the rifle from a nail above the wooden bunk and lay down. The hunter did not move on the straw and only glanced now and then toward the rifle, which he could quickly seize in an emergency. At the foot of the wooden bunk slept the dog, which noticed nothing of the spectral doings. The Männchen behaved as though at home, slaughtered the goat, roasted the meat at the fire, ate and ate, chewed and smacked its lips, and hacked off ever more pieces until only the bone of the hind leg remained.
