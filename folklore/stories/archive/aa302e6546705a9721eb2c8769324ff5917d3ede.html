He immediately climbed the ladder fixture in the threshing floor, looped a fresh straw taken from the barn floor around its highest Selle, or rung, and placed his head in the noose. At that moment, a large, fat hare shot past the onlookers, entering through one door of the threshing floor and leaving through the other, which stood open. All the young men chased it. At first, it allowed its pursuers to come close, increasing their haste, and they continued after it for a long time without managing to catch it. At last, they returned empty-handed. How astonished they were to see their companion still hanging from the straw. He seemed truly dead. One climbed the fixture to tear the straw—it could not be done; to cut it with his pocketknife—in vain! After they lowered the dead man to the threshing floor with the childish noose still around his neck, they unwound and untangled the straw without difficulty, but found an iron wire drawn through it." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Selle”: the source's regional word for the upper rung of the climbing fixture, immediately glossed as Sprosse; the young man loops the enchanted straw around it." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
