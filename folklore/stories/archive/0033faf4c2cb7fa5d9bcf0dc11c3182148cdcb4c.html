Jäger" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "The holy Father Gallus, now advanced in years, journeyed once more to the place from which he had set out, in order to strengthen through his ministry a center of Christian splendor. In Arbor Felix he mounted the pulpit for the last time, and from it movingly described to those assembled the passing of all earthly things; then he himself departed—the mighty champion, the aged hero; he departed forever from earthly pleasure and earthly pain. \"The master is no more,\" the brothers lamented mournfully in the monastery cells of St. Gall. They hurried down the winding path to retrieve his body; it led them over Rotmonten and onward along a sunken lane to the lakeshore. At the same hour, a funeral procession came toward them from Arbon, as far as Berg, toward \"Howenbühl\" [Hohenbühl], and then past Gommiswil. Only a short distance farther on, the funeral procession met the brethren of St. Gall. On that very spot a cross was raised as an eternal memorial, and this cross still stands, though it has undoubtedly been renewed many times; it is the cross at Höfen. Near the cross there soon stood a \"friendly house,\" and, wisely planning and providing for the future, \"its inhabitants\" acquired the surrounding lands and estates as their own freehold property. They called themselves \"von Hofen.\" Their line has died out, but their estates still bear the name today and probably will forever. Yet a cross alone was not to mark the place; after a short time, the brothers resolved that a chapel, simple but worthy of its purpose, should be dedicated to the memory of their master at a suitable site. And it was built on level ground. There it stood until, weakened by age and decayed, it too had to collapse. Where it once stood, a Bildstock can still be found today near the hamlet of Kappel.\nBy L.
