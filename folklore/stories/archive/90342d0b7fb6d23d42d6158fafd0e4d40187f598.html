When it has eaten the grass, gibt man nach—that is, the stake is moved. But there were also dishonest Stümper. They drove the stakes in such a way that their cattle could eat upon a neighbor's land. As is right and proper, such people receive their punishment, for after death they must continue tether-grazing. Such Stumper are often heard at night from the Studner-Riet and Stadtner-Riet. Old people relate that still greater punishment naturally falls upon those who seek to appropriate their neighbors' land by shifting boundary markers, the Marchenrücker. They never find rest in the grave and endlessly move the Marchen back to the correct place in vain.
