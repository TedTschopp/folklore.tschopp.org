During a few months in summer, an inhabited refuge hut stands there and offers refreshment and sustenance to travelers. Until only a few years ago, the pass was often crossed with mules because much wine and other grain was collected from Aosta. It is now still passable for cows, cattle, sheep, and goats, and in an emergency even for mules. This high pass, which rises 10,667 feet above the sea, is crossed especially in summer by tourists and, in good weather, easily and for the most part without danger. Before one reaches the Theodul height, which forms the boundary between Piedmont and Switzerland, a broad plain must be crossed that has been covered with snow for as long as anyone can remember. Many hundreds of years ago, a village is said to have stood on this plain, approaching a small town in size and population. If centuries ago there were periods when so mild a climate prevailed that scarcely more than the highest mountain peaks were covered with glacial firn, then this legend might not be entirely improbable, for there would have been ample room on the plain. Colder centuries arrived, however, and remaining there became increasingly difficult for these high-mountain inhabitants. One year, heavy snow began to fall early in autumn. The wind played in the driving snow, and a perceptible, unusual cold set in. Then a very old blind man sitting behind the stove is said to have asked whether the enormous mass of snow was white or, as it had been until then, still reddish. When he was told that the snow was white, he said: “Now we can remain here no longer. It is time to leave this Hochalpe and seek gentler regions.” He had spoken truly, for the snow never melted again and became a glacier.", "(told by Chaplain Mooser in Zermatt)" ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Hochalpe”: a very high Alpine pasture or high-mountain inhabited zone used seasonally; in the old man's warning it names the entire elevated setting that worsening snow and glacier conditions make uninhabitable." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
