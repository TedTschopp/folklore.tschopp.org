His wife was Lady Anna, who bore him three sons named Richard, Otto, and Marquard. But Lord Diebold was wicked and forgot the glory of his church in Paradies, showing himself ungrateful toward it by destroying its ecclesiastical liberties and seizing its property. Then the Kilchherr spoke gently with him and warned him of his grave sin. But in the hardness of his heart, Diebold threatened the priest's life and went angrily up to his castle. There he was possessed by the evil spirit, which tormented him nearly to death. Saint Michael, however, showed him mercy and healed him. Then Lord Diebold confessed his sin and vowed to return what he had stolen from the church. He would approve whatever Lady Anna and his three sons should arrange in these matters. But when he was then supposed to seal the document concerning it, he refused, grew angry, and rode in his fury away from the castle. As he came up into the forest, to the corner by the Bannacker, he was again possessed by the evil spirit, screamed and blasphemed, and died bereft of all reason.
