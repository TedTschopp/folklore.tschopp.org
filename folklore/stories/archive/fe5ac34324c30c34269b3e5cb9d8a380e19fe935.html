The confessor instructed him that when the spirit came the next time, he should ask only, ‘What revenge, what?’ When the dead man once again cried ‘Revenge, revenge!’ the following night, the innkeeper asked, ‘What revenge, what?’ The spirit answered him, ‘In Milan, in Milan!’ After that, he no longer heard the dead man cry out. The innkeeper took care never to go to Milan as a trader. Many years later, when he had entirely forgotten what the spirit had told him, he nevertheless happened to travel to Milan. At the inn where he stopped and spent the night, a whole calf's head was served at the table. When his turn came and he was about to take his portion, the calf's head began to bleed and cried three times, ‘Revenge, revenge!’ He turned deathly pale and began to tremble like an aspen leaf. They immediately seized him, and without denial he confessed on the spot that many years earlier he had murdered a merchant from Milan. The house in Milan where he was caught had in fact belonged to that very merchant. They gave him a short trial and executed him as he deserved. Long afterward, many square gold pieces were found in the house where the murderer had lived, hidden in a hollowed Wölbi-Binnu.
