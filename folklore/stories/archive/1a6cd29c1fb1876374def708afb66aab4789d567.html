After three winters, the young locusts emerged from the ground like ants from disturbed nests. The people were not alarmed but good-naturedly mocked the ground that had \"come alive\" on the dry, sandy banks of the Rhone. But when these \"leaping fleas\" grew larger every day, ranged ever farther in search of food, and within rapidly expanding circles snatched away every green thing down to the black earth, the good farmers’ eyes were opened, and they recognized with horror the danger to their fruitful meadows and fields. Orders were now given to catch and destroy the Straffeln by the bundle in the dew, but it was too late; everything was lost that the reaper could not hastily rescue from their gnawing teeth. When the devastation was complete, these creatures rose en masse into the air and moved upriver in dense clouds that darkened the sun, toward the grainfields of Gamsen and Glis. The people, who already knew these hungry immigrants from stories out of Visp, sought to defend their better fields and croplands from the uninvited attackers with weapons — that is, they kept the enemies at a suitable distance with long switches and water pumps.
