This man, who seemed pious and widely traveled, knew how to tell the carrier all manner of beautiful and remarkable things. The glass carrier did not notice that they had turned onto a side road instead of remaining on the highway. Absorbed in conversation, they reached the house of the rich people with the over-beloved child. Since night was already about to fall, they asked for lodging. The pilgrim was received with great reverence, and for his sake the glass carrier too was considered a more welcome person than he otherwise would have been. The household prepared as fine a meal for them as it could and made a place for the two to sleep in the main room, because other visitors had left no bedchamber available. When everyone lay in the deepest repose, the pilgrim awakened his companion, pointed out the child of the house, and said that he intended to kill him. Full of outrage at such wicked, cruel repayment for the friendly hospitality, the glass carrier made every effort to rouse the people with his cries so that the evil man — as he now considered the pilgrim — would receive his just reward. But his voice failed him, and the deed was already done. The stranger went and laid the dead little child back where he had taken him from. Then, just as swiftly, he seized the astonished and indignant glass carrier and, as if by a miracle, carried him out of the house beneath the open sky. There he showed him a white dove just then flying to heaven. That, the pilgrim explained, was the little child’s pure soul. It was now saved and blessed for eternity, whereas otherwise it would have been lost because of the parents’ excessive love, together with the father and mother. They too would now search their souls and, on account of their good qualities, be led through this earthly misfortune to salvation.
