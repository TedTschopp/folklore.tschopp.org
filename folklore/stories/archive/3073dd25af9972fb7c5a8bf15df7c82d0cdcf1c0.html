He wanted to settle matters with his brother and demand his paternal inheritance. Hartmann, however, brought his enraged brother’s case before their mother and her councilors, for he could reasonably hope that his own cause would stand well with the landgravine, whose favorite he was. Yet instead of helping Eberhard obtain his rights, Hartmann attacked him in bed at night and had him dragged far away into captivity. When the Duke of Austria later settled the dispute and Eberhard was freed, a reconciliation was to take place at the castle in Thun. All the knights had been invited to the table, and the old quarrel seemed at last to be ending well. After the meal, however, when the brothers sat together by the fireplace, they clashed again. Every knight present took sides, and an ugly melee followed. Count Hartmann was driven into the Schneggen, the spiral staircase, and stabbed to death by one of the knights in the tumult.
