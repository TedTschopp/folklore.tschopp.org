About a hundred years ago, a four-footed spirit resembling a horse could still be seen there, and it was called Zavudschaou. This spirit was a mischievous fellow who acted perfectly tame and friendly whenever anyone passed there at night, whether on the footpath or on the narrow, bumpy cart road. If the traveler wished to ride the steed for part of the way, it immediately showed itself willing. But as soon as the rider had mounted, Zavudschaou leaped into the nearby stream and swam upstream at the greatest speed, until the rider either lay unconscious beside the water from wetness, cold, or exhaustion and awoke the next morning half dead and shivering with fever, or was not freed from the goblin until invoking a guardian angel or another saint.
