This lasted until self-serving local officials seized control of the forest wardenship. Under their poor administration, the good communal custom of making an annual circuit around the Gemeindebann and inspecting the boundary stones one after another fell out of use. As these boundary markers gradually sank or were removed, the secure forest boundary toward the town of Zurzach was lost. The people of Zurzach took it upon themselves to catch and punish several Tegerfelden residents cutting wood in the forest, on the pretext that they had felled timber on foreign ground. But those punished maintained that this was their own land and called upon their fellow citizens for protection. The two communities could not agree. The people of Zurzach claimed the whole forest as far as the crest of the Zurzach mountain, while the people of Tegerfelden likewise held that the part of the forest belonging to them reached to that same mountain and even beyond it to the mountain meadow in the Beckenmoos. Thus they brought their suit before the governor in Baden. Because the documents were missing, witnesses were to decide, and these were to be supplied by Zurzach. They were permitted to choose one from among their own citizens; the other witness had to be an outsider. They succeeded in bribing an old man from Tegerfelden, and he was truly so devoid of honor that he not only became their arbiter but now diminished the boundaries of his own homeland himself. During the new placement of boundary stones that followed, he assigned to Zurzach everything that was not its due; and whenever his own people interrupted him, he swore and shouted into their faces with his oath fingers raised, “Hie noche und hie nein müend ihr d’Marche setze!”", "Through two villains, the people of Tegerfelden thus lost forever more than a hundred Juchart of the finest beech forest. But punishment was not long in coming. The false witness from Zurzach departed this world in an unknown manner. Now he runs about in the beech forest and even lies down in the road before the mail coach that must pass through. He does this most readily before the three holy seasons, when he runs from the boundary of the village of Reckingen to that of Rietheim across a now-wretched cleared woodland belonging to Zurzach that bears only dwarf pines. Formerly, however, it stood full of magnificent beeches, for this is precisely the tract of forest that had once served the people of Tegerfelden for the Bannbeschreitung.", "Nor did the witness from Tegerfelden fare any better. This day laborer’s bread grew too bitter for him, and at last he was found hanged by a rope on the hayloft. Whenever the anniversary of that oath-taking day comes around, he rides out on a white horse from a rear building of the Yellow Lion—regarded as the old inn premises, while the front building is of more recent origin—and turns outward toward the Grütt, a tract of Tegerfelden forest. His wicked comrade is already waiting for him there. With the unceasing cry “Hie noh und hie nein!” they travel on the Katzenstriegel to all the points where the boundaries should legally stand today. He also stretches out his hand in greeting to people who meet him there; and if one is clever enough to offer him a stick instead of one’s hand, one has the pleasure the next day of seeing the burn marks of his three oath fingers clearly impressed upon it. He wears short, baggy breeches to the knee, one white stocking and one red, and a three-cornered hat on his head. The rest of the year he is at home in the rear building of the inn. There he formerly annoyed strangers and pulled away their coverlets, so those rooms were converted into a dance hall. Out of vexation at this, he then moved up into the dovecote.
