When the barons had nothing to do and the weather was fine, they went into the courtyard, and the castellan set up the golden pins. The balls were also made of gold, and how beautiful the sight must have been when the flashing balls vigorously cleared away the golden pins. The Kegelspiel constituted the barons' treasure and could never be sold. Only the lord of the castle and the castellan knew where the pins were kept. But once there was a wicked castle steward; while the castle lords were at war, he stole the Kegelspiel and made off with it. The barons soon came home, however, and a hunt was mounted for the faithless man; he was brought in together with his plunder. The thief was executed but could find no rest in death. He must guard the Kegelspiel, and does so even now that the castle has been destroyed. On moonlit nights, he comes and sets up the game for several minutes in its customary place in the castle courtyard, only to hide it again immediately afterward.\nK.
