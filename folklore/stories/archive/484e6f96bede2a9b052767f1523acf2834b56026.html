At its foot lies a cave inhabited by little mountain men. As recently as a hundred years ago, they came down the stream at night and visited the people at the Old Mill. They brought their distaffs and competed at spinning. At midnight, they stopped and went home; even when the clock was altered so that they would not hear it strike, they vanished at the appointed hour by some other means. They were especially friendly toward children and gave them many precious stones that were preserved for a long time afterward or sold for a high price.", "A poor day laborer once worked in the field until late in the evening, a few hundred paces from the village. He saw a little mountain man float down over the height like a butterfly and settle beside him. It greeted him politely and tried to press upon him an apron full of coal. The man could not understand what use the worthless stuff might have and was already far too tired from work to debate the matter. At last, because the little mountain man would not stop urging him, he good-naturedly accepted one small coal from the apron and put it in his pocket. The little mountain man continued on his way but no longer looked as pleased as before. When the laborer dressed the next morning, he felt the piece of coal still in his pocket and intended to throw it away. But he drew from the pocket an equally large piece of gold and now understood his benefactor’s insistence of the previous day. He nevertheless later became a rich man.", "Only a few years ago, a very old farmer from Magden died. People knew that, as a fourteen-year-old boy felling wood, he had accidentally entered the cave on the Haglestä and been splendidly entertained there with Ramwähen, slices of buttered bread, and cakes. After him, another boy entered the same region and continually thought that the scent of baked goods was rising into his nose. He soon found an egg cake as broad as the entire tree trunk upon which it lay arranged like food on a platter. In his hunger, the boy tore the cake into two pieces. At that moment, the little men stood before him, took him up into their cave, showed him all the artfully made vaults, and set before him whole loads of the finest foods. Because he was very tired, he quickly fell asleep among them. In the morning, he lay at home in his little room, with his forest ax beside him in bed, driven into an enormous loaf of bread. When he cut the loaf, so many gold coins fell out that he became one of the wealthiest men in the village. Thus the dwarfs did many good deeds until the people’s curiosity drove them away. The miller’s wife had long wanted to know whether the little men had feet. In the spinning room, she scattered ashes beneath the table and bench.
