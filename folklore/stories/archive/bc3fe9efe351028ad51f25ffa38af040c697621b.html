There, between Holziken's Hard forest and the Stübisberg with the Picardie, lies a cave surrounded on all sides by blocks of Nagelfluh, the Hügeligrotte. One such boulder stands close before its entrance as though intending to block it. The opening is so low that one can enter only by crawling on one's belly, and foxes have therefore established their secure dwelling here. Several steps farther inside, the cave widens and is said to divide into several passages prettily adorned with stalactites.", "In ancient times, a beautiful castle stood here. These boulders are the rubble of its mighty foundation walls, and the entire village of Schöftland belonged to the Schlossbann. A single young noblewoman was the sole inhabitant of the whole spacious castle, a proud and ostentatious maiden who was very famous for her beauty but little liked by the valley people. Nevertheless, around springtime a poor man in the village once undertook to win her as Taufpathin to his newborn child. So that she would not have to trouble herself by visiting his humble house, he brought the little one up to the castle on the day of the baptism.", "The time arrived, and the bells began to ring for church. Below in the castle courtyard, the girls were already waiting to walk with their lady across to the village on the other side of the valley. But the vain noblewoman could not finish adorning herself or tear herself from the mirror until her maid announced that the third and final signal was already ringing in Schöftland. ‘Then let it ring in the Devil's name!’ the young lady replied. Irritably, she had the child placed in her arms and prepared to descend the mountain. But when she reached the footbridge over the Hungerbach below, she noticed that the ringing had ceased and that she was therefore late. What a disgrace for a Junker's daughter if, without song or peal, she had to enter church with a peasant child in her arms, as though she were counted among the serving girls and her godchild among those born outside marriage.", "Overcome by sudden anger at this offense by the foolish peasants, she forgot herself completely, threw the child from the footbridge where she stood into the Hungerbach, and immediately returned home. But the sun veiled itself as though night were falling, and a dreadful crashing broke loose upon the castle hill. When the storm had passed and the people hurried toward the mountain, they found only the rubble of the sunken castle. The young lady herself had vanished and remained gone. Beside it, once every hundred years, grows a little flower, a red Maddänneli, an early primrose or cowslip. Whoever plucks it can empty the money chest. But then he must also run three times around the abyss after the lightning-swift maiden, for a chasm opens beside the chest. Only one man has ever been found who, out of greed, agreed to this task.
