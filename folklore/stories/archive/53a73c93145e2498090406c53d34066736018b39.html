Deep snow covered the ground, dense fog enveloped the whole region, and a terrible storm was raging. The man grew uneasy when, on top of everything else, he heard the howling of wolves and the low growling of bears prowling for prey under the pressure of hunger. Yet he took courage, trusted in his strength, and went forward so that his beloved domestic animals would not starve. He had almost reached his destination when a great din from the livestock shed struck his ear; the sheep bleated in fear and the lambs cried pitifully. In a flash the man reached the hut, but—oh, horror!—the stable door had been smashed in and a bear had broken inside, mercilessly striking down and cruelly strangling his flock. Beneath the open doorway, a thunderous ‘Holla!’ escaped the horrified mountain man into the stable. The bear did not need to be called twice: rearing high, it meant to throw itself upon the unwelcome disturber. But with the words ‘Oho, rogue! You want to use your arms? I shall use my arms too!’ the man seized it around the chest with open arms, clung tightly to it, and caressed its neck so forcefully with his strong forehead that its jaws and paws could only bite and beat the air. A mighty dance of strength became unavoidable—the opponents had feet too healthy and courage too fiery.
