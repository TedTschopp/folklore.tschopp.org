The following legend has come down to us about one of them.", "One of these Nachhirten scarcely ever emerged from the wilderness for nearly the entire year. Indeed, he carried the hermit's life so far that he came down neither on Sundays nor on obligatory feast days to attend divine service at the parish church in Naters. He was reported to the priest for this and, at the priest's order, had to appear before him. When called to account, he was asked whether he did not know what the second commandment of the Church required and why he neither heard Mass nor received Holy Communion. He answered that he did not consider it necessary, because every day he went to a certain rock where an angel offered the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass; he served the angel there and received Holy Communion from him. Naturally, the priest could not believe this and had no doubt that the man was merely playing the hypocrite. He therefore had the man watched closely during Mass and the sermon to see how he behaved. After the service, the priest asked, “How did our Aletsch man conduct himself during worship?” “Beyond reproach,” the observer answered, “except that we saw him weep once and laugh once.” When questioned about this, the man said, “I wept because I saw two women chatter so much during the sermon that the Devil, who was writing everything down behind them, had already covered an entire cowhide. I wept over their many sins. But then the Devil tried to stretch the cowhide with his teeth so that he could make it longer and gain more space to write. The hide suddenly tore from all the pulling, and the Devil struck his head violently backward against the wall. At that, of course, I could not keep myself from laughing.” Astonished that this herdsman could see something perceptible only to a holy man, the priest dismissed him with every mark of respect and allowed him to return to his holy solitude.
