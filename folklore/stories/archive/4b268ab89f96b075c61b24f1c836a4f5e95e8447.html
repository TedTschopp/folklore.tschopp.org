The stones laid one upon another to form its pillars are so firmly fixed and cemented with good mortar, and so much weight and solidity has been given to the capitals atop these pillars, that even the strongest Alpine herder or shepherd who climbs up and shakes its pillars with both hands cannot tear anything apart. Near this Heathen Tower, and elsewhere on the Fontanen Alp, there are many large stones in which human feet and also animals’ feet are imprinted. The same is also heard of other large stones on mountains, Alps, and common lands in Obwalden. I was also told that a young man or shepherd from Giswil once stood beside this Heathen Tower and felt a powerful desire to see one of the old heathens, a ‘little heathen man.’ At once, such a heathen appeared, wrapped in rough animal skins. The young man was terribly frightened and ran away. I have also often heard people from Giswil say that they saw ghosts there in the Grundwald and Mörli belonging to ‘old heathens, little heathen men,’ usually recognizable by their Tanngrotzen or animal skins.
