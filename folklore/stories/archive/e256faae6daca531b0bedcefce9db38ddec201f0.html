One of them was snow-white, beautiful, and good; the second was an evil black witch; and the third was half white and half black, half good and half evil.—Whenever the witch wished to bring misfortune upon people and the good sister prevented it through advice and warning, the third intervened between them so that half the misfortune was permitted and the other half averted.—Once, the young men and women of Fideris made an excursion into the mountains and were caught by rain near the little house. The good sister took pity on the merry company and invited the drenched people into the room; she wished to bake cakes for them, but the witch thrust her out of the kitchen and herself baked cakes for the company that became beautiful and yellow on the outside but were poisoned within. This grieved the good sister, and she wept. The middle sister joined them, baked coarse brown little cakes from coarse household flour, and told the good sister: ‘We shall set both kinds, the yellow and the brown, before the guests. The selfish will eat the beautiful poisoned ones and die; the modest, by contrast, will eat the brown ones, and nothing will happen to them. Thus it goes half and half, as always!’—Those in the company who ate the golden-yellow little cakes died.
