Our mother had died, and her sister, Bäsi-Nänni, was our housekeeper. One evening the youngest boy said that someone was knocking on the Gwandlatte in front of the house and calling ‘Kaspar!’ That was our father's name. The rest of us heard nothing and found no one when we went out to look. The same thing happened the following evening. We told our father, but he only laughed and said it was imagination. When it happened again on the third evening, he said that next time we should call him at once. But that was no longer necessary: the following evening, when we were already in bed, he himself heard something strike the Gwandlatte and call ‘Kaspar.’ He got up at once and looked out of the window. Outside stood his brother-in-law from Brunnen, asking whether Bäsi-Anna would come to Brunnen and be godmother to a child. She did so the next morning. After a while, they brought the frail child to us for the fresh mountain air. We children were delighted, for every time Bäsi gave the little one porridge made from toasted flour, we were allowed to lick and scrape out the porridge pan. But the good care did not help.
