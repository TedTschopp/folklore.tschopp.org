Page 42." ], "english_paragraphs": [ "On the rocky shelf of the Aare's bank, where Aarau's town hall and church now stand, a strong tower called Rore rose in the earliest times. Only in the 1820s were its surrounding walls blasted apart and the traces of its rampart leveled. Its north side, incorporated into the town hall, still has several battlements above, while the large boulders from which the crude building was constructed jut from the wall. Toward the church, a sanctuary was open to all who sought protection there from oppressors; the castle was therefore known simply as the Freihof. A short lane along the rocky embankment, reached by the castle's drawbridge, had been prepared as lodging and a stopping place for vassals and retainers. It is therefore still called the Adelbändli. From there, two long rock stairways descend across the town slope to the old bank past which the Aare formerly flowed. A similar castle, piled from equally black and shapeless masses of stone, stands an arrow-shot away and is called the Schlösschen, or little castle. This is said to have been the castle of the Counts of Rore. Two villages near Aarau are named Rohr after them, one on the river and the other in a mountain basin of the neighboring Jura.", "More than eleven hundred years ago, the two brothers Guntram and Baltram lived there. One such monster lived in a cave beside the Emme. It devoured people and livestock until nothing living in the surrounding area was safe. The old chroniclers say it was a dragon; others say it was a great forest serpent.", "The two counts heard of the monster and of their people's distress along the Emme. They immediately took up their armor, went out, and hunted the creature in order to destroy it. It lay near the castle at Burgdorf, beyond the Emme in the Gysenau, where the Kesselgraben runs between two high rock walls. As Baltram sharpened his sword upon the rock and challenged the monster, it shot forth with steaming jaws and swallowed him. Guntram immediately sprang forward and split the dragon's head. It spat out the swallowed knight and died. The two brothers washed the poisonous dragon blood from themselves in the Emme. In memory of their miraculous deliverance, they built a leper house and chapel upon that spot and dedicated it to Saint Margaret.
