It rises from the many little streams that flow like silver threads from the glacier at the rear of the Alpe and runs through the middle of the splendid Kühmatte, nearly an hour long and level as a table. Not far from the Stafel, which numbers nearly thirty huts together with a beautiful chapel, at the place called the Steg, a small wooden bridge is built across the stream, over which the livestock are driven to the Alpine pastures. Near this Steg one finds a somewhat flat stone in which the strange shape of a foot is impressed; it is called the Teufelstritt. The legend says that the Devil once made an Alpine journey through here. From the Riesigen Horn he is said to have taken one step to this stone at the Steg, from there a second onto the high mountains, and finally a third onto the Gliserhorn. Because the stride across the entire Brigerzehnen was very broad, his heavy tread shattered the summit of the Gliserhorn. A great cleft in the rock is indeed said to be up there, widening ever more, and according to one legend Brig will someday be shaken by the fragments of a mighty rock from the Gliserhorn. A cross now stands on this fissured rock, perhaps to discourage the Devil from treading upon it so impolitely a second time. The legend is silent about his distant journey and why he passed through in such great leaps. In any case, they were true giant strides, worthy of an Adramelech such as Klopstock names in his Messiad.
