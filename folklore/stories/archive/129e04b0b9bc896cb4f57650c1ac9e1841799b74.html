After a small house had been built beside a certain cell on the hill, next to the Church of St. George—the present-day St. Georgen—she remained there for nearly four years, restricting herself to such great abstinence from food and drink that scarcely anyone can bring themselves to believe the narrator. For while she remained constantly in the church, night and day, she persevered in prayer and night watches, tirelessly striving for divine love. She never entered that little house except rarely, in unavoidable cases: either to enjoy some sleep or, once after three days, to refresh her fasting body. He sent several monks and ordered that she be brought to him; after honoring her with a few words and blessing her, the bishop sealed the enclosure. This happened in the year 916. The enclosure had no door; the woman shut within communicated with the outside world only through the little window.
