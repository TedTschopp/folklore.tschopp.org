In this wild, confined valley, which sheep visit in summer, lies a small mountain lake.", "Where the lake now lies, a Sennhütte once stood, surrounded by a beautiful Alp. An evil Senn lived there and allowed poor people to continue on their way without refreshment. Once, a poor, old, frail woman came along the path, tired from her long journey and faint with thirst. She knocked at the Sennhütte and asked for refreshment, but the Senn answered her with abuse: ‘Go away, old beggar! If I had to give milk to all the rabble that passes here, I myself would soon starve to death.’ For God's sake, the poor old woman begged only for a little refreshment so that she could continue on her way. ‘My knees are giving way,’ she said, ‘and my tongue burns like fire.’ But the Senn slammed the door. The woman sank down before it. When the Senn opened it again and saw her still there, he cried with a curse: ‘Are you still there, old woman? Wait, I shall now give you milk so that your thirst is quenched.’ With these words, he took the pail, milked his large red cow, then came to the woman and gave her a drink. The old woman drank, warmly thanked the Senn, and called down God's blessing upon him. But the Senn twisted his features into a scornful sneer, for he had put Magen into the milk to curdle it. The old woman had scarcely continued on her way for some distance, newly strengthened, when she felt the most terrible pains in her body. As she sank down dying, she cursed the Senn and his Alp. At that moment, thunder sounded in the heights, lightning struck the hut, and it sank with the Senn and the red cow; a lake welled up at that place. One can still see the path that led to the hut disappear into the lake and clearly see it emerge again from the water on the opposite shore and climb into the heights, for the route across the mountains to Obervaz once passed the Sennhütte. A black spot can still be seen in the middle of the lake: it is the hut that sank there. Once every seven years, amid dreadful thunder and storm, the Senn rises from the lake and milks his red cow above its turbulent waves, then sinks back again, wringing his hands with a terrible howl.
