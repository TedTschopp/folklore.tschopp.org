Her tormented spouse finally had enough of the matter, made a report to the authorities himself, and also agreed upon the method by which the cunning female fiend was to be caught. Under the pretext of buying wood, someone was to come to the councillor. On the appointed day, he arrived, bought two Klafter of firewood, and loaded it onto the wagon. Then the man said to his wife: ‘You said you needed a new pair of shoes. It is scarcely worthwhile to call the shoemaker auf die Stör for just one pair. You would do better to go into Stans yourself and buy footwear to your liking.’ The woman was satisfied with this. Now the man observed that the weather was bad and cold; instead of her old shoes, she should put on her Finken and ride there on the load of wood. She agreed again. Finally, the councillor also thought it advisable to have his wife tied to the wagon with a rope, since the draft animal was young and spirited and the road bumpy and somewhat downhill. Once more, she willingly heeded his advice and allowed it to be done. But with that, she was outwitted. In Stans, the load was set on fire and burned together with the witch.
