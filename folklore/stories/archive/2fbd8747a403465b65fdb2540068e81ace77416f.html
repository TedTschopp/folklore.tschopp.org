This property was once the subject of much dispute, and lawsuits were also pursued in the years 1732 and 34. Oberterzen was able to present its documents to the territorial court of Glarus and therefore won. The people of Mols, however, claimed that one of their opponents had given false testimony and thereby put them at a disadvantage. As punishment, the guilty man was said to be compelled to wander in the Weidtal. The people of Terzen appealed to the guardian of Mels monastery, who sent two Capuchins to bind the wandering spirit. They found none and issued the following finding: “Since the spirits have given no answer to repeated questioning, it follows undeniably that they have left the place or that none were ever there. We therefore believe that we act in the most Christian manner if the dead are allowed to rest in God the Lord.” (1760.)\nContributed by G.
