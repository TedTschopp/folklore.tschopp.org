Then they descend from their summer palaces—the inaccessible heights of the Finsteraarhorn, the Jungfrau, and their neighbors—into the lower regions. They gather in the rocky chasms and Tobel, sport with the fury of the elements, and howl cruel magic songs to which the storm whistles an accompaniment. Often, they place themselves upon the icy ridges and tauntingly challenge one another, tease each other with ridiculous replies, and hurl burdens of snow—enormous avalanches—at one another. Because they hate everything that shows life, they also hunt readily and often. With loud halloos, they whistle, clatter, and rattle in pursuit of the beautiful chamois. Their greatest hatred, however, strikes human beings. Woe, therefore, to the traveler who ventures into their domain at such a time, to the hunter who roams their territory, or to the Strahler who seeks to rob their dwellings—the mountain's interior—of their most beautiful adornment, the Strahlen or crystals.
