Skilled in war and statecraft, he stood high in his emperor’s favor, and his occupation—when the emperor did not summon him to court—was the hunt. Thus, he often traveled through his territory with his hunting companions, pursuing now the chamois, now the wild boar. One evening, as he was about to return to his castle, he climbed a rocky projection. A long blast on a gold-ornamented horn that the count carried at his side gave the signal that the hunt was over. Contentedly, the count stretched out upon the soft moss to await his companions. Beside him, with a busy murmur, a clear spring danced through its stony bed, and the last rays of sunlight trembled upon its waves; carefree, the little singers swayed on the green branches in the evening light; the clouds lay motionless in the distant west, and the gray Alpine peaks of Raetia floated in a rosy glow. The huntsman now listened to the song of the little forest dwellers and the musical chatter of that spring; their tones played around his senses in gentle harmonies.
