The lofty position of these walls and the wooded covering of the rock they crown give them a highly romantic appearance. Little is known of the castle's builder and its later inhabitants. Among the local people, however, a dark web of legends surrounds these ruins. When the weather is about to change, maidens and knights can be seen riding out from them in fiery carriages drawn by six horses. On Good Friday, a whole company of splendidly dressed lords and ladies in ancient attire basks in the sun and lays square gold pieces out in the light of day upon enormous cloths. During the revolutionary years, a treasure hunter made the surrounding farmers' mouths water with talk of the immense treasures hidden here. It was common knowledge that two enchanted noble maidens of Reifenstein had guarded them for many centuries. Before long, at silent midnight, the all-powerful Christofelgebet, which compels hell and the devil, resounded among the ruins of the old castle, interrupted by the sound of digging shovels. Soon they struck something hard. In the joy of his heart, one of the farmers cried out, “Potz Hagel! Da hämmert's!” The spirit-conjurer choked on the final third of the Christofelgebet; he stamped his foot angrily and shouted, “Nei, du Kaib!
