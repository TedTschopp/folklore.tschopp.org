Northeast of the village, on a height called Krähenberg, is a small wood. In former times, hunting noises, gunshots, barking dogs, and hallooing cries were often heard there. This occurred most strongly when rain was beginning. It is also always heard at Christmas. Usually, this hunt did not remain in the same place. It began in this little wood, moved westward behind the upper part of the village as far as the church, then went through the churchyard and from there along a village lane lined mostly with thatched houses. West of the village, again in a small wood, at a place called the Holy Stöcklein, the entire hunt usually fell silent. People who often traveled this route and could not get out of the way quickly enough immediately lay down on the ground, for they knew that then no harm would be done to them. They relate that the whole hunt rolled over them as though young pigs were passing. They had to remain in this position for about five minutes, then stood again and went on their way without the slightest harm. Anyone who did not stoop down was scratched all over. This hunt is often said to have crossed the entire region amid wild clamor." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Nachtgejäge”: literally ‘night hunt,’ the Emmental name for a roaming spectral hunt heard with shots, dogs, and cries; here it follows a fixed route and intensifies before rain and at Christmas." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
