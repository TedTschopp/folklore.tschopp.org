This is performed in a singing voice through a great milk funnel called a Volle held before the mouth. Omitting it is not only unchristian but also brings all sorts of harm in the mountains. The cows were gone, and Oswald could only stare after them. For days he climbed through every gorge of the alp, calling, whistling, and coaxing, but not a trace could be found. Seven head had been lost at once, and his heart nearly burst with grief. But he learned to master himself, and an old belief told him it was best now to behave as though nothing had been lost. Therefore, every day he went back up to his empty stable and worked there as though the seven animals still yielded sevenfold Melktern of milk. He scattered the manure with a fork and laid fresh bedding, buckled on the milking stool and sat facing the empty Baren, whistled and spoke kindly as usual while milking each cow, called them by name, offered drink, and coaxed as though he wished to lead them back onto the pasture. Thus he continued his unproductive husbandry and in doing so became more patient, pious, and hardworking than ever. Winter came, and even then he climbed the ice-covered footbridges to the empty hut. He gave up all the coarse talk with which the Sennen folk sought to ease their arduous work.
