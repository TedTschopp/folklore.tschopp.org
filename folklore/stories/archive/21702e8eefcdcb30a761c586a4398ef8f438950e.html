They let the lost little animal in from the cold and gave it a piece of meat from the meal at which they were sitting happily together on the holy evening. But in doing so, these people had brought a kobold into the house and could not rid themselves of it for seven whole years. The little animal lived on very intimate terms with one girl of the family, slept beside her, ran out with her to work in the fields, and was present, often in a wondrous way, wherever she went or stood. Toward all the other members of the household, however, it behaved all the more intolerably and maliciously. It played so many tricks that the notorious cat soon became known everywhere only as the Matthisethier.", "Whenever anyone baked, at least one loaf of bread was found eaten away down to the bare crust. The honey pot had been wiped out fingerful by fingerful, and the finest laundry in the cupboard was surely no longer clean just when someone wished to take out an item for the next feast day. The housewife was therefore always in distress over whether she should profane the holy time by washing or even by spreading an unclean tablecloth. She sometimes preferred to wash through the whole night. But this too brought her new vexation. She was discovered at her untimely housework and, according to the severity of former custom, was immediately summoned before the Sittengericht. She was deeply ashamed. Yet because no one could possibly have seen her during her nocturnal washing, she once insisted before the court that the repeated secret informer be named to her. She was told the name of a woman who had long borne a bad reputation and was dismissed with a small fine. Who would have thought that this ill-reputed woman and that Matthisethier were one and the same person? But now it was proved. Soon afterward the wicked informer was arrested for various acts of sorcery. Because she confessed even more than anyone had suspected, she was taken to Lenzburg Castle before the Bernese Landvogt, who had her burned as a witch.
