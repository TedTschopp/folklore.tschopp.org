(Archiv für Volkskunde.)" ], "english_paragraphs": [ "Between Zuzwil and Linggenwil, right beside the main road, lies a small wood called the Hasenholz, inhabited by a ghostly musician known as the Hasenholzgeiger. No one has ever seen him, but the invisible fiddler plays the loveliest melodies upon his violin. His playing is especially lively and full during holy seasons, chiefly around Christmas and on the Fronfastentage. At those times, the notes of his instrument can be heard as far away as the neighboring hamlet of Hub, and they sound as beautiful as though the world's ‘foremost’ fiddler were playing below in the Hasenholz. But he also likes to lead astray people who pass the Hasenholz at night and follow the enchanted music. With his fiddling, he lures them ever deeper into the little wood and leaves them wandering there or on the nearby Torfmoos. Only when the morning bell rings in Zuzwil or Linggenwil do they find the right path again. A man from Linggenwil who wanted to go home one winter evening at Christmastime was led astray by the Hasenholzgeiger. He did not reach home until the next morning, exhausted almost to death. When questioned, he said that he had wandered around the Hasenholz all night until the Angelusläuten without finding a way out. Since the snow was rather deep at the time, the man was curious to see the tracks of his nighttime wandering. During the day, he went into the Hasenholz with one of his household companions and was astonished to discover that, as the footprints in the snow plainly showed, he had traced a modest circle in a clearing of the wood. He must therefore have walked around and around without being able to escape this magic ring.\nG. Kessler.
