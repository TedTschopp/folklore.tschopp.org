But he also sought to increase it by stealing his neighbors' fruit and wegmarchte—moving the boundary to take away—half of their Pünten and orchards. Thus, he continues shifting boundaries and stealing after his death to this very day. But he shows which master he now belongs to by having to run about as a sheep, a buck, or a dog. Nevertheless, he serves people as an infallible weather prophet. If he is seen planting hedges, driving fresh Hagstöcke into the ground, climbing trees, and putting the stolen fruit in a basket, the coming day will certainly bring rain. Until evening, he stays behind the neighbor's stove. At the sound of the Betglocke, he sets out and wanders as far as the bend above Frick, where he lies in wait for travelers and leads them astray. Yet any tobacco smoker can drive him away there; one need only blow fire from the pipe. Once, as a black shaggy dog, he confronted two strangers. When praying would not help them, they began cursing every sign from Heaven. Now he let them continue on condition that they carry him in a coat pocket back behind the stove at Ueken. One of them was foolish enough to agree.
