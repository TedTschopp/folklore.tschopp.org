The story is told as follows.", "During a long-lasting famine, the usurer Böhni of Möhlin was almost the only one who still had supplies in his grain lofts. He owned many of them; indeed, he is said to have been able to count five of his barns from his residence at that time. All the neighbors in their want turned to him, but with him a loaf of bread now cost a Viertel of land, and he mercilessly insisted upon this price until he owned the contiguous parcels of land that stretch in a fine, level expanse from the village to the great fir forest on the left bank of the Rhine. Seven of the houses that he built in Möhlin and Ryburg at that time from such profits still stand; their stepped masonry fire gables immediately distinguish them from the others. He himself kept house in the finest one, which is at Ryburg, and from there he could visit his great fir forest without having to set even one foot on another person’s property. There he was strangled behind the stove by a hunter whom no one knew. The bloodstain on the wall can neither be whitewashed nor walled over, and the present owner of the house, called Stöcker Uerech, is therefore said to keep this room locked and not show the marvel.", "Böhni was scarcely buried before he was seen sitting at the upstairs window again, wearing a red cap and surveying his extensive estates as was his custom. Arrangements were therefore made to remove the uninvited guest. Capuchins had to bless him into a Maßflasche behind that stove. They carried him in this way to the Spitzengraben, by a slope of the mountain ridge that runs along the left side of the road from Mumpf to Möhlin. But because he had to be permitted to shorten his banishment by one rooster’s step every hundred years, in the long time that has elapsed since then he has shown himself everywhere again, so that he has already been encountered in several of his houses and in every imaginable animal form. In the village itself, people are not afraid of him and expect it will be several hundred years more before he reaches his dwelling here again. From the mountain heights toward the inn called the Krone, however, he often startles the carters’ horses. The people of Wallbach can hear his wretched cry quite clearly in their village: ‘Hubhub, hoho!’ He foresees wars with precision, and for that reason he appeared unusually often in the year 1848. As a dog, a small cat, a calf with glowing eyes, and a black man, he roams outside in the forest. Only recently, he threw backward to the ground a Swiss border guard who had to make his rounds here at night on the bank of the Rhine and pressed him so hard that his eyes remained bloodshot for many days afterward." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Viertel”: here a historical local unit of land used as the price of one loaf; the actual area denoted by a Viertel varied by place and period.", "Swiss term “Maßflasche”: a bottle holding one Maß, a regionally variable historical measure of volume; in this legend it serves as the vessel into which the Capuchins confine the ghost.", "The source word fünferlei is unusual in its sentence and may be corrupt; it is rendered conservatively as five of Böhni’s barns rather than assigning an unsupported layout or category to them." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
