Only a few years ago, the young and intrepid mountain guide Franz Zgraggen, known as Dubeler, was returning from a successfully completed mountain tour from Disentis over the Brunni Pass into the Maderanertal. From far away he saw a person standing by the abandoned hut on the Brunnialp. He thought it was a mountaineer who intended to spend the night there and then climb the Oberalpstock or cross the Brunni Pass early the next morning. It could not be an Älpler, for the wild, high-lying Stafel had already been vacated for the season. As Zgraggen came closer, however, he noticed that the figure had no head. He began to feel uneasy, for it was as clear as day that this was not happening by natural means. With hurried strides he traverses the lonely valley, but sees with terror that the spectral figure—now with a head—is following close at his heels. It does no good to quicken his pace; his pursuer does the same. Only near the inn on the Balmenegg is he released from his terror. There he reported the matter to the visiting chaplain, a devout Father from Einsiedeln, and asked him to do everything in his power to release the spirit doing penance.
