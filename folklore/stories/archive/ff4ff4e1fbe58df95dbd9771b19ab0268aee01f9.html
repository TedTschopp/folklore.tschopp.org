(Through Fritz Grob.)", "***", "In Brunnadern, the legend is told somewhat differently. The Giessen flows out of the forest, and a secure footbridge crosses it. Once, a Nixchen lived in the brook. It stopped every traveler, took his cap, and hurried joyfully away with it. But if the traveler returned by the same path, he found his cap beautifully and cleanly washed at the footbridge. A young man also often took this path when he went to his beloved in the evening. He always gladly gave the Nixe his cap and always received it back clean. If he had adorned it with lovely flowers intended for his beloved bride, the Nixe left him the flowers and took only the cap. One evening, he again crossed the footbridge with magnificent roses on his cap, but this time the Wasserfräulein took both roses and cap. Dismayed, the young man continued onward. His heart's foreboding of misfortune had not deceived him. That day, his beloved did not greet him affectionately, and when he entered the house, she told him that she wanted nothing more to do with him. He returned to the footbridge, took his cap, pulled it low over his face, and wandered far away into foreign lands.\nH.
