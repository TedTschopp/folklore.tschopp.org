If someone passed there late at night, there would first be a shrill whistle; then, the second time, such a loud whoop (ho uf'gjuzot ha) that mountain and valley echoed with it. It once happened that a man who had stayed too long at an Abendsitz wanted to return home late at night and had to pass this very footbridge, by Zelliloch, where it was not safe. People tried to persuade him to stay and go home early the next morning, for something might happen to him at that notorious place. But he firmly insisted on going past Bozenloch and on home, because he was not afraid of the Bozo. ‘And what if it whistles and whoops at you?’ they asked him. ‘Then I’ll whistle and whoop back at it!’ he replied. And so he took his leave of the company gathered for the Abendsitz and went home, although it was very late and dark. When he reached the notorious Zelliloch, he heard such a shrill whistle that it went through marrow and bone. Nevertheless, he summoned his courage and whistled back at it. Then he heard it whoop so loudly that mountain and valley trembled. And he was insolent enough to whoop back at it as well. At once he heard a loud rushing through the forest and saw a large billy goat bounding toward him in mighty leaps. It planted its forefeet on his back and shoulders. No matter how quickly he hurried forward, the goat remained on his back and finally became so heavy that he thought he would have to let himself fall. With great difficulty he reached a cross standing beside the road. He embraced it and prayed fervently that God might have mercy on him. He made the most solemn promise never again to attend wicked Abendsitze, never again to make sport of the dead, but to have Holy Masses said for this spirit and to give alms, if he could still be helped. — Instantly the goat jumped off him and disappeared noisily into the forest.
