In the uppermost houses of this district lived a poor widow with several children and a few livestock. One summer, she hired the shoemaker for several days of work auf der Stör. During this time, the woman told him how, nearly every evening at four o'clock, a great snake came down from the mountains and then ate small sheep and goats belonging to her and her neighbors. He, a vigorous man, longed for a fight. But neither in the house nor among the neighbors did he find a proper weapon. So he took a large, round Hagstecken, sharpened it well, and lay in wait for the monster with it. Soon after four o'clock, it crept down from the mountain, and the shoemaker at once ventured into battle. The struggle soon made him anxious and hot enough, but at last it ended to his advantage when he succeeded in stabbing the snake through the flank. Badly wounded, it crawled farther down the mountain, and the shoemaker, exhausted to death, pursued it no longer.
