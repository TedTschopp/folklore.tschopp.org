There, to the left, the Klariden stand proudly and steeply before his eyes with their scree slopes and wastes. Once an Alpine paradise stood there, as beautiful as any. A carpet of plants greened there, full of the most aromatic herbs, so that milk flowed in streams: every cow could be milked three times daily and each time filled two buckets of two and a half measures. Once a Senn full of youthful arrogance lived here. We might call him Otmar according to some, Jörg according to others. His little dog was named Paris, his most valuable cow Brändi. They fared well with him. But he utterly showered his beloved Kathri, also called Nidelgret, with proofs of love. From her Sennhütte to his Stafel, and from there to the chapel in the middle of the lovely high valley, the Senn had the path laid entirely with the best cheeses and spread with butter. He was not so extravagant toward his mother—not even reasonably generous, but stingy to the point of sacrilege. When the poor woman once visited him, her unnatural son did serve her food, but only a small quantity of spoiled milk fare, and he even mixed this bad food with horse excr—no, I do not wish to say it. The pitiable woman felt this mistreatment in her deepest soul and, overwhelmed with pain, uttered a mother’s curse. Heaven heard it. It ordered the earth to open its womb and swallow the unnatural villain together with his paramour. The upper Firnen and rocks collapsed as though Judgment Day had begun, and the magnificent pasture was transformed into a dreadful, desolate field of stone. When people later called to the sinner’s spirit at this wasteland, the earth shook and he threw stones down from the height. — According to some, the arrogant man also desecrated Sunday, and the punishment, like his mother’s death, struck him on his wedding day. At times he is heard lamenting that he must now remain forever in this desolate sea of stone with Kathri, Paris, and Brändi. Sometimes the latter also makes herself known with dreadful bellowing. If a person of good and pure heart could be found who courageously caught the swiftly fleeing cow and, despite the Dornenzizzen of her udder, milked her in silence, then the ice and all terror would have to vanish, the magic ban would be lifted, and the poor souls would be redeemed. Once, on a quiet night, a young herder caught the enchanted cow. He milked the full udder, but then he felt the piercing thorns so painfully that he sighed “oh, woe!”—and Brändi had fled.
