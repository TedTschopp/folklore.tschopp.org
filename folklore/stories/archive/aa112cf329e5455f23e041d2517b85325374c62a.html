He knocked softly at the house door and urgently begged the housewife, who was looking out onto the ‘Läufer,’ to assist his little wife in childbirth at Valätscha. The good woman granted his request and followed the wild Mannli to his cave. There she helped the Fänggenweiblein and had the joy of receiving the dearest twins soon afterward. Immediately after birth, the two newborns were already extraordinarily lively and active; they waved their hands, kicked their feet, and began to crawl about on the floor. When the ‘Büchel-Frau’ wished to leave again, the Mannli first told her to fill her apron with coals and then lay them upon her hearth at home. At his repeated urging the woman did so, but on the way she let almost all the coals fall from her carelessly gathered apron. The Mannli, who had noticed her inattention, called after her, ‘Je mehr zerzass't, je minder d'hast’—‘The more you scatter, the less you have.’ When the woman reached home and placed the few coals remaining in her apron upon the hearth as the Mannli had instructed, they turned into pure gold.
