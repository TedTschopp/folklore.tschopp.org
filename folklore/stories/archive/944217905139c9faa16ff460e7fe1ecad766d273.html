At last one sees a man dragging a dead cow up the steep slope behind him with immense effort; once he has reached the top of the slope, he hurls it back down into the abyss and shouts and yodels so that the mountains echo. — This man once tended the cattle as a herdsman on Alp Fanin and, bribed by rich neighbors, allowed their cows to graze on a “Grüne,” where the best herbs sprouted, but denied the rest of the livestock access to this feeding place. One day, however, another cow belonging to a poor widow broke away and, despite the herdsman’s watchfulness, made for the forbidden pasture. This vexed the unjust herdsman, and he devised a remedy. After the rich people’s cows had once more been driven onto the feeding place, the herdsman laid bark from a freshly peeled fir tree on the path; the widow’s cow duly came again, but slipped on the bark and plunged down into the abyss. — Now the herdsman must haunt the place and do terrible penance for his crime: “When the cow has slipped on the fresh bark and fallen into the abyss, he leaps from the bushes, looks down from the overhanging rock into the yawning depths, shouts so that he can still be heard across the mountain, and claps his hands—but soon, as soon as the poor animal has died shattered in the depths, he begins to groan, tears at his hair, and climbs down the steep rock face. When he reaches the cow, he puts a rope around its horns in order to drag it up the Tobel with endless exertion. At last, when he has reached the top, he gives a bright demonic laugh, removes the rope, and hurls the cow down into the Tobel so as to begin his task again from the start, and does so three times in succession. — Once every ten years he meets in the Tobel a man who carries a rooster on his back, suffers so ‘erbärmlich laid’ under its burden, and ‘jesmet’ even more terribly than the herdsman does while hauling up his accursed cow. Each time he asks his fellow sufferer why the little feathered fellow must torment him so, and receives the answer that, as punishment for likewise proving unfaithful in his charge, he had been imprisoned, killed the guard at the first crow of the cock, and escaped. Since then his soul has found no rest, and he must haunt the world, once every ten years, and carry the rooster, every feather of which certainly weighs more than the cow that he—the other one—drags uphill.
