That evening the three men from the Schächental said they wanted to spend the night in Altdorf because they feared robbers. But Vögeli assured them that they need not be afraid; he would deal with the robbers by himself. So they all rode away together. As soon as they reached a lonely place behind the Schachen below the Glattenried, where the lane ran in those days, Vögeli, who was riding in front, noticed that his horse had struck a rope with its hoof. At once they heard a bell ringing in the forest, and the robbers came storming down the mountainside with a great clatter and a cracking of branches. ‘Ja, ja, chemet nur! mer heind Geld’—‘Yes, yes, just come! We have money,’ the man from Glarus called to them. But when they were close, they suddenly stopped as though rooted to the spot, and Vögeli urged his companions, ‘So jetz, g'schauwet-s' nur!’—‘Now then, just go and look at them!’ But they were too frightened and ran away.
