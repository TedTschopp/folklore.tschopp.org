The little boy of Councilman Rütti on Arni, named Vinzenz, was to fetch his mother a diaper that she had hung up not very far from the house. He did not return. For two days, his relatives and neighbors searched for him, and at last they reported the matter to the pastor of Silenen. The pastor said that they should search for one more day and, if the child appeared, give him a sign. After all, one can look straight up toward Arni from the rectory in Silenen. Otherwise, he would have the bells rung for the child. Yet on the evening of the third day, just as the pastor was about to instruct the sexton to ring for the lost child, he saw the sign on Arni. The little boy had been found beside a tree stump, or beneath a fir tree, in the Hell—the name of an estate. He explained that a black man had taken him, led him there, remained with him, and kept him warm. He had not been especially hungry. Vinzenz is still alive.", "A close relative tells it this way: The boy, about five to six years old, was watering the cows near the stable in the evening. When his family called him, he was nowhere to be found. They searched for him well into the night and saw no trace of him anywhere. It was winter, and snow lay on the ground. The next day, the father went down to Silenen to see the pastor. When he returned home, the boy was back. They had found him near the house between two large stones. The boy said that a large man with a black face and black clothes had come while he was watering the livestock, seized him by the arm, and said: “Come with me!” He had replied that he could not come. “You must,” the man said, then took him and stood beside him throughout the night. The boy felt neither cold, hunger, nor thirst. The searchers had often passed very close to him, but he had been unable to call out. It is strange that neither the boy nor the black man left any track in the snow." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
