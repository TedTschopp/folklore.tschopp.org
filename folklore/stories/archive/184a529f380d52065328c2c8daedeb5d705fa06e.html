A certain innkeeper with whom he usually lodged once said to him: “You people of Uri have, as I have heard, a money-shitter. Bring me one sometime; I could use it.” The man from Uri promised him one. The following year, however, he forgot, and only halfway there did he remember his promise. And behold! No sooner had he thought of it than one of those little green frogs came hopping along, the kind superstition calls an Allarünä or Allrünäli. If money is placed beneath such a creature, it always defecates twice as much beside it. But one must keep it orderly and clean as though it were a child. Whoever dies while possessing an Allarünä belongs to the Devil, and when it comes into a third person's hands, its owner can no longer get rid of it and is irretrievably forfeit to the Prince of Hell. The man from Uri caught the little animal, secured it in his little snuffbox, and sold it to the innkeeper, who received it with joy. The next year, the innkeeper came jubilantly to meet the man from Uri and said that he was now a rich man. The cattle dealer was frightened and later confessed everything to his confessor. The priest explained that he had to alert the innkeeper to the danger threatening his soul's salvation. He did so, and the warned man considered how to get rid of the little frog. He too went to a Capuchin, who after long reflection told him to bring it to him, which the innkeeper gladly did. The friar hung it in a little cloth from the ceiling of his cell. During the following night, a terrible uproar arose before the monastery. A band of unknown fellows raged and shouted: “Give up the prisoner! Give up our captain!” The guardian knew nothing about any of this. He asked the friars whether one of them knew something, but none could provide any information. Great terror filled the monastery. Then it occurred to the superior that he had not yet summoned and questioned one friar, the most simple-minded of all. Perhaps in his foolishness that one had done something foolish! He immediately had the friar brought before him and examined him. After the friar had confessed, the superior sent him to the gate to negotiate with the band.
