Senn, Chronicle.", "In fact, many of our hills and mountains contain ore veins of greater or lesser richness, and in many places unknown hands dug for these treasures in time immemorial. It was therefore most natural to attribute all this mining work to the race of dwarfs. The foreign gold seekers have consequently retained many traits reminiscent of dwarfs; everywhere they are pagans. Moreover, mining may have been practiced especially by unauthorized hands, since the local people knew how to occupy themselves more usefully. A name is soon found for unknown people: whatever is not German is still summarily called welsch today, and in certain regions of Italy, every foreigner is simply an Englishman. In Graubünden, it was the exceedingly rich inhabitants of Plurs who sought gold everywhere and also found it—and it positively dripped in liquid form from the stone. But this made the people arrogant, and they turned away from God; for that reason, the mountain covered the settlement of Plurs.", "Venice, of course, was likewise a rich, brilliant state in its time, and it is easy to conclude that popular tradition knew how to trace the wealth of the beautiful lagoon city to such sources.
