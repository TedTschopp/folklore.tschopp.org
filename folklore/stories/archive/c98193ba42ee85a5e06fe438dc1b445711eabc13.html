Courageously, the young man went toward it, thinking that the key he had found might belong to that door. This was indeed the case, and when the door opened, he saw before him a great, spacious hall richly adorned with gold and precious stones, with many other treasures spread all around upon long tables. At the center of the hall stood a beautiful maiden dressed in white. She approached him and offered him three gifts, saying that she was enchanted and had waited for her redemption for more than a hundred years; whether she was freed from the enchantment or condemned for another hundred years depended upon which gift he chose. The gifts were a pot of gold, a golden Kuhschelle, and the maiden herself. Then the herdsman suddenly remembered his sweetheart at home and chose the Kuhschelle. The maiden flared up in anger, and at the same time, a terrible thunder and crashing arose. As if seized by an invisible hand, the herdsman was torn out of the hall, where he sank stunned upon the turf. When he regained consciousness, he would gladly have regarded everything as a dream, but the Kuhschelle lying beside him convinced him otherwise. Bitter remorse seized him because he had not made the right choice and was now responsible for the beautiful maiden languishing under the spell for another hundred years. From then on, without rest or peace, he wandered the world. Once, he also came to a hut in which three stone-old little men lived. When he told his story to the eldest, who was the grandfather of the youngest, the old man called to him: “Fortunate for you that I observe hospitality. Today you may still rest, but tomorrow hurry away. That maiden was my daughter.
