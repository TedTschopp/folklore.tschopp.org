No one knew of their approach. When they had crossed the summit of the pass, a deaf-mute man who had never spoken a word in his life ran like a madman from hut to hut on the Distelalp, the first alp below the pass, and everywhere stammered the words very clearly: ‘They are coming, they are coming down the Tällibörter.’ Everyone was thrown into alarm. The deaf-mute lifted a great stone slab onto his shoulder, climbed with it onto the roof of a hut, and cried loudly, ‘I shall strike dead the first man who comes.’ And one did come, wearing a bright-red coat; he had hurried ahead of the rest. The mute kept his word and struck him dead as he tried to pass. At once he stripped off the red coat, put it on himself, took the severed head of the slain man in his hands, and went forward to meet the enemy. The women of the alp took forks, scythes, and other implements in hand and followed eagerly. The enemies were so frightened by this advance that they turned back and hurried out of the country over the Mondelli Pass.
