She was considered by turns the abandoned child of a foreign horde, the child of a sorcerer, or the child of an evil spirit. She went clothed in skins, carried bow and spear, and allowed herself to be seen among the people only when sacrifices were offered to the gods. At last, Saint Justus came into this valley as a herald of the Christian faith, and most of its inhabitants were converted. From then on, the beautiful maiden vanished. Only once more did a hunter named Jngur encounter her while returning from an unsuccessful hunt. He still held firmly to the old worship of the gods, although his wife had opened her ear and heart to Christ's teaching. Silently, the girl beckoned him to follow her into a cave, where she hung two deer across his shoulders and handed him a black ring, saying: ‘As long as you wear this ring on your finger, no distress will befall you; but you must not put it on until your arrow has struck the heart of the foreign Christian teacher!’ Jngur hurried down into the valley and asked after the Christian, but the man had gone, back across the Alps to the shores of Lake Brienz. Jngur's evil plan to kill Saint Justus was thwarted. Enraged, he rushed away from his wife, who was praying to the Christian God, and wandered despairingly through the forest. Suddenly, wild courage came upon him. He seized the ring and put it on his finger. Scarcely had he done so when a thunderclap shook the air, and Jngur fell dead to the ground. From that moment, the beautiful maiden called the girl from the Schratten was not seen again for centuries. Only in later times is she said to have been seen once more below in the valley, emerging from a cave in silent grief with her hair unbound.
