A small image of the Virgin Mary hung beneath a little roof on its trunk. A child who was often sent along this path never passed the tree without kneeling and reciting the prayer that had been impressed upon it at home, and each time the child would find a few small silver coins lying beside the trunk. As the child grew older, the value of the coins found there in the grass also increased: gradually, instead of Kreuzer coins, they became groschen and six-Kreuzer pieces. Once, as the child approached the tree again, it encountered a strange old woman there. She wore a white cap over her hair and a red Heidenschopen that reached far below the hips, such as older women in the Black Forest still wore in former times; her skirt and stockings were likewise red. In her hand she held by its cords a traveling or market purse, cut in a square and adorned at its lower corners with small woolen tassels. This purse too was red. Silently she offered it to the child. The little one could tell from its weight that it must be filled with money, so took it and hurried away. When the child had already gone some distance, it stopped again and looked back toward the tree. The woman was still standing there. But now she seemed so strange and so ancient that the child suddenly became frightened, threw away the purse, and fled.
