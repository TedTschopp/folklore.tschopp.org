An immeasurable amount of money is said to be hidden in the Schwarzbälmli at the Rynächt, but it is guarded by a ghost. Once, two courageous, gold-hungry young men from Schattdorf set out, laden with large sacks, to fetch money from this mysterious cave. One went barefoot so that he could escape the ghost more quickly. The other put on Kartatschen but did not fasten them; he said that if the ghost came, the Kartatschen could quickly be thrown off. When they had advanced some distance into the cave, they found a mass of dry beech leaves. The floor was covered high with them, and they rose above the young man's Kartatschen, which, being unfastened, filled with them. One man lit a match, threw it away as soon as it went out, and lit a second. The other thought they had better light the lantern instead, lest the litter catch fire and begin to smolder and smoke, leaving them neatly trapped. So they lit the lantern, yet still found nothing at all but beech leaves. At last they turned back. Outside the cave, the young man meant to shake the leaves from his Kartatschen, and out rolled and tumbled the most magnificent gold pieces! The beech leaves had turned into gold. The young men now returned once more into the mountain to fill their empty sacks with this precious litter, but this time the ghost came toward them—and not pleasantly.
