This prince became involved in a grave war against the King of France. After both lords had taken the field fiercely against each other, fought hard in open battle without result, and both lost many men, they agreed that two men, one from each army, should fight each other, and whichever man prevailed in the contest, his side would have won. The French were pleased, for they had a man who boasted of possessing the strength of two men. This knight immediately stepped forward and asked to measure himself against an enemy. But among all his knights, the Duke of Burgundy could find none who was a match for the opponent except his guest and friend, the lord of Strättlingen, who had also gone to war with him. The duke earnestly asked him to undertake the combat and win victory for Burgundy. But the man of Strättlingen said: \"It does not become me to fight the duel when so many brave knights of Burgundy are present; they would resent it from a foreigner.\" The duke replied: \"Do not worry about that, my dear friend. I indeed count many valiant knights, but none who surpasses you; therefore I ask you once more to venture the fight.\" The man of Strättlingen could resist no longer and gave the duke his promise. He then had himself led into the ring where he was to fight, sat down on a chair, and waited for his adversary. When the latter delayed coming, the man of Strättlingen fell asleep and began to snore. His opponent then appeared, and when he saw his adversary sitting on a chair and sleeping so soundly, he was frightened and said: \"If he fears me so little that he sleeps this peacefully, I dare not fight him.\" No one could bring him into the ring. Thus the man of Strättlingen won the victory for Burgundy in his sleep." ], "notes": [] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
