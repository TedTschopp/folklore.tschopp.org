If someone lay dying and already speechless, Stiefelhans came to the bedside, held a claim for debt before the dying person’s face, and caused the head to nod with a movement of his hand. He used the most audacious tricks, which were only too often successful. Once, however, his fraud was discovered. Seized by violent anger, a Senn chopped his head from his body with a “Herdgeiter” (wood knife). But the beheaded man suddenly vanished. Ever since, the Ammann roamed Oberriet as a specter. At night he released livestock from their chains, crippled horses, and continually tormented the poor farmers. A Capuchin banished the “evil spirit” to the Schwemm, his alp beneath the Kamor. But because much traffic passes there, he again had opportunities to carry out his malicious intentions upon the passing herds. It became necessary to bring another friar, who had to banish the monster to the rearmost part of the Säntis Alp, at the Stiefel. At first, he carried on his mischief there as far as “Scheien-Rossberg,” near the Soll Alp. But as the appointed end of his life approached, the boundaries of his sphere of action also narrowed. In the last period, he haunted only the Säntis Alp itself. If someone “zauerte” and another answered with “Zauren,” that person’s livestock fell into his power. The “Ave Mariarufen” dates from that time.
