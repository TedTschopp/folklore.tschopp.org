Both gave accounts of the matter that agreed in almost the same words.", "We tell it here in the son’s words:", "It was May 1879. I was 15 years old then, and we were at the Stäfeli in Niedersurenen. Wyseli was in his third year. He was a frail child and especially sickly at that time. He still wore a dress and could not speak well. Father and Mother were working in the garden some distance from the hut. One sister had stayed behind with Wyseli and the other little ones. They played together in front of our hut. When the parents came home, Mother asked in the room where Wyseli was, and the sister said, ‘Just now he was outside in front of the hut.’ They went to fetch him but could not find him anywhere. So they searched with the neighbors by the stream and everywhere else, and did so again the following day. That afternoon Father said to me, ‘I have to go to Altdorf and have the bells rung for him. First go and fetch the sheep, but don’t come down by the Stygi; come down by the storehouses!’ We had also promised Masses. I went. At the Stygi I spotted the sheep, and when I drew near, a little way beyond the Stygi, I suddenly saw Wyseli standing before the sheep in the middle of the rock debris, at the steepest spot, a short distance below the cliff. I hurried to him and took him onto my back. He said to me, ‘Stones came rolling down there!’ With him I nevertheless climbed down the Stygi, a narrow, very poor, steep path, to our hut at the Stäfeli. Even today I could point out the exact place where Wyseli stood. At home we examined him. He was completely dry, although it had rained all night. A snail trail ran across his feet. He asked for milk and said he was hungry. A few days later he said, ‘A man took me. We walked and walked!’ For a long time afterward he would not go outdoors. He was afraid and continued for a long while to look up toward the Stygi with signs of terror. From then on Wyseli grew stronger and sturdier. He is now a healthy, strong young man. He knows nothing about it.
