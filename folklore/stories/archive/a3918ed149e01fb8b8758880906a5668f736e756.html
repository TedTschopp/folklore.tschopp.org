A good friend warned him against this person: he did not like her; there was something peculiar about her, he said. The man from Gurtnellen married her nevertheless, and she behaved quite well. She also had a child, ‘healthy and properly formed’ like any other. One evening the woman asked her husband, ‘Tell me, to whom does this child belong?’ The man looked at her in astonishment and said, ‘Why, the child belongs to me and to you.’ There were two old, large wooden pegs in the wall of the sort often found in old houses, and the woman frequently asked the man to pull them out because they tore her clothes. One evening the man gave in and pulled out the wooden pegs. The next morning the woman asked him, as she had several times before, ‘And now tell me honestly and plainly: To whom does this child belong?’ ‘Oh, you know that,’ he replied. ‘The child is mine and yours.’ When he came in from the field that evening, the woman had vanished, and one half of the child lay on the table.
