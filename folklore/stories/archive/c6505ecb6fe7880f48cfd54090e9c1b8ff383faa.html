Men and young fellows sat comfortably together in the community house and spent a merry evening, so it was always unwelcome when such fellowship was disturbed in any way. Yet scarcely had the company gathered this time and the first cups been poured when word spread in alarm that a monster had been seen at the Stafel and that the sheep must not be left unwatched if loss was to be avoided. Because no one wished to leave the community house, the unmarried daughters offered to keep watch for the night. Everyone was well satisfied with this, and a band of courageous girls climbed up to the Alpe. They found nothing hostile at all, however, and because it was a cold autumn night, they occupied an old hut so they could pleasantly shorten the evening Abendsitz together with all sorts of youthful games. Everything went as desired; they remained undisturbed. Yet scarcely had midnight arrived when a terrible storm arose, the earth shook, the old hut trembled, the forest cracked, the sheep leapt about bleating, and the terrified watchers crawled into the first hiding places they could find, where they began praying aloud. Fortunately, the commotion did not last long; everything became still and quiet again.
