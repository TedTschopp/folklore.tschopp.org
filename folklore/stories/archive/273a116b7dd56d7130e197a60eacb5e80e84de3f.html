Zgraggen, Rütlipächter, u.a." ], "english_paragraphs": [ "Older people still speak often of Süffibalzi, an eccentric, harmless vagabond who traveled through the canton of Uri about half a century ago and lived on people's charity. Ziegersüffi was his favorite dish, hence his nickname Süffibalzi. All his pockets and even the lining of his jacket were so full of pinecone cows and the wooden cows farmers carve for their children that he could move only with difficulty. He knew how to amuse himself with them for hours and hours, setting them up somewhere on a bench or stone and driving them to pasture, into the stable, and up to and down from the Alp. But another kind of cow also enjoyed his special affection: his lice, which are said to have been of extraordinary, gigantic size. When undisturbed, he reached into his shirt, brought them out, and set them up as cows too. He is said to have assembled a fine Sennten. After amusing himself with them for a while, he returned his bosom friends to their customary place.—The legend reports the following about his death:", "The Alpine herdsmen at Alpgnof in the Maderanertal mixed cheese rennet into the Ziegersüffi they set before him, and, as was his habit, he filled skin and belly with it. After satisfying himself, he set off toward the Stäfel. But when he reached the Stoffelstein on the Stäflerboden, the fermenting Ziegersüffi suffocated or burst him. At any rate, the Alpine herdsmen found him lying dead there the next morning. The place covered by his body, however, ‘did not grow green again for three years.’", "Jos.
