The place is called ‘the Rüfe’; no traces of buildings remain. The inhabitants of Zarera, so the legend relates, were wicked, godless people who dishonestly enriched themselves at the expense of the packhorse drivers and other travelers who passed through and stopped there with their horses. One night a maiden was seen riding a white horse around the village, and she was heard loudly warning the people of Zarera and calling upon them to repent. But the people of Zarera persisted in their corrupt disposition, and a great judgment of punishment came upon them. Dense black clouds gathered in the sky, lightning flashed, thunder rolled, and rain fell in torrents. The mountain streams swelled and swept away the mightiest tree trunks and boulders. It was a dreadful night, during which everyone crossed themselves, and when morning dawned, Zarera was no more. All its inhabitants met their deaths in the floods and beneath the debris. Only an old mother and her daughter were spared. Unlike the others, they were God-fearing and took joy in helping their neighbors and travelers. A gust of wind carried them away beyond the reach of the swollen Rüfe, and they remained alive." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Rüfe”: a mountain debris flow, the torrent carrying it, or the channel it cuts; here the word is also the place name for the site of Zarera, so its landform and disaster senses overlap." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
