The line ‘Guggus, Guggus, I am still alive’ may reveal the cuckoo—which plays a special role in folk belief—as a soul-animal or perhaps a carrier of the soul; a version from Brienzwiler names it explicitly. Individual motifs in the tale point to great age. Even the conception of the soul's nature leads back to the beginnings of human culture. But the mother's cruelty also speaks for this, as F. von der Leyen explains in The Folktale. For the cannibalism occurring in the narrative may not have sprung from fantasy but may lead back to humanity's childhood; dark memories thousands of years old return in the present tale. The tale still lives on today in meager remnants in various places.
