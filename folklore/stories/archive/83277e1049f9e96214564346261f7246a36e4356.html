Long years ago, it is said, a band of wanton young men took it into their heads to baptize a ram. As punishment for this impiety, the ram became a dreadful monster and tore apart countless sheep, cattle, cows, and even people, so that the country grew desolate and looked ever wilder. After a long time, however, a strange fahrender Schüler came along the road. He was versed in secret arts and advised the afflicted people how they could help themselves. He ordered them to take the firstborn calf of a strong cow and let it suckle for twelve years, from one more cow each year, until there were likewise twelve cows and the calf had grown strong enough to become a terrifying bull. Then they were to choose a pure maiden who would lead this monster by her hair cords onto the Alp, where it could give them peace. The herders did so. In a few years, the calf grew so large that they had to build a high platform for the cows from which it would continue to suckle. At last, the appointed time arrived, and when the right girl had been chosen, the giant ox, otherwise uncontrollable, followed her quietly and obediently. She climbed far up the mountainside to a large stone, mounted it, and there released the animal she had led from its cord. Soon the mountain's monster came rushing forth with terrible roaring, and a dreadful battle with the ox began, until the monster fell mortally wounded. Then the overheated bull drank from a nearby spring and suddenly fell lifeless to the ground. Yet the mountain's surface never returned to its former splendor. Even now, it is rough and covered with stones and not as rich in herbs as it once was.
