Sometimes he is the Härdli-Geist, who must move back the boundary stones with which he unjustly enlarged his holdings during his lifetime; at other times he is said to be Heiri of Zurich, who strangled the mayor of Zurzach in the forest and was executed here for it. No. 351 tells of him. A whole host of fallen warriors, from what age and war no one knows clearly, is likewise said to begin a mournful nocturnal procession from here. Many different things are related. The following, however, is certain and happened only in recent times. A mason named Wengi of Klingnau, known by the family nickname Küng, had spent the week working in the Baden town of Waldshut and was going home to his family on Saturday. Because he could not leave Waldshut until the end of the working day, it was almost midnight when he passed that bridge. It was already behind him when he thought he heard a faint moaning. He stopped, and when he heard it again, he supposed that perhaps a wood-gatherer or a tavern-goer had fallen into the depths of the notorious ravine. Wengi knew no fear; he had a very compassionate heart, and, being otherwise a bold and strong man, he dared to climb down. After forcing his way through the brush, he called toward the voice, but no one answered him. He did not like this at all, and only now—although he had heard the lamenting so distinctly several times—did he recall the stories told about the place. He therefore climbed back up to continue home. But then he heard trotting and snorting behind him, and presently a foal stood nearby which, instead of shying away, seemed intent on following him. The man thought he had made a lucky catch, took out his handkerchief, tied it around the foal's neck, and led it behind him. Before long his arm hurt and the cloth seemed too short. He turned to retie it, but, incomprehensibly, in that instant the foal had grown tall, and only with the greatest effort could he still reach up to untie his handkerchief quickly. He ran away, but the ever-growing animal sprang after him just as fast and had already swollen to a terrifying size when he reached the cross near Klingnau, where several thousand imperial soldiers lie buried who drowned during the attempted crossing of the Aare here in 1799 or fell under French bullets. The man fled to the cross, and there the animal vanished at once.
