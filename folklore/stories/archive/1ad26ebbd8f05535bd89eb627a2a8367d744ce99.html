At haymaking time he went up to his Mayensäss to gather the fodder, but the weather was poor, so that although he could mow, he could not dry what had been cut. At last, after he had mown all the grass, the weather became as beautifully fine as he could wish, but now he lacked diligent hands, for his labor alone accomplished little of the whole task. The maid was still there, to be sure, but she laughed heartily at him and mocked his distress, saying “she was capable of bringing each and every bit of hay into its proper place in one hour.” This seemed rather strange and far too colorful to our good Christof, but he readily gave his consent for her to proceed as she wished. Secretly, however, he desired to see how the “Mensch” would set about the task, and he hid himself, peering through a crack in the wall. — The maid truly came with a broom and made a sweep or stroke in each of the four corners of the Mayensäss while reciting a charm. Scarcely had this been done when all the hay flew into the barn and arranged itself in perfect order. Then the maid came, struck the hay once with the broom, and cried, “Sitz”—“Settle!”—and behold, the obedient fodder compressed itself neatly to two-thirds of its former size. — What he had seen filled the good “Gschworne” with terror and astonishment, but he was foolish and ungrateful enough to denounce the maid before the court. The court had the “Hexenmensch” arrested and taken to the Oberland, where she was burned as a witch.
