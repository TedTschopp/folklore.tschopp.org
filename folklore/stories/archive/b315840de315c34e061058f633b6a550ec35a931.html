One day the father gave Micheli, who was about five years old, some milk, but the portion was too meager for the little boy. He became angry and ran out of the hut. He played with companions for a while, and then, no one knew how, they lost sight of him. For three days the parents and neighbors searched for him, and at last they promised a Tafäla to the pilgrimage chapel of Our Lady of Sorrows at Getschwyler. They had the bell rung for him. On the third day his baptismal godfather also helped search. He found the child on the far side of the wild Brunni-Schächen beneath a little fir tree in a forest clearing, at a place they had already passed more than once while searching. ‘But how did you get across here?’ the godfather asked. ‘A big black man came,’ said the Büebli, ‘and took me by the hand and went down toward the stream with me. Then down by the stream a beautiful woman came, took me from the man, and led me over the Ronä—a tree trunk laid across the stream—to this little place here.’ ‘Yes, and what have you been doing here?’ the Büebli was asked further. ‘I g'fätterlet with Schnäggähüslänä and Steindlänä.
