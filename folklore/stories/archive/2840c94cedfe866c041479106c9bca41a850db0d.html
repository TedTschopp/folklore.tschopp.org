Since it was already the end of autumn and all the Sennen had long since descended to the valley with their herds, he was more than a little surprised when, approaching that hut, he heard voices and the ringing of cowbells. Curious, he entered the hut, but how astonished he was to find within four Sennen whom he had never seen in his life. One was one-eyed and one lame; the third had a hump on both back and breast, while the fourth appeared to have leprosy. All four, however, were yellow in the face and wrinkled like old parchment, and each lacked the second and third fingers of the right hand. Their language, entirely unintelligible to the hunter, resembled the croaking of ravens in wintertime. After regarding the newcomer sideways for some time, they invited him to sit on a thick wooden block near the fire. Although he felt somewhat uneasy at heart, he accepted the invitation but kept his rifle between his legs for safety. This seemed to concern the Sennen little; they continued their work undisturbed. First, cheese was made, then Zieger, a supply of which was already stacked on a beam in the hut. When the work was finished, the hunchback offered the hunter bread and a piece of beef. Because he was very hungry, he accepted what was offered, drew his knife from his pocket, and cut himself a bite from the meat. The bite was no larger than a fingertip. But because it tasted very bland, the hunter quietly muttered to himself, as people often do when a meal is not to their liking, ‘The salt is missing.’ Scarcely had these words crossed his lips when the four Sennen began to bare their teeth horribly and regarded the hunter with looks as though they meant to devour him. The thought then occurred to him that he was not among Christians. Commending himself to all the saints, he quickly made the sign of the cross, and suddenly everything had vanished—Sennen and cows—and the hunter was alone in the pitch-black hut. After recovering somewhat from his fright, he threw himself onto a heap of hay that he found by feeling around in one corner of the hut. But sleep did not enter his eyes. Only in the morning did he see that what he had taken for hay was a heap of ashes, and that where the cheeses and Zieger had stood on the hut’s beam the evening before, there were dry mortar and rotten wood. He hurriedly left the hut and turned his steps homeward. Halfway home, one of his boys ran to meet him, calling: ‘Father, think what happened to Meriau—Miroir, “the mirror,” here the name of a cow—last night! A piece of flesh as large as a fingertip is missing from her left thigh!’ Then the hunter knew what had happened. Without doubt, this was the piece of meat he had eaten the previous evening in the hut on the Moléson. The ghosts, an old man from his village later told him, were the spirits of a cowherd who had used a false will to appropriate the alpine pasture on which that hut stood, and of his three witnesses. Bribed with money, they had falsely sworn, as he had, that the will was genuine; therefore, all four perjurers lacked the fingers used in swearing." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Senn”: an Alpine dairyman who tends cattle and makes cheese on the high summer pasture; the plural Sennen is retained throughout for the four supernatural workers.", "Swiss term “Zieger”: a traditional Swiss whey cheese; the apparent stack in the haunted hut is revealed by daylight to be dry mortar and rotten wood." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
