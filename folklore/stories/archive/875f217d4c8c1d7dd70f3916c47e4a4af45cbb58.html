He was called the Stiefelirüter and is said to have been the abbey's estate manager. He deprived entire villages of their common property. He disputed the municipality of Merenschwand's possession of the beautiful Maiholz near Muri and the municipality of Müswangen's rights to the Schlatt woodland. He played the same trick on the town of Bremgarten; conversely, as Bremgarten's advocate, he is said to have cheated the municipality of Wohlen out of its forest—always and everywhere successfully. Because the people fell under the monastery's jurisdiction, judgments not only regularly went against them, but in addition to losing their communal property, they had to pay the accumulated legal costs. The manager continued his schemes into his seventieth year and must now wander the wrongfully acquired monastery lands in punishment. At times, he rides a Schimmel in the Eichholz and is therefore called the Schimmelrüter; at times, he hopt—calls out—in the Breithau; and at times, the Reuss bridge in Bremgarten thunders beneath his horse's hoofbeats so loudly that it can be heard as far away as the Kreuzwirt. He then fetches a nag from the stable of the old estate office there, which once belonged to Muri. In a single bound, he and his horse leap over the ring wall of Muri Abbey, where every year a fresh horse must stand ready for him in the monastery stable. Sometimes he walks through the Bünzen forest as a long, rail-thin fellow with cunning written all over his eyes. Sometimes he rides the Schimmel into the Maiengrün and is then called the Markstaller around Hägglingen. Sometimes he runs about the Rohrdorf moor and, because of his cries, is called the Hopelirüter.", "The monks endowed Masses for him and thereby somewhat diminished the terror of his apparition. For some years, he has also made less noise than before. Even now, however, older people can be heard cursing this Stiefeli, for their parents or grandparents were supposedly his contemporaries and were once mistreated by him. People firmly maintain that he was still living in the year 1740.
