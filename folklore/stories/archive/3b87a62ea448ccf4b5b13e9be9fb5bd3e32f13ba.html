A farmer from Duferswil wanted to plow a field below the little village. But something uncanny must have been about. The oxen suddenly escaped the farmhand’s control and, together with the yoke and plow, ran downhill toward the cliff. The farmer would not let go of the plow goad and shouted as loudly as he could, but it was all in vain. The oxen raced madly over the edge and down the cliff. Only then did the farmer let go. In his fright, he snatched the driving stick from the boy’s hand, threw it after the oxen, and said, ‘Well then, in God’s name!’ That prevented the disaster. The cliff is more than two church towers high, yet everything reached the bottom upright. The oxen waited below beside the Necker until the farmer arrived. He took them home again, and they followed him as though nothing had happened. At his own expense, the farmer then built the little chapel in Duferswil, and it still stands today where he placed it.", "***", "The legend of the Humplattenwand is also told more simply: one night, an alpine dairyman was driving two oxen past the upper edge. When the man went to look for his oxen, he found both uninjured at the foot of the cliff, with the staff leaning beside them.
