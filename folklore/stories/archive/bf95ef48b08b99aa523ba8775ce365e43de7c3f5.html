It harms no one who calmly lets it follow its course. Anyone who encounters it stands aside and lets the whispering figures pass. They also often stop in barns, stables, and Alpine dairy huts. That is why the doors must be left open on certain nights. Once a Senn sleeping on the Gasteren in the customary bed in the hay above the stable heard strange voices below without understanding the meaning of their words. He cautiously crept to the fodder opening to look down. He suspected that the wildes Heer had stopped at his place. And indeed, the Nachtvolk was in his hut. The peculiar figures had made themselves comfortable. Some sat around the soot-blackened hearth, where they had kindled a blazing fire, while others untied Spiegel, the finest cow in the stable, from her place, slaughtered and roasted her, and feasted merrily on her meat. Their insolence tormented the Senn to the core. He leaped up and was about to intervene, but thought better of it in time and remained silent. Then one of the mysterious company turned his eye toward him and politely offered him a bite of meat from his own property. He did not refuse and ate the piece without saying a word. When the meal was over, the Nachtvolk vanished again, the fire went out, and the Senn, still paralyzed with fear and anger, crawled back to his bed. In the morning, however, when he climbed down into the stable to tend the cattle, behold: the slaughtered Spiegel once again stood in her customary place and gave the man, astonished beyond measure, her usual morning greeting. But, oh wonder! A small piece of flesh was missing from the cow's hindquarters, and on looking closely he said to himself: that is precisely the part I ate during the night.
