In the Hornbach valley there is a farmstead likewise called Hornbach, on the little river of the same name. In the eighteenth century, so the story goes, the Maitschi of that farm were busy hanging their laundry out to dry at a low-lying place near the stream. A little man dressed in green came along, with a Hülfterchen fastened around his body. He greeted them kindly and told them to take their laundry away or it might be swept off. The Maitschi only smiled. But a heavy rain soon came, and the dark clouds massed so thickly that the girls hurried away with their laundry. A cloudburst poured over the rear part of Hornbach. The stream swelled, and the inhabitants rushed from their houses to check the feared ravages of the water if they possibly could. To the washerwomen's horror, they saw a great snake on the first mighty wave; seated upon it was the same little green man who had spoken to them that morning. He had put the Hülfterchen on the snake and rode onward over the water. On the grassy ground the snake made a leap and fell back into the water on the other side. The stream raged terribly.
