Once, when no one in Urseren was seriously thinking of war, this priest informed his countrymen beyond the Oberalp that on a certain Sunday they should take the people of Urseren by armed surprise. Everyone without exception would then be in church; he told them the hour of the service. The Sunday arrived. It was around Christmastime and bitterly cold. Great numbers of well-armed men from Graubünden crossed the Oberalp and already appeared on the slopes above the village. But a woman in childbed at Andermatt—or a little old mother—had remained at home, saw the enemy, and immediately guessed their purpose. She hurried to the carefully locked church, which then stood at the foot of the Kilchberg, smashed the windows, and shouted as loudly as she could: “The Valzauser[1] are coming! The Valzauser are coming!” Enraged, the men smashed the church pews, used the pieces as clubs, advanced against the treacherous enemy with clubs, scythes, and forks, and defeated and beat them so thoroughly that only two men in their ranks remained alive. The inhuman people of Urseren gouged out one man's eyes and cut out the other's tongue, then sent the two home. The traitorous priest, meanwhile, had deliberately prolonged the service that Sunday. They drove him out—or killed him—and requested Capuchins as their parish priests for the future.\nJos. Huber; Fr.
