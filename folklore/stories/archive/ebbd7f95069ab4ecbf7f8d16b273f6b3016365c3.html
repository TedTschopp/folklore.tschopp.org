At the corner of a house, she told him that she still had something to do there and that he should wait for her a little while. Then he could no longer see her. A black cat climbed the wooden wall of the house and entered through an open window. Before long, it returned by the same route, and the maiden stood there once more. The young man confronted her about it. She lamented to him that she had been born in an unlucky hour and had to torment people and livestock as a Schrättlig. He asked whether it was possible to free her from the affliction. She answered that he could do so if he allowed her to crush to death something that belonged to him. He gave her permission. In the morning, his finest cow lay dead in the stall. He went to the maiden and reproached her for choosing the most valuable animal of all. She said she had been obliged to do so because he had given his permission without conditions. Had he expressly designated his least valuable animal, it would have served just as well. Even so, he rejoiced with her that she had been released from the evil spell, and she rewarded him with faithful love throughout her life.\nJ. B.
