Once, so many people are said to have died that grass began to sprout on the road to the church. There were no longer enough people to bury the dead. An old man with an open wound on his leg, who was therefore spared by the disease, hired himself out for the task. He too is said to have received a Klafter-high stack of linen sheets as his burial wage. One family lived alone in a house some distance away. They shut themselves in and avoided every meeting with other people; only from time to time did one of them come out onto a hill to learn whether the dying had ceased. At last, he received the good news, and the survivors joyfully emerged to take possession of numerous inheritances. They happily divided the household goods, quarreling only over a sack full of wool, which at last fell to the family that had remained untouched. With this wool, they brought the disease into their house, and within a short time they all died. An unmarried young man fled the country across the Augstalberg when the disease broke out. When he returned after a long time, the dying had ceased, but he put on a coat that had been left behind.
