Each time he carefully pushed it back into the earth, but the following evening it was stretched upward again. At last, in concern, he informed the parish priest at that time, Imhof (died 1798, priest since 1765), who had a reputation for great learning and piety. After discovering who the child's parents were, the priest summoned the mother and told her everything. This made her very sad, and because she did not know what to do, she asked the well-loved pastor for sound advice. He asked whether perhaps she had allowed the child to go unpunished when it had deserved punishment. ‘I remember only that it once struck at me with its hand. But because it was scarcely more than two years old, I let the fault go unpunished,’ the mother confessed. ‘Then go,’ the clergyman instructed her. ‘Take a switch and strike the outstretched hand with it several times. Then we may hope that the poor child in the grave finds the rest it longs for.’ With a heavy heart, the mother went and acted upon the zealous priest's wise counsel.
