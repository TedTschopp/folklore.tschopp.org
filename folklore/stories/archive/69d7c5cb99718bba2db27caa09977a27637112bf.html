This is the surest evidence that devout believers from the surrounding area frequently made pilgrimage to this saint, performed their devotions in his church, and hung up votive tablets, some of which still survive.", "To the modern outlook it may seem strange to build a church to St. Nicholas in a rather dangerous place. It stands beneath a fissured and very loose hill called the Dorftossen and within the path of a large and dangerous avalanche. This tendency to erect houses of prayer in dangerous and terrifying places was prevalent among the ancients, whether because the human spirit could there detach itself more readily from earthly things and turn heavenward more easily in prayer, or because people wished there to implore heaven to avert grave disasters. The church in St. Niklaus was often endangered and damaged by avalanches; indeed, in 1749 it was entirely swept away except for the tower and choir. This happened just as the sacristan was ringing the morning prayer from the tower. He thought he had heard only a great gust of wind and was therefore more than a little astonished to emerge from the bell tower not into the church but into the open air.", "Once the people resolved to rebuild St. Nicholas’s church in the beautiful and safe “Feld” on the far bank of the Vispa.
