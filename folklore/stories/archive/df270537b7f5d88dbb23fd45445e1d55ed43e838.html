In neighborly friendship, old Johannes, Baron of Weissenburg, kept an open house nearby at his fortress and invited the monks to feast very often. They called upon him by day and night. The baron also had a little daughter who was without equal and was named Kunigund. He wished to give her in marriage to a man of Grimmenstein named Lord Hans. But Grimmenstein was a very severe and exceedingly hard man.", "At that time, an honorable cleric from the little monastery at Terenschatten also often came to the baron's wife at the castle. His name was Brother Gervasi; by birth he was a lord of Simmenegg and the last of his line. His office was to edify the old woman and the young lady. The latter often showed him favor. The wedding with Grimmenstein was soon to take place. But the young maiden was rebellious and wanted less and less to know of it. Brother Gervasius had won her heart; she loved him with all its ardor. She could not conceal this from him and told him through tears that she would rather die than become Grimmenstein's wife. The monk, however, had long been devoted to her and now opened his own heart. They agreed to flee together and seek their fortune in the world. At night, the maiden slipped out and, as arranged, found the brother at the little gate, well equipped and waiting for her. He lifted her in his arms and boldly carried her through the Simme, across water and cruel stones, into a mountain gorge. Here they remained hidden and built a little hut. A goatherd who owed Gervasius thanks for a kindness brought them what they needed to live. When the old baron noticed that his little daughter was missing, he sent his armed retainers far across the country after her. But they could obtain no news of the maiden. Then the baron lamented to heaven that he had pressed his child so harshly. He did penance for an entire year and afterward died filled with repentance and sorrow.", "Meanwhile, the brother and maiden conducted themselves honorably and quietly, as married people do, in chastity and complete faithfulness. They would not leave even when the herdsman told them that the old baron had died in blessedness. But afterward, the good Frau Küngold became sick and infirm. This made the brother sorrowful at heart, for he feared she would die and leave him lonely and grieving. Therefore he prayed much and also treated her with all kinds of herbs. But this did not help. She grew ever weaker. Then her husband went out and received a great grace, for in that same gorge he found a Brünnelein that was warm, sprang from the Fluh, and had a very peculiar taste. He drew a Näpflein full and gave it to his beloved to drink. It seemed to him very healing and to possess a wondrous power, for scarcely had his heart's beloved drunk a little of the curative gift when she already felt a marvelous strengthening come over her and soon recovered more and more. Thereafter, the brother dispensed water from the Brünnelein to everyone who lay sick and afflicted, so that he was blessed for giving the water until his holy death.
