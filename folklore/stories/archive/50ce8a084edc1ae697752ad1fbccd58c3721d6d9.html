On an Alp in the Meien Valley called Aistner Alp, a certain Baschi Frech worked as a Senn for many years. He truly was extraordinarily bold and never feared anything. He knew well that during winter, dairy work was also performed on his Alp by those who, as Sennen in life, had not done their work properly—whether they had neglected the Molken or the livestock, or had given one of the Mithaften a larger share than another. Afterward, these people had to wander there in winter. All the Älpler also knew that once the Alpine hut had been abandoned in autumn, no one could return until spring, or things would go so badly that God would have mercy. People in the Bernese Oberland still believe this today.", "Once, however, Baschi Frech had forgotten something on his Alp that he urgently needed. He told many of his companions that he had to return and asked whether one or two would go with him, but not one would go for a hundred crowns. Baschi nevertheless went completely alone. Everyone thought him lost and spoke in the evening parlors of his dreadful audacity. As he departed, he said that those up there would not eat him just yet. It was already late when he reached the Alp, and he stayed there overnight. He ate something and lay down in the customary Gutsche where he also had his G'lieger in summer, although only old hay remained in it. It was cold, damp, and unpleasant, but one night will not kill me, Frech thought. He did not sleep well; only from weariness did his eyes sometimes fall shut. But hallo! Around 12 o’clock, he heard from far away a company of Sennen and Alpine farmhands clattering toward the hut in wooden shoes. Baschi Frech thought to himself, “This is no time for sleep, but for watching what is played out here.” They struck open the door and entered the hut amid dreadful rattling. Without speaking a word, they began the dairy work, hung up the Kessi, lit a fire beneath it, brought milk from the storehouse, and threw it into the Kessi. It rushed and foamed; they ankten, made butter, and produced very many cheeses, all at great speed. Baschi watched them closely, and at times his hair stood on end, but he resisted every fear. The Sennen now had three kinds of Schotten: red, green, and yellow. To his great astonishment, they called for him to come down and drink Schotten with them. Boldly, he went down to the Wintersennen, through whose ribs he could see. But Baschi Frech was not at a loss. They asked which “Suffi” he wanted. Quickly, boldly, and loudly, he answered that he wanted the green, and he drank, though it was not as good as what he made himself. They told him it was fortunate that he had asked for the green; otherwise matters would have gone badly enough for him. One of the uncanny beings went and knocked for the pigs, and the pigs came with a great commotion. Baschi returned to his bed and left his knife where he had drunk the Schotten, for he had taken bread from his bag and eaten it with the whey. One of the Wintersennen took Baschi’s knife and trimmed the cheeses with it. When he had finished, he thrust the knife into Baschi’s leg so that it stuck deep inside. Early in the morning, after the spectral Sennen had vanished, Baschi had to walk home with the knife in his leg. But no one, no doctor, could remove it. The doctors of Altdorf advised him to go there once more; when that spirit again trimmed cheese, he would surely need his knife again. As soon as the spirit pulled the knife from Baschi’s leg, Baschi should leave instantly. Baschi Frech followed this advice, returned to the Alpine hut, and was freed from the knife when the spirit again trimmed the cheeses.
