A few climbs farther up the mountain, one stands before the Herrlichkeitsstein and its great cave, which extends more than fifty paces into the rock and is high enough for a man to walk upright inside. Basel territory borders Aargau here, and for that reason people without a legal home also often stay here, where they can easily escape one canton’s police when they can no longer evade the other’s. An entire band is said to have lived here undiscovered for ten years. When the Bannwart encountered them two years ago, he had to defend himself for a long time against their knives with his knobbed stick, so greatly did they fear that he would betray them to the Landjäger. Beside this cave, a second slopes down into the mountain and has been explored for only a few hundred feet. It is even more notorious; after the cheat whose spirit is now said to haunt it, it is called the Gleichaufshöhle. He had been the Klosterschaffner of Olsberg Abbey and entered a dispute with the commune of Magden over possession of a forest. Before the court he declared to the farmers, “It all comes out Gleichauf. What you have too little of on one side benefits us on the other; it therefore all comes out Gleichauf!” He also bribed the judges and disputed the testimony of the opposing witnesses, and so the people of Magden lost the entire wooded mountain, which was then awarded to the abbey. From then on, they called the administrator only Gleichauf. It did not help him for long. The abbey was dissolved, and today the forest belongs to the Iglingen farm. What became of Gleichauf was once seen by a mower walking home from his meadow to Iglingen at night in bright moonlight. A double fox came down through the forest from the Herrlichkeitsstein above, making a great noise. With fiery eyes, it ran howling along every boundary and field edge from one boundary stone to the next, then swept back into its cave amid vapor. That is the thieving Klosterschaffner, and so every midnight he must retrace his boundary crime." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Gleichaufshöhle”: literally the “Gleichauf cave,” named for the dishonest administrator and his repeated claim that everything came out Gleichauf; the cave name preserves both his nickname and the pun.", "Swiss term “Bannwart”: a Swiss communal forest or land warden charged with watching protected resources and boundaries; here he encounters the cave dwellers, though his exact legal powers are not specified.", "Swiss term “Landjäger”: an older Swiss and German-speaking term for rural or cantonal police officers, literally “country hunters”; here they are the authorities the borderland cave dwellers evade.", "Swiss term “Gleichauf”: literally “even,” “level,” or “coming out equal,” the corrupt Klosterschaffner’s cynical phrase and resulting nickname; the joke is that his supposed balance transfers the farmers’ loss to the abbey’s gain.", "Swiss term “Klosterschaffner”: an abbey’s secular steward or estate administrator, responsible for property and revenues; here he abuses that position in a boundary dispute, though the office’s exact jurisdiction is unstated." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
