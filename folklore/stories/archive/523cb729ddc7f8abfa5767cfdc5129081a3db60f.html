She brought a large sum from the abbey’s assets, entrusted it to the old man, and remained with him until the war ended. But when she wished to return to Fahr and take the rescued abbey property with her, her cousin flatly denied ever having received money from her for safekeeping. No one had been present when Joggeli took possession of the money, and no one knew of it. Thus nothing could be done to him, and the nun returned empty-handed to her convent.", "The deceiver now lived grandly and magnificently and, instead of working, rode about the surrounding area at all times. People found it strange enough that he had become rich so quickly when shortly before he had barely managed to survive, but they had to remain silent.", "Suddenly he died in the midst of luxurious living. On the second night after his death, he began disturbing his own house so severely that no one inside could close an eye. He dragged all the chains from the barn into the dwelling and hauled them up and down the stairs. Then he opened the chamber doors and violently threw people from their beds. No servant would remain in the house; no farmhand would even enter the barn. If they had to climb to the hayloft at night, he extinguished the servants’ lantern, and if they did not leave quickly, he threw them down onto the threshing floor. At last, a Capuchin was summoned to bind him. The ghost appeared in a long black coat and an exceptionally large hat and is said to have exchanged very strange and insolent words with the Capuchin. Nevertheless, in the end he was bound into an Estrichwinkel.
