In particular, he stayed in the house directly beside the great minster, called zum Loch, which in the present day is a canons' court and which he had built for himself. So that rich and poor alike might obtain justice and no one be denied access, he had a pillar erected and a Glöggli hung upon it at the place where the dear saints Felix and Regula had been beheaded, and he had it proclaimed: whoever desired justice should ring this Glöggli while the emperor was eating, and he would hear that person. After this had been the custom for some time, while the emperor sat at table, he heard ringing and immediately sent his servant there to see who desired justice. They found no one.", "And as soon as they went away from there, it rang again; this happened repeatedly.", "Then the emperor ordered someone to wait and see who did it. Thereupon, a great Wurm—a snake—came, hung from the bell rope, and rang.", "They reported this to the emperor, who rose from his meal and said that, for the honor of its Creator, the irrational creature should be granted equal justice with humanity. When the emperor came to the place, the Wurm bowed and crawled toward the water into a Rüschi, where it had laid its eggs and upon which a great Krott—a toad—had settled. When the emperor and all his court attendants saw this, he sat in judgment and pronounced that the Krott should be burned. After this had been done, several days later, while the emperor sat at table, the Wurm came to the court. This was made known to him. He therefore ordered that it be allowed to enter and that no one hinder its coming. The snake then crawled before the emperor, bowed, crawled onto the table, pushed the lid from his drinking vessel, and let a precious stone fall inside; it turned around, bowed before the emperor, and went away.", "This great wonder, and the fact that the blessed saints Felix and Regula had been martyred on this court site for the Christian faith, moved the emperor to establish an eternal memorial.", "To the praise and honor of God, he built a house of worship there, called the Wasserkirche in the present day because for much of the time, water flows around it.
