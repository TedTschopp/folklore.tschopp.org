Amid dreadful pounding and noise, these uncanny journeymen framed, sawed, hewed, and chiseled away with lightning speed. The building rose so quickly that, during that very night, all the farmer's doubt that the work would be accomplished by the appointed cockcrow melted away, which frightened him badly enough. In torment he tossed back and forth on his bed, trembling and quaking like an aspen leaf, until his wife awoke and pressed him earnestly to reveal the cause of his anguish. Once she knew everything, she comforted the poor man. And because there was no time left to lose, she quickly rose to kindle a fire on the hearth. “I'll wager the cock will crow presently, before those fellows have entirely finished the building,” she said. No sooner said than done. The fire flared up, the cock awoke and crowed his greeting, and the farmer learned that womanly cunning, which had often inconvenienced him before, could under some circumstances still be good for something.
