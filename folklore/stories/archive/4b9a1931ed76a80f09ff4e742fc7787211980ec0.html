At the boundary between Seelisberg and Emmetten, three men in old-fashioned dress often appeared, wearing red waistcoats, short trousers, buckled shoes, and tricorn hats. One behind another, they walked along the March at a measured, solemn pace; the first carried an axe in his right hand, the second bore a large open book on his bent back, and the last, following behind, pointed at this book or, as others say, wrote in it. They were Weltsmannen; Franz Truttmann saw them and swore that he could easily have walked between their legs without stooping, and yet he was not one of the short ones.", "Father Furrer († 1883) banished them; when he addressed them, he reserved the first and last word for himself, or else they would have talked him to death.
