The distinctive feature of the legend—what makes it captivating and therefore dangerous—is that it lays claim to credibility. It not only identifies with absolute precision the place where something is supposed to have happened but also knows the given names and family names of the people involved. This still happens so frequently in the districts of Sargans and Werdenberg that it shows us how extraordinarily rich in living legends the St. Gallen Oberland remains today. In many cases, obvious considerations forced us to strike out such names, much as we regretted doing so. Anyone who has personally listened to the old tellers of legends knows how to extract the poetry contained in all the marvelous trappings and is not alarmed when a hundred proofs are offered for a single one. The storyteller himself claims to have been present everywhere, or at least to have known the people who took part. Yet the core of what was told always remained one and the same, and with all its additions it passed from mouth to mouth. The teller of legends sang for the people as the minnesinger sang for the court, though in a different form and therefore with a much more enduring effect. The frivolous song has faded away; the legend endures." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Dreiböhri”: the regional name for a three-cornered hat, literally something like a ‘three-peaked’ hat; the source itself glosses the shape, but the exact local construction and origin of the name are uncertain.", "Swiss term “Venediger”: in Alpine legend, a name for supposedly Venetian or southern prospectors credited with secret knowledge of mountain ores and often treated as magicians; ‘Venetian’ gives the geographic association but not this regionally specific folkloric role, whose historical basis is uncertain." ] }, "legacy_urls": [] } ---
