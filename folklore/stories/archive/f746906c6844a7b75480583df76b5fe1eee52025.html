In the middle of the night a tremendous commotion arose among the fowl; the chickens cackled and fluttered wildly among one another until no one could sleep. No one knew what was at work there, but everyone understood that it could not be happening by natural means. On a neighbor’s advice, they finally thrust a knife into the floor of the chicken coop with its edge or point directed upward, and from that time onward the feathered domestic animals had peace. But now it entered the Gaden among the cattle, just as it had previously entered among the chickens. The tormented people went to the priest, but he wanted nothing to do with the matter and directed them to the Capuchins in Altdorf, who were powerful against magic. The most devout of them came up to the Lehmatt, which lies gently against the mountainside two hours above Spiringen. Before refreshing himself with food and drink, he confronted the haunting directly. As he stood beneath the Gaden door, a wild, fiery-red fox suddenly rushed out, passed between the robed friar’s legs, and raced across the meadow toward a steep Rübital. The Father now banished to that place the spirit—or whatever it was—that had disturbed the livestock, and ordered the people to erect a cross there.
