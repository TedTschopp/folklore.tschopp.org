A maiden lived there who was devout and upright and owned an estate of her own, free and clear, for her parents had died. Two young men sought her hand. She chose the one who seemed best to her. Early after the bridal night, before it was fully daylight and while his young wife still slept, the man rose. He looked out the window and thought he saw his rejected rival lying dead before the house. Although it was only a deception, the sight frightened him so greatly that he dressed and went far away toward Welschland without telling his wife or anyone else. In Welschland he came to a good farmer and served him faithfully and honestly for 24 years, but never said anything about his fate. His wife searched in vain for her vanished husband and was left with great heartache. At last, after the 24 years, a powerful urge to go home came over the man. He told his master that he had to return and could no longer remain there. The master answered that, however reluctant he was to let him go, he could not stand in his way. As wages, he gave him a wrapped Zelter and instructed him not to open it until he had cause for joy; to think twice before he did anything; not to let himself be dismissed at the first refusal when he wanted something; and not to punish in anger. When the man reached home, he did not enter his wife’s house but went to the neighbor’s and sat beside a window from which he could see directly across into his own home. There he saw his wife magnificently dressed and watched her repeatedly kiss and embrace a young man. The sight made his blood so hot that every moment he thought he would go over and stab both his wife and the boy. But he thought twice. At last the neighbor arrived and, not recognizing him, related that this woman had lost her husband immediately after the bridal night and had never heard anything of him again. She had, however, borne him a son who had studied, entered the clergy, and would celebrate his first Holy Mass here the following morning. He had just returned home from the bishop, and mother and son had embraced one another warmly. The man now thought: that is a different matter. He went across and asked permission to stay there overnight. They turned him away, but he would not let himself be dismissed; he said he wished to lie behind the stove. At last they allowed it. Little by little, he told the story of his life and revealed who he was.
