Around the castle, very pleasantly at the foot of the Eigelhard on a modest rise, lies the village of the same name. Within it is a fountain known throughout the surrounding country as the Äckelmümmelisbrunnen. Folk legend relates that in primeval times the spring was the favorite resting place of a miracle-working man named Neckelmümmeli. Informed people know very well, however, that this “Neckelmümmeli” is to be understood as none other than Saint Priminius. Coming over from the island of Reichenau, where he was born, he rested at the clear, shaded spring and gave himself to pious contemplation. Just as that island before the time of Priminius was plagued by all kinds of venomous crawling creatures, the region around Pfungen was afflicted by an immense number of poisonous mushrooms, which made raising livestock difficult. Just as Priminius freed the island of Reichenau from the venomous vermin, so he also cleansed the region around Pfungen of the troublesome and harmful mushrooms. As at Reichenau, he first erected a consecrated cross in the middle of the region and then, kneeling, offered his miraculously powerful prayer. Just as all the venomous creatures immediately took flight, so that Lake Constance was covered with them for three days and three nights, the mushrooms that spoiled the meadows of Pfungen completely lost their poisonous property, and the farmers could now send their livestock to pasture without danger.
