He faithfully kept his word, performed his work as a Senn superbly, and lived in the best of peace with his fellow laborers. There was nothing strange in his behavior except that he spoke neither more nor less than was necessary, and that every time after finishing his dairy work he sharpened his ax beneath the cellar door without ever using it. In autumn, during the homeward drive, he accompanied the procession as far as the alpine gate and took his leave there with the remark: ‘Next year I will come again if my fellow laborers also return to Gamidaur.’ This continued into the seventh year. During this long time, none of the laborers had dared ask the Senn what his sharpening of the ax meant. He had never given them an answer to idle questions. But with the passing years, their mutual familiarity had grown, so the Zusenn came out with the question he had long held back. Contrary to expectation, the Senn appeared delighted and said: ‘I have long waited eagerly for this question, and I will gladly give you the explanation you desire if you follow me up to Schwarzsee and do there what I command you.’", "The Zusenn declared himself ready for anything within his power, and they set out together at once. The lake lies in a wild high valley. When the two herdsmen had drawn near it, a roaring rose from the depths of the valley as though a thunderstorm were approaching. The lake’s black waves grew from minute to minute, then spat their whitish foam high against the cliffs along the shore, and finally flung a mighty bull onto the land. Without delay, it charged up the steep slope toward the herdsmen, bellowing loudly. Now the Senn handed his companion the ax and said: ‘If you wish to help me attain eternal salvation, then cut the left foreleg from that raging beast in no more than three blows. No harm will come to you in doing so.’ Scarcely had he spoken when the bull was already there and meant to attack him. But the courageous Zusenn forestalled it by cutting the rage-snorting animal’s leg off with one mighty blow.
