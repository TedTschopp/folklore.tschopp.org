Inside it, many secret passages run in curves and zigzags. Erdmännlein once lived here. Delicately and daintily built, they were about three feet tall, looked pale, had woolly hair, and otherwise resembled ordinary people, except that no feet could be observed on them. At night they often came into the dwellings and performed all kinds of tasks for the farmers, fed the cattle, and even helped with the washing. They appeared immediately after the Betglocke rang and often worked throughout the night; but when the morning bell rang, they suddenly vanished in the midst of half-finished work. The greatest radiance spread while they were present, and in the very next moment they could no longer be seen.", "They also brought beautifully baked cakes, and my grandmother's brother's son still remembers eating some himself in his youth. Once, on a long summer day, the Sigrist rang in the evening as early as seven o'clock, thinking that they would then allow themselves to be examined thoroughly in daylight. But he was mistaken, for although they instantly emerged from the bushes above, they turned just as quickly out of the daylight and went back into their caves. This trick must have angered them, for after that they were of no use for even the smallest task for many years. Then a young man from the village persuaded his companions to lower him on a rope from the top of the mountain wall to that cave. From above, they could then observe what it was like inside and whether true earth spirits with goat's feet lived there or merely some kind of people who had gone wild. This was done. The strongest rope, so thick that it could not possibly break, was tied around his body, and another was placed in his hands so that he could signal to those standing above when he wished to be pulled back up. The supporting rope had already been unrolled for a long time without any signal coming on the other one, and the companions grew afraid. How horrified they were when, upon winding it up quickly, the empty cord appeared in place of their companion: all its loops and knots had been cleanly untied, and it brought back only the lost man's hair and shoes.
