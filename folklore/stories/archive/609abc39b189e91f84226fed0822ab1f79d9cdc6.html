At last he told them that they should seek the castle where the little bird that told the truth lived; there they would obtain the information they desired. When early morning came, the younger of the two boys rode away on the miller’s stately black horse despite all the foster parents’ pleas and tears. Autumn passed, winter came, and spring returned, but no news came from afar to the quiet mountain mill. The little sister, now blossomed into a maiden and having nearly wept out her beautiful eyes over her missing brothers, could restrain herself no longer. She asked for the miller’s snow-white horse so that she might seek the pair of brothers. But the brothers, and with them thousands upon thousands of knights and noble maidens, had never returned because they had not heeded the warnings. “Beautiful maiden,” the old woman concluded, “if you wish to complete the task successfully and become the rescuer of those enchanted in the mountain castle, then go your way and do not look around, whatever may be called out behind you. It was not very long before she reached the foot of a steep mountain, where she had to leave her horse behind. Courageously she climbed the precipitous path, the magnificent enchanted castle before her upon the proud height. Then behind her arose a thunder like the ocean surf, and countless flattering and threatening voices called her name. But the courageous maiden did not look back and continued fürbass until she reached the castle gate, where a dreadful giant holding a mighty fir tree in his hand tried to block her way. But the maiden slipped nimbly past him and escaped safely into the castle’s interior. Wandering through the empty state apartments, her good fortune led her into a great hall where countless richly feathered birds in golden and silver cages cried out to her in the strangest yet comprehensible babel that they alone could reveal the truth. Only in one corner lay a plain gray little bird in a simple enclosure, silent and looking at the strange maiden with its clever little eyes. The almost-faltering maiden turned to it and learned from the bird that it was indeed the one that revealed the truth and that she must now follow it.
