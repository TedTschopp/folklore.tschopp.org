For the afternoon sermon, Beatus also traveled across the Wendelsee to Einigen on his cloak. The little church was already tightly packed when he arrived, and Saint Justus had begun the service. Modestly, Beat sat down at the back on the last pew so as not to disturb anyone. ‘Yet because the people were many, the little church grew terribly close, and so, man after man, they gradually began to doze.’ Our saint noticed this with sorrow. He was filled with utter horror when he saw the accursed Satan himself present in the church. He sat at the front beneath the pulpit, ‘squinting around among the folk, holding a goatskin stretched wide and a quill within his hand, and recording in a heap upon a lengthy register all who slumbered, all who dreamed, and neglected their souls’ salvation; thinking on the Judgment Day he might catch them all together, if he had it black on white who slept while the sermon was delivered.’ Beatus was in a desperate position. How gladly he would have awakened the sleepers so they would not fall into the Devil's power even if they failed to hear the Amen. But he could not disrupt the service. Because there were so many sleepers that all their names would not fit on the goatskin, Satan tried to stretch it. He gripped it with his teeth on one side and his claws on the other and tugged. Suddenly the hide tore, ‘so the Devil's head in flight struck the pulpit with a thundering blow.’ The crash resounded loudly through the church, and Beatus could not help laughing aloud at the foolish Devil's mishap. At that, all the sleepers awoke before the preaching Justus spoke the Amen.
