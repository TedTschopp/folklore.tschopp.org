One evening a white bird flew over the Alp and cried, “Furt, furt!” The pigs on the pasture pricked up their ears, listened, and raced away down the valley at a furious gallop. The people and the rest of the livestock did not; they remained. On the second evening, the same cry rang out. The Senn of one Sennten said that they ought to heed the warning and leave the Alp. But he was only laughed at. The third evening—the evening before St. James's Day—brought black clouds that gathered threateningly over the Alp. Again the white winged messenger appeared above the Alp and cried in an eerie voice, “Furt, furt!” One Senn had his Sennten gathered and left the uncanny Alp with the herd and his farmhands. A terrible thunderstorm broke out. When they looked back once more from Heitersbüel, the rock wall above the Alp burst apart. Together with the Jakobsee behind it, the wall crashed into the depths and buried the grazing ground, people, and livestock beneath rubble as high as houses.
