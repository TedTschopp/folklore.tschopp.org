In the Hornbach valley stands a farm likewise called Hornbach, near the little river of the same name. In the previous century, it is told, the Meitschi of that farm were hanging their laundry to dry at a low-lying place near the stream. A little man dressed in green came along with a Hülfterchen tied around his body. He greeted them kindly and told them to take down their laundry soon, or it might be swept away. The cheerful Meitschi smiled, but a heavy rain did indeed soon arrive, and the dark clouds gathered so densely that the mockers hurried with their laundry. A cloudburst poured over the rear part of Hornbach. The stream swelled, and the inhabitants poured from their houses to restrain its feared devastation if possible. To the washerwomen's terror, an enormous snake emerged from the first mighty wave, and upon it sat the same little green man to whom they had spoken that morning. He had fitted the Hülfterchen upon the snake and rode away over the water. Upon reaching the grass, the snake leaped across it and entered the water again on the other side. The stream raged terribly.
