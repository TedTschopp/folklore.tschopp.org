A path climbs diagonally across a steep slope to its summit, where a castle is said once to have stood, and leads to a pilgrimage cross. The inhabitants of the surrounding settlements visit it in great numbers as a miracle-working cross, as do people from villages in the Black Forest on the far side of the Rhine. Beside it runs a carriage road from the Buschberg toward the Lindberg. A Wittnau farmer who died in 1854 told me the following personal experience.", "About 70 years ago, when grazing livestock on these Fricktal mountains was still customary, we herdboys were tending our cattle on the Buschberg. At bright midday, music became audible, and immediately afterward a coach approached our hay barn. It was drawn by 4 magnificent white horses. On the box sat a postilion in a blood-red uniform, every button of his coat glowing; but because the coach moved so quickly, no one could see who sat inside it. It drove toward the wooden gate in the fence enclosing our pasture. One of us obligingly ran ahead to open the gate for the coach. Thinking he had earned a tip for this service, he immediately clung to the back of the coach so that he could receive his gift at the next rock wall, where it would stop. Evening finally came, but our helpful companion had still not returned. Anxiously, we searched for him all around the mountain. At last we discovered him hanging high above our heads in the crown of an old oak, his feet tangled in its branches. He was carried home half dead and died a short time later in violent fits of fever.", "Another young man from Wittnau fared better. He saw a coach traveling across his path and hurried to catch it. Despite running, he could not quite overtake it. Just as his shirtsleeve brushed the rear wheel, it rose above the crowns of the oak and fir forest, and he was left staring after it. The next morning, he rose from bed with a greatly swollen head.
