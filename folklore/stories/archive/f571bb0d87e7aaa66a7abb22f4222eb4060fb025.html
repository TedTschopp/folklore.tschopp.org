They were all Christians. But because the Valaisans were still pagans, they killed the warriors. Only one man escaped, named Longinus. For a long time, the old man wandered through the mountains. In those days no glaciers yet barred the heights. High on the Wallisberg, where today no chamois hunter can advance farther over the ice, lay a great alp of one hundred cattle pastures, commonly called Blüemliberg. The cows there could be milked three times a day. Longinus descended across this alp, hungry, thirsty, footsore, and ragged from head to foot. The valley people took pity on him, received him kindly, and gave the fugitive room in their huts. When he had won their trust, he taught them to know the Christian God. Then the witches and Strüdlen could wipe their mouths and leave. The masonry on the Burgbühl that had been used for sacrifices to idols fell into ruin. The people had become happy in the new faith.
