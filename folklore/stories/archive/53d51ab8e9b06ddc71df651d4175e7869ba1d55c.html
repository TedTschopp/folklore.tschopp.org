Her arduous path led her past the Senn’s hut on Urden, where she intended to rest. The devout pilgrim was tired and tormented by hunger and thirst. Exhausted, she stopped beside a herdsman’s hut where a coarse, miserly Senn held sway. In God’s name she asked him for a drink. The Senn rebuked her harshly and showed her the door. But she was too tired to continue and sat lamenting upon a bench. Then the inhuman man opened the door and cried: ‘Wait, old woman—you shall have enough!’ He milked his red cow, threw rennet into the milk vessel, and handed it to the old woman with mocking laughter. The little mother drank, felt strengthened, and left the hut with words of blessing. But as she climbed upward along the mountain ridge, she felt savage pains. She sensed that her end was near and recalled with horror the words, ‘You shall have enough.’ Dying, she called down God’s punishment upon the godless man, commended her soul to the heavenly Father, and expired. Soon afterward the ground all around shook, and the earth swallowed the villain together with his possessions. The place filled with water, and that is Lake Urden. Every seven years the lake roars wildly; the Senn can be seen within it milking a red cow and then sinking groaning back into the abyss. According to popular belief, whenever the lake is violently disturbed it foretells severe storms or a sudden onset of cold. The lake has one deep, black-looking place.
