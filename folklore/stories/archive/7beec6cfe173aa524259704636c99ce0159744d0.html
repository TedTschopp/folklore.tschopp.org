Around the year 922 it was subject to a King Rudolf. The king's wife was named Bertha, a beautiful, kindhearted lady well instructed in all things. Although a queen, she sewed all her husband's clothes herself, rode through the country every year, and taught the women to cultivate gardens, plant hemp and flax, spin, and sew. Whenever she rode out, she always had her Kunkel and spindle with her and spun even on horseback. In those days, as today, the flax was tied to a Kunkel; then it was twisted into thread with the fingers and wound onto a spindle. At the same time, however, there lived in Wälschland a maiden who spun the finest thread far and wide. Once she had finished a skein of yarn, the finest she had ever spun, and no one would pay her what it deserved, she gave it to the queen as a gift. Because the queen was herself a good spinner and knew the yarn's value, she delighted in the fine thread. ‘You have spun finely, my daughter!’ said the queen. ‘Therefore go and tie the thread to the latch of your house door, and as much land as you can encircle with the yarn shall belong to you and your family.’ The girl gave thanks and won for herself and her parents the finest and largest estate in the region.
