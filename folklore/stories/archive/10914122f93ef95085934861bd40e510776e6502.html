Regrettably, it was demolished in that year to enlarge the cattle-market square located there. The tower is said to have been very old and undoubtedly to have dated from Roman times. It had colossal walls 3.3 meters thick. Older men could still remember that a wooden stairway led up the outside to an opening two-thirds of the way up the tower and that a tall, pointed roof covered it.\nWomen from the Uznach region who had been declared witches are said to have been imprisoned in this tower.\nThe woman known as the Rösslerin, who is said to have lived in a small house on the Burg, was also regarded as one.\nShe was said to send violent storms over the area, especially over the fields known as the Burgeräcker in the “Weinrebe.” When she vigorously stirred pebbles around in her pewter plate, a severe hailstorm was said to arrive—and the more stones she stirred in the plate, the greater it would be.\nFerd. Not only women but also men and children were accused of Trudnerei or witchcraft, into which they were said to have entered in league with hell. When arrested, they first protested their innocence.
