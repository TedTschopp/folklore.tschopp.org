Now listen! You know the Martis-Tobel and the old walls? They belong to a castle in the Schaugehofer Ried by the Goldach; that was Rappestäh Castle, and it belonged to the Mötteli. There were two brothers in St. Gallen who became rich in trade; one was named Hans and the other Ruodolf, and they were stately men! They bought Rappestäh and received permission from the emperor to write their name from the castle, “Rappestäh, genannt Mötteli.” This happened in 1483. It had first been called Sulzberg. It alone remained standing among all those castles the Appenzellers ruined, and it has continued to be called Möttelischloß down to the present day. All sorts of things are said about it. A treasure is said to be buried there and guarded by two maidens who remain forever young and beautiful. If one knocks at midnight, a dog barks terribly and makes a frightful rattle with chains. If one is not afraid and knocks again, the two maidens approach in white, beautiful garments, wearing pretty red shoes. But both are chained, and they cry and beg movingly and wish someone would kiss them. That would bring them freedom, and otherwise there is no means by which they can be redeemed or the treasure obtained. That is an evil condition indeed, and no one has yet dared it or had the Güraschi. The dog is a terrible Otier; it always stands beside the maidens, and whoever sees it, let him not speak! If kissing alone were enough, I think the little maidens would long ago have escaped the chains and become free! But no one has yet had the heart. Therefore they must retreat again, back to the place where they were, and again weep and howl enough to move a stone to pity, and continue guarding the treasure as they have for centuries.", "What do you now think of the story?
